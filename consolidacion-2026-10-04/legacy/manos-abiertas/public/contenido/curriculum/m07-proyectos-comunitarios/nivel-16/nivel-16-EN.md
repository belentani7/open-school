# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 16
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what the mission of the project is and what it is for.
- Write a short, clear and truthful mission.
- Know that the mission guides the decisions and the work of the team.
- Get to know the difference between the mission and the concrete objectives.
- Make the mission the lighthouse of the project in moments of doubt.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Mission | The sentence that says what we are, why we exist and what we want to leave. |
| Vision | The dream of how we want the neighbourhood to be in the future. |
| Objective | The concrete goal with a date that brings us closer to the mission. |
| Lighthouse | What guides the road when there are doubts or fog. |
| Values | What we consider important and are not willing to lose. |
| Identity | What makes our project this project and not another. |

## 📚 Main lesson
After getting to know the neighbourhood, the moment has come to say who we are. Every project has a mission, even if it has never written it down in words. The mission is the sentence that says what we are, why we exist and what we want to leave. It is our letter of introduction and our guide.

The mission is not an abstract idea: it is a concrete, short and truthful sentence. "Bringing the neighbours of the neighbourhood together around a community garden" is a mission. "Doing good" is not: it is pretty, but it says nothing. A good mission fits in one sentence and fits in the heart: everyone can repeat it from memory.

To write the mission, we ask three questions. First: what are we? A community garden, a neighbourhood association, a festival group. Second: why do we exist? So the neighbours get to know each other, to have fresh vegetables, to celebrate together. Third: what do we want to leave? A more united neighbourhood, a green plot, children with a place to play.

The mission is not the same as the objective. The objective is the concrete goal with a date: "we will sow the first plot in March". The mission is the horizon: "we want the neighbourhood to meet at the garden". The objectives are the steps; the mission is the road. Without objectives, the mission is a dream without feet; without a mission, the objectives are feet without direction.

The mission guides the decisions. When a doubt arrives, we ask: does this idea bring us closer to our mission or take us away from it? A project with the mission of bringing neighbours together does not say yes to a solitary activity, even if it is good. The mission is the filter that separates what suits us from what distracts us.

The mission also unites the team. When everyone knows the mission, everyone rows in the same direction. The new participant learns the mission on the first day, like someone learning the name of a house. The mission is the roof where we all live and the table where we all sit.

The vision is the brother of the mission: the dream of how we want the neighbourhood to be in the future. If the mission says what we are, the vision says what we dream: "a neighbourhood where nobody feels alone". The vision is the distant star; the mission is the road to reach the star. Together they give the project direction and hope.

The values are the third piece. The values are what we consider important and are not willing to lose: respect, gratitude, a given word, an open door. The mission says what we do; the values say how we do it. A project can reach the mission and lose the values on the way, and then it is no longer the same project.

Identity is born from the mission, the vision and the values. Identity is what makes our project this project and not another. Two garden projects can have the same mission and be very different: one with the values of celebration, the other with the values of silence. Identity is our signature.

The mission is written once and always revisited. In moments of doubt, when the project seems to lose itself, the team returns to the mission and asks: are we still what we said we were? The mission is the lighthouse in the fog: it does not change place, and that is why it guides us.

The mission is also communicated. It is written on a sheet, at the entrance of the space, in the header of the group. When the community reads it, it knows who we are without knowing us. The mission is our oldest and most truthful poster: it is not put on the walls, it is put in the heart of the neighbourhood.

Writing the mission is a team job, not one person's. We meet, each one says what they think, and together we find the sentence that everyone feels as their own. An imposed mission is not a mission: it is a decree. A mission built by everyone is a pact.

In this level we will write the mission of our project: the three questions, the short sentence, the values and the vision. And we will hang it where everyone can see it, so no day begins without knowing where we are going.

Let us remember: the project is not only what we do, it is also who we are. The mission is our proper name. Those who know their name never get lost in the crowd.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The garden's mission.** The team writes the mission together: "Bringing the neighbours of the neighbourhood together around a community garden, so the families have fresh vegetables and a place to meet." Everyone repeats it from memory. When someone proposes closing the garden to the public, the mission reminds them that the project is for everyone.
2. **The festival's vision.** The vision of the festival team: "a neighbourhood where every summer is celebrated together, with music and food from everyone." With the vision in mind, each festival is a step towards that future. Decisions are made looking at that star.
3. **The values on the table.** The project writes its values on a sheet: respect, a given word, an open door and gratitude. When there is a conflict, the sheet comes to the table and the words remind the way. Values are not hung up: they are lived, but the sheet helps to remember them.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Bring the team together and answer the three questions: what are we, why do we exist, what do we want to leave.
Step 2: Write the answers in simple words, without pretty sentences.
Step 3: Join the answers into a short sentence. Read it out loud: does it fit in one breath?
Step 4: Check if it is truthful: does it really reflect what the project does and wants to do?
Step 5: Write the vision: how do you want the neighbourhood to be in five years?
Step 6: Write the three values you are not willing to lose.
Step 7: Write the final mission on a nice sheet and hang it in the space. In a meeting, everyone reads it out loud.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the mission? a) The sentence that says what we are, why we exist and what we want to leave. b) The list of tasks. c) The budget.
2. What is the difference between the mission and the objective? a) They are the same. b) The objective is the concrete goal; the mission is the horizon. c) The mission belongs to the coordinator.
3. What is the mission for in moments of doubt? a) To complicate. b) To guide the decisions like a lighthouse. c) To hang on the wall.
4. What are the values? a) What we consider important and do not lose. b) The project's money. c) The dates of the calendar.
5. What is the vision? a) The dream of how we want the neighbourhood in the future. b) An idea from another team. c) The project's poster.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-b, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The mission tells the truth: it does not promise what the project is not.
- The mission is built with the whole team, it is not imposed.
- The values are not negotiated: respect and a given word are not lost on the way.
- The mission serves the community, not the group's pride.
- When the mission and the values clash, the mission waits: the values come first.

The mission says who we are, the vision dreams where we want to arrive and the values guard our road. With the mission in the heart and on the wall, the project has direction and identity.
