# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 20
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Canopy
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to build and take care of the project's network of contacts.
- Get to know the people and entities that surround our community.
- Keep the contacts alive: visits, messages and thanks.
- Share resources, information and support within the network.
- Make the project a meeting point of the whole community.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Network | The group of people and entities that know each other and help each other. |
| Contact | The person or entity with whom the project has a relationship. |
| Node | Each person or entity of the network, linked to the others. |
| Bond | The thread that joins two nodes: a trust, a favour, a common project. |
| Contacts agenda | The organised list of the people and entities of the network. |
| Brokering | Putting two people or entities in contact with each other, so they help each other. |

## 📚 Main lesson
We already saw, in level 13, that partnerships multiply the project. Now we take a further step: the network. The network is the group of people and entities that know each other, help each other and share. It is not a cold list of names: it is a living fabric of relationships. Each person in the network is a node; each trust is a bond.

The network starts small and grows slowly. First, those we already know: the participants, the neighbours, the association, the baker, the teacher. Then, each one brings their acquaintances: the baker knows the blacksmith, the teacher knows the nurse at the health centre. That way, thread by thread, the fabric grows without leaving the neighbourhood.

Building the network is, above all, getting to know people. You do not get to know someone by sending a form: you get to know them by talking. A visit to the shop, a coffee with the new neighbour, a conversation at the school gate. The network grows in the look and in the word, not on the screen. True relationships need time and presence.

The contacts agenda orders the network. On a sheet or on the phone, each contact is written down: the name, what they do, how to reach that person, and when you last spoke. The agenda is not to file away: it is to take care of. A contact that is never visited is a thread that breaks.

Taking care of the network is the secret. The network is not built once: it is fed. A visit, a phone call, a message remembering someone: the small gestures keep the threads alive. And the thanks, always: after each help, a word. What is thanked is repeated; what is forgotten grows cold.

The network serves to share. Resources: whoever has a tool lends it. Information: whoever knows about a competition, warns. Support: whoever knows someone with influence, introduces them. The network is a help market where the currency is trust. What one does not have, another has; and what one knows, another learns.

Brokering is an art of the network. Brokering is putting two people in contact because they can help each other: "the baker is looking for cheap flour and I know a shopkeeper who sells it". Brokering multiplies the network without effort: each bridge you build between two nodes creates two new relationships. Those who broker enrich the whole fabric.

The network also has its rules of respect. A contact is not used carelessly: you introduce yourself politely, explain the reason and give thanks. You do not ask favour after favour without giving something in return. The network lives on balance: everyone gives and everyone receives. Those who only receive, exhaust the network; those who also give, fatten it.

The network is a door to the outside of the neighbourhood. A contact at the health centre, an acquaintance at the town hall, a university professor who studies neighbourhoods: each open door brings new resources and ideas. The neighbourhood is not an island: it is part of a city, and the network links the project to the whole city.

The network strengthens on difficult days. When the project needs a hand, a material, some advice, the network responds. The project is not alone: it is surrounded by people who know it and want it. The network is the project's big family, the one that appears when the door opens.

The network is also kept: the agenda is updated, the contacts are visited, the bonds are renewed. A network that is not cared for ages and grows cold; a cared-for network flourishes. And the network is not measured by the number of names: it is measured by the quality of the trusts. A network of ten faithful people is worth more than a hundred acquaintances.

In this level we will build our network: the contacts agenda, the first visits and the first bridges. We will take care of the threads we already have and weave the ones we are missing. We will make our project a central node, where the whole neighbourhood meets.

Let us remember: the network is like a garden. Nobody plants it in one day, and nobody abandons it without it withering. Relationships are planted, watered with visits and the help is harvested on days of need. A project with a good network is a project with roots in the whole community.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The project's agenda.** The team writes the contacts on a sheet: the association, the baker, the teacher, the nurse. They note when they last spoke and when the next visit is due. Each month, a participant visits two contacts and brings news to the group.
2. **The bridge between the baker and the school.** The project knows that the baker is looking for a place to give the end-of-day bread and that the school is looking for bread for the children's breakfast. It introduces them. A bridge is born that feeds the children and dignifies the baker. The network created a relationship that not even the project had to maintain.
3. **The visit to the health centre.** A participant visits the nurse at the centre to talk about a healthy eating workshop. The nurse accepts and offers to give a session at the garden. The network brought new knowledge to the project, and the project opened a door to the health centre.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Write your contacts agenda: the names of the people and entities the project already knows.
Step 2: Next to each name, write what they do and how to reach that person.
Step 3: Visit a contact this week: a shop, the association, the school. Talk, without hurry.
Step 4: Look for a bridge: two people who can help each other. Introduce them.
Step 5: Note in the agenda when you spoke with each person and when the next visit is due.
Step 6: Thank a help you recently received: a word, a visit, a message.
Step 7: Review the agenda once a month: the network is cared for with constancy, not with haste.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the network? a) The group of people and entities that know each other and help each other. b) The WhatsApp group. c) The list of tasks.
2. How does the network grow? a) With forms. b) By getting to know people, talking and visiting. c) Only on the internet.
3. What is the contacts agenda for? a) To file names away. b) To take care of the relationships and know when to visit. c) To show the town hall.
4. What is brokering? a) Putting two people in contact so they help each other. b) Sending messages. c) Telling secrets.
5. What keeps the network alive? a) The visits, the messages and the thanks. b) The silence. c) The social networks.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-b, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The network lives on balance: everyone gives and everyone receives.
- The contacts are used with care: introduction, reason and thanks.
- You do not ask favour after favour without giving something in return.
- The relationships of the network respect each person's privacy.
- The network serves the community, not the interest of a few.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
The network is a living fabric of relationships: getting to know, taking care, sharing and brokering. With the agenda in hand and the visits up to date, the project is a node where the neighbourhood meets. In the next level we will learn leadership: the ways of leading, the role of the leader as a servant and how to make the other leaders grow.
