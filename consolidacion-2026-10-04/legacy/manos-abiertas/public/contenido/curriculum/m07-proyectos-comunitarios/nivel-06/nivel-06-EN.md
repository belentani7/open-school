# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 06
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what roles in a team are and what they are for.
- Learn about the essential roles: coordinator, person responsible for each task and participant.
- Discover that all roles have equal value and are respected.
- Learn to share out the work fairly and in a balanced way.
- Strengthen the team with trust, a given word and gratitude.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Role | The function each person has within the team. |
| Coordinator | The person who helps bring order to the work, brings the team together and shares information. |
| Person responsible | Someone who takes charge of a concrete task until it is done. |
| Participant | Someone who helps with their strength and their time in the tasks. |
| Share out | To distribute the tasks among the people in a fair way. |
| Trust | Believing that each person does what they promise. |

## 📚 Main lesson
So far we have learned to think the project: objective, plan, stages and dates. But projects are not done alone: they are done between people. And when there are several people, each one must be placed in their spot, with their function. That is what we call roles.

Roles are not empty titles or a way to give orders to others. They are a way for nobody to get lost and nobody to do everything. It is like the kitchen at a family celebration: one person chops, another cooks, another sets the table. Each one does their part and, in the end, the meal is ready.

In community life there are three basic roles that repeat. The first is the coordinator: the person who helps bring order to the work. The coordinator is not the boss, nor the owner of the project: they are a companion who brings cohesion. They bring the team together, share information, remind about the dates and make sure nobody is left out. Their main tool is not a loud voice, but organisation.

The coordinator is chosen with a simple criterion: the person who best brings the group together. Sometimes it is the one with the most ease, other times the one with the most availability. It can also change from one project to another: today they coordinate one, tomorrow another. That way everyone learns and nobody gets tired of always being the same.

The second role is the person responsible for each task. Each task in the plan has its owner: the person who commits to making it happen. "Buying the seeds" has a person responsible; "telling the neighbours" too. The person responsible does not have to do everything alone: they ask for help when they need it. But it is them who is asked how things are going.

The third role is the participant. Most people are participants in most projects: they help with their hands, their time and their joy. It is a role as dignified as any other. Without participants there is no festival, no garden, nothing. The participant does not stand with arms crossed watching others: they join in where they can and how they can.

A team works when roles cross over and complete each other. The coordinator does not do everything; the person responsible does not give orders; the participant does not obey in silence, but takes part with opinion and initiative. Good work is born from respect between roles, not from chains of command.

There is a golden rule in sharing out the work: share with fairness and balance. Not everyone has the same time or the same strength, and that is normal. Fairness is not giving everyone the same, but giving each person what they can give. Those who can give more, give more; those who can give less, give less, without guilt and without burden.

To share out well, first you make the list of tasks and then each person marks the ones that fit best with their life. The ones left over are shared out in conversation, looking for volunteers. A task is never assigned to someone without asking them first. A task accepted with pleasure is done with pleasure.

The team only moves forward if there is trust. Trust is built with a word given and a word kept. When someone says "I will take care of this" and then does it, the team grows. When they cannot keep their word, they say so in time and the team reorganises. Hiding is what destroys trust; speaking up in time is what keeps it alive.

You also have to take care of words. The team is a garden and words are the water or the salt. A word of thanks after a finished task is worth more than a month of criticism. Giving thanks is not false courtesy: it is recognising what each person has given. When the work is thanked, we want to work again.

Clear roles also avoid misunderstandings. If everyone knows who does what, nobody thinks the other will do it and nobody does two jobs at the same time. It is like at the family table: when you share out what each one brings, in the end there is a bit of everything and nothing is missing.

In this level we will learn to put names and roles to our plan. We will choose a coordinator, assign people responsible and commit participants. We will turn our list of tasks into a map of people.

Let us always remember: the project belongs to everyone and is for everyone. Roles serve to take care of the project and the people, not to give importance to some and forget others. A happy team is a project that is already halfway there.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The summer festival.** At the festival, the coordinator is Maria, because she knows everyone. Each task has a person responsible: Pedro does the music, Ana the food, José the permits. The other participants help in each spot. In the end, everyone gives thanks and the festival was a success.
2. **The community garden.** The coordinator changes every season. In spring Luis coordinates, in autumn Carmen coordinates. Each plot has a person responsible and the participants join the working sessions. When someone cannot come, they let the group know and another covers the spot.
3. **The cleaning campaign.** A neighbourhood wants to clean the square. The group chooses a coordinator, divides the zones between people responsible and invites the neighbours. Families take part with brooms and bags. At midday, all together, there is a snack to thank everyone for their effort.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take your plan from level 05.
Step 2: Write the list of tasks in the left column.
Step 3: Next to each task, write the name of a person responsible. Ask first and respect it if someone says no.
Step 4: Think about who could be the coordinator of the project. Write the name and why.
Step 5: Write the list of participants who help with their time.
Step 6: Check the balance: is anyone carrying too many tasks? Share out again if necessary.
Step 7: Arrange with the team a word of thanks after each finished task. It can be in the group, with a simple and sincere message.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a person's role in the team? a) A way to give orders to others. b) The function each person has in the project. c) A title of honour.
2. What does the coordinator do? a) Does all the work alone. b) Gives orders without consulting anyone. c) Helps bring order, brings the team together and shares information.
3. Who takes charge of a task? a) The person who promises to make it happen. b) Someone who only does it if they are asked many times. c) Always the coordinator.
4. How do you share out the work fairly? a) By giving everyone exactly the same. b) By giving each person what they can give. c) By giving the person who complains less.
5. What builds trust in the team? a) Promising without keeping your word. b) Hiding problems. c) Giving your word and keeping it.

Answers: 1-b, 2-c, 3-a, 4-b, 5-c.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The coordinator does not give orders: they serve. Their job is to bring order, not to give orders.
- No task is imposed: always ask before assigning.
- All roles have equal value: coordinating is not more than participating.
- Gratitude is not an extra: it is a duty of care between companions.
- Anyone who sees an injustice in the sharing out has the duty to say so, with respect and in time.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Roles put each person in their spot: a coordinator who brings order, people responsible for each task and participants who lend their strength. Sharing out fairly, keeping your word and giving thanks are the keys to a united team. In the next level we will learn to speak well in public and before the group: preparing a clear message, with a beginning, middle and end, and beating the nerves.
