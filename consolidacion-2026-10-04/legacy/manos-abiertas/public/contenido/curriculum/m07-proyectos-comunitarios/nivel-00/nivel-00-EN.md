# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 00
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a project is with examples from everyday life.
- Tell a project apart from a simple daily task.
- Recognize that we have already done projects at home without calling them that.
- Discover what a community project is and who can take part.
- Lose the fear of the word "project" and of the technology that comes with it.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Project | A piece of work with a goal, a plan and an end, like organizing a family meal or fixing up a room. |
| Goal | The result we want to reach when we finish the work. |
| Plan | The list of steps we will follow to reach the goal. |
| Team | A group of people who work together to achieve something in common. |
| Community | The people who share a neighbourhood, a town or a hobby. |
| Resource | What we need for the project: time, money, tools or help from others. |

## 📚 Main lesson
The word "project" can sound like something big, reserved for experts, like building a building or launching a rocket. But the truth is much closer to home: we all do projects in our daily lives without realizing it. Fixing up a room, organizing a family meal or planting a vegetable garden are projects.

Let us think of the kitchen metaphor. Washing the dishes is a task: a concrete action that you do and you are done. In contrast, preparing a meal for the whole family is a project: you have to think about the menu, buy the ingredients, work out the time, cook each dish and set the table. Several tasks organized around a common goal. That is a project.

Every project has three important parts. The first is the goal: what we want to achieve. The second is the plan: the steps we are going to follow. The third is the end: the moment when we look at the result and say "we did it".

Let us use the garden as an example. If we want to have tomatoes, the goal is clear: pick ripe tomatoes. The plan is the steps: prepare the soil, sow, water, pull out the weeds and wait. The end comes in summer, when we fill the basket. Without a goal, a plan or an end, there would be no project: just a messy patch of earth.

The same happens at home. Fixing up a room is not just painting a wall: it is emptying, cleaning, choosing the furniture, deciding the colour and putting everything back in order. Anyone who has ever done this already knows a lot about projects, even if they have never used the word.

Now comes an important difference: a project can be personal or community-based. A personal project is done by ourselves alone, like tidying our own kitchen. A community project is done by several people together for the good of many. Examples: the neighbourhood party, cleaning the square, a reading club at the library, or helping older people with their phones.

In a community project, the community is both the starting point and the goal. It is born from a need or a shared dream: the park is neglected, older people are lonely, young people have nowhere to meet. And it ends when that need is addressed, even if only a little.

You may think: "I do not know anything about technology." Take it easy: in this module you do not need to be an expert. Technology will be an ally, just like the oven is for whoever cooks. First we will learn to think the project through and, little by little, we will add simple tools.

Another common fear is "I do not have time". No project needs all your time. Projects are built in small moments: half an hour here, an hour there. What matters is not the number of hours, but consistency, like watering the plants a little every day.

You also do not need money to start. Many projects begin with what you already have at home: a phone, a notebook, a table and the desire to help. Money comes later, if needed, and we will learn how to look for it in future levels.

The key of a community project is that nobody has to do everything. Each person contributes what they can: hands to paint, a car to transport, a voice to spread the news, or a head to organize. The sum of small things becomes something big.

In this first level we are only going to look and recognize, like someone visiting a new house: first you observe, then you touch, and at the end you live in it. There is nothing to build yet.

Let us do a mental exercise: think of your neighbourhood or your town. Is there something you would like to improve? It could be a broken bench, a dirty sidewalk, a group of neighbours who do not know each other. That small annoyance is already the seed of a project.

In the next levels we will learn to turn that seed into something real: to talk with other people, to organize ourselves, to communicate, and to make the project last over time. All at a calm pace and with simple words.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The association's meal.** Elisa and her neighbours organize a community meal in the town centre every month. They have a goal (bringing the neighbours together), a plan (menu, shopping, cooking) and an end (the day of the meal). Without knowing it, they have been doing projects for years.
2. **The corner garden.** Roberto saw an empty, dirty plot on his street. He gathered three neighbours, they asked the town hall for permission, and now they grow vegetables to share among the families of the neighbourhood. A small dream became a community project.
3. **The phone class.** Carmen, aged 67, teaches her older neighbours how to use their phones at the library. Her goal is that nobody is left alone without being able to communicate. Her project needs no money, only patience and a table.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Get a notebook or a piece of paper and a pen.
Step 2: Write at the top: "Things I would like to improve in my community".
Step 3: Think about your neighbourhood, your town or your building and write down three ideas. It does not matter if they seem small.
Step 4: Choose one of the three ideas. Ask yourself: what would I like to achieve? Write it in one sentence, for example: "That the square is clean and has benches".
Step 5: Next to it, write three steps that come to mind to achieve it. It does not matter if they are not perfect: it is your first plan.
Step 6: Keep the paper. This paper will be the seed of the project we will work with in the next levels.
Step 7: Discuss the idea with a person you trust: a family member, a neighbour or a friend. Ask what they think. Listening to others is already teamwork.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a project? a) Only building buildings. b) A piece of work with a goal, a plan and an end. c) Washing the dishes.
2. Which of these is a project? a) Preparing a family meal. b) Opening the fridge. c) Turning on the light.
3. What is a community project? a) A project done by one single person. b) A project done by several people for the good of many. c) A project by big companies.
4. Do you need money to start a project? a) Yes, always. b) No, you start with what you have and with desire. c) Only if it is about technology.
5. Who can take part in a community project? a) Only experts. b) Only young people. c) Anyone, contributing what they can.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-c.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- A community project is born from respect: it is done with the community, not over it. Before acting, it is wise to ask the neighbours what they truly need.
- Let us not make the project something belonging to one person. The community belongs to everyone, and that is how it must feel.
- Projects must be transparent: what we will do, with which resources and for whom. Clarity avoids misunderstandings and rumours.
- Let us respect other people's spaces: a plot, a park or a common room has an owner or a person in charge. Asking for permission is always the first step.
- If we use technology to organize ourselves, let us take care of the data of the team and the community: no photos without permission and no sharing of personal information.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
A project is a piece of work with a goal, a plan and an end, and we all do projects in daily life without calling them that. A community project is done by several people together for the good of many, starting from a small need or dream of the neighbourhood. You do not need to be an expert, nor to have spare time, nor money: an idea, desire and consistency are enough. In the next level we will learn how the people who form a team work together and why each one of them matters.
