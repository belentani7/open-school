# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 23
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Fruit
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn what the sustainability of a community project is.
- Get to know the ways of guaranteeing resources without depending on anyone.
- Take care of the team so the project does not run out of strength.
- Prepare the future of the project beyond the people who started it.
- Build a project that lasts and gives fruit for many years.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Sustainability | The ability of the project to maintain itself, over time. |
| Resources | What the project needs: money, materials, time and people. |
| Self-financing | Earning its own money, without depending on anyone. |
| Rotation | Renewing the people and the tasks so the project can breathe. |
| Continuity | Passing the knowledge and the responsibilities to the next generations. |
| Legacy | What the project leaves for the community, forever. |

## 📚 Main lesson
The project has grown: it has roots, trunk, branches and canopy. Now we learn to make it last: sustainability. A project that lasts is not the one with the most luck: it is the one that knows how to take care of its resources, its people and its future.

Sustainability is the ability of the project to maintain itself, over time. A sustainable project does not live on the enthusiasm of one week nor on the sacrifice of one person. It lives from what it cultivates: from its resources, its team and its memory.

Resources are what the project needs to live: money, materials, time and people. Without resources, the best project dies; with poorly cared-for resources, it also dies. Sustainability teaches us to know our resources, to spend them with care and to renew them in time.

Money is one of the resources and needs care. There is self-financing: the project earns its own money. A small sale, a workshop, a help for a service. It is not shame: it is dignity. A project that earns what it spends does not depend on anyone's goodwill.

Materials also ask for care: the tools, the seeds, the space. What is cared for, lasts; what is neglected, gets ruined. A tool put away, a seed preserved, a room fixed in time: small gestures that make the project last for years.

Time is a precious resource, and people too. Sustainability cannot erase the people: if the team runs out of strength, the project dies. That is why the tasks rotate: nobody always does the same thing, nobody carries everything alone. Rotation renews the team and shares the load.

Continuity is the heart of sustainability. Knowledge cannot leave with the one who created it: it has to pass to those who come. Those who know how to plant, teach how to plant; those who know how to lead, prepare those who lead. Shared knowledge does not divide: it multiplies.

The project also needs to rest. A community that does not stop, gets tired; a project that does not rest, gets ruined. The periods of pause are not laziness: they are part of sustainability. Those who know how to stop, also know how to continue.

Sustainability looks at the long term. It does not only ask "what do we do this year?", it asks "what will we leave in ten years?". The legacy is what the project leaves for the community, forever: the knowledge, the trust, the habit of coming together and deciding together.

A sustainable project is like a fruit tree: it needs roots, soil, water and someone to care for it. It does not give fruit every day, but it gives it every year, and it feeds many people. What we learn to cultivate with patience, gives us fruit with generosity.

Sustainability also belongs to everyone: it is not the problem of a single person. Each participant is part of what makes it last: taking care of a tool, teaching a gesture, arriving on time. A sustainable project is the sum of a thousand small cares that nobody sees.

In this level we will look at our resources and our future: how we guarantee what we need, how we take care of our people and how we leave our memory. We will build a project that does not end when enthusiasm ends: a project that gives fruit, year after year, for our community.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Self-financing.** The community organises a small bread sale twice a month. What they earn pays for the seeds and the tools. The project no longer depends on anyone for the essentials.
2. **The rotation of tasks.** The same participant always made the posters. Now the tasks rotate every three months: everyone learns and nobody runs out of strength. When someone is missing, there is always someone who knows.
3. **The passed-on memory.** The oldest participant teaches the youngest ones to prune the trees and preserve the seeds. Their knowledge does not get lost with them: it stays planted in the others.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Make the list of the project's resources: what you have and what is missing.
Step 2: Think about self-financing: what can the project earn on its own?
Step 3: Take care of one material this week: fix it, put it away, preserve it.
Step 4: Review the tasks: who carries more? Rotate them.
Step 5: Teach someone a gesture you know how to do well.
Step 6: Think about rest: when will the team stop, without guilt?
Step 7: Write the legacy: what do you want to leave in ten years?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is sustainability? a) The project living only on enthusiasm. b) The ability of the project to maintain itself over time. c) A project that lasts one week.
2. What is self-financing? a) Depending on donations. b) Earning its own money. c) Not spending anything.
3. What is rotation for? a) So nobody learns. b) To share the load and renew the team. c) To complicate things.
4. What is continuity? a) Passing the knowledge to those who come. b) Keeping the knowledge only for the founders. c) Letting the project end.
5. What is the legacy? a) What is spent. b) What the project leaves for the community forever. c) A debt.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The project lives from its resources, but not at the cost of its people.
- Earning its own money is dignity, not shame.
- Knowledge is shared: nobody takes away what they learned alone.
- Rest is part of the work.
- The legacy belongs to everyone: the project leaves more than what it consumed.
