# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 19
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to keep the project's memory.
- Make the project's diary: the stories, the dates and the people.
- Collect photographs and documents of the road travelled.
- Remember what we have learned and leave it in writing.
- Leave the memory for those who arrive and those who stay.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Memory | The group of memories and documents that keeps the project's history. |
| Diary | The notebook where the days, the stories and the progress are written down. |
| Chronicle | The short account of a day or a moment of the project. |
| Archive | The place where the papers, the photos and the documents are kept. |
| Testimony | The word of a person who lived the project. |
| Legacy | What the project leaves for the future, for the neighbourhood and for others. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Projects grow, change and sometimes end. But there is one thing that is not lost if we keep it: the memory. The project's memory is the group of stories, dates, photographs and documents that tell the road travelled. A project without memory is like a river without a memory of having run: it passes, and nobody knows where it went.

The memory begins with a notebook: the project's diary. In the diary the important days are written down: when the plot was cleaned, when the first tomatoes were sown, when the rain came and helped us or delayed us. You do not need to write much: three sentences a week are enough. The diary is the heart of the memory.

The diary is written with truth, with its victories and its difficulties. The victories are celebrated and kept; the difficulties are also written down, because they teach. The diary is not to embellish the road: it is to remember it. Years later, rereading it, the team will relive the days of work and celebration.

The photographs are the other half of the memory. One photograph of each important moment: the empty plot, the first plants, the full festival, the group working. The photos are taken on the day, with the phone, and kept in a folder or in a project cloud. Photos speak, and what the photos show nobody forgets.

The chronicles give voice to the days. A chronicle is the short account of a moment: "today eight of us came and we cleaned the first plot". The chronicle can be written by whoever lived the moment, in the group, after the session. The chronicles are gathered in the diary and form the story, chapter by chapter.

The testimonies give voice to the people. A sentence from a participant, a memory from a neighbour, a story from the oldest member of the team: the testimonies are the human heart of the memory. They are collected with a conversation, like the interviews in level 15, and kept in writing. People pass, but their words remain.

The archive keeps the papers. The event posters, the flyers, the clippings from the neighbourhood newspaper, the meeting minutes: everything is kept in a box, ordered by date. The archive is the physical memory of the project. A cardboard box, clean and marked, is enough to begin.

The memory is also organised. A sheet with the important dates, year by year, is the skeleton of the story. The names of the people who passed through the project, the roles they held, the dates they arrived and left: the list of people gives the memory its face. Order turns the papers into a story.

The memory serves those who arrive. The new participant, reading the diary and seeing the photos, understands the project without needing explanations. The memory is the silent teacher: it teaches what the papers do not say. Whoever arrives at a project with a memory, arrives at a house with a history.

The memory also serves the team. When the project goes through a difficult moment, rereading the diary recalls the successes: "we have already cleaned a plot, already sown, already celebrated". The memory is the reserve of strength for the cloudy days. Those who remember where they came from know where they are going.

The memory is a legacy. What the project leaves for the future is not only what it built: it is also what it learned. The lessons, the formulas, the avoidable mistakes: everything is written, so the next ones do not start from zero. The legacy is the generosity of experience: those who learned, leave the lesson written.

In this level we will open the project's diary, collect the photographs and organise the archive. We will write the first chronicles and the first testimonies. We will make the memory a bridge between what we were and what we will be.

Let us remember: the project does not end when the last task ends. It ends when the memory is lost. Keeping the stories is watering the roots: those who remember where they came from grow with more strength. The memory is the seed of what is still to come.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The garden's diary.** Each week, a participant writes three sentences in the diary. "Week 12: it rained, we could not work, but the plot was grateful." When the garden is two years old, the diary has a hundred entries: the whole story in three sentences a week.
2. **The box of papers.** The team keeps the festival posters, the campaign flyers and the meeting minutes in a marked box. Years later, a new participant finds the box and reads the project's history like someone reading a book.
3. **The grandmother's testimony.** The oldest participant tells how the first cleaning of the plot was. The chronicle stays written in the diary. When she no longer comes on Saturdays, her words continue to tell the story to the new generations.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open the project's diary: a notebook, with the first date and the first entry.
Step 2: Write what happened this week in the project, in three sentences.
Step 3: Make a photographs folder on your phone and keep in it the photos of the important moments.
Step 4: Write a chronicle of a moment you lived: what you saw, what you felt, what remained.
Step 5: Interview a person from the project and keep their testimony in writing.
Step 6: Prepare the archive box and organise in it the papers and documents.
Step 7: Write the sheet of the important dates: what happened, year by year, since the beginning.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the project's memory? a) The group of stories, dates, photos and documents. b) The money kept. c) The list of tasks.
2. What is the project's diary? a) The notebook where the days and the stories are written down. b) The library book. c) The coordinator's agenda.
3. What are the chronicles for? a) To give voice to the project's days. b) To use up paper. c) For the town hall's archives.
4. What is the legacy? a) What the project leaves for the future. b) What the project owes. c) The association's inheritance.
5. Who benefits from the memory? a) Only the coordinator. b) Those who arrive and those who stay. c) Nobody.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The memory tells the truth: victories and difficulties, without embellishing.
- The photographs and testimonies are used with permission and respect.
- The memory belongs to everyone: no person is the exclusive owner of the story.
- Those who left are also remembered: nobody is erased from the project.
- The memory is a legacy: it is written for those who come after.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
The memory keeps the road: diary, photographs, chronicles, testimonies and archive. The memory serves those who arrive, strengthens those who stay and leaves a legacy. In the next level we will learn the network of contacts: getting to know, taking care of and sharing with the people and entities that surround the project, our "network" in the most human sense.
