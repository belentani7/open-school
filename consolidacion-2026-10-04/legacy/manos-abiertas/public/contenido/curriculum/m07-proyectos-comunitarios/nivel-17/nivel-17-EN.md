# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 17
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to plan a community event from start to finish.
- Define the objective of the event and the right date.
- Organise the teams and share out the tasks of the event.
- Prepare the details: space, food, music, decoration and safety.
- Celebrate with the neighbourhood and evaluate after the party.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Event | The occasion that brings the community together: a festival, a fair, an open day. |
| Programme | The order of the event's activities, hour by hour. |
| Work team | The group that takes charge of one part of the event: food, music, decoration. |
| Dress rehearsal | The walk through the event's order, before the day, so nothing fails. |
| Plan B | The alternative prepared for the unexpected, like the rain. |
| Event evaluation | The review after the party: what went well and what to improve. |

## 📚 Main lesson
The event is one of the most beautiful moments of the community project: the day when the neighbourhood gathers to celebrate. A festival, a fair, an open day at the garden. But a good event is not born from improvisation: it is born from a plan. And planning an event is a small project inside the big one.

Everything begins with the objective. What is the event for? It can be to make the project known, to raise funds, to celebrate a harvest, to bring the neighbours together. The objective decides the rest: the type of event, the place, the food, the music. An event without an objective is a party without a why.

Then, the date. The date is chosen with care: it does not clash with other events in the neighbourhood, it does not fall on hard work days for most, it has likely good weather. A Saturday in spring is usually a good bet. And, above all, the date is announced early, so people can prepare.

The place is the third decision. The square, the school yard, the garden plot, the street that the association closes to traffic. The place must fit well with the expected number of people and have the essentials: shade or sun, water, toilets. A badly chosen place ruins the best party.

With objective, date and place, comes the plan: the programme. The programme is the order of the activities, hour by hour. Three in the afternoon, opening. Four, music. Five, food. Seven, raffle. The programme gives structure to the day: people know what comes next and the team knows what to do. A good programme also has rest moments, without hours that are too full.

The work of the event is shared out in teams. The food team, the music team, the decoration team, the children's games team, the cleaning team. Each team has its person responsible, as we learned in level 06, and each person responsible has their list of tasks. An event is a party prepared with lists.

The details make the party: the decoration, the music, the food, the games. They do not need to be expensive: they need to be thought about. A string of lights, coloured paper, handmade posters and food shared among the families are worth more than a hired hall with catering. The community event is beautiful because it belongs to everyone, not because it is expensive.

Shared food has a golden rule: each family brings a dish. That way the table is generous and the cost is shared. The same with the chairs, the tables and the tarpaulins: they are borrowed from the association, the school, the neighbours. The community event lives from what the community lends and shares.

Safety is also planned. At the event, the children do not get lost: people are designated to take care of them, and the parents know where the meeting point is. The food is kept fresh, the water does not run out, and there is always someone who knows first aid. Safety is not seen when it is well done, but it is noticed when it is missing.

The dress rehearsal is the key to success. In the week before, the team walks through the programme: who arrives first, who sets the tables, who connects the sound, who looks after the entrance. That way, on the day, each person knows their spot. The dress rehearsal is the walk through the house before the party, so nothing is looked for on the day.

Plan B is kept in your pocket. If it rains, we have the tarpaulin and the covered place. If the singer is missing, we have the phone with music. Plan B is not pessimism: it is love for the party. Those who have an alternative do not get scared by the unexpected, as we learned in level 08.

On the day of the event, the team arrives early, works with joy and remembers the mission: today the neighbourhood celebrates together. And when the party ends, the team does not forget two gestures: thanking everyone, out loud and by name, and leaving the space as it was or better. The event ends when the square is clean.

Afterwards comes the evaluation, as in level 12. The three questions: what went well, what went badly, what will we improve. With the answers, the next event will be even better. The party that is evaluated is a party that grows.

In this level we will plan our first event: objective, date, place, programme, teams and plan B. And we will celebrate with the neighbourhood, because the project is not only work: it is also party and joy.

Let us remember: the event is the community's party, and the party is as important as the work. A team that celebrates renews its strength. A neighbourhood that celebrates together becomes more of a neighbourhood.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The open day at the garden.** The objective: to make the project known. The date: a Saturday in May. The programme: at ten, guided tour; at eleven, planting workshop for children; at midday, shared paella. Each family brings a dish. The event fills the garden and six new volunteers arrive.
2. **The autumn fair.** The objective: to raise funds for the project. The team splits up: vegetable stall, crafts stall, cake stall. The association lends the tables and the school the yard. At the end of the day, the treasurer counts and there is money for next year's seeds.
3. **The summer festival.** The objective: to bring the neighbours together. The music is provided by a participant who plays the guitar, and the decoration by the children of the neighbourhood. In the afternoon it rains, but plan B was ready: the party moves to the association's headquarters. Nobody missed it, and everyone remembers it.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Write the objective of your event in one sentence.
Step 2: Choose the date and the place, with care and with the neighbourhood in mind.
Step 3: Write the programme, hour by hour, with rest moments.
Step 4: Split the team: food, music, decoration, children, cleaning. Each group with a person responsible.
Step 5: Make the list of materials: what is bought, what is borrowed, what each family brings.
Step 6: Write plan B for the unexpected: rain, missing music, missing food.
Step 7: Finish with the dress rehearsal: who, what, where and when, step by step.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. Where does a good event begin? a) With the food. b) With the objective. c) With the music.
2. What is the programme? a) The order of the activities, hour by hour. b) The list of guests. c) The budget.
3. How is the work of the event shared out? a) One person does everything. b) In teams, each with its person responsible. c) Only on the day.
4. What is plan B? a) The alternative prepared for the unexpected. b) A second event. c) A second party.
5. What do we do after the event? a) We forget it. b) We give thanks and evaluate. c) We lie down.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-b, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The event belongs to everyone: nobody is left out for not having money or food.
- Shared food and borrowed materials are the soul of the party.
- The space is returned as it was, or better.
- Those who helped are thanked, publicly and by name.
- The safety of the children and the people is above the party.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
The event is planned from the objective to plan B: date, place, programme, teams and thought-out details. A well-prepared party unites the neighbourhood, and the evaluation improves the next one. In the next level we will learn to raise funds for the project: the ways of financing, the campaigns and the honest management of resources.
