# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 21
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Canopy
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what community leadership is and why it is necessary.
- Get to know the different ways of leading and what distinguishes them.
- Learn to lead as a servant, not as an owner.
- Make other leaders grow in the team and in the community.
- Lead the team with listening, example and trust.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Leadership | The ability to lead a team towards a common objective. |
| Leader | The person who guides without imposing, listens without getting tired and serves without showing off. |
| Example | The best way of teaching: what the leader does is worth more than what they say. |
| Delegation | Trusting tasks to other people, without fear and with support. |
| Empowerment | Giving strength and confidence to people so they become leaders. |
| Succession | Preparing who will replace us, so the project does not depend on a single person. |

## 📚 Main lesson
We already saw the roles of the team, in level 06, and the coordinator who brings order. Now we climb one step: leadership. What makes a project advance for years and not die in the enthusiasm of the first month? Leadership. Not the one that gives orders, but the one that leads: the one that listens, guides, teaches and trusts.

There are many ways of leading, and not all of them are good. The leader who gives orders and punishes achieves things through fear, but the team works with its eyes on the ground and falls apart at the first problem. The leader who does everything alone gets tired and suffocates the others. There is also the leader who runs from decisions: leaves everything for "we'll see" and the team gets lost.

The leadership that builds is the servant's. The servant-leader is not the one who gives orders: it is the one who serves. They serve the team when they remove the obstacles from the road, when they give the floor to those who do not speak, when they make sure nobody is left behind. The servant-leader does not ask "what do I gain from this?", they ask "what does the team need?". It is the leadership of the hands in the soil, not of the pointing fingers.

The servant-leader listens. At meetings, they speak less and listen more. They know that the best ideas can come from those who speak least. And they also listen to what is not said: the tiredness, the doubt, the discouragement. The leader who listens sees what the others hide and takes care before it hurts.

The servant-leader sets the example. Nobody follows someone who says one thing and does another. If the leader arrives early, the team arrives early. If the leader keeps their word, the team keeps it. The example is the teaching that does not need words: others learn more by seeing than by hearing.

The servant-leader is not afraid to share out the work. They delegate with trust: they trust a task to another person, explain it clearly, support it to the end and thank for it afterwards. Delegating is not getting rid of work: it is making others grow. A team where only the leader does, never learns; a team where everyone does, grows whole.

The servant-leader makes other leaders grow: empowerment. They see in each participant a possible leader and take care of that seed. They give tasks with responsibility, celebrate each step, correct without humiliating. Their greatest victory is not being indispensable: it is becoming dispensable, because they left others ready.

The community leader is not chosen: they are recognised. They are not the one who speaks most nor the one who appears most: they are the one who serves most. In the community, true leadership is not imposed nor inherited: it is built with gestures, day after day, until others recognise it without anyone proclaiming it.

The leader also errs, and knows how to apologise. The servant-leader is not afraid to say "I was wrong, I am sorry". That sentence, said with truth, gives more authority than a year of being right. A team that sees the leader apologise, learns humility; and humility is the ground where trust grows.

Leadership prepares succession. No project should depend on a single person: the day they are missing, the project can fall. That is why the leader prepares who will replace them: shares what they know, trusts responsibilities, opens doors. Succession is not a farewell: it is a continuity. The project does not stop when the guiding hand changes.

The community leader knows the project belongs to everyone. They lead without being the owner: the team is not theirs, the success is not only theirs, and the last word belongs to everyone, as in the assembly of level 08. The servant-leader governs with the heart of the community: not above it, but within it.

The leader also takes care of themselves. Those who lead also get tired, and an exhausted leader serves nobody. The servant-leader knows how to stop, ask for help and share the load. It is not weakness: it is wisdom. A team that takes care of its leader and a leader who lets themselves be cared for form a circle that does not break.

In this level we will look at our own way of leading: how we lead, how we want to lead and how we will make other leaders grow. We will practise listening, example and delegation. And we will always remember: the best leader is not the one at the front, it is the one at the side.

Let us remember: community leadership is a task of hands and heart. The servant-leader does not shine for others: they shine with others. When a whole team becomes leader, the project stops depending on one and starts living from everyone. And a project that lives from everyone, does not die.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The leader who listens.** At a meeting, the coordinator notices that the new neighbour, always silent, has an idea. She gives him the floor and the idea is good: a corner of shade at the garden. He smiles, and since then he speaks at every meeting. Listening discovered a leader who was sleeping.
2. **The leader who delegates.** The coordinator stops making the posters himself and trusts them to two new participants, explaining what makes a good poster. They make them better than him. He thanks them out loud and they take over the posters of all the events. The work was shared and the team grew.
3. **The prepared succession.** The founder of the project is moving to another neighbourhood. For a year he has been preparing Maria, involving her in all the tasks and teaching her what he knows. On the day he leaves, the team elects Maria coordinator without a scare. The project did not even notice the change.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Think about your way of leading: what you do well and what you can improve.
Step 2: This week, at a meeting, speak less than you listen: give the floor to those who speak little.
Step 3: Practise the example: choose a small task and do it yourself first, in silence.
Step 4: Delegate a task with trust: explain, support, thank.
Step 5: Identify a possible leader in the team and give them a small responsibility.
Step 6: If you made a mistake with someone, apologise with truth.
Step 7: Think about succession: who could coordinate the project tomorrow? How do you prepare them?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the servant-leader? a) The one who gives orders and punishes. b) The one who serves the team, listens and sets the example. c) The one who does everything alone.
2. What is the leader's strongest teaching? a) The words. b) The example. c) The posters.
3. What is delegating? a) Getting rid of work. b) Trusting tasks with support and thanks. c) Giving orders.
4. What is empowerment? a) Making other leaders grow. b) Giving more orders. c) Having more power.
5. What prepares succession? a) Hiding what you know. b) Sharing, trusting responsibilities and teaching. c) Making the project depend on the leader.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The leader serves: their power is trust, not authority.
- The leader sets the example: they do not ask for what they do not do.
- The leader corrects without humiliating and apologises without shame.
- The leader makes others grow: their greatest work is the new leaders.
- The project is not the leader's: it belongs to everyone, and the last word belongs to the assembly.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Community leadership is the servant's: listening, setting the example, delegating with trust, making other leaders grow and preparing succession. The best leader is at the side, not at the front. In the next level we will learn strategic planning: thinking the future of the project, the scenarios and the decisions that will make it last.
