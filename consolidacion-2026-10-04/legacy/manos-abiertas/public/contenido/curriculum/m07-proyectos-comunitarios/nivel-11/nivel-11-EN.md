# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 11
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to take care of the materials and the accounts of the project.
- Make a simple inventory: what we have, where it is and how it is.
- Understand the budget: how much it costs and where the money comes from.
- Keep the receipts and write everything down, so the accounts are clear.
- Use money with transparency and answer to the team.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Inventory | The list of everything the project has: tools, materials and furniture. |
| Budget | The account of what the project needs and what it costs. |
| Income | The money that comes into the project. |
| Expense | The money the project spends. |
| Receipt | The paper that proves a purchase or an expense. |
| Transparency | That everyone sees the accounts and nobody has doubts about the money. |

## 📚 Main lesson
A community project does not live only on ideas and good will: it also lives on things and accounts. Tools, seeds, tarpaulins, a stove for the festival. And all of that costs money, even if it is little. Taking care of the things and the money is an act of respect for the team and the community.

Let us begin with the things: the inventory. The inventory is the list of everything the project has. It is done in a simple way: a sheet, and in it three columns. The first column, the name of the thing. The second, where it is stored. The third, the state: new, good, used, in need of repair. With that sheet, nobody searches for the scissors for half an hour, because everyone knows where they are.

The inventory is not made once and forgotten. Every time a new thing is bought, it is written down. Every time a thing breaks or is lost, it is also written down. The inventory is alive, like the project: it grows and changes with it. A quick review at the end of each month is enough.

Taking care of the things is also lengthening their life. Tools are washed, dried and put away in their place. Tarpaulins are folded and stored dry. What is breaking down is repaired in time, before being lost. A project that takes care of its things spends less, because it does not have to buy twice.

After the things, the money: the budget. The budget is the account of what the project needs. It is made with two columns: what we need and how much it costs. Seeds, ten euros. Shovel and rake, thirty. A hose, twenty. With the list in front of us, the project knows how much money it needs and is not surprised halfway.

The budget also says where the money will come from: the income. Income can be many things: the contribution of each participant, a small sale, support from the association or the town hall, a donation from a shopkeeper. The project's money is usually little, but, well counted, it is enough for what matters.

There is a golden rule with money: everything is written down. Every euro that comes in and every euro that goes out is written in an accounts notebook. And each purchase keeps its receipt. The receipt is the proof, and without proof the accounts are not accounts. A notebook and a small bag of receipts are enough for an honest project.

The person who keeps the money is called the treasurer, and it is a position of trust chosen by the team. The treasurer is not the owner of the money: they are the one who keeps it and writes it down for everyone. Not one euro should depend on the memory of a single person: everything written, everything clear.

Transparency is the heart of the accounts. Once a month, at the meeting, the treasurer shows the accounts: how much came in, how much was spent and how much is left. There are no forbidden questions or hidden answers. Clear accounts avoid suspicion and bring calm: nobody can doubt what everyone can see.

Mistakes in the accounts are also dealt with transparently. If a receipt is missing or a number is wrong, it is said at the meeting and corrected. To err is human; to hide is what destroys trust. A project that corrects its mistakes in front of everyone is a project that everyone trusts.

Community money has a special rule: it belongs to nobody and to everybody. Nobody can spend a euro of the project without the team knowing. Big purchases are agreed in assembly, as we learned in level 08. Money is not spent on whims: it is spent on the project's objective.

Sometimes the money is little and the needs are many. That is where creativity and the support of the community come in: borrowing, swapping, sharing, asking for help. What cannot be bought is asked for; what cannot be asked for is made. The project learns to live with what it has and to thank what it receives.

In this level we will make the inventory and the budget of our project. We will open the accounts notebook and elect the treasurer. And we will set a rule in the group: clear accounts, every month, in front of everyone.

Let us remember: taking care of the little we have is a way of giving thanks. The project was born from everyone's effort, and everyone's effort deserves things well kept and accounts well done. Transparency is an open door: those who have nothing to hide do not close the door.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The garden's inventory.** The team spends an afternoon doing the inventory. They write everything down: the shovel in the cupboard, the watering can by the tap, the seeds in the box. They discover they have more than they thought and that the hoe needs a new handle. A week later, they replace it for two and a half euros.
2. **The festival's budget.** For the festival, the group makes the list: music, food, tarpaulins and lights. They add it up: two hundred euros. The income: thirty per family and a drinks stall. With the budget in hand, they decide without scares and without debts.
3. **The accounts notebook.** After the festival, the treasurer shows the accounts at the meeting: everything came in, everything went out, and twenty euros are left for the common pot. Someone asks for a receipt and he shows it in the same moment. Everyone leaves calm and nobody is left with doubts.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Make a sheet with three columns: thing, place and state.
Step 2: Go through the project's space and write down everything you find: tools, materials, furniture.
Step 3: Review the state of each thing: is it fine? Does it need repair? Write it down.
Step 4: Make the budget: two columns, what we need and how much it costs.
Step 5: Write down the possible income: who can contribute, who can support.
Step 6: Open the accounts notebook and a place for the receipts. Elect the treasurer with the team.
Step 7: Mark on the calendar the monthly review of the accounts, at the meeting, in front of everyone.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the inventory? a) The list of everything the project has. b) The money that comes in. c) The account of the expenses.
2. What is the budget? a) The list of people. b) The account of what the project needs and what it costs. c) The place where tools are kept.
3. What do we keep from each purchase? a) The box. b) The receipt. c) The inventory.
4. Who is the treasurer? a) Someone who gives orders to the project. b) Someone who keeps and writes down the money for everyone. c) Someone who always buys.
5. What is the golden rule with money? a) Everything is written down. b) Only what comes in is written down. c) The money is kept at home.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The project's money belongs to everyone: nobody spends without the team knowing.
- The accounts are shown every month, hiding nothing.
- All receipts are kept: without proof, there is no account.
- The treasurer serves the team, does not serve themselves with the money.
- Transparency matters more than the balance: one clear euro is worth more than a hundred hidden.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Taking care of the things is making the inventory and lengthening the life of each tool. Taking care of the money is making the budget, writing everything down and showing the accounts with transparency. An honest project wins the community's trust. In the next level we will learn to evaluate our work: the indicators of success and the honest review of what went well and what we can improve.
