# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 16
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what an automation is and why it saves time.
- Learn the idea of "when this happens, do this".
- Create a simple automation with a tool like Make or Power Automate.
- Recognise the tasks of your life that could be automated.
- Use the time you save for the things that matter.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Automation | A set of steps that happen by themselves, without you doing anything. |
| Trigger | The signal that starts it: an email, a time, a click. The spark that lights the fire. |
| Action | Each step that the automation does: save, send, notify. |
| Workflow | The chain of trigger and actions, like the recipe of a dish. |
| Formula | The shape the data takes as it passes: a name, a date, a text. |
| Template | A ready-made automation that someone has already built, so you do not start from zero. |

## 📚 Main lesson
There are things in life that repeat: sending the same email every week, saving the same document, remembering the same date. Our head is tired of remembering, and our hands are tired of repeating. Today we learn how to make machines do those repeated tasks: automation. It is like having a helper who never sleeps, who does exactly what you tell him, every time.

Let us understand the idea with a simple image. Automation is a chain of "when this happens, do this". A trigger: when it is Monday at nine. An action: send the reminder to the group. Another action: save a copy. And everything happens alone. It is like an automatic watering system: the timer opens the tap, the water falls, and you do not have to be there.

The tools of this level are Make and Power Automate. They are services that connect applications: email, calendar, documents, chats. You build a workflow by connecting pieces: this triggers that, that triggers the other. It is like building with blocks, but the blocks are real tasks of your life. And the best: many workflows are already made, you copy them and adapt them.

Let us look at a daily example. Every month you have to send the community announcement. You create a workflow: when the first day of the month arrives, take the text, send the email to the list. The first time you build it, it costs you a little; from then on, it works alone. You have saved hours for the rest of your life.

Another example, very useful for the association: the list of members. When a new person fills in the form, the automation saves it in the spreadsheet, sends a welcome message and adds them to the group. Without automation, you would do three things; with automation, the machine does them. And you, meanwhile, are having coffee.

Now, the honest part: automation is not magic, and it does not replace you. It repeats the same steps, exactly, every time. If you have not taught it well, it does it wrong, and it does not realise. That is why you always have to test: run it once, check that everything is in its place, and then let it work. Trust, but verify.

Let us talk about the three virtues of automation. The first, time: it does in seconds what takes you minutes. The second, order: it does it always the same way, without forgetting a step. The third, peace: your head no longer has to remember; the machine remembers. A head free of small tasks is a head for the important things.

But be careful with a danger: automating too much. There are things that should not be automated, because they need a person: a decision, a kindness, a conversation. Automation is for the mechanical; for the human, the human. The helper who does everything alone can end up doing things that you should have done. Choose well what you hand over.

Let us look at how to build our first workflow. Choose a simple task: sending a daily reminder. Open Make or Power Automate, look for templates, choose one of reminders. Connect your account, choose the text and the time, test it. In twenty minutes you have your first helper. It is easier than you imagine, and the pride of seeing it work alone is great.

An important advice: start small. Do not automate your whole life in one afternoon. Choose one task, one that repeats, and do it well. When it works, you choose the next. Automation is learned like cooking: first a simple recipe, then more elaborate ones. And each recipe you master is time you have won.

And the last lesson: automation teaches you to see your life with new eyes. You begin to notice the repetitions: this I do every week, this I always forget, this takes me too long. Each repetition is a candidate for a workflow. The eye that discovers them is the most valuable thing of this level.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The monthly reminder**: every month you have to pay the community fee and you always forget. You create a workflow: when it is the 25th, send you a reminder to the mobile. No more forgetting, no more late fees.
2. **The welcome to the association**: a new neighbour joins the association and fills in the form. The automation saves it in the list, sends them a welcome message and invites them to the group. The new person feels received, and you have done nothing.
3. **The photo of the day**: your granddaughter sends photos to the group and you want to keep them. The automation saves each new photo in a folder on your computer. The album grows alone, and you only enjoy it.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Choose a tool: Make (make.com) or Power Automate (powerautomate.com) and open it with your account.
2. Look at the main page: find the templates section. Observe how many workflows are already made.
3. Choose a simple template: for example, a reminder or a notification.
4. Connect your account: the tool will ask you for permission to use your email or calendar. Look at what you are giving it.
5. Customise: write the text of your reminder and choose the time.
6. Test it: run the workflow once and check that everything works.
7. Correct what does not work: automation is fixed by touching it, like a car.
8. Write in your notebook: which task of your life repeats and would deserve a workflow?
9. Do not activate it yet: first understand it, then decide. You are the boss.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Automation does what you tell it, and if you tell it without care, it can do harm without realising it. A badly sent message, a document sent to the wrong person, a reminder at a bad time: the machine does not think, you do. Check each workflow before you let it work, and be careful with what you connect: an automation has access to your email, your calendar, your data. Treat that power with responsibility. The helper is good, but the conscience is yours.
