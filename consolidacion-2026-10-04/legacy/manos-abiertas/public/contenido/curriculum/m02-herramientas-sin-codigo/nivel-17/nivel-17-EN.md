# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 17
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a database is and how it differs from a spreadsheet.
- Learn to create a simple database with AI.
- Use views to see the data as a table, as cards or as a calendar.
- Ask the AI to organise, filter and understand your data.
- Put order in the lists that matter: members, stock, contacts.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Database | A place that stores data in an orderly way, like a big well-sorted cabinet. |
| Record | Each person or item in the database, like one card in the file. |
| Field | Each type of data of a record: name, date, phone. |
| View | A way of seeing the data: as a table, as cards, as a calendar. It is the same data seen differently. |
| Filter | A sieve: show me only the data that fulfils something, like sifting flour. |
| Template | A ready-made database that someone has designed, so you do not start from zero. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In level 11 we learned spreadsheets: tables that calculate. Today we take a step further: the databases. A database is a place that stores data in an orderly way, but it is much more powerful than a spreadsheet. If the spreadsheet is a wardrobe, the database is a warehouse: it stores everything, and you can always find what you are looking for. And with AI, creating and using it is within everyone's reach.

Let us understand the difference. A spreadsheet is like a single notebook page: everything in the same table. A database is like a cabinet with cards: each person, each item has its own card, with the same fields. And the magic: you can see the same data in many ways, without touching it. As a table, as cards, as a calendar. It is the same water in different glasses.

The tools of this level are Airtable and Google's new databases. They are services where you create a database with a template or from scratch, you fill it in and you ask the AI to organise it. You do not program anything: you click, you drag, you ask. It is like organising the pantry: each thing in its place, and you know where everything is.

Let us think about a real example: the members of the association. You create a database with the fields: name, phone, date they joined, quotas paid. Each member is a record. And now the magic: you ask "who has not paid the last quota?", and the database filters and shows you the list. "Who joined this year?" It filters. You have a cabinet that answers questions.

Another example: the food pantry of the community centre. A database with the food, the quantity, the expiry date. You ask "what expires this month?" and it shows you. "How many kilos of rice do we have?" It counts. The AI takes care of the numbers, and you of the people. The database gives order, and you give warmth.

How is it created? You open Airtable, choose a template or start from zero. You define the fields: for each type of data, its place. You fill in the records: one per person or item. And then you explore: the table view, the cards view, the calendar. All with the same data, each view for a different moment. It is like having several windows on the same house.

And the AI? Today the databases come with an assistant. You ask in your words: "summarise how many members have paid", "show me the visits of the month". The AI turns your question into the search and answers you. No formulas, no knowledge of databases: you ask, and it understands. The good question of our levels returns, now to organise data.

An important lesson: order matters. A database only answers well if the data is clean: the same date in the same format, the names without a space at the beginning, everything in its field. It is like a recipe: if the ingredients are not in order, the dish fails. Spend time filling in well, and the database will reward you for years.

Let us also talk about what a database is NOT for. It is not for writing letters, nor for a document, nor for a presentation. It is for data: lists, people, things, dates. If you have to calculate, the spreadsheet; if you have to store and find, the database; if you have to explain, the presentation. Each tool in its place, and you, who choose them, in charge.

And a lesson of confidence: do not be afraid of the word "database". It sounds technical, but behind it there is the simple idea of a well-sorted cabinet. You know how to sort a cabinet: this is for the cereals, this for the cans, this for the spices. A database is the same, but with data. And with AI, you do not even need to remember where each thing is: you ask.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The members of the association**: a database with names, phones and paid quotas. You ask: "Who has not paid this month?" The database filters and gives you the list. No more notebook with crossed-out names.
2. **The pantry of the centre**: a database with the food and its expiry dates. You ask: "What expires this month?" And you know what to use first. Less waste, more order.
3. **The birthday list**: a database with the birthdays of family and friends. You see them in the calendar view and you are never late again. The people you love deserve the memory you always wanted to have.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Open Airtable (airtable.com) with your account.
2. Look at the main page: find the templates section. Observe how many databases are already designed.
3. Choose a simple template: for example, a contacts or tasks list.
4. Look at the screen: see the fields (name, date, notes) and the records.
5. Add a real record of yours: a person or a thing that matters to you.
6. Change the view: from table to cards, to calendar. Look at how the same data changes appearance.
7. Ask the AI: "How many records are there?" or "Show me the ones from this month." Try it.
8. Add a filter: show only what you are interested in.
9. Write in your notebook: which list of your life deserves to become a database?

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
A database stores people: names, phones, dates. That is personal data, and taking care of it is a responsibility. Do not put in a database data that should not be there, and do not share it with anyone without permission. The association's list is not for selling, nor for gossip: it is to serve. And remember that the AI sees what you put in it: a database with everyone's data is a treasure that demands honesty. Good data, used well, for good ends.

## 🔓 Open tools
- **Airtable** (airtable.com) — the friendly database with templates, free in its basic version.
- **Google Tables** (tables.google.com) — Google's database, simple and connected to the family of tools you know.
- **Notion** (notion.so) — a space that combines documents and databases, with a free part.
- **LibreOffice Base** (libreoffice.org) — the open-source database that lives on your computer.
- **NocoDB** (nocodb.com) — an open-source database that you can have at home.

You have created, filled in and asked: you already know how to put order in data.
