# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 04
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a prompt is and why it is the most important skill of all.
- Learn the three basic ingredients of a good question: role, task and details.
- Practise improving an answer by adding details.
- Learn to correct and redirect the chatbot when the answer does not convince you.
- Create your first "prompt recipe" that you can reuse.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Prompt | The question or instruction you write to the chatbot. Your way of giving orders. |
| Role | Who you ask the chatbot to be, for example a teacher, a doctor or a cook. |
| Task | What you ask it to do: explain, write, summarise, translate, list. |
| Details | The specific information you add so the answer fits you: length, tone, audience. |
| Reformulate | Ask the chatbot to change the answer: shorter, simpler, with examples. |
| Recipe | A prompt that has worked well and you save to use again and again. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous level we learned to talk to the chatbot. Today we go one step further: we learn to ask well. And this is the most valuable skill of the whole course, because the answer is a reflection of the question. A vague question produces a vague answer; a precise question produces a precise answer. It is like ordering in a restaurant: if you say "bring me something to eat", you get a surprise; if you say "grilled fish, with salad and no salt", you get what you want.

Let us give it a name: the prompt. A prompt is everything you write to the chatbot before it answers. It can be one line or ten. The good news is that a good prompt has only three ingredients, and today we are going to master them.

The first ingredient is the role. It means telling the chatbot who you want it to be. It is like telling a friend "put yourself in the doctor's shoes". If you ask "act as a teacher of ten-year-old children and explain fractions to me", the answer will be simple and didactic. If you ask the same question without a role, the answer will be more generic. The role focuses the chatbot, like a pair of glasses focuses your sight.

The second ingredient is the task. It is the verb that tells it what to do. Not the same is "explain me", "summarise", "translate", "write", "list" or "compare". Choosing the right verb changes everything. If you ask "summarise this text in five lines", you will not get a long explanation; you will get a summary. The task is the order you give, so choose it well.

The third ingredient is the details. They are the seasoning of the prompt. Length ("in two paragraphs"), tone ("formal", "kind", "simple"), the audience ("explain it to my 80-year-old mother"), the format ("with bullet points", "as a table"). The more details you add, the closer the answer is to what you need. It is like a tailor: with your measurements, he makes the suit that fits you.

Let us see it with a real example. Bad prompt: "Tell me about the pension." Vague. The chatbot will give you a very generic text. Good prompt: "Act as a social security advisor and explain to me in simple words what the retirement pension is, how it is calculated in general, and give me a list of the three things I have to do to apply for it." Same theme, but the answer is useful and practical.

Now, a great trick: you do not have to get it right first time. The chatbot allows you to continue the conversation and refine. If the answer is too long, tell it "shorter". If it has technical words, tell it "explain it to me as if I were a beginner". If you want a different tone, ask it to rewrite. This continuous adjustment is what we call reformulating, and it is the sign of someone who has understood how to work with the machine.

Another valuable trick is to correct with concrete examples. Instead of saying "no, that's not it", tell it what you want: "No, I want it with a more formal tone and with an example at the end." The chatbot learns better from what you ask for than from what you reject. Think of it as giving a recipe: you do not say "this dish is bad", you say "add more salt and less oil".

You should also know the four most useful tasks in daily life. Summarise: "summarise this article in five lines". Translate: "translate this letter into Portuguese". List: "make me a list of ten ideas for..." Generate formats: "write this as a table". With these four verbs you solve half of your digital life.

A practical habit: when a prompt works well, save it. Write it down in your notebook or in a document. That is your recipe box. Over the next levels, we will build recipes together and you will see how much time they save you. A saved good prompt is like a recipe inherited from your grandmother: you do not have to think about it again, it just works.

And one final warning, very important. A good prompt improves the quality of the answer, but it does not guarantee the truth. You can ask very well and still get a wrong answer if the chatbot does not have the information. The prompt is your way of ordering; checking is still your responsibility. Precision in the question, prudence in the answer.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The complaint letter**: you want to complain about a late delivery. You write: "Act as a consumer advisor. Write a polite but firm complaint letter about a delivery that arrived three weeks late. Formal tone, maximum fifteen lines." The result is a letter you only have to put your data on.
2. **Explain the medical test**: the doctor told you a term you did not understand. You write: "Act as a friendly doctor. Explain to me in simple words what the creatinine test measures and what a high result means. Avoid technical words." Clear answer, at home, before asking the doctor.
3. **The holiday list**: you are going on a trip with your siblings. You write: "Make me a list of ten things to check before a week-long trip in summer. Format: bullet list." Practical and complete, without forgetting anything.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Open your chatbot (ChatGPT or Gemini).
2. Write this vague prompt: "Tell me about olive oil." Press enter.
3. Look at the answer. It is probably generic and long. This is a "surprise dish" prompt.
4. Now write a complete prompt with the three ingredients: "Act as a nutrition advisor. Explain to me the benefits of extra virgin olive oil for a person over fifty. Simple words, three paragraphs, and a final tip."
5. Compare both answers. Which one is more useful?
6. If the second one is still long, reformulate: "Now shorter, with a list of three benefits."
7. Save the good prompt in your notebook. Label it "Recipe: olive oil".
8. Repeat with another topic of your daily life and save it as well.
9. Reflect and write down: what changed between the first answer and the second?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What are the three ingredients of a good prompt?
2. What is the "role" for in a prompt?
3. What happens if you ask without details?
4. What does it mean to reformulate and why is it useful?
5. Does a good prompt guarantee that the answer is true?

**Answers:** 1. Role, task and details. 2. It tells the chatbot who to be, which focuses the style and tone of the answer. 3. You get a generic answer, like a surprise dish. 4. Continuing the conversation to ask the chatbot to adjust the answer; it is useful because you do not have to get it right first time. 5. No: it improves the quality, but you must always check the important data.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
The quality of a question can also serve to do harm. A well-written prompt to invent a scam, an insult or a false rumour works the same way as a well-written prompt to help. You are the one who gives the order, and you are responsible for it. Never use your new skill to deceive, insult or spread false information. Also remember that the chatbot does not have to replace your judgment: a prompt can ask for advice, but decisions about your health, money or family are yours.
