# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 00
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what an application (an "app") is, with examples from everyday life.
- Recognise the difference between an application, a programme and a website.
- Identify the apps that are already on your phone and your computer.
- Learn to open an application and use it with confidence.
- Overcome the first fear: realising that you have been using applications for years without noticing.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Application (app) | A tool with a purpose: it lets you do one thing well, such as sending a message or seeing a photo. |
| Programme | An application installed on a computer, like a recipe kept in a drawer to be used whenever you want. |
| Website | A page you see through the internet, like a shop window you visit without buying anything. |
| Icon | A small picture on the screen that opens an application when you tap or click it. |
| Screen | The surface where you see everything; a window is like a table where one task is placed. |
| Install | The action of placing an application on your device so it stays ready to use. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Let us start with a very simple idea: an application is a tool with a purpose. Just as in your kitchen you have a knife for cutting, a spoon for stirring and a pot for cooking, on your phone you have an application for writing messages, another for taking photos and another for talking to the bank. Each application does one thing well, and that makes life easier.

Think about your telephone. When you receive a message, a small bell rings and a number appears. That is an application announcing that someone has written to you. When you look at the weather before going out, you are using another application. When you send a photo of your grandson to your sister, yet another one. You already use applications every day without calling them by their name. What we are going to do in this course is simply give them a name and understand them better.

Why is this important? Because if you understand that an application is just a tool, you will lose the fear of touching it. A tool does not bite. If you press the wrong button, nothing breaks. Like in the kitchen: if you use the wrong spoon, the soup is still soup.

There are two large families of applications. Those that live on your device and those that live on the internet. An application that lives on your device, such as the calculator or the photo gallery, works even without internet. It is like a sewing machine: it is at home, it is yours, and it does not need anything else. An application that lives on the internet, such as the bank or the news, needs a connection. It is like a bus: you do not have the bus at home, but when you need it, you wait for it and it takes you to the place.

The programmes on a computer follow the same idea. A computer programme is a larger application, made to do bigger tasks. Writing a letter, editing a photo, keeping the accounts of the house. Do not be afraid of the word "programme": it is only the application of the computer, the same tool you already use on the phone, but with a bigger table.

Something very useful is to know that all applications work in a similar way. They open with a small picture, the icon. Once open, they show you a window with buttons and words. And at the bottom or top there is usually a menu, which is like a restaurant menu: it lists everything the establishment can serve you. If you know how to read one menu, you can read them all.

When you cannot find an application, do not despair. You can ask someone, or look for it on the screen, or simply press the button of the telephone that shows all the applications at once. That button is the drawer of your house: everything is in there, you just have to know that it exists.

New applications appear every day. You do not need to know them all. You only need three or four that solve your real life: communication, photos, the bank and the news. The rest are extras. In this module we will learn tools that help you with everything, and all of them are opened in the same simple way.

And here is the key idea of the whole level: an application is a tool, you are the person who uses it, and the tool never has more authority than you. You decide when to open it, when to close it and what to do with it. Whoever understands this is already prepared for the rest of the course.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Messages with the family**: your son sends you a voice note. You tap the messages icon, listen to the note and reply with a written message. You have used three application skills without noticing: open, listen, reply.
2. **The bank on the phone**: you want to check that your pension has arrived. You open the bank application, look for the balance and see the amount. It is the same as looking at the slip, but through a window that fits in your hand.
3. **Photos of the grandchildren**: a friend sends you a photo of the last school trip. You open the gallery, find the photo, and send it to your sister. The gallery is an application that stores memories.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Take your phone or computer and look at the screen.
2. Identify three icons you use often: for example, messages, phone and photos.
3. Open each one, one at a time, by tapping or clicking on the icon.
4. Look at the menu: write down in a notebook the words you see at the top or bottom.
5. Close each application when you have finished, using the back button or the cross.
6. Repeat the same with a programme on your computer, for example the one you use to write or to browse the internet.
7. Write in the notebook the name of the application and what you used it for.
8. End the day by answering this question to yourself: which application did I not know I had?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is an application in simple words?
2. What is the difference between an application that lives on your device and one that lives on the internet?
3. What is an icon and what is it for?
4. Name three applications you use without realising you are using them.
5. If you press the wrong button in an application, what happens in most cases?

**Answers:** 1. A tool with a purpose that helps you do something on the phone or the computer. 2. The one on your device works without internet; the one on the internet needs a connection. 3. A small picture that opens the application when you tap or click on it. 4. Messages, camera and alarm clock are valid answers. 5. Nothing breaks; you can go back and try again.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Every application asks you for permission and data: your name, your number, sometimes your location. Before accepting, ask yourself: does this tool really need that information? A calculator does not need to know where you live. Read what you accept, even if it is long, and when in doubt, ask a trusted person. Remember that the application works for you, not the other way around.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
An application is a tool with a purpose; it opens with an icon and has a menu that lists what it can do. You use applications every day without noticing, and you do not need to know them all. You decide what to open and when to close it. In the next level we will learn to travel through the internet with the browser, the door that gives access to millions of applications and websites.
