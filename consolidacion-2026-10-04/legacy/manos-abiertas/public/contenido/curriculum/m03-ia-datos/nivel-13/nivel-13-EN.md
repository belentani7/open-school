## 📚 Main lesson
So far we have been learning pieces: cleaning data, making charts, building dashboards. Today we step up and see the whole landscape: the journey a piece of data makes from birth to becoming a decision. Professionals call this the "data life cycle". Knowing where you are in the cycle is what separates people who work with data from people who just press buttons.

The cycle has five stages, like the stops on a journey. The first is collect: getting the data. It can come from a survey, a form, a website or a measuring device. This stage decides what is asked and who is asked, and this is where the game is won or lost: if we ask badly or ask the wrong people, every later step is useless. A bad collection cannot be fixed afterwards.

The second stage is clean, which we practised in level 11: removing duplicates, filling gaps, fixing formats. The third is analyse: looking for patterns, calculating averages, comparing, distrusting the false correlations of level 10. The fourth is visualise: turning the numbers into charts and dashboards that are understood at a glance, as in level 12. The fifth is decide: taking an action based on all of the above. And the last stage returns to the first: after deciding, new data is collected to see whether the decision worked. The cycle turns.

This scheme is the key to not getting lost. When someone asks you to "produce a report on the building's water consumption", the first thing is not to open a spreadsheet: it is to work out where you are. Do we have the data? No. Then the stage is collect: you must record each home's consumption every month. Only when you have the data do you move to cleaning, and so on, step by step. Trying to skip stages is the most expensive mistake of all: visualising data that was never cleaned produces false reports.

Beginners always want to start with the pretty part, visualisation. Professionals start from the end: they first think about the decision to be made. What is this report for? Who will read it? What action will that person take with it? If there is no clear decision, the project has no direction. This way of thinking, from the end back to the beginning, is called "thinking backwards" and is the most valued skill in data teams.

Another essential idea of the cycle is that each stage has its own tool and its own attitude. Collecting requires care and clear handwriting on the form. Cleaning requires patience and method. Analysing requires curiosity and scepticism. Visualising requires taste and honesty (remember the tricky axes of level 12). Deciding requires courage and responsibility. There is no small stage: a single neglected one ruins the journey.

Here is an everyday example: Mrs Garcia wants to know whether she should switch electricity suppliers. Full cycle: collect (she notes down her consumption for the last 12 months from the bills), clean (she realises two bills are duplicated), analyse (she calculates the monthly average and sees she spends more in winter), visualise (she draws a line with the months), decide (compare tariffs with her real average). If she had skipped the cleaning, she would have compared with a false average and may have picked the wrong supplier.

AI, the heart of this module, fits into this cycle at the analysis stage: it is a very powerful tool for finding patterns in clean data. But AI does not collect, does not clean and does not decide; people decide. That is why this course does not just teach you to use AI: it teaches you the whole process, so that you are the one deciding with your head, not the one who lets a machine decide for you.

Next week we will see where to find data when we do not have our own: open data, those public treasures that governments and organisations publish for free. Before that, plant this idea in your head: a data project is not a chart, it is a five-stop journey, and every journey begins with the decision you want to make. If you remember this, you are already thinking like a professional.

## 💡 Practical examples
### Example 1: The neighbourhood association
They want to know whether it is worth installing solar panels on the shared roof. Cycle: collect (the building's electricity bills), clean (remove duplicates), analyse (monthly and seasonal average), visualise (annual line), decide (request a quote with real data).

### Example 2: The book club
The club wants to decide which book genre they like most. Cycle: collect (a survey of the 20 members), clean (some wrote "adventure" and others "adventures"), analyse (count genres), visualise (bars), decide (order more titles of the winning genre).

### Example 3: The vegetable garden
Someone says drip irrigation saves water. Cycle: collect (measure the water of two plots, one with drip and one without), clean (correct wrong readings), analyse (compare averages), visualise (two bars), decide (install drip irrigation if the data confirms it).

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1. Choose a small problem from your daily life that can be solved with data (example: your supermarket spending each week).
Step 2. Write at the top of a Google Sheets sheet the decision you want to make: "I want to know if I spend too much on treats".
Step 3. Draw five cells in a row with the stage names: Collect, Clean, Analyse, Visualise, Decide.
Step 4. In the Collect stage, note what data you need and where you will get it from.
Step 5. Simulate a collection: write 10 purchases with their prices in one column.
Step 6. In the Clean stage, review the data: is there any impossible price? any repeated row? Correct it.
Step 7. In the Analyse stage, calculate with =AVERAGE(...) the weekly spending.
Step 8. In the Visualise stage, make a bar or line chart with the 10 values.
Step 9. In the Decide stage, write a sentence with the conclusion and the action: "I spend €15 a week on treats; I will set myself a maximum of €10".
Step 10. Close the cycle: write what new data you would collect in a month's time to check whether you met your goal.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What are the five stages of the data life cycle?
2. Why does a bad collection ruin the whole project?
3. What does "thinking backwards" mean?
4. In which stage of the cycle does AI fit?
5. How does the cycle return to the beginning after deciding?

Answers: 1. Collect, clean, analyse, visualise and decide. 2. Because if the data is wrong from the start, no later step can fix it. 3. Starting by thinking about the decision you want to make before collecting data. 4. In the analysis stage, looking for patterns. 5. New data is collected to check whether the decision worked.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
The data life cycle ends in decisions that affect people. A company that collects its customers' data badly and decides with it can discriminate without meaning to; a report with a neglected cleaning stage can lead to cutting services that are actually needed. People who work with data have the responsibility to travel the five stages with care and to remember that behind every number there is a person. Ethics is not one more stage: it is an attitude that accompanies the whole journey.

- AI fits into the analysis, but decisions are made by people.
