# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 08
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what personalised exercises are and why they help so much.
- Learn to ask AI for exercises made to measure.
- Practise with exercises of gradual difficulty: easy first, difficult later.
- Use exercises to train memory and understanding.
- Create a small daily practice routine with AI.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Exercise | A task to practise what we have learned, like kneading so the bread rises. |
| Personalised | Made to measure, like a tailor-made suit or prescription glasses. |
| Difficulty | How hard the exercise is: easy, medium or difficult. |
| Gradual | That goes up in level little by little, like the stairs of a house. |
| Correction | Checking whether the answer is right, like tasting the salt in a stew. |
| Repetition | Practising several times to make it stick, like rehearsing a song. |

## 📚 Main lesson
A good exercise is like a key that opens the door of learning: understanding is not enough, you have to practise. Let us think of sewing. Nobody learns to sew just by watching the machine: you have to sit down, thread it, sew straight lines and, if it comes unstitched, start again. Exercises are the straight lines of our learning.

The wonderful thing about AI is that it makes exercises to our measure. There is not one single book for everyone: we can ask it for easy exercises if we are starting, or difficult ones if we already master the topic. It is like having a tailor who makes our clothes to our size, instead of buying a one-size-fits-all from the shop.

To ask for personalised exercises we use the same language as for explanations: clarity and context. We say: "I have learned [topic]. I want to practise. Make me 5 easy exercises to start". AI understands the topic, the number and the difficulty. The more concrete, the better the task.

Gradual difficulty is the key to training. If we only do easy exercises, we never advance; if only difficult ones, we get frustrated. Good practice climbs steps: three easy, three medium, three difficult. It is like learning to swim: first in the small pool, then in the big one, and only then in the sea.

We can ask AI: "Make me a ladder of 9 exercises: 3 easy, 3 medium and 3 difficult". With that ladder we train without skipping steps. Each exercise we overcome prepares us for the next, like the dough gaining strength with each knead.

What kind of exercises can we ask for? Many. For memory: "true or false questions". For understanding: "short answer questions". For vocabulary: "match each word with its explanation". To converse: "ask me about the topic as if you were an interviewer". AI is an endless exercise factory.

After doing an exercise comes the correction. We ask it: "Correct my answers and tell me where I went wrong". AI checks them one by one and explains the mistake. Correcting is not a punishment: it is the most valuable moment of learning, when we know exactly what to tune, like when the teacher points out the point of the needle.

When we make a mistake, let us ask for reinforcement: "Make me more exercises about the part I failed". AI detects where we got stuck and gives us more practice on that point. It is like when a child will not eat a food: we give them that food again, another way, until they taste it.

Repetition also matters. Doing the same type of exercise with different topics trains the skill in general. For example: if we practise "explaining in one sentence" with ten different topics, we get better each time. Varied practice is like varying the recipes: the same knife, different foods.

A good system is the daily practice routine. When each lesson ends, we spend ten minutes on AI exercises. Those ten minutes are the watering of our garden. At first it will be hard, but with time (we already know) it becomes a habit, and the habit becomes learning.

How many exercises should we do? Better few and well done than many and rushed. Five exercises done with attention are worth more than twenty answered without thinking. Like in the kitchen: better one well-cooked dish than five half-done. Exercise quality beats quantity.

Beware of one risk: AI giving us the answer without us thinking. An exercise only works if we try it first. If we fail, better: the mistake teaches us. If AI answers for us, we are not training, we are watching someone else train. The one who has to sweat is us.

We can also ask for exercises "with hints". We say: "If I get stuck, give me a hint, not the answer". AI will guide us: first a small hint, then another. It is like teaching someone to ride a bike: hold the saddle, then let go, then they ride alone.

In this level we will create our first battery of personalised exercises about the topic we are learning. We will do them in a ladder, correct them with AI and repeat the part we fail. When we finish, we will feel the progress in our own hands.

Practice turns us from spectators into protagonists. Seeing, hearing and reading is easy; practising costs more, but it is the only thing that turns knowledge into skill. And with a tutor that makes exercises to our measure, practising is easier than ever.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The kitchen ladder.** Marta asked: "Make me 9 exercises about making bread: 3 easy, 3 medium and 3 difficult". She started with the easiest and climbed without frustration.
2. **The mistake reinforcement.** Pedro failed a question about his homeland. He asked: "Make me 5 more exercises just about that part". The next day he already mastered it.
3. **True or false.** Ana asked for 10 "true or false" sentences about her lesson. She answered them and AI corrected each one with an explanation.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your AI assistant and write: "I have been learning about [your topic]".
Step 2: Ask: "Make me 3 easy exercises about this topic".
Step 3: Try to answer them WITHOUT looking at your notes. Write your answers.
Step 4: Ask: "Correct my answers and explain my mistakes".
Step 5: Now ask: "Make me 3 medium difficulty exercises".
Step 6: Answer and ask for another correction. If you fail something, ask: "Make me 3 more exercises about what I failed".
Step 7: To finish, ask: "Make me 3 difficult exercises to challenge me".
Step 8: Write in your notebook what you failed and what you got right. It is your progress map.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a personalised exercise? a) An exercise made to our measure. b) An exercise the same for everyone. c) A break.
2. What is gradual difficulty? a) Going up in level little by little. b) Starting with the most difficult. c) Never practising.
3. What do we ask for after doing the exercises? a) The correction and the mistakes. b) Another coffee. c) Nothing.
4. If we fail a part, what do we do? a) Ask for more exercises about that part. b) Give up. c) Forget it.
5. Why must we try the exercises before AI corrects them? a) Because the effort is what teaches. b) To show off. c) We do not have to try them.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Do the exercises yourself: if AI answers them for you, you are not learning, you are pretending.
- Do not use AI exercises to pass someone else's exams or to cheat in a course.
- AI's correction is not infallible: if it marks a correct answer as wrong, review it and discuss it.
- Do not turn practice into anxiety: failing is part of the path, not a shame.
- Share your exercises and techniques with other older people: practising in community multiplies learning.

Exercises turn understanding into knowing how to do, and AI makes them to our measure.
