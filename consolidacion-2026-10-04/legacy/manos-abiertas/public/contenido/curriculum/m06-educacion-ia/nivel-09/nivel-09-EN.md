# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 09
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a study plan is and why it orders learning.
- Learn to ask AI for a weekly plan with days, topics and breaks.
- Set a clear and realistic goal with AI's help.
- Divide study into small blocks, as the week's food is divided.
- Follow the plan for a few days and adjust it when necessary.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Study plan | The road map of our learning: what to study and when. |
| Goal | The target we want to reach, like the destination of a trip. |
| Study block | A piece of time dedicated to a topic, like one meal of the week. |
| Calendar | The place where the plan is marked: days, hours and topics. |
| Break | The necessary pause so the mind can settle, like the stew resting. |
| Adjustment | Modifying the plan if it does not work, like moving a shelf that does not fit. |

## 📚 Main lesson
So far we have learned to converse with AI, to summarise, to explain and to practise. But there is something that unites all that: organisation. Without a plan, studying is like a pot where we throw ingredients at random: something may come out, but it will not be the stew we wanted. A study plan is the written recipe: what, when and how much.

Every good plan is born from a goal. Where do we want to get? It could be: "understand the history of my town in a month", "keep a basic conversation in English", "learn to use the phone without fear". The goal is the lighthouse of the journey. Without a lighthouse, the boat goes round in circles; with a lighthouse, even if slow, it advances.

AI helps us put a date and a measure on the goal. We say to it: "My goal is [what we want]. Is it realistic in a month? How do I divide it into parts?". AI answers whether the goal fits in the time or whether it is better to lengthen or shorten it. It is like asking the tailor whether the fabric is enough for the dress.

With the goal clear, the weekly plan arrives. We ask it: "Make me a study plan for one week, with one topic per day and breaks". AI returns a table: Monday such topic, Tuesday such another, Wednesday review, etc. It is like the week's shopping list: it tells us what is due each day.

The plan must respect our real times. A plan of three hours a day is useless if we only have twenty minutes. That is why we tell AI how much time we have: "I only have half an hour a day". AI adjusts the plan to our life, not the other way round. The plan adapts to the person, like the shoe to the foot.

It is also important to put rest into the plan. A plan without rest is a plan that breaks halfway. AI can set the rest days and the pauses. Let us remember that sleeping and resting help fix what we learned: rest is not the enemy of study, it is its ally.

Each study block must be small and concrete. Instead of "Monday: history", better "Monday: the founding of the town, 20 minutes". What is concrete gets done; what is vague gets forgotten. It is like a recipe: "a pinch of salt" works, but "salt to taste" leaves us without knowing how much to add.

Once we have the plan, we mark it on the calendar. We can write it by hand or ask AI to do it as a list. The important thing is that the plan leaves the screen and enters our life: on the wall, on the fridge, in the notebook. A plan that is not seen is not followed.

Now comes the hardest and the most beautiful part: following it. The first days there is enthusiasm; after a few days, routine asks for its toll. That is why the plan must be kind: if one day we cannot, the plan has a "plan B" (review a little), and if we fail, the plan continues the next day. The kindness of the plan keeps us inside.

After a few days, we review the plan. Does it work? Are there days that escape us? Are there topics that cost us more? We tell AI: "The plan is not going well, I can never on Tuesdays". It adjusts it: it changes topics, times or breaks. Adjusting is not giving up: it is tailoring, sewing and cutting until the suit fits.

AI can also remind us of study days, as we saw with alarms. We can ask it to remind us: "Set Monday and Thursday as my history days". Those reminders are the taps on the shoulder that help us not to lose the way.

A good study plan is like a well-organised wardrobe: everything in its place and a place for everything. With the plan, learning stops being a jumble and becomes a house with rooms. And inside that house, everything is easy to find.

In this level we will create our first study plan with AI: with a goal, days, topics, times and breaks. We will mark it on the calendar and try it for a few days. If necessary, we will adjust it. It will be our made-to-measure suit for learning.

Let us not seek perfection: the best plan is not the prettiest, but the one we truly follow. A simple plan that is fulfilled is worth more than a perfect plan abandoned. Constancy, we already know, beats brilliance.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Marta's lighthouse.** Marta wanted "to know how to make bread in a month". AI divided the month into weeks and each week into a step: doughs, fermentations, ovens and decorations. The goal had its map.
2. **The adjusted suit.** Pedro only had half an hour on Tuesdays and Thursdays. AI made him a plan with half-hour blocks and rest on Wednesdays. The suit fit him well.
3. **The remembering fridge.** Ana printed AI's plan and stuck it on the fridge. Every day, as she passed by, she saw what was due to study. The plan became part of her home.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your assistant and write your goal: "My goal is [your aim] in [time]".
Step 2: Ask it: "Is it realistic? How do I divide it into small parts?"
Step 3: Tell it how much time you have: "I have [X] minutes a day to study".
Step 4: Ask: "Make me a study plan for one week with one topic per day, blocks of [X] minutes and breaks".
Step 5: Read the plan. Does it fit your real life? If not, say: "Adjust it, I cannot on [day]".
Step 6: Mark the plan on your calendar or hang it on the fridge.
Step 7: Follow the plan for three days. Each day, mark in your notebook whether you fulfilled it.
Step 8: On the third day, tell AI how it went and ask for the necessary adjustments.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a study plan? a) The road map of what to study and when. b) An exam. c) A shopping list and nothing more.
2. Where is every good plan born from? a) From a clear goal. b) From luck. c) From doing nothing.
3. What must the plan include so it does not break? a) Breaks and small blocks. b) Endless hours. c) Nothing.
4. If the plan does not work, what do we do? a) We adjust it with AI. b) We throw it away. c) We punish ourselves.
5. What is the best study plan? a) The prettiest. b) The one we truly follow. c) The longest.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The plan is a guide, not a straitjacket: if your life changes, the plan changes with you.
- Do not turn the plan into a reason for guilt: missing one day is not failing, it is information.
- Respect your rest: do not ask AI for impossible plans that rob you of sleep.
- An honest plan tells the truth about your time: do not promise what you cannot fulfil.
- If you share your plan with the community, help them adjust it to their lives, not copy it as it is.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
A study plan orders learning: first a clear goal, then a week with topics, blocks and breaks, and then marking it on the calendar. The plan adapts to our life, is followed with kindness and is adjusted when necessary. We already have our road map. In the next level we will start the Stem band: we will use AI to assess what we have learned and detect where we are missing ground.
