| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Ethical evaluation | Evaluating with fairness, respect and without harming the person. |
| Bias | A prejudice of AI: something it repeats that is not true, like a crooked mirror. |
| Criticism | Looking at things carefully, without swallowing the first thing we see. |
| Fairness | Treating each person fairly, giving each one what they need. |

| Verdict | The final decision about a piece of work or an exam. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Evaluating is a delicate task, almost as delicate as cooking for someone we love: it must be done with care so as not to hurt. Evaluation with AI can be a great help, but it also has its dangers. Ethical evaluation is the one that puts fairness at the centre: the machine helps, but the fairness is put there by us.

What is a bias? It is a prejudice. AI learns from texts made by people, and people sometimes have prejudices. The machine can repeat them without meaning to, like a parrot repeating what it heard. For example, it may link certain trades with a gender, or treat names from other countries differently. Bias is a crooked mirror that shows an image that is not true.

Why does this matter to us when evaluating? Because if AI corrects with bias, it can be unfair without meaning to. It can value a piece of work written in a different style less, or mark a normal word from another region as strange. That is why, when AI evaluates, we have to stay alert, like the cook tasting the stew before serving it.

The first rule of ethical evaluation: AI evaluates the text, not the person. A piece of work with mistakes does not mean the person is a failure. The mistakes are information about the work, not a verdict on their life. When AI says "this is wrong", it is not saying "you are worthless". That distinction is made by us, with words and with affection.

The second rule: AI corrections are reviewed. We never hand over an AI correction without reading it. We ask ourselves: is it fair? Does it respect the person? Does it make sense? If something does not fit, we change it. AI proposes, we decide. It is like the recipe: the machine suggests quantities, but the cook adds the taste.

Feedback must be constructive. Saying "bad" or "so-so" is not enough. Good feedback says what was good, what needs improving and how to improve it. We ask AI: "Correct this work with encouragement and with advice to improve, not with criticism". This way, whoever receives the evaluation feels helped, not crushed.

Praise must also be fair. Some AIs tend to praise too much ("Excellent work!") even when there are mistakes. That is also a bias: the bias of flattery. False praise confuses. We ask AI: "Be honest: say what is good and what is wrong, without exaggerating". Sincerity with affection is the best feedback.

Ethical evaluation also protects privacy. We do not paste into AI an exam with a person's name or intimate data. We only give it the work, without identifying anyone. We evaluate the work, not the person. That separation is an act of respect and ethics.

And real exams? Here there is a very clear line. AI can help practice and prepare, but it must not be used to cheat or to impersonate the person being examined. In an official evaluation, the person must show what they know. Academic honesty is the foundation of all serious education.

When we evaluate others, we also evaluate ourselves. Have we been fair? Have we given each person what they needed? Have we looked at the effort, not just the result? Ethical evaluation begins in our own gaze. With AI as help, but with our conscience as judge.

The inclusive classroom of the previous level is completed with fair evaluation. Evaluating with fairness is another way of including: it is telling each person "your effort counts". Those who feel evaluated with fairness dare to keep learning. Those who feel judged with cruelty give up. It depends on us which path we take.

We never leave important decisions in the machine's hands. A scholarship, a final grade, a future: those are decided by people, with AI as a support tool, not as an oracle. The final responsibility is human. It is like the doctor: the machine gives data, but the diagnosis and the decision are made by the person.

In this level we will practice the complete ethical evaluation: ask AI to correct a sample piece of work, review its correction with a critical eye, detect whether there are biases or injustices and rewrite the feedback with fairness and encouragement. When we finish, we will know how to evaluate with AI without letting AI take away our conscience.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The exaggerated praise.** Marta asked to correct an essay. AI said "Excellent!". Marta reviewed it and there were three mistakes. She asked: "Be honest and point out the mistakes". That is how she discovered the flattery and corrected it.
2. **The word bias.** Pedro noticed that AI marked a word from his region as strange. He checked it and it was correct. He learned that AI also gets things wrong with regional variations.
3. **The encouragement in correction.** Ana asked that the correction "come with advice to improve, not with criticism". She received feedback that said what to reinforce and how. Her student left encouraged.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Prepare a sample piece of work (an essay, some answers, a script).
Step 2: Ask AI: "Correct this work and tell me what is good and what is wrong".
Step 3: Read the correction calmly and with a critical eye. Is it fair? Is there anything that does not fit?
Step 4: Look for signs of bias: does it judge the person? Does it look down on a style? Does it exaggerate praise?
Step 5: Ask for a second version: "Rewrite the correction with encouragement: advice to improve, without hurtful criticism".
Step 6: Adjust the final correction with your own words: add what the machine does not see (the effort, the context).
Step 7: Return the evaluation to the person (or to yourself) with respectful words.
Step 8: Note in your notebook: "AI evaluates the text, the person evaluates with fairness".

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

2. What does AI really evaluate? a) The text or the work. b) The person. c) The future.
3. What do we do with AI's correction? a) We review it with a critical eye. b) We hand it over without reading. c) We delete it.
4. How should feedback be? a) Sincere and with encouragement. b) Cruel and short. c) Pure flattery.
5. Who decides the important evaluation decisions? a) People. b) The machine alone. c) Chance.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Fairness in evaluation is a human responsibility: it is not delegated to the machine.
- AI can repeat prejudices: always review it and correct whatever is unfair.
- A mistake in a piece of work does not define the person: treat them with respect and encouragement.
- Do not use AI to cheat in real evaluations: honesty is the foundation.
- Protect privacy: never upload to AI works with names or intimate data.
