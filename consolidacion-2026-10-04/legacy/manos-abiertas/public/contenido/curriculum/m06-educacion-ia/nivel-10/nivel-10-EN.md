# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 10
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what assessing what we have learned is and why it is as useful as studying.
- Learn to ask AI for a practice test about what we have studied.
- Practise self-assessment: answering, correcting and learning from mistakes.
- Know the difference between assessing ourselves and being assessed by others.
- Use assessment to know what to review, not to punish ourselves.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Assessment | Checking what we know and what we are missing, like weighing the flour before kneading. |
| Practice test | A rehearsal without fear, like the dress rehearsal before a show. |
| Self-assessment | Assessing yourself: answering yourself honestly. |
| Correction | Reviewing the answers and learning from the mistake. |
| Feedback | What AI gives back to us: what we did well and what to improve. |
| Improvement zone | The point where we fail and, therefore, where there is ground to cover. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the kitchen, before serving the food to the guests, we taste it: we taste the salt, the texture, the cooking point. That "tasting" is the cook's assessment. Assessing is not punishing: it is tasting our learning to know whether it is at its point. AI helps us taste our stew with practice tests.

Assessing ourselves is one of the most powerful tools for learning. For a simple reason: when we answer a question, the memory works. Answering an exam is like weaving the memory path again, and that fixes it. That is why whoever assesses themselves learns twice: they study and, by assessing themselves, they study again.

AI is a great examiner. We ask it: "Make me a test of 10 questions about [topic]". And it makes it for us. It can make easy and difficult questions, multiple choice, true or false, short answer. We answer them and it corrects them. It is like having a private teacher who gives us an exam every week.

The best thing about this exam is that there is no fear. A practice test does not grade anything, it is not shown to anyone, it does not punish. It is a rehearsal: it serves to know where we stand. It is like the theatre dress rehearsal: if we fail there, we fix it before the real show.

Self-assessment requires honesty. When we answer, we do not look at the answers before thinking. Failing is fine: failing is the lighthouse that shows us the improvement zone. If we hide the mistakes, we lose the map. The most sincere assessment is the one we give ourselves.

After answering, we ask for the correction: "Correct my answers and tell me what I failed". AI gives us feedback: what is right, what is wrong and why. That explanation of the mistakes is pure gold. One corrected and understood mistake is worth more than ten thoughtless successes.

The correction is not the end: it is the beginning of the review. Everything we failed becomes our to-do list: "review this, reinforce that". It is like when the doctor tells us what hurts: with the diagnosis we know what to take care of. Assessment gives us the diagnosis of our learning.

We can ask for exams in different forms depending on what we want to know. A multiple-choice exam checks that we remember. A short-answer exam checks that we understand. An "explain in your own words" exam checks that we master it. Each type of question measures a different part of learning, like different tools measure different things.

We can also assess ourselves several times on the same topic. When we repeat the exam days later, we compare the marks: have we gone up? Do the old mistakes now come out right? That comparison is the thermometer of our progress. Watching the mistakes disappear is watching our garden grow.

Assessing also teaches us to assess. When AI corrects, it shows us how correcting is done: what makes an answer good or bad. That serves us to examine ourselves even without AI. In time, we will be able to ask ourselves: "What did I not understand well?" and we will know how to answer.

Beware of the temptation to deceive ourselves. If we look at the answer before thinking about it, we are cheating our own learning. Assessment is a mirror: if we lie to the mirror, the mirror is useless. Let us be generous with ourselves, but honest: the truth of the mistakes is what moves us forward.

Assessment also has its social side. We can assess ourselves with a classmate or family member: asking each other questions. It is like playing "guess what I know". Assessing ourselves in company brightens study and makes it more alive. AI can prepare the questions for both.

In this level we will live the full cycle: study a topic, ask for a practice test, answer it without looking, correct it with AI and note the mistakes to review them. When we finish, we will have a clear list of our improvement zone. That list, and not the guilt, is the fruit of assessment.

Let us always remember: assessing ourselves is an act of affection towards ourselves. It is like the person who weighs themselves on the scale: not to get angry, but to know where they are and be able to advance. Assessing is not judging yourself, it is orienting yourself. With the compass of assessment, we never get lost in study.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Marta's rehearsal.** Marta asked for a 10-question test about the history of her town. She failed 4 and wrote them down. This week she reviews them one by one. Now she only fails 1.
2. **Pedro's thermometer.** Pedro assessed himself on vocabulary on Monday and assessed himself again on Friday. Monday's mistakes were successes on Friday: his thermometer was rising.
3. **Ana's mirror.** Ana answered an exam without looking at her notes, even though she itched to look. She failed, but she saw the real mirror: now she knows what to review.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose the topic you want to assess (for example, the one from your study plan).
Step 2: Write to AI: "Make me a test of 10 questions about [topic]. Mix easy and difficult".
Step 3: Answer ALL the questions WITHOUT looking at notes or answers. Write in your notebook or on the screen.
Step 4: Ask for the correction: "Correct my answers and tell me which are right and which are wrong, and why".
Step 5: Read carefully the explanation of each mistake. If you do not understand something, ask: "Explain this mistake better to me".
Step 6: Write in your notebook the list of "my improvement zone": the topics you failed.
Step 7: Keep the list. That list is your review guide for the coming days.
Step 8: Assess yourself again on the same topic in a week to see your progress.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is assessing what we have learned for? a) To know what we know and what to review. b) To punish ourselves. c) Not to study.
2. What is a practice test? a) A rehearsal without fear. b) An official exam. c) A punishment.
3. What does self-assessment require? a) Honesty: answering without looking. b) Lying to yourself. c) Not answering.
4. What do we do with the mistakes? a) We turn them into a review list. b) We hide them. c) We erase them.
5. What does assessment show us? a) Our improvement zone. b) That we are useless. c) Nothing.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Assess yourself honestly: the mirror of assessment is useless if you lie to it.
- Do not use AI exams to cheat in other people's real assessments.
- Mistakes do not define your worth: they are information about your path, not a verdict.
- Do not share the answers of a real exam: academic honesty protects everyone.
- If an AI error in the correction harms you, review it calmly and with respect.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Assessing is tasting the stew before serving it: a practice test done honestly tells us what we know and what we are missing. AI makes and corrects the exams, and the mistakes become our review list. Assessing ourselves is orienting ourselves, not judging ourselves. In the next level we will use assessment to detect knowledge gaps: the holes we still have to fill.
