## 📚 Main lesson
So far we have planted the seed, created the habit, understood memory and organised our study tools. Today it is time to meet our new companion: the artificial intelligence assistant. It is like having a librarian nearby who never tires and answers any question we ask in writing.

What is an AI assistant? It is a program we write a question to and it answers us with text. It is like sending a message to a friend who knows a lot. It has no voice (though some do), it is not a person, and it never tires or gets angry. It can explain, summarise, give examples and make lists.

To use it we only need two things: a screen (the phone or the computer) and access to the internet. There are free, easy-to-use assistants. Some work in the browser, without installing anything. In this level we will learn to open one and ask it our first question.

The first step is to write the question well. It is like when we ask a neighbour for a recipe: if we only say "give me a recipe", they will give us any one. If we say "give me the lentil recipe for four people", they will give us exactly what we want. The clearer the question, the better the answer.

A good trick for questions is to include three things: the topic, what we want and for whom. For example: "Explain what memory is, in simple words, for an older person." That way the assistant knows what to explain, how to explain it and in what tone. It is like giving instructions to the tailor: measure, fabric and style.

The answer we receive can be long. It is fine if we do not understand it all at first. We can ask it to make it shorter: "Now shorter", "with examples", "as if I were ten". The assistant accepts this gladly and repeats it in other words. It is like asking for the explanation again, without shame.

It is very important to know one thing from the start: AI is not perfect. Sometimes it makes mistakes, just as a person who remembers halfway can. That is why, for important facts (numbers, dates, medicines), we always check another source. AI is a companion, not an oracle.

In the world of study, AI serves many purposes. It can explain a concept we do not understand, summarise a long text, make a list of ideas, prepare review questions and much more. But in this level we focus on the most basic and beautiful thing: talking with it and discovering that it answers.

How do we talk to an assistant? Just like with a person: greeting, question, answer, thanks. We can keep adding messages in the same conversation, and the assistant remembers what we told it in that chat. It is like having a get-together where we can ask everything we want.

Some assistants ask us for an email account. It is like registering the library card. The account is created once and then we only have to log in with the email and a password. At Manos Abiertas we help you create your account if you do not have one. It is a simple step that opens many doors.

When we talk to AI, let us not tell it personal secrets or intimate data. It is a study tool, not a confessional. We ask it about what we want to learn, but we do not give it health, money or family data. That prudence always protects us.

The first conversation can go like this. Us: "I want to learn about the history of my town. Where do I start?" The AI: proposes sources, questions and a simple plan. We continue: "Explain why agriculture is important in my town." And so, step by step, we build our own learning.

Practising conversation with AI is like learning to dance: at first we feel clumsy, but each message makes us more agile. There are no bad answers or silly questions. Everything we write to it teaches us to communicate better with it.

By the end of this level we will have made a huge leap: from studying alone to studying accompanied. From now on, AI will be our constant companion, and in the coming levels we will learn to get the best out of it: as a tutor, as a summariser, as a creator of exercises and as a planner.

Let us always remember that the assistant is a tool and we are the ones who learn. It suggests, we decide; it answers, we reason. It is like the stove and the cook: the stove heats, but the cook makes the stew.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The bread question.** Marta did not understand why her bread came out hard. She asked AI: "Why does homemade bread come out hard? Explain it simply." AI gave her three possible causes and she tried them one by one.
2. **The vocabulary review.** Pedro asked AI: "Make me five questions to review the words I learned this week." With the questions, he practised out loud.
3. **Grandmother's plan.** Ana asked: "I want to learn the history of my town, make me a list of topics to start with." The list served as her guide for the following weeks.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open the browser on your phone or computer.
Step 2: Go to a free assistant like the one in the Manos Abiertas class (your teacher will give you the link) or search for "free artificial intelligence chat".
Step 3: If it asks you to create an account, follow the instructions with the help of a relative or the teacher.
Step 4: Write your first question: "Explain in simple words what [your level 00 topic] is".
Step 5: Read the answer. If it seems complicated, reply: "Make it shorter and with an example from everyday life".
Step 6: Now ask it: "What is the most important thing I should know about this topic?"
Step 7: Copy into your study notebook the sentence you liked best or the example that helped you understand best.
Step 8: Close the conversation with a farewell message, as you would with a person: "Thank you very much." Then turn everything off calmly.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is an AI assistant? a) A person who knows everything. b) A program that answers our questions in writing. c) A food calculator.
2. How do you get a good answer? a) With a clear question and context. b) By shouting in capitals. c) By not asking.
3. Does AI always tell the truth? a) Yes, always. b) No, sometimes it makes mistakes and you have to check. c) It never gets it right.
4. What do we tell AI? a) Our secrets and card numbers. b) Study questions, without intimate data. c) Everything, without limits.
5. Who really learns in the conversation? a) Only the AI. b) Us, because AI suggests but we reason. c) No one.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- AI is not an oracle: cross-check its answers on important topics, as you would with any advice.
- Do not give it intimate data: not health, not money, not family.
- Do not settle for an answer just because the machine says it: ask yourself whether it convinces you and why.
- Use AI to learn and create, never to deceive others or to cheat on an exam.
- If AI answers something you do not understand, do not be ashamed: ask it to repeat it in another way.
