# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 00
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand that learning has no age limit and that our minds are still ready to learn.
- Recognize what it means to learn in adult life, with examples from everyday life.
- Discover that artificial intelligence can accompany us while we learn.
- Lose the fear of the word "study" and recover the joy of learning.
- Identify a personal topic we would like to learn in this module.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| To learn | The process of gaining new knowledge or skills, like when we learned to cook. |
| Study | The time we set aside to learn something with attention and a purpose. |
| Knowledge | Everything we know: recipes, trades, stories, facts. It is like the contents of the pantry. |
| Artificial intelligence | A technology that can read, summarise, explain and answer, like a very patient helper. |
| Tutor | A person who teaches and accompanies. In this module we will learn to have a digital tutor. |
| Habit | A custom we repeat often, like watering the plants every week. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Let us imagine a seed. A seed does not ask how old it is or whether it is too late to sprout: it only needs soil, water and sun. Our mind is similar. Over the years we have learned things: to cook, to sew, to drive, to tend a vegetable garden, to do sums. Each of those learnings was a seed that sprouted. Learning has no age.

Sometimes we think studying is only for the young, for school. But all of life is a school. When we learned to use the phone, we learned. When someone taught us a new stew, we learned. The difference is that today we will do it with a purpose and with company, and that company can be artificial intelligence.

What is artificial intelligence? It is a technology that can read texts, answer questions, summarise explanations and suggest ideas. It is like having a very well-read person nearby who never tires and who has great patience. It does not think like us, but it helps organise what we want to learn.

In this module we will deal with a precious topic: education. We will learn to learn better, to study with the help of AI, to create teaching materials and, later on, to teach others. All calmly, with small steps and with metaphors from everyday life.

To begin, let us think about how we learn at home. When we want to learn a new recipe, what do we do? We look for someone who knows it, or we check a book, or we try on our own and correct ourselves. That is learning: observing, practising, making mistakes and trying again. AI can be that book that always answers.

There is something very important we should know from the start: learning takes effort, but it also brings joy. It is like planting a tomato: you have to water it, wait, care for it. And when the first red tomato appears, the joy makes up for all the effort. Studying with AI is similar.

You do not need to be an expert with computers. If we can use a phone to talk to the family, we know enough to begin. The technology we will use in this module is simple: you write a question and the machine answers. It is like sending a message and receiving a reply.

In this first level we will not use any tool yet. We will prepare the soil, like the gardener before sowing. We will look at what we want to learn, why we would like to learn it and how much time we can give it. Without these answers, everything else works worse.

A good way to start is to ask ourselves three questions. First: what topic would I like to learn or improve? It could be a language, the history of my town, how to use my mobile phone, a hobby. Second: what do I want it for? To talk to my grandchildren, to work, out of curiosity. Third: when do I have a little time to study it? With the morning coffee, on Sunday afternoons.

These three answers are the foundation. Just as a house needs foundations before walls, learning needs a reason before a book. AI will help us a lot later on, but the first step is ours, looking inward.

Let us write the answers on paper or on the phone. It does not matter if they are imperfect. What matters is having them at hand throughout the module. When studying gets hard, we will return to those answers to remember why we started.

Let us remember that this is a course for people of forty years of age or more, with no computer knowledge. No one will judge our questions. Here every doubt is welcome, because every doubt is a seed of learning. Whoever asks, learns; whoever stays silent, stays the same.

A suggestion: let us talk about this with someone we trust. Telling a relative or a friend that we are going to learn can encourage us. Besides, sharing what we learn is the best way to fix it: whoever teaches, learns twice.

In the coming levels we will go step by step: first we will understand how we learn best, then we will build the study habit, then we will discover AI study tools and finally we will use AI as a tutor, as a planner and as a creator of materials. It is a long road, but it is walked little by little.

Today we planted the first seed. Like every seed, it needs one more thing: that we believe in it. Let us believe that we can learn, because it is true that we can.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The new recipe.** Marta wants to learn to make homemade bread. Instead of giving up on the first try, she practises on Sunday mornings. Learning is repeating without losing heart.
2. **The grandchildren's language.** Pedro wants to understand his grandchildren, who speak English. His reason is clear: to feel close to his family. That reason will be his engine for months.
3. **The town's history.** Ana wants to learn the history of her town for the festivities. She starts by asking the older people and taking notes. That is how she learned all her life, without realising it.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take a piece of paper and a pen, or open a note on your phone.
Step 2: Write the first question: "What would I like to learn?" Answer with a single idea, the first one that comes to mind.
Step 3: Write the second question: "What do I want to learn it for?" Answer honestly: out of love, for work, out of curiosity.
Step 4: Write the third question: "When can I set aside time for it?" Think of a realistic moment: on waking, at nap time, before sleeping.
Step 5: Read your three answers out loud. Do they sound like you?
Step 6: Keep the paper somewhere visible, like the fridge door, so you do not forget your purpose.
Step 7: Congratulate yourself: you have just taken the first step of the course, which is the most important one.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Learning is a right of all people, at any age. No one should be ashamed for wanting to study.
- The effort of learning is ours: AI helps, but the curiosity and the persistence are ours.
- There are no silly questions. Every doubt deserves respect, ours and that of others.
- Sharing what we learn enriches the community: teaching is a way of caring for others.
- If someone laughs at you for studying at your age, remember that whoever sows today, harvests tomorrow.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Learning has no age and our mind is like a seed that can always sprout. Artificial intelligence will be our companion for studying, but the first step is ours: choosing what to learn, why and when. We have written down our purpose so we do not forget it. In the next level we will discover how to turn learning into a habit, just as we water the plants: a little, but every day.
