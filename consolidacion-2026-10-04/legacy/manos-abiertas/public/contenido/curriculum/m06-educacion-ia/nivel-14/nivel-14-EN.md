# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 14
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what teaching material is: everything that helps teach and learn.
- Learn to ask AI to create clear notes and study sheets.
- Generate simple presentations and activity lists with AI.
- Adapt the material to who is going to learn: children, older people, beginners.
- Save and organise the created materials in our notebook and phone.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Teaching material | Any help for teaching: notes, sheets, games, lists. |
| Study sheet | A short page with the essentials of a topic, like a big card. |
| Clear notes | Ordered notes with titles and points, like a staircase of ideas. |
| Presentation | A set of pages to show a topic, like a book of plates. |
| Activity | A practical task for the learner, like an exercise. |
| Audience | Who the material is for: child, adult, beginner. |

## 📚 Main lesson
When we learn something and want to share it, teaching material is needed: notes, sheets, games, summaries. It is like the tablecloth and the plates: the stew (knowledge) needs a stage to be served on. AI is a great cook of materials: we ask it and it prepares notes, sheets and activities to our measure.

Why create materials? Because teaching is the best way to learn. When we prepare a material to explain something, we order our ideas, look for the essentials and put them in simple words. That process teaches us twice. It is like someone learning a recipe and writing it to give away: by writing it, they really learn it.

The first material we can ask for is clear notes. We say to it: "Make me notes about [topic] with a title, sections and points". AI returns an ordered page. It is like the organised pantry: everything in its place. Those notes are the base of everything else.

For long topics, we ask for a study sheet: "Make me a sheet of [topic] that fits on one page". The sheet gathers the essentials in a small space, like the card from level 12 but bigger. It is perfect for reviewing before explaining or for the learner to use.

The sheets adapt to the audience. If we are going to teach a child, we ask for "with drawings and easy words". If we are going to teach an older person, we ask for "with big letters and examples". AI adjusts the material like the tailor adjusts clothes: depending on who it is dressed for, the suit changes.

Another very useful material is the presentation. We ask it: "Make me a 5-page presentation about [topic]: each page with a title and 3 points". AI gives us the slides. We can flip through them on the phone or computer screen. It is like having a book of plates to teach at a meeting.

Activities are another material. After explaining, practice is needed: "Give me 3 activities to do after reading this sheet". They can be questions, a matching game, or something manual: "draw", "tell", "prepare". AI makes them according to the learner's age.

For the material to truly work, we tell AI who is going to learn. "I am going to teach this to my 8-year-old grandson" or "to my group at the older adults' club". With that information, AI chooses appropriate words, examples and activities. It is like asking who the food is for before cooking: the same stew, different presentations.

A precious trick is to ask for material that "teaches how to teach". We say to it: "Give me a small guide to explain this topic to a beginner". AI gives us advice: what to say first, what to ask, what example to use. It is like having a teacher advising us before our class.

Materials must not stay on the screen: we save them. On the phone we can create a folder called "My materials". In the notebook, we copy what we want by hand. Having them saved allows us to reuse and improve them. It is like the family recipe book: every time someone cooks, it is consulted.

AI can improve our materials with each use. If the sheet we made did not work, we tell it: "The group got lost, make it simpler". AI remakes it. Thus, materials improve with practice, like the stew that comes out better each time because we adjust the ingredients.

Beware of one danger: the material being pretty but not teaching. A teaching material is worth its clarity, not its decoration. If reading it we do not understand the essentials, it is failing. The proof is simple: a person who does not know the topic understands it with our material. If so, it is a good material.

In this level we will create our first complete material: notes, a sheet and an activity about a topic we master. We will do it thinking of a concrete person to teach it to. Then, in the next levels, we will use that material to make educational videos and games.

Creating teaching materials turns us into teachers. And anyone can be a teacher of something: of their trade, of their hobby, of their experience. With AI as an assistant, sharing what we know has never been so easy. It is a gift for the community.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose a topic you know well and want to teach (your trade, your hobby, a course topic).
Step 2: Write to AI: "Make me clear notes about [topic], with a title, 5 sections and points".
Step 3: Read the notes. Are they understandable? If not, ask: "Make them simpler, for beginners".
Step 4: Ask for the sheet: "Make me a one-page sheet with the essentials, to review".
Step 5: Decide who you are going to teach (a child, an older person, a friend) and ask: "Adapt the sheet for [person]".
Step 6: Ask for the activity: "Give me 3 simple activities to do with this sheet".
Step 7: Save everything in a phone folder called "My materials" or in your notebook.
Step 8: Test the material: show the sheet to someone and ask if they understand it. If yes, your material is good.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is teaching material? a) Any help for teaching and learning. b) A toy. c) A bill.
2. What does a study sheet contain? a) The essentials of a topic in a small space. b) The whole book. c) Drawings without text.
3. What do we adapt the material to? a) The audience that is going to learn. b) Nobody. c) The weather.
4. What do we ask for after explaining a topic? a) Activities to practise. b) A difficult exam. c) Nothing.
5. What is the proof that a material is good? a) That someone who does not know the topic understands it. b) That it has many colours. c) That it is long.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Always review the material AI creates: what you sign as yours must be correct.
- If you use texts or ideas from others, say where they come from: honesty is part of teaching.
- Adapt materials to each person and respect their pace: not everyone learns the same.
- Do not create materials that confuse or frighten: clarity is a form of respect.
- Share your materials with the community and let them improve them, as recipes are shared.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Creating teaching materials is teaching and, by teaching, learning twice. With AI we make clear notes, one-page sheets and activities adapted to who is going to learn. A good material is understood by someone who does not know the topic. We are already teachers with tools. In the next level we will take a big step: we will turn our materials into educational videos, with a script, images and voice.
