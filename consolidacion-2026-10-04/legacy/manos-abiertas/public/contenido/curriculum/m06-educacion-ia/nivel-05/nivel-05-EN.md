# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 05
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a personal tutor is and what it can do for us.
- Discover how AI can act as our private tutor.
- Learn to introduce ourselves to AI: who we are and what we want to learn.
- Use AI to resolve questions step by step, without rushing.
- Practise the role of an active learner: ask, doubt and confirm.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Tutor | Someone who teaches and accompanies a student, like a private teacher. |
| Personal tutor | A tutor who focuses only on us, at our pace. |
| Active learner | Someone who asks, thinks and takes part, instead of only listening. |
| Context | The information we give to AI so it understands us: who we are and what we want. |
| Step by step | Explaining one thing after another, skipping nothing, like assembling furniture with instructions. |
| To confirm | Asking AI whether we have understood well, to make sure. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous level we met the artificial intelligence assistant and learned to talk to it. Today we will give it a more important role: that of personal tutor. Let us think about when we were little: there was someone who taught us to read, to add, to ride a bike. A tutor does not give us the answers: it accompanies us until we learn on our own.

A personal tutor is someone who adapts to us. If we go slowly, it goes slowly. If we get stuck, it goes back. If we already know, it moves on. That is the great advantage of AI as a tutor: it is never in a hurry, it never gets angry if we repeat the same question, and it is always willing to explain again in other words.

The difference between an assistant and a tutor is the relationship. The assistant answers loose questions; the tutor knows our learning path and guides us along it. For AI to be our tutor, we have to show it the map: what we want to learn, where we start from and where we want to get.

Introducing ourselves to the tutor is simple. We write something like: "I am going to be a student. I want to learn about [topic]. I do not know anything yet. Start from the beginning and go little by little, asking me if I understand." With that message, AI changes its mode: from answering to teaching.

From there, the conversation becomes a private lesson. AI explains a small piece to us and asks us a question to check we have understood. We answer, and it corrects or encourages. It is like when the teacher asked questions in class: the questions were not to test us, but to help us learn.

Being an active learner is the key. Let us not stay passive reading answers. Let us ask "why?", "how?", "can you give me another example?". The more we ask, the richer the conversation becomes and the better we learn. A good student is not the one who stays silent, but the one who asks.

There is a wonderful trick called "teach me as if it were something else". We can ask the tutor: "Explain photosynthesis as if it were a cooking recipe" or "as if it were sowing a vegetable garden." By translating the topic into something we know, we understand it much better. It is like learning to sew by comparing it to knitting: the threads change, but the idea is the same.

Another tutor technique is "explain and then ask me". We ask: "Explain this point and then ask me a question to see if I have understood it." That way we turn reading into conversation and fix what we learned, as we saw in the memory level: explaining and being asked leaves a mark.

When we do not understand something, let us ask it to lower the level: "Simpler, please", "in everyday words", "as if you were talking to my grandmother." The tutor will obey and rephrase. There is no shame in asking someone to repeat: we have all asked for a recipe to be repeated.

The tutor can also encourage us. We can ask it: "If I make a mistake, explain it to me kindly." And it will answer patiently. Not because it feels affection, but because that is what we asked. We set the tone: with good manners, we receive good answers.

It is important to remember that the tutor does not know everything. If the tutor answers something that sounds strange, let us say so: "That does not add up, can you check it?" Sometimes it corrects itself and sometimes it insists. In any case, we have the last word: no one forces us to believe what the machine says.

To get the most out of it, let us prepare the class. Before starting, let us look in the notebook at what questions we have pending (we wrote them in level 03). Those questions will be the script of our conversation with the tutor. Like going to the doctor with the questions written down.

In this level we will experience a full tutoring session: introduce ourselves, ask for a step-by-step explanation, answer its questions and close with a review. When we finish, we will feel that we had a real private lesson.

Tutoring with AI is a gift of our time. Before, having a private tutor was expensive and only for some. Today, anyone with a phone can have a patient teacher accompanying them. That is equality of opportunity: learning is no longer a privilege, it is a possibility.

And always with the same foundation: the tutor suggests, we decide; the tutor explains, we understand. It is the bridge, but the one who crosses the bridge is us.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The photosynthesis lesson.** Marta did not understand what photosynthesis was. She asked the tutor: "Explain it to me as if it were watering my garden." The tutor compared the light with the sun and the leaves with the roots, and Marta understood.
2. **Grandmother's exam.** Pedro asked: "Explain what cholesterol is and then ask me three things to see if I understood." He answered the three questions and the tutor told him where he was failing.
3. **The kind review.** Ana asked the tutor to correct her mistakes "kindly". When she made a mistake, the tutor said "you almost have it" and rephrased. Ana felt no shame and kept learning.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your AI assistant on your phone or computer.
Step 2: Write to it: "I want to be your student. I am going to learn about [your level 00 topic]".
Step 3: Add: "I do not know anything yet. Start from the beginning, go little by little and ask me if I understand".
Step 4: Read the first explanation and answer its question. If you do not understand it, write: "Explain it to me with a kitchen example".
Step 5: When it asks you, answer in your own words. If you are right, continue; if you fail, ask it: "Explain it again, please".
Step 6: Ask it for a review: "Ask me three questions to check what I have learned today".
Step 7: Write in your notebook a sentence you liked from the class and a question you want to ask tomorrow.
Step 8: Say goodbye politely and close. Your first private lesson is over.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

2. What do we introduce to the tutor so it gets to know us? a) Our topic, our starting point and our goal. b) Our photos. c) Nothing.
3. What does it mean to be an active learner? a) Asking, doubting and taking part. b) Staying silent and obeying. c) Sleeping.
4. What do we ask for when we do not understand something? a) To repeat more simply and with examples. b) To speak louder. c) Nothing, better to stay quiet.
5. Who has the last word about what we learn? a) The tutor. b) Us. c) The neighbour.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The tutor is not a human being: it has no feelings, even if it speaks kindly. Treat it well, but remember it is not a person.
- Do not confuse the tutor's answers with the absolute truth: cross-check what is important.
- Do not use the tutor to do your tasks for you: cheating robs you of learning.
- The student-tutor relationship is yours: do not share the conversation with other people's personal data.
- If the tutor gives you advice about health or money, always confirm it with a professional.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
AI can be our personal tutor if we introduce ourselves and say what we want. A good tutor explains step by step, asks to check and adapts to our pace. The active learner asks, doubts and confirms, and the tutor encourages them to continue. We have had our first private lesson.
