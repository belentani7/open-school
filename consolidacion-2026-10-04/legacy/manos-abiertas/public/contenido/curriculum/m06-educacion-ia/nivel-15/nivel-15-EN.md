# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 15
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what an educational video is and why they teach so well.
- Learn to write a video script with AI's help.
- Learn the "pipeline" (the work chain) of a video: script, voice, images, editing.
- Ask AI for the step-by-step script of our first video.
- Prepare the images that will illustrate the video.

## 📚 Main lesson
Educational videos are windows through which knowledge enters. Seeing and hearing at the same time fixes twice in the memory: the eye remembers the image and the ear remembers the voice. That is why videos teach so well, to children and to older people. And the good news is that today anyone can make them, with AI as an assistant.

Making an educational video is like making a paella: it looks difficult, but it comes down to following the steps in order. That order, that work chain, is called a "pipeline" in the world of technology. It is like the recipe: first the ingredients, then the soffritto, then the rice. If we respect the order, it turns out well.

The pipeline of a video has four stations. The first: the script, the text of what is going to be said. The second: the voice, which reads that text (we will make it with a synthetic voice, as we saw in the audios). The third: the images that accompany it. The fourth: the editing, which joins voice and images. Four stations, like four pots on the fire.

The first station is the most important: the script. Without a script, the video is a pot without ingredients. With a good script, everything else flows. And here AI shines: we ask it for the script and it writes it for us, adapted to our topic and our audience.

How do we ask for a good script? With clarity, as always. We say to it: "I want to make a 2-minute educational video about [topic] for [audience]. Write me the script with an introduction, 3 parts and a closing". AI returns the ordered text, like the floor plan with the rooms marked.

The script must be short and clear. For a 2-minute video, about 250 words are enough, because you speak slower than you read. If the script is too long, the video becomes eternal. The rule is simple: fewer words, more clarity. It is like soup: if it is concentrated, one bowl is enough.

Each part of the script is a scene. The introduction scene presents the topic: "Today we are going to learn...". The three middle parts explain, one idea each. The closing summarises and says goodbye. It is like a meal: starter, main course and dessert. AI helps us keep the menu balanced.

When the script is ready, we move to the second station: the voice. In the next level we will see how to give the script a voice with text-to-speech tools. For now, we only prepare the perfect text: well punctuated, with short sentences and with the words exactly as we want them spoken.

The third station is the images. An educational video needs illustrations: photos, drawings, graphics. Images are the bread of the paella: without them, the dish is half done. AI will help us choose them and create them in a next level. For now, we only think about which image goes with each scene.

The fourth station is the editing: joining everything together. That will come in the following levels. For now, this level's work is to prepare the first station: the script. Because a good video rests on a good script, just like a house on good foundations.

While we write the script, we read it out loud. Reading out loud tells us whether the sentences sound right and whether they breathe at the right moments. If we run out of breath reading a sentence, it means the sentence is too long. We cut it, like cutting the branch that gets in the way.

AI can also help us improve the script. We ask it: "What is extra? What is missing? How would you make it simpler?". It suggests cuts and changes. It is like the food critic: it tells us if the stew is missing salt. We accept what is good and we decide.

This level's goal is simple and precious: to have our first complete script, about two minutes, about a topic we know. With that script in hand, the next levels will turn it into a voice, into images and into a real video. It is the first brick of our video house.

Let us not be afraid to start small. A well-made one-minute video is worth more than a boring hour. We start small and we grow. Like the whole course: step by step, with AI always by our side.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The bread script.** Marta asked for a 2-minute script about "how bread is made" for her grandchildren. AI wrote her: introduction, the 3 ingredients and the oven, and a loving closing.
2. **The club's script.** Pedro asked for a one-minute script about "how to put on hiking boots" for his club. Short, clear and with the steps in order.
3. **The neighbourhood script.** Ana asked for a script to tell the history of her town's square. AI divided it into scenes: the origin, the fountain and the festivals.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose the topic of your first educational video (one you know well).
Step 2: Decide the audience (grandchildren, neighbours, club) and the duration (1 or 2 minutes).
Step 3: Write to AI: "I want to make a [X]-minute educational video about [topic] for [audience]. Write me the script: introduction, 3 parts and closing".
Step 4: Read the script out loud. If a sentence leaves you breathless, ask: "Cut the long sentences".
Step 5: Ask: "Make it simpler and with examples from daily life".
Step 6: Note how many words it has. If it exceeds 250, ask: "Shorten it for 2 minutes".
Step 7: Save the script in your "My materials" folder on your phone or in your notebook.
Step 8: Read the final script out loud one more time, calmly. You already have the first station of the video ready.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The script you use is your responsibility: review and check it before spreading it.
- Do not put false claims or ideas that could harm someone in your video.
- If the video deals with health or money topics, always say that AI is a help, not a professional.
- Respect the audience: adapt the tone to whoever will watch the video, without fear or mockery.
- Give credit when you use other people's ideas: teaching with honesty strengthens the community.
