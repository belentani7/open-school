# Module 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — Level 25
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Fruto
## Estimated time: 8 hours

## 🎯 Level Objective
- Know the current state of AI research: where it's going and what remains unsolved.
- Understand global AI governance: agreements, institutions, geopolitical tensions.
- Reflect on the future of humanity with AI: scenarios, risks, opportunities.
- Define your own ethical and civic stance toward AI.
- Synthesize all module learning into a personal commitment.

## 📖 Essential Vocabulary
| Term | Simple Explanation |
|---|---|
| AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) | An AI that matches or exceeds human capability in any intellectual task. |
| Superintelligence | An AI that vastly surpasses human intelligence across all domains. |
| Existential risk | A risk that could extinguish humanity or permanently curtail its potential. |
| Global governance | International coordination of norms, standards, and controls for AI. |
| Legacy | What we leave to future generations: knowledge, values, decisions. |
| Human agency | The capacity to choose, decide, and act with responsibility. |

## 📚 Main Lesson
This is the final level of the module, and the highest on the tree: the Fruit. Here there are no closed answers, only open questions each person must answer with their life.

AI research advances at breakneck speed. What was science fiction yesterday is an arXiv paper today, a product tomorrow. Recent milestones: models that reason step by step, use tools, code, do science. The declared goal of major labs is AGI: AI that matches or exceeds humans in any intellectual task. Some say years; others, decades. No one knows for certain.

If AGI arrives, the next frontier is superintelligence: an AI that surpasses us in everything. Then alignment (Level 24) stops being a technical problem and becomes existential. A misaligned superintelligence doesn't make mistakes: it optimizes its objective with an effectiveness we cannot counter. That's why safety experts demand capability research be accompanied by alignment research, and pauses, evaluations, and controls before crossing certain thresholds.

Global governance is the attempt to put rules on this race. The EU has its AI Act; the US issued an executive order; China has its regulations; the UN created an advisory body; the G7, G20, OECD have principles. But there's no binding global treaty. Geopolitical tension (whoever gets there first rules) pushes to race, not coordinate. Global citizenship must demand cooperation, not just competition.

What futures are possible? Scenario A: Aligned AI, effective governance, shared benefits. Cured diseases, clean energy, personalized education, work freed for the human. Scenario B: Misaligned AI, uncontrolled race, power concentration. Extreme inequality, total surveillance, loss of agency, existential risk. Scenario C: Something in between, with advances and setbacks, society adapting in fits and starts.

None is written. The future depends on decisions made today: in labs, parliaments, companies, homes. Your vote, your consumption, your voice, your teaching, your demand for transparency, your refusal to use opaque systems, your support for safety research: all of it counts.

Human agency is the heart. AI can be immensely powerful, but who chooses the objectives, who sets the limits, who decides what matters, who cares for the vulnerable, who teaches the next generation, is the human being. Don't delegate your agency. Don't say "technology just advances on its own". Technology advances where humans steer it.

Your legacy isn't what you know about AI, it's what you do with what you know. Do you teach your granddaughter to think critically? Do you demand your city council's AI be explainable? Do you support organizations working for safe AI? Do you vote thinking about the technological future? These are the Fruit's questions.

This module began with "what is AI" and ends with "what do we want AI to be". The path from Seed to Fruit has been long: you understood, used, designed, evaluated, managed, taught, explored the frontier. Now it's your turn to decide.

There's no final exam. Life is the exam. Every day, with every click, every conversation, every decision, you're answering: what kind of future do you want to help build?

## 💡 Practical Examples
1. **Letter to your representative:** write asking them to support an international AI safety treaty.
2. **Club talk:** organize a discussion on "what do we want AI to do in our neighborhood".
3. **Consumption choice:** choose services that audit their AI, protect data, explain decisions.

## 🛠️ Guided Activity
Step 1. Write on a sheet: "My stance on AI is..." and complete it with 3-5 honest sentences.
Step 2. Identify one concrete action you'll take this month (write your representative, organize talk, switch service, donate to AI safety).
Step 3. Review your "digital legacy": what data you leave, what footprint, what you want to remain.
Step 4. Talk with a younger person (child, grandchild, neighbor) about the future with AI. Listen to their fears and hopes.
Step 5. Write a one-page personal commitment: how you'll use AI, what you'll demand, what you'll teach.
Step 6. Save it. Reread it in a year. See what changed and what didn't.

## ✍️ Self-Assessment Exercises
1. What is AGI and why does its arrival change the alignment problem?
2. Why isn't there a binding global AI treaty yet?
3. Describe the three future scenarios (A, B, C) in your own words.
4. What is human agency and why must it not be delegated?
5. Write your personal commitment in three sentences.

**Answers:** 1) AI matching/surpassing humans in any intellectual task; if it arrives, alignment becomes existential because a superintelligence would optimize its objective unchecked. 2) Geopolitical tension: whoever gets there first rules; the race pushes to run, not coordinate. 3) A: aligned, governance, shared benefits. B: misaligned, uncontrolled race, existential risk. C: intermediate, advances and setbacks. 4) Capacity to choose, decide, act responsibly; delegating it means renouncing authorship of the future. 5) (Personal, no single answer).

## ⚖️ Ethical Dimension
The Fruit's ethics is total responsibility. There's no "they decide": you decide with your life. The greatest risk isn't AI, it's human abdication: letting others choose for us, staying silent from comfort, believing it doesn't matter. Human dignity demands participating, demanding, teaching, caring. Legacy is built in the everyday.
