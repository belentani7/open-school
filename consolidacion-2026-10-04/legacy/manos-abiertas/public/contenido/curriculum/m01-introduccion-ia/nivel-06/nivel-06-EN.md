## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root

## 🎯 Level objective
- Distinguish the three big types of AI: narrow, general, and superintelligent.
- Recognize that almost all the AI we use today is narrow AI.
- Understand what general AI is and why it does not exist yet.
- Understand the concept of superintelligence and why it generates debate.
- Place each everyday example in its correct type of AI.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Narrow AI (ANI) | AI that does one specific task better than humans, but only that one. |
| General AI (AGI) | A machine that would reason like a person in any task. It does not exist today. |
| Superintelligence (ASI) | AI that would surpass human intelligence in everything. It is a topic of debate and fiction. |
| Specialist | In AI, a system that masters a single skill, such as translating or recognizing faces. |
| Generalist | A system that could do everything like a person. There is not one yet. |
| Hallucination | When AI invents data or false answers with total confidence. |

## 📚 Main lesson
When we talk about artificial intelligence, it is worth clarifying which one we mean. There are three big types, and confusing them is the cause of many fears and many fantasies. Today we will start by distinguishing them well, as one distinguishes a bicycle, a car, and an airplane: all three take you from one place to another, but they are not the same.

The first type, and the only one that exists today, is narrow AI. "Narrow" means it is specialized in a single task, and in that task it can be better than any human. The phone translator is narrow AI: it translates very well, but it cannot cook or drive. Facial recognition, the email spam filter, the voice assistant: all of these are narrow AI, experts in their single craft.

Narrow AI is like an admirable specialist: a brilliant surgeon who cannot change a light bulb. Each current system masters its own domain and nothing else. It does not get "bored" or decide to do something else. That is why, when we read that AI "masters chess" or "writes poems", it is not the same machine doing many things: they are different systems, each trained for its own purpose.

The second type is general AI, or AGI. It would be a machine that reasons, learns, and adapts like a person in any task: it could hold a conversation, learn to cook, fix a faucet, and write a book, with the same flexibility as a human being. Today, it does not exist. We are not even close, according to experts, although there is much debate.

Why does it not exist? Because current systems, however good, only know how to do what they learned. A translator does not "understand" what it translates; a face recognizer does not "know" who the person is. They do not generalize. General AI would require a leap of real understanding, not just more data and more power. And that leap has not happened yet.

It is important to say this clearly because many news stories exaggerate: when a robot converses naturally, there is no "mind" behind it; there is a highly trained narrow system. Appearances deceive. If you hold a fluid conversation with an assistant, it is narrow AI conversing, not AGI. The day AGI exists, we will all know, and the conversation will be very different.

The third type is superintelligence, or ASI. It would be an intelligence superior to human intelligence in every field: faster, more creative, and wiser than the best human. Here we enter the realm of speculation, science fiction, and the serious debate of many scientists. Some believe it is possible and that we must prepare; others think it is unrealistic and that AI's danger is today, not in the future.

It is worth clarifying that superintelligence does not exist, nor is it "around the corner", in the opinion of most experts. When you see a movie or news about all-powerful machines, remember: it is fiction or speculation. The debate about superintelligence is important, but it must not make us forget what already happens today with narrow AI.

And what happens today is enough to worry us and keep us busy: narrow AI decides what we watch, what is recommended to us, whether a loan is approved, or whether a photo is ours. Those decisions are already here. That is why this course pays so much attention to ethics: we do not need superintelligent machines for technology to affect our lives.

There is one more nuance: some current systems "seem" general. A large language model can talk about medicine, cooking, and football in the same chat. Is that not general? Not quite: it is a very large narrow system. It was trained to predict words and it sounds intelligent, but it has no real understanding or will. It is an impressive trick, but a trick nonetheless.

Compare with a person: you do not just answer sentences. You decide, feel, remember, contradict yourself, and learn from mistakes. Narrow AI does none of that. Every time an assistant "fools" you with a convincing but false answer (that is called hallucination), you are seeing the limit: the system does not know what is true; it only knows what sounds good.

Understanding the three types changes the way we read the news. When you hear "AI will do such and such", ask yourself: which type are they talking about? If it is narrow AI, it is already happening or achievable. If they talk about AGI or ASI, it is probably speculation or science fiction. That question is a very powerful mental tool.

In summary: we live in the world of narrow AI, surrounded by brilliant but limited specialists. AGI is the dream of research, and ASI is the ground of debate. Knowing which is which is the first step to not being fooled.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The phone translator:** narrow AI that translates one language at a time, without understanding what it says.
2. **The email filter:** the narrow AI that separates spam from important email; move it to another job and it knows nothing else.
3. **The voice assistant:** it answers naturally, but it is a specialist in conversing, not a general mind.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1. Take a piece of paper and a pen.
Step 2. Write down five devices or apps you used today (phone, email, car, photos, music).
Step 3. Next to each one, write what specific task it does (translate, filter, recognize, recommend).
Step 4. Now ask yourself: is that task unique and concrete? If the answer is yes, it is narrow AI.
Step 5. Write "narrow" next to each one.
Step 6. Think: could any of them learn to do your gardener's or baker's job? Write down the answer.
Step 7. Share your list with a family member and explain what narrow AI is with your examples.
Step 8. Now define in your own words what it would take for one of those machines to be general AI.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is narrow AI and which example from your daily life fits it?
2. Does general AI exist today? Why do current systems not reach it?
3. What is an AI hallucination?
4. Why is a large language model that converses about everything NOT general AI?
5. What question should you ask when you read news about "AI will..."?

**Answers:** 1) It is AI that does a single task very well; for example, the phone translator or the spam filter. 2) It does not exist; current systems only repeat what they learned and do not understand or generalize like a person. 3) It is when AI invents a false answer with total confidence. 4) Because it is a very large narrow system: it predicts words that sound good, without real understanding or will. 5) Ask which type of AI they are talking about: narrow (likely) or general/superintelligent (speculation).

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Confusing the types of AI has ethical consequences. Exaggerating current AI (saying it "thinks") makes us delegate decisions to it that should be human. And ignoring the debates about AGI and superintelligence leaves us unprepared. The mature attitude is the middle one: use narrow AI with confidence and care, demand transparency today, and take part in the debate about the future. Neither panic nor naivety.

- Superintelligence is speculation and debate, not current reality.
