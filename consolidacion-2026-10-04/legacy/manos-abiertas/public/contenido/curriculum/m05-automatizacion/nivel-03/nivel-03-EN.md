## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous level we set up reminders. Today we move to another very practical automation: the email filter. The inbox is like the mailbox at home: all kinds of letters arrive. If we don't sort them, everything piles up and the important is lost. The filter is the postman who delivers each letter to its drawer without us doing anything.

Email is one of the most used tools, but also one of the messiest. Every day bank notices, offers, news, letters from relatives and, sometimes, scams arrive. Looking for one important email among hundreds is like looking for a needle in a haystack. The filter solves that.

A filter is a rule like the ones we saw in level 0: "if the email is from this person, then take it to this folder". Email already comes with automatic filters built in: many services separate adverts and scams on their own into a folder called "Spam" or "Junk email". We are going to add our own.

Filters are based on signals in the email itself. The most used is the sender: who sends it. If we want the emails from the grandchildren's school to go into a folder, we make a filter that says "if it comes from the school, put it in the School folder". You can also filter by subject: "if the subject says invoice, put it in the Invoices folder".

To create a filter you use the email settings. There is no programming involved: you choose a condition and an action. It is like putting a label on a drawer and teaching the postman to leave each letter where it belongs. The email offers us menus with the options already written.

The condition is the "if": if the email is from a certain person, if the subject contains a certain word, if it comes with an attachment. The action is the "then": put it in a folder, mark it as important, forward it to another account or delete it. That condition-action pair is the heart of the filter.

An important tip: don't start filtering everything at once. Start with two or three rules that solve your biggest problems. For example, a "Bank" folder for money movements and a "Family" folder for letters from your loved ones. With that, the inbox can breathe again.

Filters also protect. Email is the door through which most scams enter. A filter that sends emails from unknown senders to the trash reduces the danger. But careful: scammers change their sender constantly, so the filter is not enough: you always have to look with suspicion.

Fake email, called "phishing", disguises itself as a bank or an official company to steal data. The golden rule: a bank never asks for your password by email. If an email says "your account is blocked, click here", it is almost certainly a scam. Don't click, don't reply and delete it.

The filter gives us peace: we know the important stuff is in its folder and that the rest doesn't steal our time. It is like having a home where everything has its place. A clean inbox takes five minutes to check, not an hour.

Another advantage: filters work even when we're not there. While we sleep, the email classifies the messages that arrive. In the morning, everything is organized. Automation works at night, like bread that rises on its own.

If you make a mistake creating a filter, no problem: it can be edited or deleted, and emails already sorted can be moved by hand. Nothing is final. It is like changing a label on a jar: you take it off and put another one on.

When you finish this level you will have your inbox organized by itself. It will be an automation that works without rest, every day, so nothing important escapes you. And you will have done it without writing a single line of code.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your email on the computer or phone (Gmail, Outlook or whichever you use).
Step 2: Look at your inbox and choose a person or company you receive emails from often (the bank, the school, a shop).
Step 3: In Gmail: open an email from that person, press the three dots (⋮) and choose "Filter messages like these". The email will fill in the condition for you.
Step 4: On the filter screen, type the sender in "From" and press "Create filter". Tick the box "Apply the label" or "Move to folder" and choose to create a new one called "Bank" (or whatever name you want).
Step 5: Press "Also apply filter to X messages" so the old emails move to the folder, and confirm.
Step 6: Check in your inbox that emails from that person no longer appear: they are in the "Bank" folder in the sidebar.
Step 7: Create a second filter with another important person and a new folder.
Step 8: Check your main folder once a day: it should be much cleaner. If something went to the wrong folder, open it, move it to the inbox and review the filter.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Never put your personal data (passwords, account number) in an email, not even when replying to a supposed bank.
- Filters help, but suspicion is your best defence: always check before clicking a link or an attachment.
- Don't forward chain emails or message chains: they are often false or scams.
- Respect other people's privacy: don't open or forward other people's emails.
- If a filter deletes emails automatically, make sure you don't delete important things: start by moving to folders, not deleting.
