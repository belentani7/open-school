## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous level we talked to the assistant for the first time. Today we're going to teach it to do several things at once, with a single phrase. That's called a routine, and it's the most powerful tool of voice assistants. A routine is like a complete recipe that runs on its own: one phrase and the assistant does everything we've programmed.

Let's think of an organised person's morning. The alarm rings, they get up, turn on the light, put on the coffee maker, listen to the weather, read the headlines. That's six tasks. With a routine, you say "OK Google, good morning" and the assistant does it all in a chain: the light, the coffee maker, the weather and the news. One phrase, many actions.

Routines are created in the assistant app, not by speaking. You go into the settings, look for "Routines" and press buttons to add actions. The app shows us a list of things the assistant can do: play music, read the news, tell the weather, turn on devices, say phrases, and many more. We choose the ones we want and order them.

Each routine has two parts. The first is the activation: how it starts. It can be a phrase we say ("good morning") or a fixed time (seven in the morning). The second is the actions: the list of things it does, in order. It's exactly the "if-then" structure we already know: if I say "good morning", then do these five things.

Time activation is very useful: the routine fires on its own, without us saying anything. For example, "at seven in the morning, turn on the kitchen light and play the news". It's like the programmed thermostat from level 0: the rule fulfils itself on its own at its time. We only create it once.

For routines to be truly ours, we personalise them. Every home is different: some have breakfast early, others late; some want news, others music. The routine adjusts to us, not us to it. The app lets us add, remove and order actions until the routine is perfect for our day.

A tip: start with a small routine and improve little by little. A two-step routine that works is better than a ten-step one that breaks. Over time, we add steps calmly. It's like learning to cook a menu: first one dish, then two, then the dessert.

Assistants also integrate with the tools we already know. Many assistants can send information to apps or receive data from them. For example, a routine can jot down a reminder we later pick up in the calendar, or read out loud the summary our Stem band automation generated. Voice joins integration.

We must care for the routines, just as we care for the rest of the automations. If our schedule changes, the seven o'clock routine adjusts. If an action stops working (for example, a device disconnects), we remove or fix it. Reviewing routines once a month is a good habit.

Another detail: naming routines well. A good name makes phrase activation easy to remember: "good morning", "good night", "guest mode". Clear names, simple phrases. The assistant recognises them better and we remember them better.

When you finish this level you'll have at least one routine working: a phrase of yours that does several things. You'll have moved from talking to the assistant to programming it. In the next level we take the step to the whole house: we'll connect devices and automate the home.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The "good morning" routine.** At seven, the speaker turns on the hallway light, reads the weather and plays the news. Ana wakes up with everything ready.
2. **The "good night" routine.** When she says it, the assistant turns off the lights, lowers the heating and plays soft music for half an hour.
3. **The medication routine.** At eight in the evening, the assistant says out loud: "Time to take your medicine". Carmen no longer depends on looking at her phone.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open the assistant app on your phone (Google or Alexa, depending on which you have).
Step 2: Look for the "Routines" section (on Google: "Settings" and then "Routines"; on Alexa: "More" and then "Routines").
Step 3: Press "New routine" or "Create routine". Give it a name: "Good morning".
Step 4: Define the activation. Choose "At a time": set seven in the morning and the days you want it (you can mark all or only weekdays).
Step 5: Add the first action: "Time and date" or "Weather". The app lets you choose with buttons.
Step 6: Add a second action: "News" or "Music", whichever you prefer at that time.
Step 7: Save the routine with the "Save" or "Add" button.
Step 8: Test it: say "OK Google, good morning" or tap the test button. Watch how the actions run in order.
Step 9: Create a second small routine: for example, "good night", with two actions: turn off the light (if you have a connected bulb) and set a notice.
Step 10: Review your routines once a month: remove what no longer works and adjust the times to your life.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- A routine that turns on devices or moves money must be reviewed carefully: a fault can have real consequences.
- Don't use activation phrases that anyone could guess for sensitive actions (opening doors, paying).
- If there are children or elderly people at home, explain what each routine does and why: transparency avoids scares.
- Routines that record voice keep your words: review the history and delete what you don't want.
- Don't run the same health routine with invented data: medication alerts must be exact.
