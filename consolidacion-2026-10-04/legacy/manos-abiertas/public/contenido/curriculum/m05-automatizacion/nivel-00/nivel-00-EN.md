# Module 5: Automation and Integration — Level 00
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 1 hour

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what automating means with examples from everyday life.
- Recognize that machines and phones can do tasks for us.
- Tell the difference between a task we do by hand and an automatic task.
- Identify automations we already use without noticing.
- Lose the fear of the word "automation".

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| To automate | To make a machine do a task for us, without us having to keep an eye on it. |
| Automation | The result of automating: the task that does itself. |
| Repetitive task | A job that is done over and over again in the same way. |
| Device | An electrical appliance: the phone, the washing machine, the television. |
| Rule | A fixed instruction: "if this happens, then do that". |
| Instruction | A clear order you give to the machine. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Today we start module 5, called "Automation and Integration". The word sounds complicated, but the truth is that automation has been part of our lives for many, many years. Let's think of the washing machine.

Long ago, washing clothes was a long job: filling water, scrubbing, rinsing, wringing and hanging out to dry. The washing machine does all of that for us. We put in the clothes, choose the programme and press a button. The machine does the rest. That is automating: giving a machine a task that repeats and that it does on its own, while we do other things.

Another example: the alarm clock. We don't need to remember to wake up at seven because the alarm clock reminds us. We have programmed the clock and it takes care of it. It is a very simple and old automation.

Also the heating thermostat. If we programme it to turn on at six in the afternoon, the house will be warm when we arrive, without us doing anything. The machine follows the rule we have given it.

Today phones automate too. When the phone makes a backup of the photos at night, it is automating. When the bank app warns us about a money movement, that too. When the phone changes to daylight saving time by itself, that is another automation.

The key to all automation is the rule. Every automation follows the same pattern: "if this happens, then do that". If it is seven o'clock, then the alarm clock rings. If a message arrives, then the phone warns us. If the washing machine finishes, then it warns us with a sound.

To automate you need three things. First, a task that repeats in the same way. Second, a clear rule that says when to do it. Third, a device able to do it. When the three come together, we can automate.

Not everything can be automated. Tasks that change a lot, that require deciding at every moment, still need a person. The machine repeats; the person decides. That difference is very important and we will come back to it throughout the whole module.

Automatic does not mean perfect. If the rule is badly set, the result will be bad. If we programme the alarm clock for six but wanted to get up at seven, we will wake up early. The machine does what we tell it, not what we want.

That is why the most important step in automating is thinking the rule through before programming it. Writing clearly what we want to happen and when. It is like writing a recipe before cooking: if the recipe is clear, the dish turns out well.

In this module we will learn to automate household tasks, office tasks, email and work tasks. We will start very slowly and with simple things, and we will end up connecting apps and building small assistants.

Today we are only going to look. We are going to observe our own home and our own phone and discover all the automations already working around us without us noticing. Seeing is the first step before doing.

When we learn to look, technology stops being mysterious. The washing machine is not magic: it is a rule carried out by a machine. Neither is the alarm clock. And when we understand the rule, we can create our own automations.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Get a piece of paper and a pen. Draw two columns: "I do it" and "The machine does it".
Step 2: Think about your morning on a normal day and write each task in the right column: waking up, putting on the coffee maker, turning on the tap, checking your email.
Step 3: Look on your phone for the "Automatic time" setting (in Settings, under "Date and time"). Check it and you will see it is on.
Step 4: Open the alarm clock app and create an alarm for tomorrow at your lunchtime. Press "Save" or "OK".
Step 5: Now look at the phone's email and see if notifications arrive on their own. That is another active automation.
Step 6: On your paper, write at the end the rule of one of your automations: "If ..., then ...". If you can't think of one, use the alarm clock's: "If it is 14:00, then the alarm rings".
Step 7: Keep the paper. We will use it in the next levels to build our own rules.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What does automating mean? a) Making a machine do a task for us. b) Turning off the appliances. c) Doing everything by hand.
2. What is the basic rule of all automation? a) "If this happens, then do that". b) "Always do the opposite". c) There are no rules.
3. Which of these tasks can be automated easily? a) Deciding which gift to buy. b) Setting an alarm at the same time every day. c) Comforting a friend.
4. Does an automatic machine do what it wants? a) Yes, it decides on its own. b) No, it does what the rule tells it. c) Only sometimes.
5. Does the phone use automations? a) No, never. b) Only the camera. c) Yes, for example automatic time and photo backup.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-c.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- A badly designed automation can cause problems: if a rule is confusing, the result will be confusing. Think carefully before programming.
- Not everything should be automated. Decisions that affect people, like attending to someone or apologizing, are human.
- Automating a task does not mean washing your hands of it: you always need to check that the machine does it well.
- Share your automations with the family so everyone understands what each device does and why.
- If an automation surprises you or makes you uncomfortable, you can pause it or turn it off. You are in charge.

Automating is making a machine do a repetitive task for us, following a clear rule of the type "if this happens, then do that".
