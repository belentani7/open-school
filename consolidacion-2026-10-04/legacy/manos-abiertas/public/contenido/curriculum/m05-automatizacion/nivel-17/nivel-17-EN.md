# Module 5: Automation and Integration — Level 17
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what home automation is.
- Learn the basic devices: smart bulbs, plugs and sensors.
- Connect a smart device to the voice assistant.
- Create scenes that change several devices at once.
- Start with a single room and grow little by little.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Home automation | Automating the home: devices that obey on their own. |
| Smart device | A device that connects to the internet and is controlled remotely. |
| Smart plug | The adapter that makes a normal device "smart". |
| Sensor | A device that detects something: movement, light, temperature. |
| Scene | A set of settings applied to several devices at once. |
| Hub or bridge | The device that talks to all the others (sometimes it's the speaker). |

## 📚 Main lesson
Today we make the big leap of the Branch band: we automate the house. Home automation means the home's devices obey the voice, the schedule or the sensors without us doing anything. It's the same idea as always: rules that fulfil themselves, now in the physical world.

Let's start from the beginning: you don't need to fill the house with expensive devices. Home automation is built step by step, starting with one room and two or three simple devices. The best entry is the smart plug: an adapter that plugs into a normal socket and makes "smart" the device we put on top of it. An old lamp, a fan or a coffee maker become devices we can turn on and off by voice or by schedule.

The smart plug installs in seconds: you plug it in, connect it to the app and give it a clear name, "living room lamp". From then on, the device on top is controlled from the phone and the assistant. It's like giving the lamp a new remote.

Smart bulbs are another very popular first step. They screw in like a normal bulb, connect to the app and let you turn on, turn off and change the colour or brightness. With a smart bulb, "Alexa, dim the living room light" really works. It's the door to home automation magic.

Sensors add the third way to automate: besides voice and schedule, the sensor detects the world. A motion sensor turns on the hallway light when we pass and turns it off when no one is there. A door sensor warns if it opens. A temperature sensor regulates the heating. The sensor is the "eye" of the smart home.

In home automation, the rules are still the if-then ones: if the sensor detects movement, then the light turns on; if it's ten at night, then lower the heating; if I arrive home (detected by the phone), then turn on the hallway light. Everything we learned in the previous levels applies, now with physical devices.

The scene is the jewel of home automation. A scene is a set of settings applied to several devices at once. The "cinema" scene turns off the big light, dims the sofa one and raises the volume. The "good night" scene turns off everything except the hallway one. With one phrase or button, we change the whole house. It's the level 16 routine, but with physical devices.

To connect the devices you use an app, usually the device brand's or the assistant's. The app guides you step by step: find the device, connect it to the home network and give it a name. Then the assistant recognises it and we can use it in routines and scenes. If we know how to create a routine, we already know almost everything.

A very important piece of advice: don't buy everything at once. Start with one plug or bulb in a single room, master it and then add more. Home automation that grows slowly is understood and maintained; the one that grows suddenly becomes a mess of devices nobody knows how to control. It's like the garden: better one well-cared plot than five abandoned ones.

We must also be careful with security. Smart devices connect to the internet, and that has risks. You need strong passwords on the home network and accounts, update the devices when they ask and not buy unknown brands without checking. A smart home must be, above all, a safe home.

Another piece of advice: think about whether it really serves you. Not every device needs to be smart. Automating the hallway light can be wonderful; automating the toaster, a silly idea. The value rule (level 1) still applies: we automate what we repeat and what tires us, not everything that occurs to us.

When you finish this level you'll have your first smart device working, and it will obey your voice or your schedule. It's the beginning of the automatic house. In the next level we'll see a free and powerful way to manage it all: Home Assistant.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose a room and a single thing you want to automate: a lamp to turn on by voice or schedule.
Step 2: Buy or get a smart plug or a simple smart bulb (ask for advice in a trusted shop or from someone who already has one).
Step 3: Plug in the device and download its brand's app (it's indicated on the box).
Step 4: Follow the app to connect the device: it usually asks you to turn on the phone's bluetooth and home wifi. Give it a clear name: "living room lamp".
Step 5: Test the device from the app: turn it on and off with the on-screen button.
Step 6: Connect the app with your voice assistant (in the assistant's settings, look for "Devices" or "Add device" and link the brand's account).
Step 7: Test the voice: say "turn off the living room lamp" and then "turn it on".
Step 8: Create a simple routine (level 16): "at eight in the evening, turn on the living room lamp".
Step 9: Write on your paper what worked, what didn't and what you'd add later.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Smart devices collect data about your life (schedules, movements): review what each app keeps and what it lets you share.
- Set strong passwords on the wifi and accounts: a poorly protected automatic home is an open door.
- Don't share device access with strangers or with companies that just "pass through".
- Think about the other people in the house: home automation should help everyone, not confuse the elderly or children.
- If a sensor fails (for example, the light doesn't come on at night), review and fix it: a neglected security automation is dangerous.
