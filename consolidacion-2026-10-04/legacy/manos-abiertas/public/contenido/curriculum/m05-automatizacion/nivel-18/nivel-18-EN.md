# Module 5: Automation and Integration — Level 18
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what Home Assistant is and why it's different from the brands' apps.
- Learn the concept of a "smart home without depending on the cloud".
- Learn what a server is and how Home Assistant can live on a small device.
- Discover open and free alternatives to commercial assistants.
- Decide whether Home Assistant is for you or whether you keep it simple.

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous level we connected our first smart devices. Each brand brings its own app, and that has a problem: if we have a bulb from one brand and a plug from another, we need two apps, two accounts and two ways of thinking. Home Assistant is the answer: a program that brings all home devices under one roof, without depending on any brand.

Let's think of the kitchen. If every appliance had its own remote control with its own way of being used, cooking would be chaos. We prefer an organised kitchen where everything has its place. Home Assistant is that order: a single dashboard from which all the home devices are seen and controlled.

The big difference with the brands' apps is where the data lives. The apps send our data to the cloud, to the company's computers. Home Assistant works locally: everything lives on a small computer at home. The data doesn't leave the house, the house works even if the internet fails, and no one else sees it. It's like having the pantry at home and not in someone else's warehouse.

What device do we need for Home Assistant? A small computer that always runs. It can be a device bought on purpose (there are boxes designed for it) or an old computer we no longer use. You install the program once and leave it working, like an oven that watches everything. You don't need to know how to program to start: the program guides you on screen.

Once installed, Home Assistant "discovers" the home devices. You add integrations to it: each integration is the way to talk to a brand or a device. On the dashboard, we add our bulb's integration, our plug's, our sensor's. Little by little, everything appears on a single screen.

The real power comes with Home Assistant's automations. They're the same if-then rules as always, but without brand limits: we can say "if the motion sensor activates after nine at night, then turn on the hallway light". The condition can look at any device and the action can touch any other. The whole house becomes a single flow.

Besides, Home Assistant integrates with voice assistants. We can keep saying "OK Google, turn off the light" and have that command reach our Home Assistant. Or we can use free assistants, without the big companies. The house can speak our language, even with its own voice in Spanish.

Home Assistant isn't for everyone, and that's fine. If you have a single device or prefer things simple, the brand's app and the assistant are enough. Home Assistant shines when there are several devices, from several brands, and we want total control and privacy. It's like choosing between the TV remote or a universal remote: depending on how many devices you have.

The learning curve is a bit higher than the brands' apps. It's not hard, but it asks for patience and a desire to explore. That's why it's recommended to start with a known device and a simple automation, and grow from there. There's no rush: the house will still be there tomorrow.

A point in favour of open: it doesn't depend on a company. If a brand closes or changes its service, Home Assistant keeps working with our stuff. And if a device loses support, the community usually finds another way to integrate it. It's like sowing in your own garden: you don't depend on the neighbourhood shop.

Home Assistant's community is huge and kind: forums, guides in Spanish and videos that explain every step. When something gets stuck, there's always someone who already solved it. Learning with others is easier than learning alone. That sharing spirit is part of this course.

When you finish this level you'll understand what Home Assistant is, how it differs from the brands' apps and whether it suits you. In the next level we'll move from the house to messages: we'll create simple chat bots that reply on their own.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The multi-brand home.** Rosa had lights from one brand and plugs from another. With Home Assistant she controls them all from a single screen, without separate accounts.
2. **The night automation.** "If it's ten and the sensor detects no movement in the hallway, turn off the lights in the whole house." One rule, the whole house.
3. **The internet outage.** Jorge's internet went down and the brands' apps stopped working. His Home Assistant kept turning on the lights: it works locally.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Learn about the devices where Home Assistant lives: search online for "Home Assistant Green" or "Home Assistant on Raspberry Pi" and read what it is.
Step 2: Decide whether you already have an old computer or prefer a ready-to-use box. Don't buy yet: only research.
Step 3: Visit home-assistant.io and watch the introduction videos you find in Spanish. Note two ideas that catch your attention.
Step 4: Check the list of integrations for your current device (if you have one): look up your brand's name on the Home Assistant integrations site and confirm it exists.
Step 5: Write on your paper an automation you'd do with Home Assistant that you can't do with your current app.
Step 6: Search online for a Spanish forum or group about Home Assistant and read a beginners' thread.
Step 7: Decide and write down: is Home Assistant for you now, later, or do you prefer to keep it simple? There's no wrong answer.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

2. Where do Home Assistant's data run? a) In a company's cloud. b) Locally, on a small computer at home. c) On the neighbour's phone.
3. What do you need to install Home Assistant? a) A small computer that always runs. b) A television. c) Mandatory fibre internet.
4. Can it be used with voice assistants? a) No, never. b) Yes, with the commercial ones and also with free assistants. c) Only in English.
5. Is Home Assistant for everyone? a) Yes, always. b) No, depending on how many devices you have and your desire to explore. c) Only for qualified technicians.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Having the data at home is more private, but it's also your responsibility to protect it: set strong passwords and update the system.
- Home Assistant collects information about your daily life: you decide who sees that information and who doesn't.
- If your automatic home manages doors or alarms, make backups and review the system: physical safety depends on it.
- Share what you learn with the community, but don't upload personal data or photos of your home to the forums.
- Free technology doesn't mean free from care: you're still responsible for what you build.
