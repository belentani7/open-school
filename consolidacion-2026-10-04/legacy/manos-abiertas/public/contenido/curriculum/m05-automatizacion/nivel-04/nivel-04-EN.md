## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Rule | A fixed instruction: "if this happens, then do that". |
| Do not disturb mode | A setting that silences the phone so you don't bother and aren't bothered. |
| Automation | The rule in action, the task that does itself. |
| Routine | A set of rules that work together, like a full recipe. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the three previous levels we set up reminders and email filters. Today we are going to bring all the pieces together and learn the most important idea of this module: the rule. The rule is the brick with which all automation is built, and it is always written the same way: "if this happens, then do that".

We already know examples of rules. The alarm clock: if it's seven o'clock, then it rings. The filter: if the email is from the bank, then it goes to the Bank folder. The reminder: if it's eight o'clock, then it reminds you to take your medicine. They are all the same idea with different names.

Today we are going to put rules on the phone itself. The modern phone has built-in automations we can activate without installing anything. One of the most useful is Do not disturb mode: if it's night, then the phone goes silent and doesn't ring until morning.

Do not disturb mode is like the "quiet hour" at home. We can programme it: for example, from eleven at night to seven in the morning, the phone makes no noise. Emergency calls still get through if we allow it. So we sleep without every notification waking us.

Another very practical rule is automatic sound mode: if it's meeting or class time, then the phone goes silent; when it ends, it rings again. It is like the traffic light that changes by itself according to the time. The phone knows when it should be quiet.

We can also make the phone enter battery saver mode when it drops below 20 percent, or turn on aeroplane mode at night, or set the screen to a warm tone from a certain time. All these rules follow the same if-then pattern.

Time to bring it together. A routine is a set of rules that work together, like the steps of a recipe. Our morning routine could be: at seven the alarm rings, at a quarter past seven the medicine reminder rings, at eight the phone in meeting mode. Three rules, one routine.

You don't need any special tool to set up a routine: you create the rules one by one and the phone follows them all. It is like planting several furrows in the garden: each one gives its fruit, and together they form the harvest.

The important thing about a good rule is that it is clear and simple. "If it's eleven at night, then silence" is clear. "If it's night, roughly, when I'm tired" is not. The simpler and more concrete the condition, the better the machine will carry it out.

You also have to review the rules from time to time. Our hours change: if we start working later, the seven o'clock routine no longer works. Rules are edited, paused or deleted just like a reminder. Keeping them up to date is part of the craft.

A very common mistake is activating too many rules at once and not knowing which does what. Better a few rules that are well understood than many confusing ones. We start with two or three rules and add more calmly.

When we understand the rule, we understand automation. Everything that comes in this module, however complicated it looks, will always be the same thing: an "if" and a "then", perhaps with more steps. The rule is the seed; the next levels will be the tree.

Today we finish the Seed band, the first five levels. We already know what automating is, which tasks repeat, how to create reminders, how to filter email and how to put rules on the phone. That is much more than it seems. Now comes the next step: connecting apps to each other.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Rest.** Luis programmed Do not disturb mode from 23:00 to 7:00. Now he sleeps without the neighbourhood group notifications waking him.
2. **The yoga class.** Ana has yoga class on Tuesdays and Thursdays at nine. She programmed the phone to go silent at half past eight on those days.
3. **The battery.** Carmen activated the battery saver rule at 20 percent. The phone takes care of itself and warns her when it needs charging.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open the phone Settings (the gear icon).
Step 2: Look for "Sound" or "Sounds and vibration" and inside "Do not disturb" (or "Do not interrupt"). Enter it.
Step 3: Press "Schedule" or "Add a schedule" and choose "Night". Set the start time (for example, 23:00) and the end time (07:00).
Step 4: Choose which sounds you allow: let calls from your favourites or important contacts through, and keep the rest silent. Turn the rule on with the switch.
Step 5: Now look for "Battery" in Settings and see if there is an "Automatic battery saver" or "Save at 20%" option. Turn it on.
Step 6: Go back to Settings and look for "Display": turn on the "Night light" or "Digital wellbeing" option so the screen is warm at night.
Step 7: Write on your paper the three rules you activated with their "if" and their "then". For example: "If it is 23:00, then silence".
Step 8: Tonight, observe: the phone should go silent on its own. Tomorrow, check it: what doesn't ring at night will be because of your rules.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. How is an automation rule written? a) "If this happens, then do that". b) "Do whatever you want". c) "Always yes".
2. What is Do not disturb mode for? a) To make the phone louder. b) To silence it at certain times and protect rest. c) To delete photos.
3. What is a routine? a) Several rules that work together, like the steps of a recipe. b) A single button. c) A type of filter.
4. Can the rules be changed later? a) No, once set they last forever. b) Yes, they are edited, paused or deleted. c) Only technicians.
5. Is it better to have many rules or a few clear ones? a) Many, the more the better. b) A few, well understood. c) None.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Do not disturb mode protects your rest, but don't use it to ignore someone who may need you: let calls from your important contacts through.
- A rule that silences the phone at work could make you miss an urgent notice. Think about what exceptions you need.
- Never share your unlock code with anyone, not even to "try something".
- Review your rules when your life changes (schedules, work, health): an old rule can do exactly the opposite of what you need.
- Automations help you, but important decisions remain human.
