## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous levels we built our automations step by step, from scratch. Today we're going to learn the most valuable shortcut of the trade: templates. A template is a ready-made automation that another person has built, checked and published so anyone can copy and adapt it. It's like a family recipe shared around the neighbourhood.

Let's think of sewing. We can learn to cut and sew a pair of trousers from scratch, measuring, drawing and trying. Or we can use a pattern: the trousers' outline already drawn, which we only need to adapt to our size. The pattern is the template. It's not cheating: it's working smart, taking advantage of what others have already solved.

Make and Zapier have a huge library of templates: thousands of automations already assembled, ready to copy. There are templates for storing data, sending notices, organising emails, generating documents and much more. Each template comes with its explanation: what it does, which apps it uses and what it needs.

Using a template is very simple. You search for the one that interests you, press "Use template", and the tool copies it to your account, already assembled with its triggers, actions and fields. Then we only have to adapt it: connect our accounts, choose our apps and review the fields.

The magic of the template is knowing what to change and what to leave. The pieces that don't depend on us (the logic, the order, the steps) are left as they are. What depends on us (our accounts, our sheets, our messages) is changed. It's like the recipe: the technique is respected, the ingredients are adjusted.

A good piece of advice: before touching anything, read the template's explanation. It will tell us what it's for and what we'll need. Reading the documentation is like reading the back cover of the book: it saves us starting from the end. Five minutes of reading avoid an hour of mess.

Templates also teach. When we open a well-made template, we see how others organise their steps, what fields they put and how they name things. Copying well is a way of learning: we copy, we adapt, and in doing so we understand. It's like copying the handwriting of a good scribe to improve our own.

We must be careful about one point: the template uses the author's apps, not ours. The text will say "Google Sheets", but it will be our sheet that we have to connect. Adapting is exactly that: telling the template "these are my data, this is my account".

Not all templates work equally well. Some haven't been updated in a long time or use paid apps. Before using one, let's check that the apps are ours and that the free plan is enough. The tool warns us if a piece needs a paid version.

When we adapt a template, let's test it just as we did in level 6: with test data, before activating it. The template is a good starting point, not a promise that it works with our data. Testing remains our duty.

When you finish this level you'll have your second automation built in a fraction of the time of the first. And, most importantly, you'll have learned the habit of searching before inventing: in the world of automation, most things are already made. You only have to find them and adapt them.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your integration tool (Make or Zapier) and log into your account.
Step 2: Look for the "Templates" section in the main menu.
Step 3: Type something that interests you in the search box: "welcome", "reminder", "payment", "form". Look at the results.
Step 4: Choose a simple template and press "Use this template". The tool will create it in your account.
Step 5: Read the template's explanation: what it does and which apps it needs. Write it on your paper.
Step 6: Connect your accounts: where the template says "Connect", press and authorise your app (for example, your Google).
Step 7: Review the fields: change the sheet name, the folder or the message text so it's yours.
Step 8: Press "Test". See if it works with a test piece of data. If it fails, read the notice and fix the fields.
Step 9: Activate the adapted template. Check that your second automation is running.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Other people's templates can contain errors or doubtful intentions: review what each step does before activating it.
- Some templates move data to third-party services: check where your data travels.
- Respect template creators' permissions: use them for their intended purpose and don't sell them as your own.
- Adapting a template doesn't free you from testing it and caring for the data it moves.
- If you improve a template, share it: open knowledge grows when everyone contributes.
