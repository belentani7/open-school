# Module 5: Automation and Integration — Level 14
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective

- Test the complete project step by step.
- Document the project so it can be maintained.

## 📚 Main lesson
We've reached the end of the Stem band. In these five levels we learned if-then logic, variables, artificial intelligence and error handling. Today we're going to put it all together in a complete project, like someone cooking a whole wedding menu after having practised each dish separately.

The secret of big projects is order. You don't build directly in the tool: first you draw the plan. Before touching the scenario, we take a piece of paper and draw the flow: what trigger starts it, what conditions it decides, what variables it uses, where AI comes in and what happens if something fails. That drawing is the map of the journey.

Drawing first has two advantages. First: we see the whole project before spending time building. Second: we can show it to another person and ask "is this what you want?". Changing a paper is free; changing an already-built scenario costs. It's like measuring twice before cutting the fabric.

A Stem band project could be the "smart message classifier". The trigger: a new message arrives in a sheet or form. The variable: the message text. The AI: classifies the message as urgent, normal or advertising. The condition: if it's urgent, warn immediately; if normal, save for the report; if advertising, discard. The error handling: if the AI fails, the message is marked "pending review" and someone is warned.

Notice the pieces: the flow isn't hard, it's orderly. We learned each piece separately in levels 10, 11, 12 and 13. The project doesn't invent anything new: it joins the known in a chain. That's the big lesson: complexity is mastered by dividing it into pieces we already know how to handle.

When building, we go by phases. First phase: the trigger and capturing the message. Second: the condition that the text isn't empty. Third: the AI step with its prompt. Fourth: the branches according to the reply. Fifth: error handling. Each phase is tested before moving to the next. Building by phases is like raising a house: first the foundations, then the walls, then the roof.

When the project works, documentation arrives. Documenting is writing, in a document or on the same paper, what the flow does, what decisions it makes, what errors it has and how it's maintained. It's not for others: it's for us, three months from now, when we've forgotten why we put that condition. Documentation is the project's memory.

Documentation doesn't have to be long. A few sections are enough: what it does, how it's activated, what variables it uses, what conditions it has, what errors it watches and what needs to be reviewed from time to time. It's like the washing machine's maintenance sheet: we read it when something strange happens.

Maintenance is the last phase and it never ends. Each week, a glance at the log. Each month, check that the accounts are still connected and that the flow is still useful. A flow that no longer serves can be paused or deleted without regrets. Maintaining also means knowing when to retire things in time.

When you finish this level, we close the Stem band with a complete, documented project. You already know how to read data, decide, think with AI and watch errors. That's a high level of mastery. In the next band, Branch, we leave the screen: we'll talk to voice assistants and automate the house.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The neighbourhood classifier.** Each notice arriving through the form is classified with AI, the urgent ones are announced immediately and the rest goes into the weekly report.
2. **The suggestion manager.** Neighbours' suggestions are stored, the AI summarises them every month and error handling warns if something gets lost.
3. **The sheet watchdog.** If the amount exceeds 50 euros, the treasurer is warned; if the AI detects a health topic, it's referred to the responsible person.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take a piece of paper and draw your project's map: the trigger on the left and the steps to the right.
Step 2: Choose a simple project: "Message classifier". Write the trigger (a new message in a sheet or form).
Step 3: Draw the first condition: if the text is empty, stop. Write the "yes" and "no" branches.
Step 4: Draw the AI step: ask it to classify as urgent, normal or advertising. Note the exact prompt you'll write.
Step 5: Draw the reply branches: urgent (notice), normal (report), advertising (discard).
Step 6: Draw the error handling: if something fails, a review notice.
Step 7: Build the flow in the tool following the map, phase by phase. Test each phase with test data.
Step 8: Document in a document: what it does, variables, conditions, errors and maintenance.
Step 9: Activate the project and schedule a weekly review in your calendar.
Step 10: Share the map and documentation with a trusted person and ask them if they understand it: if they do, so do you.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What's the first thing you do in a big project? a) Build directly in the tool. b) Draw the plan on paper. c) Activate and see.
2. Why do you draw the flow first? a) Because the tool demands it. b) To see the whole project and change the paper for free. c) To colour in.
3. What is documenting? a) Writing what the flow does so it can be maintained. b) Signing the project. c) Taking photos.
4. How is a complex project built? a) All at once, without testing. b) By phases, testing each one. c) At random.
5. What do we do with a flow that no longer serves? a) Keep it forever. b) Pause or delete it. c) Hide it.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Documenting a project is also taking responsibility: knowing what your automation does lets you answer for it.
- A project that decides (classifies, prioritises) must be explainable: anyone should be able to understand why a message is "urgent".
- Before letting the automation decide about people, ask whether it's fair and reviewable.
- Don't build projects that move people's data without them knowing that flow exists.
- Maintenance isn't only technical: it also checks that the project stays ethical and useful over time.
