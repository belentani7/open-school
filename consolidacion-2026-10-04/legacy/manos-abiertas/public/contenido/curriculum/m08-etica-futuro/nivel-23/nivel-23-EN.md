# Module 8: Ethics, Future and Governance — Level 23
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Fruit
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what artificial general intelligence (AGI) is and how it differs from current AI.
- Learn the difference between a machine that does tasks and one that reasons like a person.
- Know that AGI is still a project, not a reality.
- Reflect calmly on what a very intelligent machine would mean.
- Distinguish between movie fears and the serious questions worth asking.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Artificial general intelligence (AGI) | A machine capable of reasoning and learning like a person in any task. |
| Current AI | A machine very good at specific tasks, but which does not reason like a person. |
| Reasoning | The ability to think, relate ideas and solve new problems. |
| Self-awareness | Knowing that one exists and being aware of oneself. |
| Milestone | An important moment on the path, a great step. |
| Projection | The estimate of what the future will be like according to what we know today. |

## 📚 Main lesson
We are at the top of the tree, at the fruit. Fruits are what the tree gives to the community, and they also contain the seeds of the future. Talking about the future of humanity and artificial intelligence is talking about that fruit: where we are going and what we leave behind. Let us start with one of the most talked-about words: artificial general intelligence, or AGI.

The AI we use today is very different from the one imagined in films. Today's is a specialist: a machine that writes texts, another that recognises photos, another that plays chess. Each one is an expert in its task, like a craftsman very good at their trade. But none of them reasons like a person. None understands what it does: it only repeats learned patterns, even if it does so with astonishing skill.

AGI would be another thing: a machine capable of reasoning and learning like a person in any task. It would be like a universal apprentice, able to understand a conversation, fix a tap, write a letter and learn to play the guitar, all with the same judgement as a person. To this day, that machine does not exist. It is a project, an aspiration, a topic of debate. We must not confuse what we have with what is imagined.

How is a current AI distinguished from a future AGI? By reasoning. Current AI can write a perfect letter, but it does not understand what it means to be tired, or what raising a child costs, or why a joke is cruel. It can imitate reasoning, but not truly reason. It is like a parrot that imitates our words: it may seem to converse, but it does not know what it says. AGI, in theory, would know.

Another important word is self-awareness: knowing that one exists and being aware of oneself. Some people think AGI could come to have something similar. But there are also those who believe machines will never be conscious, because consciousness is not something that can be manufactured with data and calculations. It is an open, deep question with no definitive answer. And it is good that we do not have one: it is the big questions that make us reflect.

What would having an AGI mean, in practice? Nobody knows for sure. Some experts believe it is far away; others, that it could be near. The honest thing is to say it is uncertain. When we talk about the future of AI, we must distinguish between what already exists (today's AI, which we have already studied), what is likely (machines increasingly better at specific tasks) and what is speculation (a machine that thinks like a person). Each thing in its drawer.

An important reflection: we do not need to wait for AGI to ask ourselves serious questions. Today's AI already makes us wonder about ethics, data, work, trust. Everything we have learned in this module is worth something today, with current AI. AGI, if it arrives, will pose new questions, but we have already built the foundations of ethics.

We must be careful with movie fears. Stories of machines rebelling against humanity are fascinating, but they must not cover up reality. The real dangers of today's AI are others and we have already seen them: biases, discrimination, fake news, scams, job loss. They are concrete and human dangers, not robots with swords. Focusing on distant fears can distract us from caring for the nearby dangers.

What role do older people have in this conversation? A very valuable one. Those who have lived for decades know that the future always arrives different from what was imagined. We have seen television, the mobile phone and the internet arrive. We know that every new technology brings wonders and problems, and that we people adapt. That wisdom of experience is exactly what is needed to talk about the future with our feet on the ground.

In this level we have opened the door to the future. We know what AGI is (an aspiration, not a reality), what differentiates it from current AI (reasoning and understanding), and what big questions it raises. We also know not to be carried away by movie fears: the dangers that matter are already here and they are human. The future is prepared calmly, with knowledge and with care.

## 💡 Practical examples

2. **The drawer of futures.** Antonio uses his kitchen drawer to organise: what already exists, what is likely and what is speculation. AGI goes in the third drawer, labelled uncertainty.
3. **The family conversation.** Carmen talks with her grandchildren about the machines of the future. She does not let herself be frightened by the films: she reminds them that the dangers of today's AI are the ones to take care of.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take your notebook and write: "The future of AI".
Step 2: Draw three drawers on a sheet and label them: "What already exists", "What is likely", "What is speculation".
Step 3: Write in the first drawer two examples of current AI (chat, recommendations, assistants).
Step 4: Write in the second drawer two likely advances (machines better at tasks, more integrated into life).
Step 5: Write in the third drawer AGI and robots that think like people.
Step 6: Ask a free AI chat: "What is artificial general intelligence and when could it exist?". Read the answer and note whether it seems near or far to you.
Step 7: Reflect aloud: which real danger of today's AI worries you most?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is AGI? a) A machine capable of reasoning and learning like a person in any task. b) A type of phone. c) A computer virus.
2. Does AGI exist today? a) Yes, for years. b) No, it is a project or aspiration. c) Only in some countries.
3. Does current AI reason like a person? a) Yes, completely. b) No, it imitates patterns but does not understand what it does. c) Only the very big ones.
4. What is self-awareness? a) Knowing that one exists and being aware of oneself. b) An examination of conscience. c) A type of memory.
5. Why must we be careful with movie fears? a) Because they are frightening. b) Because the real dangers of today's AI are concrete and human. c) Because there are no dangers.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-b, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Distinguishing the real from the speculative is an ethical duty: let us not alarm with invented futures.
- The real dangers of today's AI are human: biases, fake news, scams, unemployment.
- The big questions (like AGI) deserve calm reflection, not fear.
- Life experience is valuable to talk about the future with feet on the ground.
- Preparing the future begins with taking good care of the present.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Current AI is a specialist: it imitates tasks without understanding them. AGI, a machine that reasons like a person, is still an uncertain project. We must separate the real, the likely and the speculative, and not be carried away by movie fears. The dangers that matter are already here and they are human.
