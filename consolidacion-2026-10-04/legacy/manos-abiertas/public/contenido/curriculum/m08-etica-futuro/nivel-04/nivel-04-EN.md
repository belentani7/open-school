# Module 8: Ethics, Future and Governance — Level 04
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 1 hour

## 🎯 Level objective
- Review everything learned in the Seed band: ethics, privacy, passwords and AI chats.
- Create a small personal protection plan, step by step.
- Learn to review our permissions and passwords from time to time.
- Understand that digital security is a habit, like watering plants.
- Feel proud of the first steps taken.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Habit | An action we repeat until it becomes a custom. |
| Protection plan | Our personal list of things to take care of in the digital world. |
| Review | The act of looking from time to time at what permissions and accounts we have. |
| Digital hygiene | The daily care of our accounts, passwords and data. |
| Alert | The warning we receive when something goes wrong (a message, a security alert). |
| Confidence | The calm security that comes from knowing we did the right thing. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the Seed band we have planted many seeds. Now it is time to water them and let them take root. As in the garden, seeds are planted in one day, but plants are cared for every day. Digital protection is the same: it is not a one-day task, it is a habit that is nurtured with consistency.

Let us review the path travelled. In level 00 we learned that ethics is deciding between right and wrong, and that machines have no conscience: responsibility is human. In level 01 we discovered that our data is valuable and that we all leave a digital footprint. In level 02 we learned to protect our accounts with strong passwords and two-step verification. In level 03 we understood what AI shares when we talk to it. Now it is time to join all those pieces.

Let us think about the house. A well-protected house is not secured in one day and that is it: you check that the locks work, that the windows close, that there are no careless entry points. Digital hygiene is exactly the same. From time to time, it is worth looking at our accounts, reviewing permissions and changing whatever is needed. You do not have to live watching over everything, but you do have to take care.

A good habit is to do a small review once a month, like the weekly watering of the pots. In that review we can do three things. First: check if any app asks for permissions it does not need. Second: verify that our important passwords are still strong. Third: check the email in case a security alert from some account arrives.

The personal protection plan is our own list. Each person has a different life and different accounts. That is why the plan is not the same for everyone. It is a paper or a notebook where we write down: what accounts we have, which are the most important, what new password we use and what date we set for the next review.

It is normal for everything to seem like a lot at first. There is no problem with moving slowly. A single changed password is already a step. A two-step verification activated is already a victory. Protection is built little by little, like a crochet blanket: one stitch, then another, and in the end we have a whole blanket.

It is also worth remembering that we are not alone. We can ask a trusted relative, a friend or the person teaching the course for help. There is no shame in asking: technology changes and we all learn. The important thing is not to keep our doubts bottled up.

There is a feeling we want to cultivate: calm confidence. It is what we feel when we know our doors are closed, our passwords are strong and we have not handed our life over to any machine. That confidence allows us to use technology with joy, without fear.

To finish the Seed band, we will create our personal plan. It will be our starting document. We will keep it safe and review it in later levels. When we reach the end of the module, we will be able to look back and see how much we have grown.

One last idea: taking care of our digital security is not just taking care of ourselves. It also protects our family. If our email account is opened, a hacker could write to our children or ask for money in our name. Taking care of our accounts is taking care of those we love. It is a gesture of love in the form of prudence.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Marta's list.** Marta writes in her notebook the four accounts she uses most and the date of the next review: the first Monday of every month.
2. **The security alert.** Antonio receives an email from the bank warning about a strange access. He does not panic: he changes the password and calls the bank. The alert reminded him that the monthly review was due.
3. **The crochet blanket.** Carmen compares her security to her crochet blanket: one stitch at a time, and she already has three accounts protected with two-step verification.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Get the paper you wrote in level 00 with your questions about machines and people. Read it.
Step 2: Get a new notebook (or a sheet) and write at the top: "My protection plan".
Step 3: Write the list of your accounts: email, bank, WhatsApp, social networks, others.
Step 4: Mark with a star the two most important accounts (probably the email and the bank).
Step 5: Write next to each account what protection you have: strong password, two-step verification, none yet.
Step 6: Write a review date, for example "first Monday of every month", and the first task of the next review.
Step 7: Keep the plan in a safe place. Congratulate yourself: you have just finished the Seed band of the module.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Protecting our accounts is also protecting the people around us.
- There is no shame in asking for help: we all learn, at any age.
- The monthly review is an act of responsibility towards ourselves.
- If we receive a security alert, acting calmly and quickly is the right thing to do.
- Taking care of our digital life is a form of dignity and respect for others.
