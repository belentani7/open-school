# Module 8: Ethics, Future and Governance — Level 15
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand how AI changes the world of work, without doom-mongering or false promises.
- Learn which tasks AI can take on and which remain human.
- Discover that AI also creates jobs and new trades.
- Know how to prepare: learn what machines do not know.
- Value the experience and wisdom of older people at work.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Automation | That a machine does tasks that people used to do. |
| Repetitive task | A monotonous job, always the same, easy to automate. |
| Human task | A job that needs empathy, judgement or creativity. |
| Professional retraining | Learning new skills to keep working. |
| Lifelong learning | Keeping learning all your life, at any age. |
| Complement | Something that adds to what already exists, instead of replacing it. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Every great invention has changed work. When washing machines arrived, the laundress trades disappeared; when cash machines arrived, many counter jobs were transformed. But new trades were also created. Artificial intelligence is a similar revolution: it will change jobs, remove some and create others. The important thing is to understand it without fear and without naivety.

What does AI do at work? Above all, repetitive tasks: filling in papers, sorting data, transcribing documents, answering simple questions, reviewing forms. These are tasks that always follow the same pattern, like sorting thousands of chickpeas to remove the stones: a machine does them fast and without getting tired. That frees up people's time for more valuable things.

And which tasks remain human? Those that need empathy, judgement, creativity and experience. Comforting a patient, negotiating with an angry customer, deciding a difficult case with good judgement, teaching with affection, innovating. The machine can organise a patient's data, but it cannot hold their hand. It can draft a text, but it does not know our life. Those tasks are the heart of human work.

A good way to see it: AI is like the washing machine in the laundry. The washing machine did not remove the job of washing clothes: it changed it. Now people devote themselves to other tasks, and clothes are washed better and faster. Technology rarely eliminates an entire trade; it almost always transforms it. Whoever adapts, survives; whoever understands the tool, thrives.

It is true that some jobs will disappear. We are not going to hide it. But it is also true that AI creates new jobs: there are people who teach the machines, who check they do not make mistakes, who correct their errors, who explain them to people. These are trades that did not exist before, just as there was no computer repairer before. History says that every technological wave brings more jobs than it takes, even though it hurts during the transition.

And people aged 40, 50 or 60? They have an enormous advantage that is sometimes forgotten: experience. The machine knows data; the person knows trades. Knowing how things were done before, knowing the customers, having judgement formed over years of work: no machine replaces that. Work wisdom is a treasure that AI cannot copy from a file.

Another advantage: older people know how to deal with others. Empathy, patience, knowing how to listen and how to behave are valued more than ever in a world full of machines. The trades of attention, care, teaching and companionship will gain importance. The machine can answer, but it cannot accompany. Whoever knows how to accompany will always have work.

How to prepare? With lifelong learning. Learn what AI does not know: use the new tools, understand them, direct them. Every course, every workshop, every conversation that explains something new to us is a step of preparation. You do not need to become a programmer: understanding enough not to fall behind is enough. It is like learning to use the cash machine: whoever learns it does not queue.

It is also important to know how to ask for training at work. If the company introduces AI, workers have the right to learn to use it. Good companies train their people instead of replacing them. Training is not a gift: it is an investment that benefits everyone. Whoever trains adapts; whoever adapts, stays.

One last idea: work is not only earning a living, it is also dignity, relationship and meaning. AI can help us work better, but it cannot replace the value of a job well done or the satisfaction of helping. That is why the future of work is not "machines or people", but "machines and people": each in their own, complementing each other.

In this level we have looked at the labour future with our feet on the ground. Without promising everything will be easy, but also without painting a world of fear. AI changes trades, and whoever understands the change can navigate it. Experience, empathy and the desire to learn are our best boats.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The bank's washing machine.** Rosa works in an office where AI transcribes documents. Instead of typing, Rosa now reviews and corrects, and teaches the new employees the tricks of the trade.
2. **Antonio's new trade.** Antonio, 55, trains as an "AI answer reviewer": he checks that the system's answers do not make things up. A trade that did not exist before.
3. **The value of dealing with people.** Carmen works in customer service. The company put in a chatbot, but customers ask to speak to Carmen for the difficult problems. The machine attends; Carmen resolves.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take your notebook and write at the top: "My work and AI".
Step 2: Write your trade or main activity. Below, make two columns: "repetitive tasks" and "human tasks".
Step 3: In the repetitive column, write two or three of your tasks a machine could do.
Step 4: In the human column, write two or three tasks that need your experience, judgement or personal touch.
Step 5: Ask a free AI chat: "Which tasks of my profession could AI automate and which not?" (describe your trade). Read the answer and compare it with your columns.
Step 6: Write one skill you would like to learn in the coming year to keep up to date.
Step 7: Reflect aloud: what of what you do would a machine not do well?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. Which tasks does AI take on at work? a) Those that need empathy. b) The repetitive ones, which follow a pattern. c) All, without exception.
2. What advantage do older people have over AI? a) None. b) Experience, judgement and dealing with people. c) Only retirement.
3. Does AI only take away jobs? a) Yes, always. b) No, it also creates new trades. c) I do not know.
4. What is lifelong learning? a) Learning throughout life, at any age. b) Training the machines. c) A type of contract.
5. Is the future of work "machines or people"? a) Yes, one replaces the other. b) No, it is "machines and people" that complement each other. c) Only machines.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- AI must serve workers, not simply dismiss them: companies have responsibility in the transition.
- Training employees is an ethical duty of companies that introduce AI.
- Experience and human dealing are valuable: nobody should belittle them.
- The decision to automate must take people into account, not only savings.
- Learning at any age is a right and a defence against fear.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
AI changes work: it takes on repetitive tasks and leaves the human ones to people. It creates new trades and transforms existing ones, like the washing machine did with the laundry. Experience, empathy and lifelong learning are our best tools. In the next level we will talk about our rights in the digital world, the digital citizenship bill of rights.
