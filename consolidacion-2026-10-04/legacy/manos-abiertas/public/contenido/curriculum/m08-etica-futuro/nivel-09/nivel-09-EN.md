## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root

## 📚 Main lesson
We finish the Root band. As in the garden, roots hold up everything that will grow later: a tree with weak roots falls at the first wind. In this band we have put down roots of knowledge and protection. Now it is time to review, join the pieces and plant the defence plan that will accompany us for the rest of the module.

Let us review the path. In level 05 we learned what a bias is: an inclination of thought that comes from data. In level 06 we saw that, when that bias harms real people, we talk about algorithmic discrimination, which is invisible and affects loans, insurance and housing. In level 07 we learned to distrust hoaxes and deepfakes, and to check before believing. In level 08 we learned to protect ourselves in daily life: phishing, impersonation, updates and backups.

If we put it all together, a clear message comes out: in the digital world, as in life, you have to look before you trust. Critical thinking is our best tool. It is not about distrusting everything, but about asking: is this true? Who says it? Why does it reach me? Those three questions have saved us many times in life, and they also save us on the internet.

The warning signs can be summarised in a few words: hurry, fear, prize, data. If a message rushes us, if it frightens us, if it promises us a prize, or if it asks us for personal data, it is better to stop. It is like the street saying: "gifts in a hurry, devil's traps". Our experience tells us that what urges too much usually hides something.

Now let us think about the defence plan. A good plan is simple and fits in one hand. First: strong and different passwords for each account, with two-step verification on the important ones. Second: do not open suspicious emails or messages, and do not click strange links. Third: check the news before believing or forwarding it. Fourth: keep the phone and computer updated. Fifth: keep backups of what we love most.

Digital self-care also has an emotional part. No one should feel ashamed for having fallen for a scam: scammers are experts and attack everyone. What makes us strong is talking about it, telling a relative, learning and moving on. Shame is the scammers' best ally, because it silences us.

Community is another pillar. When a neighbour warns us of a scam going around the neighbourhood, they protect us. In the digital world it is the same: warning the family group about a hoax or a false message is an act of care. We are stronger when we protect ourselves together, like in the village when there was a danger and everyone passed the word.

We should also remember that we are not at the end of the road: the Stem band awaits us with the laws. So far we have talked about personal habits. In the coming levels we will see that there are also written rules, created to protect us: the right to know what they do with our data, the right to delete it, the European law on artificial intelligence. Our roots prepare us to understand those laws.

An idea to close the band: safety is not a destination, it is a path. You never reach being "completely safe": there will always be new threats, because technology changes. But whoever has roots, habits and community walks calmly. It is like driving: you do not drive safely because you have a perfect car, but because you have good driving habits.

When you finish this level, you will have a written, simple and realistic defence plan. We will keep it and review it. When we reach the end of the module, we will be able to look back and see how much we have grown since the first seed.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Rosa's saying.** Rosa sums up her defence in four words: "hurry, fear, prize, data". If a message brings two of those, she does not open it.
2. **The group warning.** Antonio receives a "courier" message asking him to pay a fee. He recognises it as a scam and warns the family group so nobody falls for it.
3. **Carmen's review.** Carmen does her monthly review: she checks her passwords, looks at the phone's permissions and checks whether her photos are saved in the cloud. All in order.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take your protection plan (you wrote it in level 04) and read it.
Step 2: Add below a new section: "Defence plan (Root band)".
Step 3: Write the five rules of the plan: strong passwords, do not open suspicious messages, check news, update the phone, make backups.
Step 4: Next to each rule, write a concrete action for this week (for example: "change the bank password" or "activate the photo backup").
Step 5: Write the four warning signs: hurry, fear, prize, data.
Step 6: Write who you would call first if you had a security doubt (a relative, a friend, the consumer association).
Step 7: Save the plan and mark the next monthly review in the calendar.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

3. What do we do if we have fallen for a scam? a) Hide it. b) Tell a relative, warn our contacts and learn. c) Carry on as if nothing happened.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Warning the community about scams is an act of care, not of alarmism.
- Shame is the ally of scammers: talking makes us strong.
- Critical thinking protects our minds and also those of the people around us.
- Our defence plan is not for living in fear, it is for living with confidence.
- Protecting ourselves together is easier than protecting ourselves alone.
