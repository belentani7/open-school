# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 06
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Write letters with AI while respecting their rules of courtesy.
- Tell formal letters (bank, administration) apart from personal letters (family, friends).
- Use the greeting, body and closing appropriate to each case.
- Personalise a generated letter by adding your details and your tone.
- Know when a letter is better in writing than on the phone.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Formal letter | A serious and respectful letter for banks, administrations or companies. |
| Personal letter | A warm letter for family and friends. |
| Greeting | The first sentence: "Dear Mr Lopez" or "Dear Carmen". |
| Letter body | The main content, where you explain what you want to say. |
| Closing | The last sentence: "Yours faithfully", "With love". |
| Register | The level of formality: very formal, formal, informal. Like clothes depending on the occasion. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Writing a letter is like visiting a house: you ring the bell before entering, speak with respect and say goodbye politely. In letters, that protocol is called greeting, body and closing. AI can help us, but it must know what kind of letter it is and who it is addressed to.

The first step is to decide whether the letter is formal or personal. A letter to the bank or the town hall is formal: you use "Dear Sir", you explain the reason clearly and you close with "Yours faithfully". A letter to a sister or a friend is personal: you use "Dear", you write with your heart and you close with "With love".

Register is like clothes: you do not go to a bank meeting in pyjamas, nor to a family lunch in a suit. AI needs to know the register to choose the words. If we do not tell it, it chooses by default, and it may not be the right one.

A good letter prompt includes: the type (formal or personal), the recipient (who they are), the reason (what we want to say) and the tone (firm, kind, understanding). For example: "Write me a formal letter to the bank explaining that I have received a charge I do not recognise and asking them to review it, with a respectful but firm tone".

The formal letter has an almost fixed structure: greeting, introduction, reason, specific request and closing. AI knows this structure. We only have to give it the content. It is like giving the dress pattern and the threads; it sews and we choose the fabric.

The personal letter is freer, but it also has rules. If we write a condolence letter, we want it to be warm and sincere, without clichés. We can ask the AI: "Write me a condolence letter to my neighbour for her mother, with warmth and without being too long". AI suggests and we adjust with our own words.

A useful trick: tell the AI the exact relationship with the recipient. "My 80-year-old aunt", "the headteacher of my grandson's school", "my former work colleague". This way the AI chooses the right degree of trust and closeness.

After generating, you must personalise. Add your details, the account number if needed, the exact dates, the specific reason. The generated letter is the skeleton; we add the flesh of the true details.

You should also review the greeting and closing. If the AI writes "Dear Sir" and the bank is "Dear Sirs", you correct it. If it is personal and you prefer "Dear", you change it. The protocol is decided by the author.

A common mistake: using a formal letter for something personal, or the other way round. A complaint ending with "With love" is not taken seriously; a congratulations with "Yours faithfully" sounds cold. AI can mix them if we are not clear. The solution is to always indicate the type of letter.

When is it better to write than to call? When we want a record (a complaint), when the conversation is difficult (condolences, a complaint) or when the procedure matters. What is written stays; what is spoken is carried away by the wind.

By the end of this level, we will know how to ask for all kinds of letters and, above all, how to make them ours: with our style, our details and our respect for whoever will receive it.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The bill complaint.** Rosa receives an electricity bill she does not recognise. She asks for a formal letter to the bank, adds the bill number and sends it. She gets a reply within two weeks.
2. **The greeting to her granddaughter.** Pedro asks for a birthday letter for his 10-year-old granddaughter, personal and with a touch of humour. He personalises it with a family anecdote.
3. **The condolence.** Carmen asks the AI for a condolence letter to her friend from the neighbourhood. She reviews the text, makes it shorter and adds a personal memory.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open ChatGPT or Gemini.
Step 2: Write: "Write me a formal letter to the town council asking them to fix the street lighting on my road, with a respectful tone".
Step 3: Press enter and wait. Notice the structure: greeting, reason, request and closing.
Step 4: Write below: "Add that we have been without light at the corner for three months and that there are elderly neighbours".
Step 5: Now write: "Write me a personal letter to my cousin thanking her for the weekend visit, with warmth".
Step 6: Compare the two letters: how do the greeting, vocabulary and closing differ?
Step 7: Copy the council letter, add your address and today's date, and save it in Google Docs.
Step 8: If you want, copy the letter to your cousin and send it by WhatsApp or email.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What distinguishes a formal letter from a personal one? a) The colour of the paper. b) The register: seriousness and respect versus closeness. c) Nothing.
2. What should you always indicate in a letter prompt? a) The type of letter and who it is addressed to. b) Your phone number. c) Nothing, AI knows.
3. What is the greeting? a) The first sentence of the letter. b) The signature. c) The envelope.
4. Why is a letter better than a call for a complaint? a) Because it is longer. b) Because it leaves a written record. c) It is never better.
5. Should you personalise the generated letter? a) No, just as it is. b) Yes, with your details and your tone. c) Only if the AI asks.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Do not use generated letters to pretend they are from someone else or to impersonate identities.
- In letters to institutions, always tell the truth: do not ask for things you are not entitled to.
- Respect the recipient's privacy: do not include third parties' data without their consent.
- An apology or condolence letter must carry sincerity. AI suggests, but the feeling is yours.
- Check that the AI does not add false data: addresses, numbers or dates that do not exist.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Letters have rules of courtesy: greeting, body and closing, with a formal or personal register depending on the recipient. With AI we write all kinds of letters and then personalise them with our details and our truth. In the next level we will use AI to summarise long texts: another very useful task.
