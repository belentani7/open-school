# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 23
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Fruit
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what makes content "professional".
- Apply everything learned in the module in a complete project.
- Create a final piece with presentation quality.
- Present and defend your own project.
- Value your own work and your classmates' work with respect.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |

| Final project | The complete work that shows everything learned in the module. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Throughout the module we have learned to create text, images, voice, video and audio, and to do it with ethics and organisation. Now comes the most beautiful and demanding part: bringing it all together in a professional production. Professional content is not the most expensive or the most complicated: it is content that is cared for in every detail. Like party food: you do not need to spend a lot, you need care.

What makes content professional? First, the idea is clear: what I want to say and to whom. Second, the materials are well made: clear text, nice images, a voice that is understood, a well-edited video. Third, it has identity: its own colours, fonts and style (level 16). Fourth, it is well reviewed (level 22). And fifth, it is honest and respectful.

The final project is the chance to demonstrate everything: we choose a topic that matters to us and create a complete piece. It can be a video with voice and subtitles about the vegetable garden, a podcast about neighbourhood stories, a poster with the club's history or an illustrated story with voice. The important thing is to put into practice what we learned and that the topic is ours.

Planning is the first step of the project. Before creating, we answer: what do I want to make? For whom? What steps will I follow? What tools will I use? How much time do I have? With these answers, the project has a map. The pipeline from level 20 helps us not to lose our way.

During creation, we apply everything we learned. We use the chat to improve the script, Piper for the voice, Whisper for the subtitles, the editors for the edit and the templates for the identity. We do not need to use every tool in every project: we choose the ones our piece needs. The tool is ours, not the other way round.

Finishing is the detail that makes the difference. We review the text, listen to the voice, watch the whole video, correct what does not fit. Good finishing shows: everything sounds and looks cared for. It is like the hem of a dress: if it is well made, you do not see it; if it is badly made, you see it from miles away.

Presentation is part of professional production. Learning to show the work and explain it is a valuable skill: "I made this, I made it this way, and I made it for this reason". Presenting is not showing off: it is sharing and accounting for our work. Classmates will give us feedback, and we will give it to them, with respect.

The portfolio is the collection of our best works. We keep the pieces we are most proud of in a folder or a document: it will be our calling card. Over time, the portfolio shows the path travelled: from the first drafts to the polished pieces.

Valuing your own work is part of maturing. It is not about being hard on ourselves or showing off: it is about looking honestly at what went well and what we can improve for the next time. Each project makes us better. Mistakes are part of learning, not failure.

By the end of this level, we will have created a complete, professional production that brings together the whole module: idea, script, materials, editing, review and presentation. The fruit of the tree we planted in level 0 is now ripe.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The vegetable garden video.** Pedro creates a complete two-minute video about the club's garden, with voice, subtitles, visual identity and final review.
2. **The neighbourhood podcast.** Rosa records an episode about the history of the street, edits it with free music and presents it to her neighbours.
3. **The illustrated story.** Carmen writes a story, illustrates it with generated images, gives it voice with Piper and gives it to her grandchildren.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose the topic of your final project (something that matters to you: family, garden, recipe, neighbourhood).
Step 2: Answer the plan on paper: what I make, for whom, what steps I follow, what tools I use.
Step 3: Write the script with the chat's help and review it with AI (level 22).
Step 4: Create the materials: voice with Piper, images, photos or clips.
Step 5: Edit the piece (video, audio or poster) with your visual identity.
Step 6: Add the subtitles if it is a video and review them.
Step 7: Do the complete final review: text, sound, image, ethics.
Step 8: Present your project to the class or your family: what you did, how and why.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What makes content professional? a) That it is cared for in every detail. b) That it is expensive. c) That it uses many tools.
2. What is the first step of the final project? a) Planning. b) The edit. c) The presentation.
3. What is finishing? a) The final polish: reviewing and retouching until it is right. b) The first draft. c) The title.
4. What is the portfolio? a) The collection of our best works. b) A type of camera. c) A program.
5. When a project comes out with mistakes, what do we do? a) We get angry and give up. b) We learn and improve for the next one. c) We hide it.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Give credit to the tools, materials and ideas you used in your project.
- Respect the image and voice of the people who appear in your work.
- Present your work honestly: do not sell it as something it is not.
- Feedback to classmates is given with respect and received with humility.
- Share what you learned: professional production is also taught.
