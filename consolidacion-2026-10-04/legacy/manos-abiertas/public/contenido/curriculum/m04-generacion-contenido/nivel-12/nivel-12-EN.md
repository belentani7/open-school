# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 12
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a video is and how it is edited: joining images, sound and text.
- Get to know free video editing tools with AI help.
- Create a simple video by joining clips, photos and audio.
- Add transitions and basic text.
- Publish or share the finished video.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Video | A sequence of moving images with sound. |
| Editing | Joining and ordering the pieces of video to tell a story. |
| Clip | A piece of video, like a puzzle piece. |
| Transition | The visual effect between two clips (for example, a fade). |
| Timeline | The band where clips are placed in order to edit them. |
| Export | Saving the finished video as a final file. |

## 📚 Main lesson
We have all made videos: a birthday, a trip, a recipe. But a raw video, without editing, can be long and boring. Editing is the art of keeping the best parts and ordering them to tell a story. With AI help, editing has become much easier.

Editing is done in a program called a "video editor". There are free editors with AI features, such as CapCut, or the Canva editors. They work like a workbench: a horizontal band called a "timeline" where we place the clips in order, like puzzle pieces.

The first step of editing is selecting the material. From a half-hour video, we keep the three minutes that are worth it: the best part of the toast, the song at the end, the jump into the pool. Cutting is the editor's basic craft: less is more.

AI helps with editing in several ways. Some editors analyse the video and propose automatic cuts, removing silences and dead moments. Others let you create the video from a text or from an idea, generating the scenes. AI speeds up the mechanical part; the creative decision is still ours.

Transitions connect the clips: a fade to black, a sweep, a dissolve. They are like commas in a text: they help us breathe. But be careful: too many transitions make people dizzy. One or two per video is enough.

Sound is half of the video. We can add background music (from free libraries, respecting the licences) or the narrated voice we learned to generate with Piper. Balance is key: the music must not cover the voice.

Text also enters the edit: a title at the beginning, people's names, a date. Modern editors make adding text easy, and some generate automatic subtitles (we will see that in the next level).

Once edited, comes the export: saving the video as a final file. We normally choose the quality (for example, 1080p) and the format (mp4). The result is a file we can send by WhatsApp, upload to a social network or save in the cloud.

There is no rush. The first edit is always slower, but with practice it becomes natural. Like learning to cook: the first time is hard, the tenth comes naturally.

Before exporting, you must review the whole video from start to finish. We look for mistakes: a clip that is left over, music that does not fit, badly written text. The final review is what separates a good edit from a mediocre one.

Copyright also matters here: if we use other people's music, it should be from free libraries; if we use other people's photos, let us remember to ask for permission. Everything learned in the previous levels applies to video.

By the end of this level, we will know how to edit a simple video: cut clips, order them, add music, text and transitions, and export it. A first step towards turning our recordings into little stories.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The birthday video.** Marta edits the best moments of her husband's 70th birthday: she cuts clips, adds soft music and a title with the date. She sends it to the whole family.
2. **The trip video.** Pedro joins the clips of the hiking club outing, puts a fade between scenes and adds the name of the mountain. He shares it in the group.
3. **The recipe video.** Ana films the steps of her pies, cuts each step and adds text with the ingredients. That way her family can follow her at home.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open the free video editor you use in class (for example, CapCut or the Canva editor) on your phone or computer.
Step 2: Press "New project" and give your video a name.
Step 3: Import 3 or 4 clips from your holidays or from a special day (import or add button).
Step 4: Place the clips on the timeline, in the order you want them to be seen (drag each clip).
Step 5: Cut each clip: select the clip and use the cutting tool to keep only the good part.
Step 6: Add a transition between two clips (transitions button and choose a fade).
Step 7: Add a title at the beginning with the text "My holidays" and choose a legible size.
Step 8: Export the video in medium quality (for example, 720p), save it and send it by WhatsApp to a relative.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the timeline in a video editor? a) The band where clips are ordered. b) The camera. c) The speaker.
2. What is a transition? a) A visual effect between two clips. b) A type of font. c) A song.
3. What is cutting a clip for? a) To keep the good part. b) To make the video more expensive. c) It is useless.
4. Where is the finished video saved? a) It is only watched, not saved. b) It is exported as a final file. c) It is printed.
5. Can I use any music in my video? a) Yes, always. b) No, it is better to use free music or ask permission. c) Only radio music.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Do not upload videos with other people's image to the internet without their permission.
- Use free music and photos, or ask for permission. Remember copyright.
- Do not edit videos to deceive or manipulate anyone: an edit can change the meaning of what happened.
- If the video is from a public event, respect the privacy of the people who appear in it.
- Review the video before publishing it: what you upload stays on the internet.
