# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 22
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Canopy
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what an editor does: reviewing and improving the work of others.
- Use AI to review texts, correct them and improve them.
- Learn to give good review instructions to AI.
- Review your own work with an editor's eyes.
- Create quality content following a review guide.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Editor | Someone who reviews and improves content before publishing. |
| Review | Reading a text looking for mistakes and improvements. |
| Correction | Fixing errors, odd sentences or confusing facts. |
| Feedback | The advice we give or receive to improve. |
| Style guide | The content rules: tone, length, format. |
| Draft | The first version, which is not yet finished. |

## 📚 Main lesson
All good content passes through an editor's hands. The editor is not the one who writes: it is the one who reviews, corrects and suggests improvements. In newspapers, nobody publishes without an editor reviewing. Now, AI can be our editor: reading our text, finding mistakes and proposing improvements. Learning to ask for that review is an art in itself.

The review has several levels. The first is correction: spelling mistakes, repeated words, sentences that are not understood. The second is improvement: is the idea clear? is the tone right? is something missing or too much? The third is judgement: is it correct, is it fair, is it honest? AI helps us with all three, but the final decision is always ours.

For AI to review well, we must give it good instructions. Saying "review this for me" is not enough. It is better to say: "Correct the mistakes in this text without changing the meaning" or "Make this paragraph clearer and simpler" or "Is there anything unnecessary in this letter?". The more specific the request, the better the answer. It is like asking for help in the kitchen: if you explain clearly what you need, the help works.

Feedback is the advice to improve. We can ask AI to tell us what fails and how to fix it. But be careful: not everything AI says must be accepted. Sometimes its suggestions do not fit our voice or our goal. We accept what improves and discard what does not. Editing is a dialogue, not an order.

Reviewing your own work is hard because we grow attached to what we write. An editor's trick: let some time pass and read it again, or read it aloud. Another help: ask AI to read our text as if it were a new reader. "What is not understood? What would make you stop reading?". A fresh eye finds what we do not see.

The style guide also serves for editing. If the club has rules (friendly tone, short sentences, simple words), AI can review following that guide: "Review this text with a friendly tone and simple words for older people". The guide makes the review independent of the mood of the day: it is a fixed rule.

Editing also applies to audio and video. With Whisper's transcription we can read what we said and correct it before publishing. With Piper's voice we can regenerate the sentences we do not like. Editing is not only correcting text: it is polishing the whole piece.

Ethical review is part of editing. Before publishing, we ask: does this respect people? Is it true? Does it harm anyone? Is it well labelled? A good editor does not only fix the style: they also make sure the content is honest. Ethics is the last filter.

The editor's craft is learned with practice. We will start by reviewing short texts: a note, a letter, a script. Each review makes us better creators, because by editing we also learn to write better. Whoever reviews well, writes better.

By the end of this level, we will know how to use AI as an editor: ask for specific reviews, correct mistakes, improve clarity and apply a style guide. Our content will come out more polished, and we, with an editor's eyes.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Write a short note of 5 or 6 sentences (for example, the notice of the club meeting).
Step 2: Open the AI chat and write: "Correct the mistakes in this text without changing the meaning: [your text]".
Step 3: Read the answer and compare: what has it corrected? Do you agree with everything?
Step 4: Ask for an improvement: "Make this text clearer and friendlier, with short sentences".
Step 5: Ask for a review with judgement: "Is anything important missing? What would you remove?"
Step 6: Choose the best of each version and write your final version.
Step 7: Apply your mental style guide: is it respectful, clear, honest and simple?
Step 8: Publish or send the corrected note and save the final version with a clear name.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

2. How do you ask AI for a good review? a) With specific, clear instructions. b) Saying "review this for me". c) You cannot ask.
3. Do you have to accept everything AI suggests? a) Yes, always. b) No, we accept what improves and discard what does not. c) Never.
4. What is the style guide? a) The content rules: tone, length and format. b) A type of font. c) A program.
5. Which filter is the last in editing? a) The ethical one: that the content is honest and respects people. b) The price one. c) None.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-b, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Editing must not change the meaning or truth of what is written: correct, do not manipulate.
- Do not edit other people's content without permission.
- Use the ethical review as the final filter: respect, truth and transparency.
- Give credit when you use other people's or AI's ideas in your content.
- Help others improve their content with respect, as you would like them to do with you.
