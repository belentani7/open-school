# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 11
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what transcription is: turning voice into text.
- Get to know Whisper, the free tool that does the transcription.
- Transcribe your own audio (voice note, interview, meeting).
- Correct and organise the transcribed text.
- Use the transcription to save and share information.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Transcription | Turning an audio into written text. Like dictating to a secretary. |
| Voice note | An audio message recorded with the phone. |
| Whisper | A free AI tool that transcribes voice into text. |
| Automatic transcription | The text generated by the machine without writing by hand. |
| Diarisation | The process of knowing who said each sentence when several people speak. |

## 📚 Main lesson
How many voice notes do we receive a day? Sometimes it would be more convenient to have them in writing: to look up an address, remember a list or keep an important message. Transcription turns voice into text. And with Whisper, a free tool, it does it automatically.

Whisper is an artificial intelligence program created by OpenAI (the same people as ChatGPT) and it is free. It works with many languages, including Spanish, Portuguese, Catalan and English. It listens to an audio and writes what is said, like a tireless stenographer.

The process is simple: we give Whisper an audio file (a voice note, a recording) and it gives us back a written text. Then we can copy it, correct it and use it wherever we want. It is like asking someone to take notes on a conversation.

To transcribe, we first need the audio on the computer. Phone voice notes can be shared and saved on the computer. The most common files are mp3, wav and m4a. Whisper accepts them all.

Automatic transcription is not perfect. It can confuse names, numbers or unusual words. That is why we must always review the result, as we would review a text dictated to a secretary. Correction is part of the job.

What is transcription useful for in daily life? To keep a voice note with the shopping list in writing, to transcribe an interview or a conversation with the doctor so you do not forget the instructions, to turn club classes into documents, or to search for information inside a long audio.

A very valuable use: meetings. Instead of taking notes by hand while people speak, we record the meeting with the phone and transcribe it afterwards. This way nothing escapes us and we can read the conversation calmly. It is like having an automatic minutes.

Whisper can also transcribe videos. If a YouTube video or a conference interests us, we can extract the audio, transcribe it and keep a text that we can read, summarise or translate. Written information is much easier to search and share.

An advanced option is "diarisation": Whisper can try to separate who says each sentence. This is useful in conversations between two or more people. It is not always perfect, but it helps a lot to understand who said what.

You do not need to be an expert to use Whisper. There are windowed versions (visual program) and online versions that work from the browser. At Manos Abiertas we teach the simplest version: upload the audio, wait a few seconds and copy the text.

Privacy is important. Before transcribing an audio, think about whether it contains other people's data. If the conversation is private or belongs to others, transcribe it only if necessary and do not share it without permission. Whisper can also work offline, on your own computer, which protects privacy.

Transcription and the synthetic voice (from the previous level) are two sides of the same coin: one turns text into voice and the other turns voice into text. Together, they let us move information from one format to the other effortlessly.

By the end of this level, we will know how to turn any audio into useful text, correct it and save it. The information trapped in a voice is freed as words we can read, search and share.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The doctor's note.** Rosa records the doctor's instructions with permission and transcribes them at home. Now she has the dose and the review dates in writing.
2. **The shopping list.** Carmen receives a voice note from her daughter with the shopping list. She transcribes it and turns it into a text list so nothing is forgotten.
3. **The club class.** Pedro transcribes the talk on urban gardens given by the technician. Now the members have the content in writing to consult.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Record a short voice note with your phone: for example, "tomorrow I have to buy bread, milk and tomatoes, and call the pharmacy".
Step 2: Send the note to your email or share it to save it on the computer.
Step 3: Open the transcription tool we use in class (Whisper in its simple version) from the browser or the installed program.
Step 4: Load the audio file by pressing the upload button or dragging it to the window.
Step 5: Wait for it to finish. Whisper will write the text in a few seconds.
Step 6: Read the transcribed text and correct it: check that the names and numbers are right.
Step 7: Copy the text and paste it into a note or Google Docs.
Step 8: Add a title ("Tuesday list") and save it. You have your first transcription.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Do not transcribe other people's conversations without permission. People's voices are part of their privacy.
- If the audio contains personal data, treat the transcribed text with the same confidentiality.
- Do not use transcription to monitor or spy on anyone.
- Correct the text before sharing it: a wrong transcription can change the meaning.
- If you share a meeting's transcription, notify the participants.
