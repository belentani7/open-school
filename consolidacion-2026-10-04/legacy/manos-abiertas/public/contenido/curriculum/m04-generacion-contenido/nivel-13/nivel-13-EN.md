## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Subtitle | The text on screen that says what is heard. |
| Automatic subtitling | Subtitles generated by the machine without writing them by hand. |
| Synchronisation | That the text appears exactly when it is said. |
| Legibility | That the text reads well and without effort. |
| SRT file | A text file that stores the subtitles and their timings. |
| Automatic translation | Converting the subtitles to another language with AI help. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Subtitles are the text that appears at the bottom of the screen while watching a video. They make content easier to understand: they help people with hearing difficulties, allow watching videos in noisy places and make learning another language easier. With AI, adding subtitles is almost automatic.

Subtitles are written in a special file called SRT: simple text where each sentence carries its start and end time. For example, "00:05 – 00:09: good morning". Video programs read that file and show the text at the right moment. It is like the score of a song: it indicates when each word comes in.

Generating subtitles by hand is very slow. That is why we use AI: the same transcription technology we saw in level 11 (Whisper) can listen to the video and write the subtitles automatically. The program analyses the audio, turns it into text and stores the timings of each sentence.

Once generated, the subtitles are not finished. They must be corrected: misspelled names, odd words, cut-off sentences. Correction is quick because AI does the heavy work and we only polish the result. It is like ironing: the washing machine already washed the clothes.

Synchronisation is key. A subtitle that arrives late or leaves early is confusing. Most tools adjust the timings automatically, but it is worth checking that the text appears exactly when someone speaks.

Subtitles are also for accessibility. It is not just a technical matter: it is an open door for deaf people or people with hearing loss. A video with subtitles can be watched and understood by many more people. Thinking about others makes our content better.

Another advantage is translation. With free tools we can translate the subtitles into another language and thus reach more people. For example, the gardening club video can have subtitles in English and Portuguese. Text is easier to translate than audio.

Video platforms like YouTube offer automatic subtitles, but their quality varies. For an important video, it is better to generate the subtitles with a dedicated tool and review them by hand. A badly written subtitle can change the meaning of what is said.

In this level we will also learn to embed the subtitles in the video or leave the SRT file separate. Each option has its use: the SRT allows changing or translating them without touching the video; embedded, they are always visible.

Ethics also matters: if the video is someone else's, do not remove or change their subtitles without permission. And if you translate a video, make sure the translation is faithful, not a summary that changes the message.

By the end of this level, we will know how to turn a video into a video with subtitles: generate the text automatically, correct it, synchronise it and save it. A small step that makes our content accessible to everyone.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The accessible video.** Luis records a Christmas message for the family and adds subtitles, so that his sister-in-law with hearing loss can also enjoy it.
2. **The cooking course.** Ana uploads the video of her recipe to the WhatsApp group and generates subtitles in English and Portuguese, for the family living abroad.
3. **The club video.** Pedro adds subtitles to the gardening talk video so that the members can read it in silence at the library.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Have the video from the previous level ready (your holiday edit or the one you created in class).
Step 2: Open the subtitling tool you use in class (for example, the Clipchamp subtitle editor or the Canva option).
Step 3: Load the video into the tool.
Step 4: Press the "Automatic subtitles" button and wait for the AI to write the text.
Step 5: Play the video and correct the mistakes: names, numbers and cut-off words.
Step 6: Check the synchronisation: that each sentence appears exactly when it is said.
Step 7: Choose the text style: size and colour that read well (white letters with shadow or dark background).
Step 8: Save the subtitles as an SRT file or export them embedded in the video.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Subtitles make content accessible: think about people with hearing loss.
- Do not modify or remove the subtitles of someone else's video without permission.
- If you translate, the translation must be faithful to the original message.
- Correct the subtitles before publishing: a mistake can change the meaning.
- Respect privacy: do not add subtitles that reveal personal data without consent.
