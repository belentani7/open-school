# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 00
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what digital content is using everyday examples: photos, messages, recipes and videos.
- Tell the difference between digital content, which lives on screens, and content on paper.
- Know where digital content is kept: on the phone, on the computer and on the internet.
- Recognise that artificial intelligence can also create content, such as text and images.
- Lose the fear of looking at, touching and exploring the content on our own devices.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Digital content | Everything you see or hear on a screen: photos, messages, videos, music and documents. |
| File | An "object" stored on the phone or computer, like a photo or a document. It has a name. |
| Photograph | An image captured with the camera of your phone or a photo camera. |
| Video | A series of moving images with sound, like a home movie. |
| Text | Written words: messages, notes, letters and recipes. |
| Internet | The worldwide "highway" that connects phones and computers to each other. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Let us imagine we have a photo album from our whole life. The old photos are printed on paper and kept in a cupboard. Today, new photos live inside the phone. That is digital content: content that does not take up a cupboard, but is seen on a screen.

Digital content is everything we create or receive with electronic devices. A photo of the grandchildren on the phone is digital content. A voice message in the family group chat is too. A lentil soup recipe saved on the phone, a birthday video, a song we like: all of that is digital content.

To understand it better, let us use the kitchen metaphor. In a kitchen we keep ingredients in cupboards and the fridge. In the digital world, the "ingredients" are files, and we keep them inside the phone or computer. They take up no physical space, but we need to know where they are.

Each file has a name and a type. A photo is usually called something like "IMG_2025.jpg". A text document ends in ".txt" or ".docx". Those endings are like the labels on the jars in the pantry: they tell us what is inside each jar.

You do not need to learn every type. The important thing is to know that, just as every object at home has its place, on the phone every type of content has its folder: the gallery for photos, messages for conversations, email for digital letters.

Digital content can be copied without wasting paper. If we send a recipe by WhatsApp, the person who receives it has it just as complete as we do. This is a great advantage, but it also means we must be careful about what we share, because once it is sent it is hard to get it back.

Another feature: digital content can be searched. How many times have we lost a paper recipe? With a digital document, we type the word and find it in seconds. It is like having a housekeeper who remembers where everything is.

It can also be edited. A digital photo can be cropped, brightened or turned without ruining the original. A text can be corrected again and again. On paper, a mistake forces you to start over; digitally, you delete and fix it in seconds.

Digital content travels over the internet. We can send a photo of the vegetable garden to a daughter living in another country and she receives it within seconds. Thirty years ago, this meant posting a letter and waiting for weeks.

Today artificial intelligence also creates content. When we talk to the phone and it answers in writing, it is a machine that has created a text. When an app puts a funny face on one of our photos, it is creating a new image. All of that is part of this module.

There is no reason to be afraid. Technology does not bite. Just as we learned to use the TV remote control, we can learn to use the phone and the computer. It is about taking small steps, calmly and without rushing.

In this level we are not going to create anything yet. We are only going to look and recognise. Like when you visit a new house for the first time: first you look, then you touch, and finally you live in it.

A practical suggestion: before continuing, open the gallery on your phone and look at your photos. Each one of them is a digital file. Touch them, zoom in, observe. You are already handling digital content without realising it.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The family album.** Marta keeps all the photos of her grandchildren on her phone and shares them on WhatsApp with the family group. Twenty years ago, those photos would have been developed and put into a paper album.
2. **Grandma's recipe.** Jorge has his mother's lentil soup recipe saved as a document on his phone. He searches for it with one word, "lentils", and finds it in seconds.
3. **The birthday video.** Ana recorded her partner's birthday with her phone. The video is digital content: it is seen on a screen, it can be forwarded and it is stored in the gallery.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take your phone and unlock it.
Step 2: Find the "Gallery" or "Photos" app (it usually has a drawing of a flower or a landscape). Open it by touching it with your finger.
Step 3: Look at the photos and slide your finger to move from one to the next. Stop at one you like.
Step 4: Touch the photo to zoom in with two fingers, then set the zoom back the way it was.
Step 5: Press the share button (usually an arrow or three dots) and choose "WhatsApp" to send it to a relative. If you feel shy, you can cancel.
Step 6: Now find the messaging app (WhatsApp) and open the family group. Notice how text messages, photos and voice notes live together in the same conversation.
Step 7: Think out loud: which of everything you have seen is digital content? The answer: all of it.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is digital content? a) Only videos. b) Everything you see or hear on a screen. c) Paper magazines.
2. Where are the phone's photos kept? a) In the cupboard. b) In the gallery or photos app. c) They are not kept.
3. What is a file? a) A piece of furniture. b) An "object" stored on the phone or computer, with a name. c) A type of paper.
4. Can digital content be copied without wasting paper? a) Yes. b) No. c) Only photos.
5. Can artificial intelligence create content? a) No, never. b) Only music. c) Yes, it can create texts and images.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-a, 5-c.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- What we share on the internet can be very hard to delete. Before sending a photo, think about whether you would like everyone to see it.
- Do not publish or forward photos of other people without their permission. Asking permission is a sign of respect.
- Not everything on the internet is true. We will learn how to tell the difference in later levels.
- The content you create (photos, texts) belongs to you. Nobody else gets to decide what to do with it.
- If something seems strange or scares you, stop and ask someone you trust. There are no silly questions.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Digital content is everything you see or hear on a screen: photos, videos, texts, music and messages. It lives in files inside the phone or computer, and it can be copied, searched, edited and sent over the internet. Artificial intelligence can also create content. In the next level we will learn how to ask AI to write a text for us.
