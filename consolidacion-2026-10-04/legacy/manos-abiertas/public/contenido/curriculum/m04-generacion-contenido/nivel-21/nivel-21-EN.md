# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 21
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Canopy
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a deepfake is and why it is dangerous.
- Learn to detect false and manipulated content.
- Get to know the ethical rules for creating content with AI.
- Know what to do when faced with suspicious content.
- Commit to creating honest and transparent content.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Deepfake | A fake video or audio created with AI that makes someone appear to say or do what they did not do. |
| Manipulation | Changing content to deceive whoever sees it. |
| Verification | Checking whether content is true or false. |
| Transparency | Saying clearly how content was made. |
| Disinformation | False information spread with the intention of deceiving. |
| Source | The place where information comes from (person, media, website). |

## 📚 Main lesson
AI is not only for creating: it can also create deception. A deepfake is a fake video or audio in which a person seems to say or do things they never did. With the techniques we have learned (generating voice, editing video, changing images), a very convincing lie can be manufactured. That is why ethics is half of this craft.

Deepfakes are made with advanced AI, but the idea is simple: use a person's real face or voice to put them in false situations. A well-known example is a fake video of a politician, a celebrity or a relative. The person did not say that, but the video shows it. If whoever watches it does not know, they may believe it.

Why are they dangerous? Because they create distrust and confusion. They can damage a person's reputation, manipulate elections, scam people or turn families against each other. A fake audio of a relative asking for money is a real scam that already happens. Knowing they exist is the first defence.

Learning to detect deepfakes is a new and necessary skill. Some signs: the lips do not match the voice, the blinking is strange, the skin looks too smooth, there are odd edges on the face, the voice sounds metallic or choppy. But be careful: deepfakes improve every day and often cannot be detected by eye.

Verification is the main tool. Before believing and sharing, we ask: where does this content come from? Who published it? Is it a known, serious source? Do other sources confirm it? If something seems odd to us, we do not share it. A good reflex: when the surprise is too big, first doubt and then check.

There are also technical verification tools: reverse image search (upload an image and see where it appeared before), metadata analysers and programs that flag content generated with AI. Some platforms already label AI-created content. We will learn to use the simplest ones.

The ethics of creation has clear rules that we have been seeing. First: we do not create false content that harms people. Second: if we generate content with AI, we say so (transparency). Third: we ask permission before using other people's image or voice. Fourth: we respect copyright. Fifth: we do not share what we have not verified.

Transparency is the antidote to deepfakes. When we create content with AI, we label it: "voice generated with Piper", "image created with AI", "edited video". Labelling does not remove merit; on the contrary, it builds trust. Whoever says how they did something shows they are hiding nothing.

Honesty is also a community value. In the club, the family and the neighbourhood, trust is the most precious asset. Content that deceives breaks it. That is why, when we create, we think: would this cause harm if believed? Would I like it done to me? The golden rule works again.

If we find suspicious content that affects someone close, we act calmly: we warn the affected person, we do not spread it and, if it is a scam, we report it. Speed matters: sharing a deception makes it grow. Not giving it wings is the best response.

By the end of this level, we will know how to recognise the risk of false content, detect signs of manipulation, verify before sharing and create with transparency and honesty. AI gave us powerful tools: using them well is our responsibility.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The grandson's fake audio.** Marta receives an audio that "sounds like" her grandson asking for money. She remembers the level, doubts, calls her son and discovers it is a scam.
2. **The politician's video.** Pedro sees a fake video of a politician and does not share it; he looks for the source and discovers it is a deepfake.
3. **The generated voice.** Carmen labels her video: "voice generated with AI". Her friends trust her because they know how she did it.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Read this situation: "A video of a neighbour saying the neighbourhood water is poisoned arrives by WhatsApp".
Step 2: Before sharing, write down the verification questions on paper: where does it come from? Who published it? Is it a serious source? Do other sources confirm it?
Step 3: Search the internet for the topic and check whether there are other news items confirming or denying it.
Step 4: Learn to use a reverse image search: upload a suspicious image and look at where it appeared before.
Step 5: Check whether the platform labels the content as generated with AI.
Step 6: Decide with your answers: would you share it? Write your decision and the reason.
Step 7: With your own content: review the last video you created and think about whether the voice, images or text could confuse someone.
Step 8: Write a transparency label for your content (for example, "Video with AI-generated voice") and put it on your next work.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a deepfake? a) False content created with AI that shows someone doing what they did not do. b) A very high-quality video. c) A long song.
2. What is the first defence against deepfakes? a) Knowing they exist and doubting before believing. b) Not using the phone. c) Sharing them quickly.
3. Before sharing strange content, what do we do? a) We share it without more. b) We verify the source and look for confirmation. c) We always keep it.
4. What is transparency in creating with AI? a) Saying clearly how the content was made. b) Saying nothing. c) Using many filters.
5. If false content affects a relative, what do we do? a) We spread it to warn people. b) We warn the person, do not spread it and report it if it is a scam. c) We do nothing.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-b, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Never create deepfakes that damage a person's image: the harm is real even if the image is fake.
- Label your AI-generated content: transparency builds trust.
- Ask permission before using anyone's face or voice.
- Do not spread unverified content: sharing a deception makes you part of it.
- Help older and less informed people detect these traps.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Deepfakes are false content created with AI that can deceive and cause harm. We defend ourselves by doubting, verifying the sources and not sharing without confirming. When creating, we are transparent and honest. In the next level we will use AI as an editor: reviewing, improving and polishing other people's and our own content.
