# Project 2 — Civic Desk Responsive Experience

## Context

Civic Desk helps residents report a local issue, understand what happens after submission, and follow the response without repeatedly contacting the service. Users may be on the street with an unstable connection, on a tablet at home, or at a desktop computer.

## Challenge

Create a responsive reporting and tracking experience. The design must support a report with a category, description, optional location and optional photo; it must explain permissions, preserve progress, and represent report status without relying on color alone.

| Milestone | Required artifact | Review question |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Method choice and task scenarios | What can analytics reveal, and what needs observation? |
| IA | Content inventory, labels, navigation tree | Can residents predict where privacy and reporting options live? |
| Responsive design | 360, 768 and 1440 layouts with behavior notes | What changes beyond width? |
| Accessible UI | Component states and focus order | Can the task be completed with keyboard and clear feedback? |
| Testing | Findings matrix and prioritised iteration | Which issue has evidence, impact and a next test? |

## Success criteria

The product preserves task continuity, makes permission and error states legible, offers an accessible alternative to map-only information, and documents how content and navigation reorganize across breakpoints.

## Assessment

Use the **Information Architecture**, **UI Design**, **Responsive & Mobile UX**, **Accessibility**, **Usability Testing**, and **Documentation** rubrics. Evidence must not contain real personal or location-sensitive participant data.
