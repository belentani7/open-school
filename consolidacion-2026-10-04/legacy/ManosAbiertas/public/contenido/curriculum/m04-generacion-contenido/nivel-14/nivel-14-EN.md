# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 14
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Bring together everything learned in levels 10 to 13: voice, video and subtitles.
- Create a complete audiovisual piece: a video with narrated voice and subtitles.
- Plan a small production from start to finish.
- Review the final result before sharing it.
- Share the piece with the family, club or group.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Audiovisual piece | A complete video with image, sound and text. |
| Script | The text that says what we are going to tell, in order. |
| Narration | The voice that narrates the video (it can be human or synthetic). |
| Production | The whole process of creating a piece: planning, recording, editing. |
| Soundtrack | The music that accompanies the video in the background. |
| Final review | Watching the whole video before publishing to spot mistakes. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous levels we learned to generate voice with Piper (level 10), to transcribe with Whisper (level 11), to edit video (level 12) and to add subtitles (level 13). Now comes the moment to bring it all together: create a complete audiovisual piece. It is like cooking a whole dish with the ingredients we already know how to prepare.

Every good piece starts with the script. First we write what we want to tell: what we say, in what order, with what tone. It can be a Christmas greeting, the presentation of the club's recipe or a summary of the holidays. The script is the map of the journey: without it, we get lost.

With the script in hand, we generate the narration: the voice that will narrate the video. We can record it ourselves with the phone or use Piper's synthetic voice. The advantage of Piper is that we can repeat it until it sounds perfect, without getting tired. It is like having an announcer always available.

Then we put the images together: the clips and photos that accompany the text. Each part of the script has its image. For example, if we say "we arrived at the mountain", we show the clip of the path. The image and the voice must tell the same story at the same time. That is synchrony.

The next step is the edit: we place the clips on the timeline and put the narration underneath, adjusting the timings so they fit. Then we add background music at a soft volume that does not cover the voice. Music is the salt of the food: little and just right.

Then come the subtitles. With AI we generate them automatically, correct them and adjust them. This way the video is understood in silence, in noisy places and by people with hearing loss. A video with subtitles reaches more people.

Before exporting, we do the final review: we watch the complete video, from start to finish, twice. The first time to enjoy it; the second time with detective eyes, looking for mistakes: badly written text, an image that is left over, a voice that cannot be heard. Review is the step that separates a good job from a mediocre one.

The export saves the video as a final file. We choose the quality and the format (mp4 is usually the most compatible). Then we can share it by WhatsApp, by email or on a club social network. The video is now ready for the world.

Sharing responsibly is part of the production. If other people appear, let us remember to ask for permission. If we use music, it should be free. If the video is from a club event, let us respect those who do not want to be on camera. What we publish represents our home.

A complete piece does not have to be long. Often one well-made minute says more than ten messy minutes. Quality lies in clarity: what I want to say, to whom, and what I ask of them. A clear, short video is a gift to whoever receives it.

By the end of this level, we will know how to produce a complete audiovisual piece from start to finish: script, voice, images, edit, subtitles and export. The audiovisual cycle, mastered with free and open tools.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The Christmas greeting.** Rosa writes a one-minute script, generates the voice with Piper, puts together family photos, edits the video and adds subtitles. She sends it to the whole family.
2. **The club's recipe.** Carmen produces a video with the steps of the pie, with her own recorded voice, soft music and subtitles for the neighbourhood group.
3. **The trip summary.** Pedro creates a two-minute piece with clips of the Way of Saint James, narrated voice and subtitles in English and Portuguese, for his foreign friends.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Write a short script (5 or 6 sentences) about a simple topic: "How I make my coffee in the morning".
Step 2: Generate the narration with Piper or record your voice with the phone reading the script.
Step 3: Find or record 4 or 5 photos or clips to accompany each sentence.
Step 4: Open the video editor and place the images on the timeline.
Step 5: Add the narration and adjust the timings so each sentence matches its image.
Step 6: Add soft background music (from a free library) at low volume.
Step 7: Generate the automatic subtitles, correct them and adjust them.
Step 8: Review the whole video twice, export in mp4 and share it with a relative.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. Where does an audiovisual piece begin? a) With the script. b) With the edit. c) With the subtitles.
2. What is the Piper narration for? a) To narrate the video with a voice we can repeat. b) To remove the sound. c) To print photos.
3. What is synchrony? a) That image and voice tell the same story at the same time. b) That the video is short. c) Music at full volume.
4. How many times should you review the video before exporting? a) At least twice. b) None. c) Only if there is time left.
5. What do we do if other people appear in the video? a) Publish it without more. b) Ask permission before sharing. c) Always delete the video.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Ask people for permission before they appear in your video, and respect those who do not want to appear.
- Use free music and material, or with permission from their authors.
- Do not manipulate the video to deceive: images and voice must be faithful to reality.
- Check that the subtitles do not reveal personal data without consent.
- Publish carefully: what you upload to the internet stays forever.
