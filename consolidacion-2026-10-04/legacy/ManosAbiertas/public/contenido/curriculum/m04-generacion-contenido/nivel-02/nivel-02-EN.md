# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 02
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Use text AI to write everyday letters, messages and lists.
- Ask for the text to have the right tone: warm, formal, brief or detailed.
- Adapt a text to the person who will receive it.
- Correct and improve your own draft with the help of AI.
- Understand that AI is a help, not a replacement for our own words.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Tone | The style of the text: serious, warm, formal, close. Like the background music of a conversation. |
| Formal | Written with respect and correctness, for example for a bank or a public office. |
| Informal | Written with closeness and trust, for example for family or friends. |
| Draft | A first version of the text, which can be corrected. |
| Recipient | The person to whom the text is addressed. |
| Review | Read the text carefully to correct mistakes. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous level we learned how to ask AI for a text. In this level we are going to put it into practice with three tasks we do many times: writing letters, sending messages and making lists. These are texts that sometimes cost us effort because we do not know how to start or which words to use. AI gives us that first push.

Imagine you want to claim a refund from the bank. Writing a formal letter from scratch is a chore: how do you start? What tone do you use? AI can draft it in seconds if you explain the situation. Then you read it, correct it and sign it with your own hand, because the text carries your voice.

The key to a good text is to explain three things to the AI: what you want (a letter, a message, a list), who it is for (the recipient) and in what tone (formal, warm, brief, detailed). The more information you give it, the more useful the response will be. It is like giving instructions to a cook: if you only say "make me some food", you will eat anything.

Let us take an example. If you write "Make me a letter to the bank because they overcharged me", the AI answers well. If you write "Make me a formal letter to the bank claiming the refund of 45 euros from the July electricity bill, because I had already paid it", the answer will be much more accurate. Information is the seasoning of the text.

Another very practical use: messages. Sometimes we want to give bad news or ask something uncomfortable, and we cannot find the words. AI can suggest a kind way of saying it. For example: "Help me tell my brother that this year I cannot invite him to dinner for my birthday without making him feel bad". The AI suggests a warm sentence and you decide whether to use it.

Also lists. The shopping list, the task list, the Christmas gift list. You can ask: "Make me a task list for preparing a move" or "Tell me what I need to fix a dripping tap". The AI organises the information into points, and you adapt it to your reality.

A very useful trick is to ask for the text in several tones. You can say: "Write me this complaint letter in a formal tone and then in a warm tone". The AI gives you both versions and you choose. It is like trying on two dresses in the shop: one formal and one casual, and keeping the one that suits you best.

Another trick: improve your own drafts. Write a message your own way, paste it to the AI and say "correct it" or "make it clearer". The machine respects the content and improves the form. It is like taking the draft to a proofreader.

You must always review the result. The AI may use strange words, make mistakes or invent details. You are the owner of the text. If you do not like a sentence, you change it. If the text is too long, ask "summarise it in three sentences".

Be careful with personal data in letters. If the AI asks for or generates account numbers, ID numbers or full addresses, you must check they are yours and only write the ones that are essential. In a real letter, you write those details by hand at the end; the AI does not need to invent them.

AI also helps you choose words. Sometimes you know what you want to say but you lack the expression. You can ask: "How do you say in a letter that I do not agree with the charge?" The answer gives you options you can use as if they were your own.

Writing is a skill that is trained. AI does not replace it: it accompanies it. Just as a refrigerator does not cook for us but keeps our ingredients, AI stores and organises words, and we decide the recipe.

At the end of this level, the main idea is this: text AI is an efficient secretary that never gets tired. You dictate the idea, it drafts it, and you add the judgement, the correction and the signature.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The bank complaint.** Luisa asks: "Write me a formal letter to the bank claiming 45 euros overcharged". The AI drafts the letter, Luisa corrects it, adds her details and sends it through the online office.
2. **The awkward message.** Tomás does not know how to tell his daughter he cannot go to her party. He asks the AI for "a warm way to apologise". He copies the sentence he likes best and adapts it.
3. **The moving list.** Elena asks for a "task list for a house move". The AI gives her organised points; Elena crosses out the ones she does not need and adds her own.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open ChatGPT or Gemini as in the previous level.
Step 2: Write this prompt: "Write me a formal letter to the city council asking for information about grants to repair the heating".
Step 3: Press enter and wait for the response. Read it out loud.
Step 4: Write below: "Make it shorter and with a warm tone". Press enter again.
Step 5: Compare the two versions. Choose one.
Step 6: Now write: "Make me a shopping list for a weekly menu for four people with economical dishes".
Step 7: Review the list and add by hand the products you want using the chat's edit option (or copying the text to a document).
Step 8: To finish, write: "Correct this message: [write a three-sentence message of your own]". Read the correction and decide whether you like it.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What three things should you explain to the AI for a good text? a) What I want, who it is for and in what tone. b) The colour, the size and the weight. c) You do not need to explain anything.
2. If the text comes out too long, what do you write? a) "Make it shorter". b) "Delete it". c) Nothing, leave it.
3. Can you improve your own draft with AI? a) No, it only creates from scratch. b) Yes, by pasting my text and asking for correction. c) Only in English.
4. Who owns the final text? a) The AI. b) The person who reviews and signs it. c) The program.
5. Should you ask the AI to invent your ID number or bank account? a) Yes, it is convenient. b) No, I write those details myself. c) It does not matter.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Texts for official procedures must reflect the truth. Check that the AI does not add false data.
- Do not ask the AI to invent documents, receipts or signatures. That is fraud.
- Respect the privacy of recipients: do not write about third parties without need.
- A text with your name is your responsibility, whether the AI wrote it or not. Always read it before sending.
- Use AI to express yourself better, not to deceive: writing for someone who does not know you are writing for them is dishonest.
