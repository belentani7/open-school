# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 18
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what voice-over is: giving voice to a text.
- Get to know dubbing: putting a new voice on an existing video.
- Use AI to generate voices with different tones and styles.
- Apply a generated voice to your own video.
- Synchronise the voice with the images and the text.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Voice-over | The voice that reads a text for a video or presentation. |
| Dubbing | Replacing the voice of a video with another voice. |
| Tone | The character of the voice: serious, cheerful, warm, firm. |
| Synchronisation | That the voice matches the lips, image or text. |
| Intonation | The music of the voice: going up and down to express. |
| Synthetic voice | The voice generated by the computer, like Piper's. |

## 📚 Main lesson
The voice is the soul of audiovisual. The same text read with a serious voice or with a cheerful voice says different things. Voice-over is that art of giving voice to a text, and today AI lets us generate varied voices, in several languages and with different tones, without recording studios. It is like having a team of announcers at hand.

Voice-over is used in many things in life: recipe videos, club presentations, congratulation messages, stories for the grandchildren. Before, you had to record with a microphone, repeat if you made a mistake and watch out for background noise. With Piper's synthetic voice we can generate the voice-over in seconds and repeat it as many times as we want.

Voice tones matter. Piper offers different voices and we can choose the tone with the words of the script: short sentences give energy; long, soft sentences give calm. Intonation, the rising and falling of the voice, is the music that gives life to words. AI proposes the voice; we choose the feeling.

Dubbing is one more step: putting a new voice on an existing video. We can dub a home video into another language so that the family living abroad understands it, or give a voice to a silent presentation. With the tools we know (extracting audio, generating a new voice, joining again) dubbing is within our reach.

The dubbing process is simple if we divide it into steps. First, we take the audio out of the original video. Second, we transcribe it with Whisper to have the text (level 11). Third, we correct the text and translate it if needed. Fourth, we generate the new voice with Piper. Fifth, we join the new voice with the video using FFmpeg (level 17). Five steps, and the video speaks in another voice or another language.

Synchronisation is the challenge of dubbing. Ideally the new voice should fit the lip movement or the rhythm of the video. It is not always perfect, but with practice we adjust the duration. For videos without people speaking (landscapes, recipes, presentations) synchronisation is easier and the result is very good.

Dubbing is also a tool for access. A video from the town hall or the club can be dubbed into other languages so that more people understand it. Giving voice in the listener's language is a gesture of respect and inclusion. Technology helps us build bridges.

The ethics of dubbing is especially important. Dubbing someone else's video and passing it off as original is deception. Dubbing a video of a real person with a voice that says things they did not say is manipulation. AI gives power: let us use it to create, not to impersonate. Before dubbing, we ask: who is the author, who appears, do I have permission?

Our life videos, the club's and our own projects are safe territory for dubbing. From there on, care and permission. The rule is the same as always: do not do to others what you do not want done to you.

Practice makes perfect. We will start by dubbing simple, short videos: a greeting, a recipe, a club announcement. With each dub we learn to adjust the tone, speed and synchronisation. The first time is hard; the tenth goes smoothly.

By the end of this level, we will know how to give voice to a text with AI, dub our own video into another language and respect the ethical limits of dubbing. Voice, narration and dubbing no longer hold secrets for us.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose a short text of yours (for example, the recipe from level 12 or a greeting).
Step 2: Open Piper and generate the voice with a tone that fits the text (serious or cheerful).
Step 3: Listen to the voice and, if it does not convince you, change the words or the speed and generate again.
Step 4: Have a short video of yours without voice ready (for example, some photos with movement).
Step 5: Open the command line and join the voice with the video using FFmpeg (ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -i voice.mp3 -c:v copy -c:a aac video_with_voice.mp4).
Step 6: Play the result and check that the voice sounds good and goes with the images.
Step 7: If the original video has sound and you want to change it, use FFmpeg to remove the old audio first (ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -an video_without_sound.mp4) and repeat step 5.
Step 8: Share the dubbed video with a relative and tell them how you did it.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Do not dub other people's videos without permission or pass them off as originals.
- Do not use the generated voice to make someone say things they did not say.
- Ask for consent before dubbing videos where real people appear.
- Indicate when a video is dubbed with AI: transparency builds trust.
- Use dubbing to create, include and translate, never to deceive.
