# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 19
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a podcast is: an audio programme you listen to whenever you want.
- Plan an episode: topic, script and length.
- Record or generate the voice with free tools.
- Edit the episode with music and pauses.
- Publish and share the podcast.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Podcast | A recorded audio programme listened to on demand. |
| Episode | Each edition of the podcast, like each chapter of a series. |
| Microphone | The device that captures the voice when recording. |
| Jingle | The short music that opens and closes the podcast. |
| Interview | A conversation with questions and answers. |
| Publish | Making the episode available for others to listen to. |

## 📚 Main lesson
A podcast is an audio programme you can listen to whenever and wherever you want: walking, on the bus or in the kitchen. Like a spoken magazine. Anyone can create one with the tools we already know: the voice (human or with Piper), narrative (level 15) and audio editing. It is a perfect format for the club, the family or a hobby.

The first thing is to choose the topic and the audience. What will we talk about? Gardening, recipes, neighbourhood stories, music? For whom? The audience sets the tone: a podcast for grandchildren is not the same as one for the club. Once decided, everything flows more easily.

Then comes the script. It does not have to be a text read word for word: often it is better to have a guide with ideas and questions, like notes in your hand. Speaking naturally sounds better than reading. The script is the map; the conversation, the journey.

To record the voice we need silence and, if possible, a microphone. Phones record well in a quiet room. If we prefer not to record, we generate the voice with Piper: we write the script and the computer reads it. Both options are valid; everyone chooses the one that suits them best.

The episode has structure: a short jingle at the beginning (free music from a library), the introduction of the topic, the content (the story, the interview, the explanation) and a closing with a goodbye and the jingle. It is like a full meal: starter, main course and dessert. With audio programs (like Audacity) we assemble the pieces.

The music must be free and respect the licences. Libraries like Pixabay or Jamendo have free music. A few seconds of jingle is enough. We remember what we learned: do not use other people's music without permission.

Audio editing is simple with Audacity: we cut the mistakes, remove the long pauses and adjust the volume. It is like ironing the sound: the recording comes out wrinkled and editing smooths it. A clean fifteen-minute episode sounds much better than a long one with mistakes.

Publishing a podcast has several options. The simplest is to send the audio file (mp3) by WhatsApp or email to those interested. For a public podcast, there are free audio platforms where you can upload it. We will start by sharing with the club group or family; if they like it, it can grow.

Podcasts appreciate regularity: every week, every two weeks. It does not need to be long; ten or fifteen quality minutes are worth more than a scattered hour. Consistency is what creates the listening habit.

We must also think about ethics: if we do interviews, let us ask permission to record and publish. Do not tell other people's intimate stories without consent. A podcast is a window to the world: let us take care of what we show through it.

By the end of this level, we will know how to plan, record or generate, edit and share a podcast episode. Voice, narrative and audio are now our tools for communicating.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The club podcast.** Pedro creates a weekly fifteen-minute episode with garden news, tips and a free jingle.
2. **The family podcast.** Rosa records family stories with her phone and edits them with Audacity to send to her grandchildren.
3. **The recipe podcast.** Carmen records with Piper the voice of her recipes and publishes them as short episodes for her friends.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose a simple topic for your podcast (for example, "growing vegetables at home").
Step 2: Write a mini script: introduction (2 sentences), content (5 ideas) and goodbye (1 sentence).
Step 3: Decide whether you record your voice with the phone or generate the voice with Piper.
Step 4: Record or generate the audio in a quiet room.
Step 5: Open Audacity and load the audio.
Step 6: Cut the mistakes and the long pauses with the selection tool and the delete key.
Step 7: Add a short jingle of free music at the beginning and end, at low volume.
Step 8: Export the episode as mp3 (File, Export) and share it by WhatsApp with a relative.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

2. What is an episode? a) Each edition of the podcast. b) The microphone. c) The music.

4. What do we do with mistakes in the recording? a) We leave them. b) We cut them with the editing. c) We restart the computer.
5. Can I use any music in my podcast? a) Yes. b) No, it is better to use free music with permission. c) Only live music.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Ask permission before recording and publishing interviews or other people's voices.
- Do not tell other people's intimate stories without consent.
- Use free music and respect the licences.
- Be honest in what you tell: the podcast must not spread misinformation.
- Think about privacy: do not share personal data unnecessarily.
