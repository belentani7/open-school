# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 07
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Summarise long texts with AI (documents, news, letters, contracts).
- Ask for summaries of the right length: one sentence, three points or one page.
- Extract the main ideas without losing the original meaning.
- Ask questions about a long text to understand it better.
- Know that a summary never replaces full reading when it matters.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Summary | A short version of a text that keeps what matters most. |
| Main idea | The essential thing the text wants to say. Like the stone of a fruit. |
| Summary length | How long it should be: one sentence, three points, one page. |
| Paraphrase | Say the same thing with other words. |
| Notes | Short notes we take to remember. |
| Context | The situation or topic the text is about. |

## 📚 Main lesson
How many times do we receive a long document we do not have time to read entirely? A contract, a bank circular, an eight-page news article, the terms of an insurance policy. AI can summarise the essentials for us in seconds. It is like having a fast reader who tells us what it is about before we decide to read it all.

The golden rule of a summary is that it must keep the main ideas and remove the accessory. AI does this quite well, but it needs us to tell it exactly what we want. A good summary prompt indicates: the text, the desired length and the level of detail.

Asking for the text is very easy: you copy it from the document and paste it into the chat. If it is very long, AI can process it in parts. Then you write something like: "Summarise this text in five sentences" or "Give me the three most important points".

The length must always be requested. "In one sentence", "in three points", "in half a page". If we do not say it, AI chooses on its own and the summary may come out too short or too long for what we need.

A one-sentence summary is good for a quick idea. A summary in points is good for remembering a list of things. A one-page summary is good for sharing with someone else. Each length has its use, just as each key has its lock.

We can also ask for summaries adapted to a specific use: "Summarise this contract pointing out what it obliges me to do", "Summarise this news to tell it to the family group". AI adapts the summary to the purpose.

Another very useful feature: ask questions about the text instead of a summary. We can say: "What date do I have to submit the application?", "How much will they refund me?", "Is there any small print I should know about?". AI searches the text and answers. It is like having a secretary who has read the document for us.

AI summaries are not perfect. It can make mistakes or leave out an important detail. That is why, when the document matters (a contract, a procedure), the summary is there to guide us, but the decision is made after reading the essentials or asking someone you trust.

A common mistake is trusting the summary blindly to sign something. No machine can know how much a clause matters to us. The summary is the map; we are the travellers, and the map does not walk for us.

It is also worth saving the useful summaries. We can copy them into Google Docs or into a phone note. This way we have our own file of notes: summaries of the things that matter to us.

The summarising technique is trained. The more we use AI to summarise, the better we understand its strengths and its limits. And the better we understand the limits, the better we direct it.

By the end of this level, we will know how to turn any long text into a clear idea: for ourselves, to explain to others or to remember the essentials. A skill that saves time and avoids scares.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The insurance contract.** Marta receives a 20-page contract. She asks for a one-page summary and then a three-point one. She understands the essentials before calling her daughter to help her decide.
2. **The bank circular.** Pedro receives a circular with changes to the terms. He asks the AI: "What affects me if I have the account without a salary?" and gets the concrete answer.
3. **The news for the group.** Ana wants to share a long news story with her family. She asks for a five-sentence summary and sends it to the group, which reads it all.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open a long text you have at hand (a news story or a bank letter). Copy it by holding your finger or with Control+C.
Step 2: Open ChatGPT or Gemini.
Step 3: Paste the text into the chat (hold your finger or Control+V) and write below: "Summarise this text in three points".
Step 4: Press enter and wait. Read the summary.
Step 5: Now write: "Summarise in one sentence". Compare with the previous one.
Step 6: Write: "What should I do first according to this text?" Notice how the AI answers concrete questions.
Step 7: Copy the three-point summary and paste it into a note or Google Docs.
Step 8: Check the summary: read the original text and look for any missing important idea. If it is missing, ask the AI to add it.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What must a good summary keep? a) The whole text. b) The main ideas. c) Only the ending.
2. What must you always ask for with a summary? a) The desired length. b) The author's name. c) Nothing.
3. Can you ask questions about the text instead of a summary? a) No. b) Yes, the AI looks for the answer in the text. c) Only if the text is short.
4. Is the AI summary always perfect? a) Yes. b) No, it can leave out important details. c) Only in English.
5. Before signing a contract, is the summary enough? a) Yes, always. b) No, you must read the essentials or consult someone you trust. c) You never need to read.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Do not sign or decide something important based only on an AI summary. The summary guides, it does not decide.
- Check that the summary does not change the meaning of the original or add false data.
- If you share a summary, say it is a summary and not the full text.
- Respect privacy: do not paste documents with other people's data into the AI without need.
- For sensitive documents (contracts, procedures), always seek human help when in doubt.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Summarising with AI is copying the text, asking for the desired length and checking that the meaning is kept. Summaries help us get oriented, save time and explain to others, but they do not replace full reading when the decision matters. In the next level we move from text to images: we will learn to control style and refine results.
