# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 05
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Write precise prompts to get quality articles.
- Structure a request: topic, audience, length and tone.
- Ask the AI to develop each part of an article step by step.
- Review and personalise the article so it sounds like you.
- Recognise the difference between a vague prompt and a well-built one.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Prompt | The instruction you give to the AI. It is like the recipe you hand to the cook. |
| Precise prompt | An instruction with all the details: what, for whom, how much, in what tone. |
| Structure | The shape of the article: beginning, development and end. Like the skeleton of a body. |
| Tone | The emotional style of the text: formal, warm, cheerful, serious. |
| Audience | The people the article is aimed at. |
| Review | Read, correct and adapt the generated text before using it. |

## 📚 Main lesson
An article is a text that explains a topic to a group of people. It can be for the neighbourhood newsletter, for a family group, for an association or for the village magazine. AI can write it, but the quality depends a lot on how we ask. This is where the art of the "precise prompt" begins.

The difference between a vague prompt and a precise one is like the difference between telling a cook "make me a meal" and telling him "make me chicken with rice, for four people, no spice, with seasonal vegetables". With the first, you will eat whatever; with the second, you will eat what you wanted.

A good article prompt has four ingredients: the topic (what we are talking about), the audience (who it is for), the length (how much text) and the tone (how it sounds). For example: "Write me an article about how to make homemade preserves, for older people who live in the village, about 300 words, with a warm and practical tone".

The topic must be explained in detail. "Write about the market" is not enough. Better: "write about the new Saturday market: what stalls there are, the opening hours and why it is worth shopping there". AI needs raw material to work, like dough for bread.

The audience is key. An article for children is not written the same way as one for members of an association. If we tell the AI who will read it, it adapts the vocabulary and examples. It is like changing language without changing topic.

The length is also requested. "Three hundred words", "one page", "five sentences". If we do not say it, the AI improvises and may give us a very long or very short text. Asking for the measure is part of a good recipe.

The tone gives the flavour. "Warm and close", "serious and formal", "cheerful and optimistic". The same news can be told in many ways; the tone is the music with which we tell it.

A very effective trick: ask for the article in two stages. First: "Give me an outline of an article about X". The AI gives us the skeleton: title, three parts, closing idea. We review it and, if we like it, we ask: "Develop the outline". This way we control the structure before the text grows.

Another trick: give it an example. If we have an article we like, we can say: "Write me something similar, with the same style, but about Y". The AI learns from the model and gets closer to what we are looking for.

After generating, our work always comes: review. Change words, remove false data, add examples from our neighbourhood, correct the tone. AI gives the first draft; we give the truth.

We can also ask for versions: "make it shorter", "make it cheerfuller", "add a final tip". The conversation with the AI is a workshop where we adjust the piece until it fits.

Beware of a typical mistake: leaving the article just as it is without reading it. An article with invented data can cause problems, especially if it talks about people or dates. Always read it, as you would read a letter before signing it.

The goal of this level is not to be writers, but directors of writing. We decide what is told, how it is told and who tells it. The AI is the hand; we are the head.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The neighbourhood newsletter.** The residents' association wants to announce the end-of-year party. Carmen asks for a 200-word article, cheerful tone, for all neighbours, and personalises it with the real times.
2. **The club magazine.** Pedro writes for the hiking club. He asks for an article about the new trail, 400 words, enthusiastic tone, and adds photos from the last outing.
3. **The family group.** Ana wants to explain to her family how to shop safely online. She asks for a simple article, for people with no experience, with a patient tone.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open ChatGPT or Gemini.
Step 2: Write this prompt: "Write me an article about the benefits of walking every day, for people over 60, about 250 words, with a warm and motivating tone".
Step 3: Press enter and wait.
Step 4: Read it. Does it have a beginning, development and end? Mentally mark the three parts.
Step 5: Write below: "Give me a three-point outline of this article". Notice how the AI shows you the structure.
Step 6: Now write: "Develop the second point with more everyday examples".
Step 7: When you have the full text, write: "Make it a bit shorter and add a final encouraging sentence".
Step 8: Copy the result, paste it into Google Docs and add a title and a photo of yours (or generated). You now have your own article.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What are the four ingredients of a good article prompt? a) Topic, audience, length and tone. b) Colour, taste, smell and texture. c) None.
2. If I do not specify the length, what does the AI do? a) It asks. b) It improvises and may give a very long or very short text. c) It does not write.
3. What is the purpose of asking for an outline first? a) To waste time. b) To control the structure before developing. c) It only works in English.
4. Can I ask it to develop part of the outline? a) No. b) Yes, in the same conversation. c) Only if I pay.
5. Should I review the generated article before using it? a) No, the AI does it perfectly. b) Yes, always: it may contain invented data. c) Only if it is long.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- An article with your signature is your responsibility. Check there are no false facts or wrong dates.
- If the article talks about real people, respect their privacy and ask permission if you mention their data.
- Do not publish an AI text as yours without reading it: what it says commits you.
- Distinguish opinion from fact. AI mixes both; you must separate them.
- If the article is used in a medium, indicate whether it was created with AI help. Transparency builds trust.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
A good article starts with a good prompt: topic, audience, length and tone. We ask for an outline, develop it in parts and review the result with a critical eye. The AI is the hand; we are the head.
