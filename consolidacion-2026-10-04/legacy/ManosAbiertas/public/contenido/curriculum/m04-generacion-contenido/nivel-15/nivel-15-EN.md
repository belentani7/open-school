# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 15
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what narrative is: the way of telling a story.
- Get to know the basic structure of a story: beginning, middle and end.
- Use AI to improve story ideas and texts.
- Write your own short story with AI help.
- Know what story to tell and to whom, depending on the audience.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Narrative | The way of telling a story so that it interests people. |
| Structure | The order of the story: beginning, development and end. |
| Hook | The opening that catches attention and makes you want to continue. |
| Character | The one who lives the story: a person, an animal, an object. |
| Conflict | The problem or challenge that gives life to the story. |
| Resolution | The ending that solves the conflict and leaves the lesson. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Telling stories is as old as humanity. Grandmothers tell them around the table; clubs share them in their meetings. Narrative is the way of ordering that story so that it interests people. And today, AI can help us write better stories without taking away our credit: the idea and the life are still ours.

Every simple story has three parts, like rice pudding: it is prepared, cooked and served. The first is the beginning: we introduce the character and their world. The second is the middle: the conflict, problem or challenge appears. The third is the ending: the problem is solved and something remains. That structure is called "beginning, middle and end".

The hook is the opening that catches attention. A good story begins with something that arouses curiosity: a question, a mystery, an emotion. Instead of "I went on a trip", better "the day I lost my suitcase at the airport, I started to live". The hook is the front door: if it does not call, nobody enters.

AI can help us write in several ways. We can ask it for story ideas: "give me three ideas for children's stories with a vegetable garden". We can give it our opening and ask it to continue. We can ask it for an alternative ending or to make the text more emotional. AI is like a companion at the table who makes suggestions while we weave the story.

But be careful: AI does not know our life. Only we know what is moving, which memories are valuable, which club anecdote is real. AI proposes; we decide. The heart of the story is always human.

Telling stories is also choosing for whom. A story for the grandchildren is not the same as an anecdote for the gardening club. The audience sets the tone, the words and the length. A well-told story adapts to the listener like clothes adapt to the season.

Personal stories are the most powerful. The first time we planted a tomato, the wedding fifty years ago, the first computer. AI can help us order those memories, give them rhythm and polish them, without inventing them. Our life already has stories: we just need to tell them well.

Writing is rewriting. The first draft is not the final story: it is the clay we shape. AI lets us try versions without effort: ask for a happier tone, a more tender ending, a shorter sentence. Each version is a chance to improve.

Memory and truth matter. If we tell a real story, let us not exaggerate it until it is distorted. AI can invent details that never existed: they must be reviewed. A true story told honestly is worth more than a thousand invented ones.

By the end of this level, we will know how to structure a short story, use AI as a writing companion and choose the tone according to the audience. We will have made the step from consumers to creators of stories.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The story for the grandchildren.** Carmen writes a story about her childhood in the village, with AI to give it rhythm, and reads it at the Christmas dinner.
2. **The club anecdote.** Pedro asks AI to turn the giant tomato anecdote into a short three-paragraph story for the newsletter.
3. **The thank-you letter.** Luis structures a story of thanks to his school teacher to read at the alumni reunion.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose a real memory: an anecdote from your life (a trip, a wedding, a vegetable garden).
Step 2: Write it in 4 or 5 sentences as you remember it, without polishing.
Step 3: Identify the three parts: beginning (where it starts), middle (what problem happened), ending (how it finished).
Step 4: Open the AI chat you use in class and write: "I have this anecdote: [your text]. Help me improve it as a story, keeping the real facts".
Step 5: Ask for a version with a more striking hook: "Start with something that arouses curiosity".
Step 6: Choose the version you like most and correct it: make sure it does not invent facts, that it sounds like you.
Step 7: Read it aloud. If something sounds strange, ask AI for another sentence.
Step 8: Save the story in a document with your name and the title. Share it with a relative.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What are the three parts of a simple story? a) Beginning, middle and end. b) Beginning and end. c) Title and photo.
2. What is the hook? a) The opening that catches attention. b) The sad ending. c) The character's name.
3. Can AI know my life and my memories? a) Yes, always. b) No, AI proposes and I decide with my experience. c) Only if I ask.
4. What is more powerful for a story? a) Real and personal facts. b) Exaggerations. c) Copying someone else's story.
5. Who is in charge of the final story? a) AI. b) Me, with AI help. c) The audience always, without exception.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-b, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Do not tell other people's stories as if they were yours: the credit belongs to whoever lived them.
- If the story is real, do not distort it: honesty gives strength to the narration.
- Check that AI does not invent facts or people that never existed.
- Respect privacy: do not reveal intimate data of others without their consent.
- Use AI as a support tool, not as a substitute for your voice.
