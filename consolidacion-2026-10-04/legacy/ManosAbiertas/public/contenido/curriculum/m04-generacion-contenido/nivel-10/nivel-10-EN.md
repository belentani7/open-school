# Module 4: Content Generation — Level 10
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a synthetic voice is and how text-to-speech (TTS) works.
- Get to know Piper, a free tool that turns text into voice.
- Generate a voice audio from a written text.
- Choose the right voice and speed for each use.
- Use the generated audio in personal and educational projects.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Synthetic voice | A voice created by a machine, without recording a person. |
| TTS | English acronym for "text to speech": turning written text into sound. |
| Audio | A sound file, like the ones you hear on the phone. |
| Voice | The "timbre" of the speaker: male, female, young, adult. |
| Speed | How fast it speaks: slow, normal, fast. |
| Audio file | The saved result, usually in mp3 or wav format. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Has an automatic voice ever answered the phone? That voice was not recorded by a person: it is a synthetic voice, created by a machine. In this level we are going to learn how to create our own synthetic voices with a free tool called Piper. It is surprisingly easy and very useful.

The technology that turns text into voice is called TTS, the English acronym for "text to speech". It works like a radio reading a script: we give it a text and it gives us back an audio where a voice says it out loud. It is like having a personal announcer.

Piper is a free, open-source synthetic voice program. It works on the computer, without needing the internet or paying. Its quality is very good: there are voices in Spanish, Catalan, Portuguese, English and many other languages. It is the tool Manos Abiertas uses for its educational audios.

What can a synthetic voice be used for? To create audio content, to give voice to a long text that is tiring to read, to make an announcement, for a personal gift, or to learn a language by listening to sentences. It is also the basis of the educational videos we will see later.

The process is simple: we write the text, choose the voice and the program generates the audio. Then we save it as a file and we can send it by WhatsApp, put it in a video or listen to it whenever we want. It is like writing a letter and having someone else read it aloud.

Choosing the voice is like choosing a suit: it depends on the occasion. A deep male voice sounds good in a formal announcement; a warm female voice fits a greeting. Piper offers several voices per language. We can try several and keep the one that suits us best.

The speed is also adjustable. A learning audio goes slower; an announcement goes faster. Piper allows you to adjust the speed with a simple setting. It is like the pace of reading aloud: depending on the audience, you read one way or another.

An important detail: the synthetic voice does not intone like a person. It reads the words, but sometimes the pauses or emphasis sound odd. To improve it, we can add punctuation, commas and full stops, which help the voice breathe and make pauses. A well-punctuated text sounds much better.

It is also a good idea to write numbers as they are read. Instead of "1990", write "nineteen ninety" if you want the voice to read it that way. Symbols like "€" or "10%" are best written in words. It is like giving the announcer a prepared script.

The audios we create can be saved in the downloads folder or on the phone. Then we use them like any other audio: we share them, paste them into a presentation or keep them in a "my audios" folder.

The educational use is huge. We can turn a story into audio for the grandchildren, the club's lessons into podcasts to listen to while walking, or family recipes into audios you can listen to while cooking. The synthetic voice democratises the voice: any text can speak.

We must be honest about the use: a synthetic voice must not be used to impersonate a real person (for example, imitating someone's voice to deceive). For your own, transparent content, it is a wonderful tool. The ethical dimension of fake voices we will see in advanced levels.

By the end of this level, we will know how to turn any text into a voice audio. With this, content is no longer only for the eyes and starts to be for the ears: a huge step in content generation.

1. **The story for the grandchildren.** Marta turns a classic story into audio with Piper and sends it to her grandchildren to listen to before bed.
2. **The club announcement.** Pedro creates a 30-second audio announcing Saturday's hike and sends it to the hiking club's WhatsApp group.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open the browser and search for "Piper TTS" or go to "github.com/rhasspy/piper" to see the instructions. At Manos Abiertas we offer a simple guide in every class.
Step 2: Download and install Piper following the guide (it works on the computer).
Step 3: Write a short test text in a document: "Hello, this is my first voice generated with artificial intelligence".
Step 4: Choose a voice in your language (for example, the English voice "en_GB") and generate the audio by pressing the button or typing the command the guide indicates.
Step 5: Listen to the audio. Does it sound natural?
Step 6: Add commas and full stops to the text and generate again. Notice how the pauses improve.
Step 7: Change the speed to slower and listen to the difference.
Step 8: Save the audio you like best in your "My audios" folder and send it to yourself by WhatsApp to have it on your phone.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- A synthetic voice is a fake voice: do not use it to impersonate a real person or to deceive.
- If you create content with a synthetic voice, you can say it is a generated voice. Transparency avoids misunderstandings.
- Do not put false statements or statements that could harm someone into the synthetic voice's mouth.
- Respect privacy: do not turn texts with other people's personal data into audio.
- The synthetic voice is a tool: use it to create, never to manipulate.
