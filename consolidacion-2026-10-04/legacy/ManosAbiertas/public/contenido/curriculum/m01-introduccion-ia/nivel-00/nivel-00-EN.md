# Module 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — Level 00
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Explain in simple words what artificial intelligence is and what it is not.
- Recognize everyday situations where AI is already present without us noticing.
- Treat the computer as a friendly tool, just like a washing machine or an oven.
- Lose the fear of "breaking something" by touching the keyboard or the screen.
- Understand that data (what we write, say, or photograph) is the fuel of AI.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Artificial intelligence (AI) | A program that imitates some human abilities, such as understanding, seeing, or speaking, but is not human. |
| Program or app | A digital tool designed for a specific task, like a recipe for a dish. |
| Data | Information in the form of text, numbers, images, or sound. For example, the contacts in your phone. |
| Algorithm | A list of steps a computer follows to solve a problem, just like a cooking recipe. |
| Voice assistant | A digital helper that understands what we say and answers by voice. |
| Touch screen | A screen you control by touching it with your finger, like the one on a phone. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Imagine sitting at your kitchen table. In front of you is a brand-new oven. At first it feels intimidating: it has many buttons, lights, and a digital clock. But with patience, someone teaches you that one button is for switching it on, another for choosing the temperature, and another for the timer. In the end, the oven becomes your ally. The computer and the phone are exactly the same: machines that follow instructions and that you learn to use step by step.

Artificial intelligence, or AI, is a type of computer program that seems to have human abilities: it understands questions, recognizes faces in photos, translates languages, or plays music you like. But it does not think or feel like a person. It has no emotions and no intention. It is like a very fast helper that has learned from many examples.

It is important to say what AI is NOT. It is not a robot shaped like a person that will take everyone's job, nor a machine that thinks by itself. Today's AI is not conscious. It is just another tool, like a hammer or a calculator, but much smarter because it learns from data.

Think about your phone. When you dictate a message and the text appears correctly spelled, that is AI. When the phone suggests the next word you were going to type, that is also AI. When a photo organizes itself into albums by the people who appear in it, that is AI. All of this is already in your pocket, and you use it every day, perhaps without knowing it.

The computer, for its part, is a general-purpose tool: you can write, look at photos, shop, communicate, and much more. You do not need to be an engineer to use it. Just as you do not need to know mechanics to drive a car, you do not need to know computer science to use a computer. All you need is curiosity and a little patience.

Data is the raw material of AI. Every time we write, search, speak, or take a photo, we generate data. AI feeds on it: it looks at millions of pieces of data, finds patterns, and learns. That is why it is so important to take care of it and to know what we share.

A good metaphor: AI is like a cooking apprentice who has seen thousands of recipes. It does not invent food from nothing; it combines what it has learned to propose new dishes. And because it has seen so many examples, it almost always gets right what we ask for.

And why is AI talked about so much now? Because today's computers are very fast and cheap, and because there is a huge amount of data available. The combination of speed and data has made AI leap forward enormously in recent years. What used to be science fiction is now just another app on your phone.

It is normal to be afraid. Everything new feels intimidating. But fear turns into confidence when we understand what is behind it. This course is designed for people like you: with little knowledge of computers, with a desire to understand, and with no intention of being left behind.

A golden rule: if you do not understand something, ask. Asking is not a sign of weakness; it is a sign of interest. Here, all questions are welcome, and none is silly.

By the end of this level, you should be able to say: "I know what AI is, I know where I use it every day, and I know I can learn to use it." That is already a big step.

Remember: technology is here to serve us, not the other way around. The computer is your tool. AI is your helper. And you are the one in charge.

In the next level, we will discover how AI already lives in our homes, starting with voice assistants.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **At the bank:** when the bank warns you on your phone about a strange movement in your account, there is AI analyzing the spending to detect fraud.
2. **With family:** when the phone organizes photos of your grandson into an automatic album, AI has recognized his face without you doing anything.
3. **At the supermarket:** when you see ads for products you are actually interested in, it is because AI has studied your previous purchases.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1. Sit in front of the computer calmly. Take a deep breath. You cannot break anything important with a click.
Step 2. Turn on the computer with the power button (it usually has a round symbol with a line).
Step 3. Wait for the main screen, or "desktop", to appear.
Step 4. Find the icon of a browser (usually a blue sphere, a colored circle, or a fox). It is the window for viewing the internet.
Step 5. Click once on that icon and wait for it to open.
Step 6. In the bar at the top, type the word "Wikipedia" and press the Enter key.
Step 7. In the results, click on the first link. You have just used an internet search tool.
Step 8. Type "what is artificial intelligence" in the search box and read the first sentence of the article. You have completed your first digital task.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is artificial intelligence in a few words?
2. Does AI feel emotions like a person? Why?
3. Mention three things in your daily life where AI is present.
4. What is data and why is it important for AI?
5. What should you do if you do not understand something about technology?

**Answers:** 1) A program that imitates human abilities such as understanding, seeing, or speaking. 2) No, it has no emotions or intention; it only learns from examples. 3) Phone dictation, word suggestions, photo albums, bank alerts. 4) It is information (text, numbers, images, sounds) and it is the fuel that AI learns from. 5) Ask, because asking is a sign of interest, not weakness.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
AI feeds on our data. Every click, every search, and every photo leaves a trace. Taking care of that trace is everyone's responsibility. Before sharing personal data, ask yourself: is it necessary? Who will see it? At the beginning of the road, the simplest rule is to share as little as possible and to trust only known people and services. And remember: the responsibility to use technology well is always human.
