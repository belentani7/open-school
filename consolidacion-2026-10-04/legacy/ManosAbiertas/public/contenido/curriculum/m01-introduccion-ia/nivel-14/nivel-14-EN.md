## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what generative AI is.
- Distinguish between an AI that analyzes and an AI that creates.
- Know the types of content it can generate: text, image, voice, video.
- Recognize the risks of deepfakes and of fake content.
- Create an image with a free tool and examine its details.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Generative AI | An artificial intelligence that creates new content (text, images, sound, video) instead of only analyzing it. |
| Diffusion | The method used to create images: starting from noise and building the image step by step. |
| Generated image | An image that does not exist in reality and that the machine created out of nothing. |
| Deepfake | A false but very realistic video or audio in which a person seems to say or do what they never said or did. |
| Copyright | The legal rights of the person who creates a work; with generative AI they are in dispute. |
| Prompt (instruction) | The text you write to ask the AI to create something. |

## 📚 Main lesson
So far we have seen intelligences that analyze: they classify photos, translate, predict words. But there is a more astonishing family: generative AI. Instead of looking at the world and saying what it is, it creates world. It can write a poem, draw a landscape, sing a melody, or record a voice that never existed.

Think of the difference between a food critic and a cook. The critic tastes dishes and evaluates them: he analyzes. The cook combines ingredients and makes something that did not exist before: he generates. Generative AI is the cook: it takes what it learned and produces something new with it.

How does it draw an image? The most popular methods use something called diffusion. Imagine a photo covered in static, like the snow of a TV without an antenna. The machine, step by step, removes a little "noise" and clarifies the shape: first a blur, then a profile, then a face, then the landscape. In the end, a new image remains that nobody took with a camera.

The amazing thing is that the image is original: it is not copied from any photo. The machine learned what cats, sunsets, or grandmothers look like by looking at millions of examples, and now it can draw "an orange cat on a sunset over the sea" even though that exact scene never existed.

The same happens with text. We already saw that language models predict words. By chaining millions of predictions, they create texts that did not exist: letters, stories, scripts. With voice, there are systems that clone a voice with a few seconds of sample and make it say anything. With video, complete scenes are already generated from a single sentence.

That ability has a bright side and a dark side. The bright side: people without money can create illustrations, music, subtitles, presentations, or audiobooks. Creation is democratized. The dark side: very convincing lies can also be manufactured.

The best-known example is the deepfake: a video in which a famous person (or a neighbor) seems to say and do things they never did. With few tools and a few minutes of video of the person, any word can be put in their mouth. And the image, the voice, and the movement are so real that it is hard to believe they are fake.

That is why a new and necessary skill in this era is the critical eye. Some clues betray generated images: hands with too many fingers, texts that come out deformed, glasses that change shape, strange ears. But these clues disappear every year. The general rule: if something is important, do not trust only what you see.

There is also a legal and ethical issue that is still being resolved: copyright. Whose is an image created by a machine that learned from millions of human works? And what if the result looks a lot like a specific artist? There are open trials, and there are still no definitive answers. As a user, it is worth knowing and being cautious.

Another important rule: do not use generative AI to create fake images or voices of real people without their permission. It can cause real harm: harassment, lies, theft with a cloned voice. The tool is neither good nor bad; the intention decides. And you, as a person, can choose to be one of those who build, not one of those who deceive.

In daily life, generative AI is already on many screens: wallpapers, small business logos, news illustrations, background music for videos. Knowing that it exists and how it works allows you to enjoy it with open eyes.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Illustration for a business:** a bakery asks for "fresh bread on a wooden table, warm light" and gets a professional image without paying a photographer.
2. **Voice translation:** an English video is translated and "re-spoken" in Spanish with a cloned voice, making it look like the speaker masters the language.
3. **Example deepfake:** a fake video of a famous person saying something they never said circulates on social media; the critical eye avoids believing it.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1. Open the browser and go to a free image generator such as Bing Image Creator (bing.com/create) or Craiyon (craiyon.com).
Step 2. Write a clear instruction: "an orange cat sitting on an armchair reading a newspaper".
Step 3. Press the create button and wait a few seconds. You will see several generated images.
Step 4. Choose an image and look at the details: are the hands or paws correct? Is there any deformed text?
Step 5. Modify the instruction by adding details: "at sunset, watercolor style". Compare the results.
Step 6. Now search the image search engine for "real cat photo" and compare it with the generated one: what clues betray the generated one?
Step 7. Think: could a person without technical knowledge create a convincing fake image today? What implications does that have?
Step 8. Write a conclusion about how to tell the real from the generated.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the difference between an AI that analyzes and a generative AI?
2. Explain in one simple sentence what diffusion is.
3. What is a deepfake and why is it dangerous?
4. What clues sometimes betray a generated image?
5. Why is copyright in dispute with generative AI?

**Answers:** 1) The one that analyzes says what what it sees is; the generative one creates new content that did not exist. 2) It is a method that starts from noise and builds the image step by step. 3) It is a very realistic fake video or audio in which someone seems to do or say what they never did; it is dangerous because it deceives with the appearance of truth. 4) Hands with too many fingers, deformed texts, glasses that change shape, or strange ears. 5) Because the machine learns from millions of human works and it is not clear who owns the result or whether it copies a specific artist.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Generative AI is a double-edged tool: it democratizes creation, but it also manufactures very real lies. Deepfakes can harass, spread misinformation, and steal identities. Do not create fake images or voices of real people without their permission, and warn those around you that this exists. Also, respect copyright and always verify important information before sharing it.

## 🔓 Open tools
- **Bing Image Creator** (bing.com/create): generate images for free by writing a description.
- **Craiyon** (craiyon.com): free and simple image generator.
- **Stable Diffusion** (stability.ai): open image model, available on several websites.
- **This Person Does Not Exist** (thispersondoesnotexist.com): human faces generated that do not exist, ideal for practicing detection.
- **Canva** (canva.com): includes generative AI for small business designs.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
- Generative AI creates new content: text, image, voice, and video.
- Diffusion builds images step by step from noise.
- Deepfakes manufacture lies with the appearance of truth.
- The critical eye is the new essential skill.
- Respecting permission and copyright is everyone's responsibility.
