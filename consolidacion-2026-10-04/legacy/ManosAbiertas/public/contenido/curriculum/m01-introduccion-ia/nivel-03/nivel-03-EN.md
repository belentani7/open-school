# Module 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — Level 03
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Discover how AI organizes the photos on your phone without you doing anything.
- Understand what facial recognition is and how it works in broad strokes.
- Search for photos by people, places, or words thanks to AI.
- Learn about automatic retouching and restoration of old photos.
- Reflect on the privacy of our faces and those of our family.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Facial recognition | AI's ability to identify that two photos show the same face. |
| Automatic album | A folder that the phone creates on its own, grouping photos by person or moment. |
| Metadata | Invisible information that every photo stores: date, place, camera. |
| Image search | Finding photos by typing words, such as "beach" or "birthday". |
| Restoration | Fixing old damaged, blurry, or black-and-white photos with AI. |
| Automatic retouching | The light and color improvement that the phone applies to photos without asking. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Look at the photo roll on your phone. You have thousands of photos: weddings, birthdays, landscapes, yesterday's dinner. Finding one particular photo by hand would take hours. But AI has solved it for us. When the phone groups your grandson's photos into an album with his name, or when you type "beach" in the search bar and all your beach photos appear, artificial intelligence is at work.

How does it recognize a face? It does not look at the face the way we do. It divides the photo into thousands of tiny details: the distance between the eyes, the shape of the nose, the line of the jaw. It turns all of that into an "identity number" for each face. When it sees another photo, it calculates that same number and compares: if they match, it is the same person. It is like a fingerprint, but of the face.

Facial recognition is neither magic nor infallible. It makes mistakes with dark photos, profile shots, or people wearing hats. That is why the phone sometimes asks "Is this the same person?" and lets us correct it. Each of our corrections teaches the system and makes it more precise. AI improves with our "yes" or "no".

Another wonder: search by description. Type "dog" and the phone will find the photos with dogs, even though nobody tagged them. How? AI "looks" at each image and recognizes objects, animals, landscapes, and places. It is like having a librarian who has looked at all your photos and knows what is in each one.

There is also recognition of places and moments. The phone knows when it is a wedding, a beach, or a birthday, and it creates "memories": videos and automatic slideshows with music. That feature that sometimes moves us when we see photos from ten years ago is a decision of AI, not of a Google employee.

Retouching is also AI. When the phone brightens a dark photo or removes the background of an image, it is using models trained on millions of photos. And restoring old photos is a huge gift: with a torn or faded photo of your grandparents, AI can rebuild the missing pieces, remove the noise, and colorize the black-and-white image. This is technology that gives us memories back.

Think about the emotional value. For older people, photos are the visual memory of a whole life. Being able to find in seconds that photo of the first trip, or fix the only sharp photo of your father, changes lives. AI applied to photography is, without a doubt, one of its friendliest faces.

But every coin has two sides. Facial recognition is also used for other purposes: unlocking the phone, confirming payments, or identifying people in security cameras. That same technology that organizes our photos can be used for surveillance. That is why it is so important to know when and how our face is used.

Another delicate point: other people's faces. When you upload a family photo, the faces of your children or grandchildren are added to recognition systems. It is wise to ask before publishing: would they agree? Who is this photo going to reach? The golden rule: do not upload photos of minors to public services without thinking about it.

You should also know that the phone stores photos "in the cloud": that is, on the company's computers (Google, Apple), not only on your device. The cloud lets you recover photos if you lose your phone and view them from any device. But it means your memories are in the hands of a company, protected by its privacy policy.

The good news is that you have control. In the Google Photos settings, you can turn off facial recognition, delete the detected "people", or prevent the photos from being scanned. Knowing where that button is is part of being the owner of your own data.

In summary: AI has turned the photo roll into an organized, searchable, restorable archive. It gives us back the pleasure of looking back without effort. In exchange, it is wise to be aware of which faces we share and with whom.

In the next level, we will talk about the most common fears about AI, what is real and what is exaggerated, and how to lose fear through knowledge.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Finding a photo:** you type "my grandson" or "beach" in the photo search bar and in two seconds all the photos you were looking for appear.
2. **Restoring a memory:** you scan a 1970 wedding photo, torn and in black and white; AI colorizes and repairs it to give as a gift to your sister.
3. **Automatic memories:** the phone shows you a video of last birthday's photos with music, without you asking for it.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1. Open the Google Photos app on your phone.
Step 2. In the search bar at the bottom, type a word such as "beach", "birthday", or "food".
Step 3. Look at the results: AI has found photos without manual tags.
Step 4. Tap the icon of your face or look for "People" in the menu.
Step 5. You will see groups of photos grouped by each person who appears often.
Step 6. Tap on a group and check that the photos are of the same person.
Step 7. Open an old photo and tap "Edit" and then "Enhance": you will see AI's automatic retouching.
Step 8. Now go to Settings and look for the facial recognition option to see where it can be turned on or off.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. How does AI recognize a face in two different photos?
2. How can the phone find photos by typing "dog" if nobody tagged them?
3. What is the "cloud" and what advantages does it have for our photos?
4. Why is it delicate to upload photos of other people, especially minors?
5. What can you do in the settings to control facial recognition?

**Answers:** 1) It turns each face into an identity number and compares it between photos. 2) AI "looks" at the images and recognizes objects and animals without tags. 3) It is the company's computers where copies of our photos are stored; it lets us recover them and view them from any device. 4) Because other people's faces are added to recognition systems and can reach people we do not want. 5) Turn off facial recognition and delete the detected people.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Our face is one of the most personal pieces of data there is: it cannot be changed like a password. Facial recognition is useful and exciting, but it can also serve surveillance and control. Before publishing a photo, ask yourself who will see it and whether the people pictured would agree. And spend five minutes reviewing the privacy settings of your photo app: you should decide what the machine recognizes.
