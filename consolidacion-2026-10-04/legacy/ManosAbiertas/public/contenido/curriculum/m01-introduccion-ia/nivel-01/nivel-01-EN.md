# Module 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — Level 01
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Recognize the voice assistants that already exist on your phone and at home.
- Understand how they "hear" and "understand" what we say to them.
- Use a voice assistant for useful tasks: alarms, reminders, and questions.
- Know what a voice assistant can and cannot do.
- Take care of privacy when talking to these devices.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Voice assistant | A digital helper that listens to our voice and responds by speaking or writing. |
| Voice command | The short phrase we say so the assistant does something. Example: "set an alarm for 8 o'clock". |
| Speech recognition | The ability to turn what we say into text that the computer understands. |
| Smart speaker | A small device with a microphone and a speaker that is controlled only by voice. |
| Waking the assistant | Saying the magic word (like "OK Google" or "Hey Siri") so it starts listening. |
| Microphone | The small "ear" of the device that captures the sound of our voice. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Every day, millions of people talk to their phone as if it were a person. They ask it to call their son, to play music, to read the weather, or to remind them of an appointment. These digital helpers are called voice assistants, and they are one of the most everyday forms of artificial intelligence.

How does it work? Imagine an interpreter at a tourist office. You speak in English, the interpreter listens, translates, and answers. A voice assistant does something similar in three steps. First, the microphone hears what you say. Second, an AI program turns those words into text, just as if you had typed them. Third, another program "understands" the text and looks for an answer or performs the action. All of this happens in less than a second.

To wake the assistant, a magic word is used. On Android phones, you say "OK Google". On iPhones, "Hey Siri". At home, smart speakers respond to "Alexa" or "Hey Google". That magic word tells the device: "attention, from now on, listen to me."

It is important to know that the assistant does not listen all the time. Normally, the microphone is "half asleep" and only starts recording when it hears the magic word. Even so, the devices are connected and catch stray words, so it is wise to think about what we say in front of them.

What can we ask it to do? A great many things. We can ask it to call someone, to set an alarm, to calculate what 15 times 3 is, to tell us tomorrow's weather, to read a message, or to translate a word into another language. The assistant becomes a helper at home that never gets tired.

But there are also things it cannot do. It does not understand tone or jokes, it gets confused by accents, and if the sentence is not clear, it sometimes answers with something absurd. It is not stupid: it is simply that machines still do not understand context the way people do. That is why it is best to speak slowly and use short sentences.

A huge advantage of voice assistants is accessibility. For a person with poor eyesight or tired hands, speaking is much easier than typing. The assistant lets you use the phone without touching the screen. It is technology that opens doors.

Think about the difference between writing and speaking. Writing is like leaving a note on the fridge. Speaking is like asking something of a waiter. The assistant prefers that you speak as you would in real life: with simple sentences and a clear verb. "Play music" works better than "I would find it interesting to hear something pleasant".

Assistants also learn with use. The more we use them, the better they recognize our voice and our habits. Like a new employee who learns how we like our coffee, the assistant adapts to us. That is why it is best to start early and not give up if it fails at first.

A practical tip: start with single-sentence tasks. "What time is it?" "Set an alarm for 8 o'clock." "What's the weather today?" Once you master those, move on to more complex ones. Practice helps the assistant understand us better and helps us learn to ask for things clearly.

Privacy is the most delicate topic. These devices keep a record of what we tell them, and some companies use it for improvement. In the phone's settings, you can listen to the history, delete it, or turn off the microphone. You are the one in charge, and you should know it.

In short, the voice assistant is the friendliest gateway to AI. It does not require knowing how to type, it does not require knowing about apps; it only requires speaking as we always speak. It is technology that adapts to us, not the other way around.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **In the morning:** "OK Google, set an alarm for 7:30" — the alarm rings at the exact time and you do not need to look at the clock.
2. **In the kitchen:** "Alexa, how many tablespoons are 100 grams of sugar?" — the assistant converts measurements while your hands are busy.
3. **With the grandchildren:** "Hey Siri, tell me a joke" — the assistant entertains the little ones while you prepare the snack.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1. Pick up your phone and check that it is on.
Step 2. Look for the assistant icon: on Android it is usually a colorful circle or a "G"; on iPhone, Siri is activated by holding the side button.
Step 3. Say the magic word: "OK Google" or "Hey Siri".
Step 4. Wait until the screen shows that it is listening (colorful dots or a wave).
Step 5. Say slowly: "What time is it?"
Step 6. Listen to the answer. You have just spoken to your first voice assistant.
Step 7. Now ask it something useful: "Set an alarm for five minutes from now."
Step 8. If the assistant does not understand you, repeat the sentence more slowly and with fewer words.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the magic word to wake up the voice assistant on your phone?
2. Mention the three steps an assistant performs when we speak to it.
3. Does the assistant hear everything we say at home, all the time? When does it start recording?
4. Give three useful tasks you can ask a voice assistant to do.
5. What can you do in the settings to protect your privacy?

**Answers:** 1) "OK Google" on Android or "Hey Siri" on iPhone. 2) Listen with the microphone, turn the words into text, and understand the command to answer or act. 3) No, it normally only starts recording when it hears the magic word, but it is wise to be careful about what is said in front of the device. 4) Set alarms, make calls, check the weather, convert measurements, read messages. 5) View and delete the voice history and turn off the microphone.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Voice assistants keep a record of our conversations, and those recordings are personal data. Before asking for something sensitive (account numbers, passwords, health topics), think twice. Take a moment to review your phone's privacy settings: there you can view, listen to, and delete the history. Knowing that you control that data is your first defense against misuse.

## 🔓 Open tools
- **Google Assistant** (on your Android phone): free, answers in English and many other languages.
- **Siri** (on your iPhone): free, built into the phone.
- **Alexa** (app and Echo speakers): free on the mobile app.
- **Mozilla Common Voice** (commonvoice.mozilla.org): an open project to donate your voice and help improve AI in your language.
- **YouTube** (youtube.com): search "how to use a voice assistant" to see tutorials.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
- Voice assistants are AI that listen, understand, and respond.
- They are activated with a magic word and used with short sentences.
- They are an accessible gateway: you do not need to know how to type.
- You must take care of privacy and review the voice history.
- Practice helps the assistant understand us better.
