# Module 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — Level 17
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a prompt is and why it is a skill you learn.
- Learn the basic techniques: context, role, format, and examples.
- Learn to ask for step-by-step answers.
- Improve an answer by iterating: asking again, correcting, refining.
- Compare in practice a poor prompt with a well-built one.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Prompt | The message you write to ask a text AI for something. |
| Role | Telling the AI who it should be when answering: a tutor, a doctor, an accountant. |
| Format | How you want the answer: list, table, letter, three-line summary. |
| Examples (few-shot) | Showing it one or two examples of the kind of answer you expect. |
| Chain of reasoning | Asking it to think step by step before giving the final answer. |
| Iterate | Asking again while refining what you request, to improve each attempt. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Knowing how to talk to an AI is a skill, like knowing how to give instructions to a new person who starts working with you. If you say "make me something nice", you will get an improvised result. If you explain the context, the goal, and the format, you will get quality work. Learning to ask well is called prompting.

Think of a new assistant in an office. They are very capable, but they do not know you or the company. If you assign them "write a letter", they will write something generic. If you tell them who it is for, what tone to use, what it must include, and how you want it to end, the letter will be useful from the first try. The same happens with AI.

The first technique is to give context. Instead of "summarize this", say: "I am a grandfather who wants to understand this medical article; summarize it for someone who knows nothing about medicine." The model will use those clues to choose the tone and level. Context is the compass of the answer.

The second technique is to assign a role. "Act as a friendly accountant", "as a history teacher", "as a neighbor who knows the neighborhood well". The role tells the machine from what point of view it should answer. It is not magic: it is guiding how it chooses the words.

The third technique is to ask for a concrete format. If you want to compare options, ask for "a table with three columns". If you want to decide, ask for "a list of pros and cons". If you want to remember, ask for "five short sentences". The format turns an open answer into a tool that serves you.

The fourth technique is to give examples. If you expect a specific type of answer, show it one: "Like this example, but about my case: [example]". An example is worth more than a thousand descriptions, for people and for machines. It is like teaching a recipe: better to see it made than to read the theory.

The fifth technique is to ask it to think step by step. If you ask for a diagnosis of a home problem, say: "first think about what can cause this problem, then rule out the least likely causes, and finally give me the most likely cause with its solution." That reduces the risk of jumping to a wrong answer.

The sixth technique, and perhaps the most valuable, is to iterate. Rarely is the first answer perfect. The good news is that you can ask for more: "shorter", "with a warmer tone", "in another format", "point out which part is the most important". Each iteration refines the result, like when a tailor adjusts a suit.

It is also worth knowing that the AI does not remember between conversations (unless it says so) and that each new question starts from scratch. That is why it is best to put all the context in a single message, instead of leaving crumbs along the way.

A common mistake is expecting the perfect answer on the first try. Even AI professionals iterate several times. Think of prompting as a conversation: you guide, correct, qualify, and the answer improves. It is not divination; it is direction.

Another mistake is giving it too much freedom. If you ask "what do I do with my money?", you will get generic advice. If you give clear context and ask for a concrete format, you will get something useful. The precision of the question is the precision of the answer.

In practical life, prompting serves you for everything: writing formal letters, preparing questions for the doctor, summarizing documents, translating messages, organizing shopping lists, or planning a family meal. It is a universal tool.

And like every tool, it has limits: you should not ask it for personal data, yours or others', you should not trust it for serious decisions without verifying, and you should know that sometimes it makes mistakes with great elegance. Prompting improves the answer, but it does not guarantee it.

In the next level, we will see multimodal AI: machines that see images, hear voices, and understand videos at the same time.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Formal letter:** instead of "write a letter", ask: "act as an accountant, write a formal letter to the city council requesting a certificate, with a respectful tone and a courteous ending".
2. **Summary:** instead of "summarize", ask: "summarize this document in 5 bullets, each one line long, written for my mother who does not know the topic".
3. **Comparison:** ask: "compare two phones in a three-column table: price, battery, and who it is best for".

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1. Open a free assistant (ChatGPT at chatgpt.com or Gemini at gemini.google.com).
Step 2. Write a poor prompt: "write something about health". Look at the generic answer.
Step 3. Now write a complete prompt: "Act as a friendly family doctor. I want a list of 5 tips to sleep better. Each tip in one simple sentence, without technical words, written for a 60-year-old person."
Step 4. Compare the two answers: which was more useful? What changed in the prompt?
Step 5. Iterate on the good answer: ask "shorten each tip to fewer than 10 words" and see how it improves.
Step 6. Add an example: "For example, like this one: 'Turn off your phone one hour before'. Make me the other four in the same style."
Step 7. Ask it to think step by step: "before answering, think about what causes make me sleep badly at this age and rule out the least likely ones".
Step 8. Write a conclusion: which three techniques gave you the best result?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a prompt and why is it considered a skill?
2. Explain with the example of the new office assistant why context matters.
3. What does assigning a role and giving a format mean?
4. What is iterating and why is it the most valuable technique?
5. What limits must you remember when using prompting?

**Answers:** 1) It is the message we write to ask an AI for something; it is a skill because the better we ask, the better it answers. 2) The assistant does not know us or the company; if we give context, the letter is useful from the first try. 3) Assigning a role is telling it who it should be when answering; giving a format is telling it how we want the answer (list, table, letter). 4) Asking again while refining the request to improve each attempt; almost never is the first answer perfect. 5) Do not ask for personal data, do not trust it without verifying on serious decisions, and know that sometimes it makes mistakes with great confidence.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Prompting reveals what we share with the AI: do not write personal, banking, or other people's data. Also, beware of "prompt injection": a web page can hide instructions that manipulate the assistant; if something strange appears, do not follow it. And remember that the responsibility for the final answer is always yours: the AI proposes, you decide and verify.
