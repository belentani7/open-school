# Module 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — Level 22
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Copa
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level Objective
- Understand the steps to manage an AI project from start to finish.
- Know how to break a project into clear tasks and assign dates.
- Use AI as a planning aid, not as the decision-maker.
- Learn to teach what you've learned to another person with patience.
- Verify that what is taught is truly understood.

## 📖 Essential Vocabulary
| Term | Simple Explanation |
|---|---|
| Project | A piece of work with a beginning, end, and concrete result. |
| Milestone | An important marker on the project calendar. |
| Task | Each small step that makes up the project. |
| Check-in meeting | A brief session to review project progress and correct course. |
| Teach | To accompany another person so they understand and can do. |

## 📚 Main Lesson
We arrive at project management. So far we've learned to use tools, evaluate news, and design solutions. Now it's time to put them into action in a real project. A project isn't an idea floating in the air: it's a plan with tasks, dates, and responsible people. And AI can be a magnificent help in organizing it.

The first step of any project is to write the objective in one clear sentence. Not "I want to use AI," but "I want the block's neighbors to receive a weekly summary of the meeting minutes." A clear objective is the compass: when doubts arise, you look at the objective and know what to do.

The second step is to break the project into small tasks. If the objective is the weekly summary, tasks might be: record the meeting, transcribe the audio, summarize with AI, review the summary, send by email. Each task must be doable in one sitting. A task that doesn't fit in an afternoon gets split in two.

The third step is to set dates. Each task has a start day and an end day. This is called a schedule. You don't need a complex program: a sheet of paper, a spreadsheet, or a calendar will do. What matters is that dates exist and are respected. A date without respect is just a wish.

The fourth step is milestones. A milestone is an important marker: "first summary sent," "fifth meeting summarized," "all neighbors read the summary." Milestones provide encouragement: seeing progress motivates continuing. Mark milestones on the calendar and celebrate each one, even if just with a coffee.

The fifth step is the check-in meeting. Once a week, fifteen minutes: what got done? What got stuck? What needs help? AI can prepare the agenda: "these are this week's three topics." But the meeting happens between people, face to face or by video call.

The sixth step is closure. When the objective is met, the project closes: save what works, learn from what didn't, thank those who participated. A project without closure stays open and consumes mental energy. Closure is the period that allows starting the next one.

And now the most beautiful part: teaching. You already know a lot. You've climbed from Seed to Copa. Teaching isn't giving a lecture: it's accompanying. If your neighbor wants to learn to use the translator, sit with her, do it step by step, let her press the buttons. Patience is the main tool.

To teach well, use what you already know: start from the other person's problem, not the technology. Use metaphors from their life: cooking, gardening, sewing. Ask questions: "How's it going?" "Does that make sense?" "Shall we try again?" And celebrate every small win: "You sent your first voice message!"

The best way to verify understanding is to ask them to teach it to someone else. If your neighbor can explain to her husband how to use the translator, she truly understands. Teaching to teach: that's how the tree grows.

AI can help prepare teaching materials: a step-by-step guide, a cheat sheet, some exercises. But teaching happens between people. Technology serves; the person teaches.

## 💡 Practical Examples
1. **"Minutes Up-to-Date" Project:** objective, tasks, dates, milestones, weekly check-ins, and closure after three months.
2. **Teaching the Neighbor:** sit with her, let her handle the phone, use the recipe metaphor, celebrate the first message.
3. **Club Cheat Sheet:** AI generates a one-page guide with steps to make a video; it's printed and distributed.

## 🛠️ Guided Activity
Step 1. Choose a small, real project (meeting summaries, club newsletter, family album).
Step 2. Write the objective in one sentence in the first row of a spreadsheet.
Step 3. Break the project into 5-7 small tasks, one per row.
Step 4. Assign start and end dates to each task.
Step 5. Mark three important milestones on the calendar.
Step 6. Schedule a weekly 15-minute check-in meeting.
Step 7. Identify someone you can teach something you've learned.
Step 8. Use AI to prepare a one-page cheat sheet for that person.
Step 9. Stay with them as long as needed, let them do it, celebrate each step.
Step 10. When the project ends, do closure: save, learn, give thanks.

## ✍️ Self-Assessment Exercises
1. Why is a clear objective the project's compass?
2. What size should a task be to be manageable?
3. What are milestones for in a project?
4. What's the difference between giving a lecture and teaching by accompanying?
5. How do you verify the other person truly understood?

**Answers:** 1) Because when doubts arise, the objective shows which path to take. 2) It must fit in one sitting; if not, split it. 3) They mark important progress and provide motivation to continue. 4) A lecture is one-way; accompanying means letting the other person do, asking, and adapting to their pace. 5) By asking them to explain it to someone else or do it alone next time.

## ⚖️ Ethical Dimension
Managing a project with AI isn't delegating responsibility. Decisions about dates, tasks, and people are human. And teaching demands respect: don't do the work for the other person, don't judge their pace, don't use excluding language. The patience and dignity of the learner stand above any tool.
