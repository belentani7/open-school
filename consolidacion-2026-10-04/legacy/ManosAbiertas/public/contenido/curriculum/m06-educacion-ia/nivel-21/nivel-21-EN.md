# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 21
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Canopy
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what accessibility is: education reaching all people.
- Learn how AI adapts study to each need: reading, sight, hearing, language.
- Learn to ask for accessible materials: large print, with audio, with images.
- Discover inclusion: that no one is left out of the classroom.
- Create an accessible version of one of our materials.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Accessibility | That something can be used by all people, with or without difficulties. |
| Inclusion | That no one is left out: all people count. |
| To adapt | Adjusting a material to each person's needs. |
| Easy reading | Texts with short sentences and simple words, so everyone understands. |
| Large print | Texts with large size, for those who see less. |
| Audio | The sound version of a text, for those who find reading difficult. |

## 📚 Main lesson
A table where not everyone fits is not a good table. The same happens with education: a classroom that leaves someone out is not a good classroom. Accessibility and inclusion talk about that: removing barriers so that all people can learn. And here AI is a great ally, because it can adapt almost any material to almost any need.

What barriers exist to learning? Many. Tired eyes, difficulty reading long texts, hearing problems, complicated words, lack of practice with technology. Each barrier is a closed door. AI gives us the keys to open them: not always, but yes in very many cases.

The first key is large print. A material with small print leaves out many older people. We can ask AI: "Rewrite this worksheet for me in large print with wide spacing". AI does not change the physical size, but it gives us clean text to print big. It is like putting a magnifying glass over the material: the information can be seen.

The second key is easy reading. There are people who get lost with long sentences and difficult words. Easy reading uses short sentences, simple words and one idea per sentence. We ask AI: "Rewrite this text for me in easy reading". It is like the stew cut into pieces so everyone can eat it without choking.

The third key is audio. Those who find reading difficult can listen. We already know how to turn text into voice. For accessibility, we ask: "Read this material out loud to me" and we turn it into audio. It is like having the worksheet in your ears: you learn walking, in the kitchen, on the bus. The voice opens the door that the eyes closed.

The fourth key is simple language with examples. Some people understand better with a concrete example than with an abstract explanation. We ask: "Add an example from daily life after each idea". The example is the ramp that helps climb the step of understanding.

AI also translates. If there are people in the classroom who speak another language, we can ask for the translation of the material. This way, no one is left out because of language. Translation does not replace a human translator for important documents, but for learning in the classroom it is a great help. It is like putting the same table with chairs for everyone.

Inclusion is not just adapting materials: it is adapting the way of teaching. With AI we can prepare questions with more response time, activities with several ways to participate (speaking, writing, drawing, pointing) and spaces for each person to shine in their own way. Inclusion celebrates differences instead of hiding them.

Each person is a world. That is why accessibility is made to measure: we ask the person what they need, and with AI we adapt the material to that specific need. It is like the tailor who measures before cutting. No one-size-fits-all: each material is adjusted to the person who will use it.

Technology does not fix everything. A real ramp, a quiet room, the patience of a good teacher: those barriers we remove people. AI is one more hammer in the inclusion toolbox. That is why we combine technology with human treatment, which is irreplaceable.

Accessibility benefits everyone, not only those who need it. A material in easy reading is appreciated by those in a hurry, those who are tired, those who do not master the language. A worksheet with audio can accompany any person. Accessibility is like a zebra crossing: we all use it, even though some need it more.

Let us think of older people, who are our audience. With the years, eyesight and hearing ask for help. A course that does not take this into account leaves out many people with a great deal to contribute. AI allows us to build a course where age is not a barrier: large print, audio, own pace and patience. That is the inclusion we seek.

In this level we will create an accessible version of one of our materials. We will take a worksheet we made and turn it into a large-print version, an easy-reading version and an audio version. Then we will test it with an older person or someone with difficulties, and we will see how the same information reaches more people.

When education is accessible, the whole community is enriched. We lose nothing by including: we gain people, voices and ways of seeing the world. AI helps us reach out our hand. And reaching out a hand is, perhaps, the most human thing that exists.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The large-print worksheet.** Marta asked for her bread worksheet "in large print, with wide spacing, for her mother-in-law". Her mother-in-law read it without a magnifying glass and was encouraged to make bread.
2. **The audio for the club.** Pedro turned his safety script into audio for a colleague who finds reading difficult. The colleague listens to it on the bus and no longer falls behind.
3. **Ana's easy reading.** Ana asked for "the history of the square in easy reading". With short sentences, even the neighbourhood children told it.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose a material you have already created (the worksheet from level 14, for example).
Step 2: Ask AI: "Rewrite this text for me in large print, with short sentences and wide spacing". Paste the text.
Step 3: Ask for the easy-reading version: "Rewrite me the same text in easy reading: short sentences, simple words, one idea per sentence".
Step 4: Ask for an example for each idea: "Add an example from daily life after each idea".
Step 5: Convert the text into audio with your text-to-speech tool (level 16).
Step 6: Compare the three versions: original, easy reading and audio.
Step 7: Test the accessible version with an older person or someone with difficulties. Ask them if it is easier to understand.
Step 8: Save the three versions in "My materials". You now know how to do accessible education.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

2. What is easy reading? a) Texts with short sentences and simple words. b) Texts without periods. c) Coloured letters.
3. What is inclusion? a) That no one is left out. b) That everyone dresses the same. c) A building.
4. How does audio help accessibility? a) Those who find reading difficult can listen. b) It sounds better on the radio. c) It does not help.
5. Does technology fix all barriers? a) No: some barriers we remove people. b) Yes, all of them. c) None.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Ask the person what they need before adapting: do not impose your idea of help.
- Accessibility is not a favour: it is a right. All people can learn.
- Do not use AI to replace people where human help is needed: combine both.
- Protect the personal data of those who use the accessible materials.
- Create accessible materials from the beginning, not as a last-minute addition.
