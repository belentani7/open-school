# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 24
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Fruit
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what educational AI policies are: the rules of the game.
- Learn about the principles that protect learners: privacy, equality, transparency.
- Learn to write a small guide to the responsible use of AI.
- Know what centres and communities can decide about the use of AI.
- Write our own "decalogue" of ethical AI use in study.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Educational policy | The rules and principles that guide education, like the house rules. |
| Regulations | The set of written rules everyone must follow. |
| Privacy | The right that our data are not used without permission. |
| Transparency | Telling the truth about how technology is used, hiding nothing. |
| Decalogue | A list of ten principles or rules, like the ten lessons of the course. |
| Governance | The way of deciding and managing the rules, like the community council. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Every house has its rules: where the key is left, who waters the plants, what time dinner is. Without rules, the house is chaos. The same happens with education and AI: rules are needed, called educational policies, that protect those who learn and those who teach. Policies are the house rules of education.

Educational AI policies are not a punishment: they are a protective fence. They define what is right and what is wrong to do with AI at school, at the club, at home. For example: when AI can be used, when it cannot, and how to say it has been used. They are like road signs: they do not slow us down, they protect us while we travel.

One of the pillars of these policies is privacy. Students' data are sacred: their name, their grades, their lives must not go to AI without permission. The policy says: no personal data in AI tools. It is like the underwear drawer: there are things you do not share with just anyone.

Another pillar is equality. AI must serve everyone equally and not discriminate. A good educational policy says: review the biases, make sure AI does not harm anyone. It is fairness made into a rule, as we already saw in ethical evaluation. Equality is not improvised: it is decided and written down.

Transparency is the third pillar. When a student uses AI, they must be able to say it without fear. Honest policies do not hide the use of AI: they regulate it. A piece of work done with AI help is presented as such. Transparency turns the tool into an ally and prevents cheating, like the label that says what the jar contains.

Policies also define what AI can do and what it cannot in the classroom. It can help explain, practise and generate materials. It must not decide important grades or replace the student's effort. That boundary is written in the policy, so everyone knows where the line is, like the garden fence that marks how far the children can run.

Who writes these policies? The communities: the centres, the clubs, the associations. They are not only for governments. Each community can write its own guide, adapted to its people and its needs. At Manos Abiertas, for example, we have our own way of using AI, and we have written it down so everyone knows it.

A good policy is written in simple language, so everyone understands it. A policy full of strange words that only lawyers understand is useless. It is written as we speak at home: clear, brief and fair. AI helps us write it: we ask "summarise these principles for me in simple language" and it does.

A precious tool of policies is the decalogue: ten clear principles. For example: "1. I will use AI to learn, not to cheat. 2. I will not give it my personal data. 3. I will say when I have used AI..." Ten rules that fit on one page and that any person can follow. AI helps us write our own decalogue.

The decalogue is hung, read and reviewed. A policy nobody reads is wet paper. That is why it is placed somewhere visible (the classroom, the WhatsApp group) and updated when necessary. The house rules are reviewed when life changes. Educational AI policies are also reviewed, because technology changes every year.

Policies must also protect the teacher. The teacher who uses AI deserves training, time and support, not overloads or fear. A fair policy takes care of those who teach just as much as those who learn. It is like good kitchen rules: they take care of the cook and of the eater.

And the consequences? A policy with rules needs its way of acting when they are broken. But consequences must be educational, not cruel punishments. If someone uses AI to cheat, the response is to teach them to use it well, not to humiliate them. The policy corrects with affection, like a good teacher.

In this level we will write our own decalogue of ethical AI use in study, with AI's help. Ten sentences we commit to following. Then we will share it with our community, because real policies are built among everyone.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Think about the principles you want to protect: privacy, honesty, equality.
Step 2: Write to AI: "Help me write a decalogue of ethical AI use in study, in simple language, for older people".
Step 3: Review the decalogue. Do you agree with the ten points?
Step 4: Adjust each point with your own words, until it sounds like you.
Step 5: Add a point about privacy: "I will not give personal data to AI".
Step 6: Add a point about transparency: "I will say when I have used AI".
Step 7: Copy the final decalogue into your notebook or onto a nice sheet of paper.
Step 8: Share it with your family or your club and ask for their ideas. Policies are built in community.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Policies are written to protect, not to prohibit: regulation is an act of care.
- Privacy is a right: rules must always protect it, in every tool.
- Transparency is practised: always say when AI has taken part in your work.
- Policies are reviewed and adapted: technology changes and so do the rules.
- Write policies in simple language: a rule that is not understood is not followed.
