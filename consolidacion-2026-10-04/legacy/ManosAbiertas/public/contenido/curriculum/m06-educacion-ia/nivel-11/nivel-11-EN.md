# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 11
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a knowledge gap is: the hole that remains to be filled.
- Learn to use AI to detect what we are missing about a topic.
- Learn the fan of questions technique to explore a whole topic.
- Make a list of gaps and order them from basic to advanced.
- Start filling the gaps with the tutor's help.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Knowledge gap | The hole that remains: what we do not know about a topic, like the missing piece of a puzzle. |
| To explore | Going through a whole topic to see everything it contains. |
| Topic map | The outline of all its parts, like the floor plan of a house. |
| Fan of questions | Asking about all the parts of the topic, one after another, like opening a fan. |
| Priority | What comes first: filling first the gaps that hold up the rest. |
| Foundation | The base on which everything else rests, like the foundations of a house. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Let us imagine a wall with a hole. If we do not fill it, the hole grows and the wall weakens. The same happens with knowledge: if an important part is missing, everything on top wobbles. We call those holes knowledge gaps, and AI is a great detective at finding them.

We all have gaps, and that is fine. Nobody is born knowing everything. When we memorise a phrase without understanding it, it is because underneath there is a hole. When it is hard for us to follow an explanation, it is almost always because an earlier step is missing. Detecting those missing steps is half the work.

The first tool for detecting gaps is the topic map. We ask AI: "Make me the complete map of [topic]: all its parts, from basic to advanced". AI returns the floor plan of the house: rooms, corridors, doors. With the plan in front of us, we can walk through it and point out which rooms we know and which we do not.

To walk the map, we use the fan of questions. We ask AI: "Ask me about each part of the map to see which one I master and which I do not". AI goes room by room asking. We answer honestly. The rooms we cannot answer are our gaps. It is like taking attendance in class: the absences are marked.

The fan must open little by little, without leaps. We start at the door (the basic) and move inward (the advanced). If we fail at the door, there is no point moving to the kitchen: first we close the entrance. That is why AI orders the fan from easy to difficult, like the steps of a staircase.

When we have the list of gaps, we order it by priority. Which is the foundation? Which gap stops the others from advancing? We ask AI: "Order these gaps for me: which to fill first". It proposes the order. It is like repairing a house: first the leak in the roof, then the paint.

Once ordered, we start filling them one by one. With the gap identified, we ask: "Explain only this to me, from scratch". The tutor focuses on the missing piece, without distractions. It is like sewing a loose button: we do not redo the whole dress, only the button that was missing.

Filling a gap is like placing the missing piece of the puzzle. Suddenly, neighbouring pieces that did not fit now fit. That is how knowledge works: by completing a gap, things that seemed from another topic improve. That is why detecting gaps is so valuable: they are keys that open many doors.

Let us not fall into the trap of wanting to fill everything at once. Gaps are filled one by one, calmly, as the pantry is filled: one jar each week, not the whole market the same day. The study plan helps us: each study block can be dedicated to filling a concrete gap.

Gaps also appear in what we thought we already knew. Sometimes an AI explanation reveals that we understood "halfway". That is not bad: it is an opportunity. Detecting a gap is gaining a more precise map. The wise person is not the one with no gaps, but the one who knows where they are.

AI can accompany us through the whole process: making the map, asking us, ordering the gaps and explaining each one. It is like having a bricklayer who first looks at the wall, then marks the holes and then, with us, fills them. But the one who holds the trowel, ultimately, is us.

In this level we will detect our real gaps in the topic we are studying. We will ask for the map, do the fan of questions, list the gaps and order them. Then we will start filling the first one. It will be a detective and builder experience at the same time.

Remember the end of this path: when the gaps are filled, knowledge becomes solid, like the repaired wall. And a solid wall holds roofs, houses and dreams. Detecting gaps is not a defeat: it is the first step of a construction.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The missing step.** Marta did not understand an advanced cooking topic. AI's map showed she was missing a basic technique. She filled that gap and the advanced topic became easy.
2. **Pedro's detective.** Pedro asked for a fan of 15 questions about local history. He failed 5: founding, flag, ancient festivals, agriculture and emigration. Now he has his review list.
3. **Ana's puzzle.** Ana struggled to follow the economy news. AI's map revealed she did not know what inflation was. She filled that gap and the news started to fit.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose the topic from which you want to detect your gaps (it can be the one from your plan).
Step 2: Ask for the map: "Make me the complete map of [topic], from basic to advanced".
Step 3: Ask for the fan: "Ask me one by one about each part of the map to see which one I master and which I do not".
Step 4: Answer honestly, without looking at notes. Mark each mistake in your notebook.
Step 5: Write the list of mistakes: that is your list of gaps.
Step 6: Ask for the order: "Order these gaps by priority for me: which to fill first".
Step 7: Choose the first gap and ask: "Explain only this to me, from scratch, with examples".
Step 8: Note what you learned and cross out that gap from your list. Tomorrow, on to the next one.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a knowledge gap? a) The hole we have left to know. b) A machine error. c) A recipe.
2. What is the topic map? a) The floor plan of all its parts. b) A random drawing. c) A shopping list.
3. What is the fan of questions? a) Asking about each part of the topic to see which we master. b) Asking a single question. c) Not asking.
4. How do we order the gaps? a) By priority: first the foundations. b) At random. c) They are not ordered.
5. Who really fills the gaps? a) AI alone. b) Us, with AI's help. c) Nobody.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Recognising what we do not know is a virtue, not a shame: it is the beginning of all knowledge.
- Do not use AI's map to show off knowledge you have not yet filled.
- Other people's gaps deserve respect: do not ridicule someone who has a hole you already filled.
- Prioritise the gaps you really need, not the ones that "look good" to say you have.
- When you fill a gap, verify again: new knowledge must rest on true foundations.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Knowledge gaps are the holes in our wall: without filling them, everything wobbles. With the topic map and the fan of questions, AI helps us detect them. Then we order them by priority and fill them one by one, from the foundations. We are already detectives and builders of our knowledge. In the next level we will learn spaced review with AI, so that what we fill does not empty again.
