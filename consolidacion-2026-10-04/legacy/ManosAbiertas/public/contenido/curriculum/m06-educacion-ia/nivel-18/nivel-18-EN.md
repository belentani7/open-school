# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 18
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what editing is: joining voice and images into a single video.
- Learn about open video editing tools, simple and free.
- Learn to place the image track in order and synchronize it with the voice.
- Adjust the duration of each image to what the narration says.
- Export and save our first finished educational video.

## 📚 Main lesson
We already have the first three stations: the script, the voice and the images. Now comes the fourth and last one: the editing. Editing is joining the pieces, like assembling the puzzle: we place the images in order and below them we put the voice that tells them. When everything fits, we have a real educational video.

The editing is done in a program called a video editor. It is like the potter's workbench: on it the clay is given its final shape. There are free and simple editors, some we already know from other modules, like OpenShot. The editor is a place where the pieces are placed and given order and rhythm.

When we open the editor, we see horizontal lines called tracks. On one track we put the voice (the audio of the narration). On another track, above or below, we put the images. It is like a clothesline with two cords: on one hang the audios and on the other the photos, in the same order.

Synchronization is the key to editing. Each image must appear when the voice talks about it. If the voice says "the flour" and on screen we see the oven, something grates. That is why we listen to the voice and place each image at the right moment. It is like in dancing: the steps must fall with the music.

How long should each image be seen? Whatever the voice takes to talk about that scene. If a part of the voice lasts 15 seconds, the image lasts 15 seconds. The editor lets us lengthen or shorten the duration of each image by dragging its edges, like stretching a dough to the right thickness.

Between image and image, we can put transitions: fades or small cuts that make the change smooth. Transitions are like the doors of a house: they take us from one room to another without bumping into the wall. A simple fade at the beginning and at the end gives a clean, professional finish.

There is no need to overcomplicate. A good educational video can be very simple: image, voice, image, voice, and so on until the end. Simplicity is a virtue in editing. It is like a good table: no need to decorate it with a thousand things, a clean tablecloth and a well-set plate are enough.

After placing everything, we watch the whole video from start to finish. We look at it like someone tasting the stew: is it understandable? Is there any image that is barely seen? Any pause that is too long? We adjust what fails. Watching the complete result tells us what to polish. Editing is a process of trial and adjustment.

When we like the video, the time comes to export it: save it as a definitive video file, usually in mp4 format. Exporting is like taking the bread out of the oven: all the work becomes something that can be shared and saved. The editor asks us about the quality and the format; we choose the recommended option.

The exported video lives in the "My materials" folder or in the phone's gallery. From there we can send it on WhatsApp, put it in the club meeting, or share it with the family. It is harvest time: our knowledge, made into a video, travels to the world.

Making educational videos is like cooking for others: we feed the mind. When we share our video, we do not just give information, we give attention, affection and time. And whoever receives that gift can learn something useful. That is the magic of teaching.

The whole pipeline, from script to editing, can be done with open and free tools. And it can be done over and over again: each video will be easier and better than the previous one, like each stew gaining mastery. Practice, we know, is the mother of the craft.

In this level we will complete our first educational video: editing the voice and the images, synchronizing them, watching the result, adjusting and exporting. When we finish, we will have in our hands a video made by us, from start to finish. An enormous achievement, the kind that fills you with pride.

2. **Pedro's synchrony.** Pedro noticed that the boots image appeared before the voice talked about them. He dragged it a few seconds and everything fit.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your free video editor (OpenShot or another one your teacher indicates).
Step 2: Create a new project and give it the title of your video.
Step 3: Import your audio from level 16 and place it on an audio track.
Step 4: Import your images from level 17 in order and place them on the image track.
Step 5: Listen to the voice and adjust each image: make it appear just when the voice talks about it.
Step 6: Add a smooth fade at the beginning and at the end of the video.
Step 7: Watch the whole video. If something fails, correct it and watch again.
Step 8: When you like it, export the video in mp4 format and save it in "My materials". Congratulate yourself: you have made your first educational video.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Your video is your signature: review that everything it says is true and respectful.
- Do not use in your videos music or images with rights without permission: use open material.
- If the video shows people, ask their permission before sharing it.
- On health or money topics, remember in the video that the information is informative, not professional.
- Share your videos to teach, not to show off: humility magnifies those who teach.
