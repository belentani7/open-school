## 📚 Main lesson
We have reached the Fruit band, the highest one on the tree. And it is time to harvest: use everything we have learned to design a complete course, from start to finish. That is called curricular design. Let us think of a house: the curriculum is the plan that says where each wall, each door and each window goes, before laying a single brick.

A course is not a random list of topics: it is a thoughtful path. Just as a trip has a destination, a course has an objective: at the end, the student will know how to do something they did not know before. That objective is the lighthouse of all the design. Without a lighthouse, the course is a ship without a course.

How do we define the objective of a course? With a sentence that says what the student will do. For example: "At the end of this course, you will know how to make homemade bread without help". Or: "you will know how to use the phone to communicate with your family". The objective must be clear and realistic. AI helps us round it out: "Is it realistic? Can it be measured?".

When the objective is clear, we divide the course into modules. Each module is a stretch of the path, a station. For the bread course: module 1, the ingredients; module 2, kneading; module 3, fermenting; module 4, baking. AI helps us divide the topic into balanced modules, like cutting the cake into equal portions.

Within each module, we order the contents from easy to difficult. First the basics, then the advanced, without jumps. That order is the heart of the curriculum. AI proposes the sequence and we review it with common sense: is it ordered the way one really learns? It is like the lessons of this very module: they go up in level step by step.

Each module needs its own way of evaluating. Teaching is not enough: we must check that learning happened. The evaluation can be a practice, some questions, a game, a video. AI suggests how to evaluate each module without overwhelming: "What can the student do to show that they have learned it?".

The curriculum also includes practice. A course with only theory is a course that is forgotten. Each module must have activity: doing, touching, creating. In our bread course, each module ends by kneading something. AI helps us invent practices for each topic: "Give me a practical activity for this module".

Curricular design has the shape of an hourglass: the course starts wide (the introduction), narrows (the difficult topics) and ends wide (the projects and the close). At the beginning we motivate, at the end we celebrate. AI helps us make sure the course has rhythm: a good start, a firm development and an ending that leaves you wanting more.

The skeleton of the course is the table with everything: module, objective, content, practice and evaluation. AI can build that table for us: we give it the course topic and it returns the structure. It is like the scaffolding of a building: once it is up, we can lay the bricks (the materials) that we already know how to create.

Let us not fall into the mistake of overloading the course. Fewer topics, better explained, are worth more than many half-taught topics. A course must fit into the student's life: with time to practice, rest and enjoy. AI reminds us to leave breathing space in the design, like a good stew leaves time to rest.

The curriculum adapts to whoever learns. A course for children is not the same as one for older people. The audience defines everything: the words, the examples, the pace, the evaluations. AI helps us adjust the design to the audience, like a tailor adjusts the suit to the body. That adaptation is what makes the course really get used.

A good curricular design is reviewed and improved. After the first edition, we ask: what worked? What got stuck? AI helps us analyse the results and adjust the course for the second edition. Designing a course is not a one-time act: it is a cycle, like sowing and harvest that return every year.

In this level we will design the skeleton of our own course: objective, modules, contents, practices and evaluations, with AI's help. It will be the architecture of a course we could really teach, with everything we have learned in this module. The fruit of the tree is beginning to ripen.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Marta's bread course.** Objective: "make homemade bread in a month". Modules: ingredients, kneading, fermenting, baking and special breads. Each module with its practice and its final tasting.
2. **The club's course.** Pedro designed "mountain safety" in 4 modules with game-based evaluations. The club taught it using the level 20 class plan.
3. **The neighbourhood course.** Ana designed "the history of the square" in 3 modules, with a practice of interviews with the elders and a final video made with the pipeline from levels 15 to 18.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose the topic of your course and think about who will take it.
Step 2: Write to AI: "I want to design a course about [topic] for [audience]. Help me define the objective: what will the student know how to do at the end?"
Step 3: Adjust the objective with AI until it is clear and realistic.
Step 4: Ask for the structure: "Divide the course into balanced modules, from easy to difficult".
Step 5: Review the modules and adjust the order with common sense.
Step 6: Ask for each module: "Give me the content, a practical activity and a simple evaluation".
Step 7: Build the course table: module, objective, content, practice, evaluation. Ask AI to generate it for you.
Step 8: Save the skeleton in "My materials". It is the architecture of your course.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Design honest courses: that the student really learns, not just that it looks like they learn.
- Do not promise results the course cannot give: objectives must be sincere.
- Adapt the course to the student, not the student to the course: each person has their own pace.
- Include everyone in the design: think about accessibility from the beginning.
- Review and improve the course with experience: a good teacher corrects themselves.
