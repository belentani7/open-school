# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 03
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn the basic study tools: notebook, calendar, notes and browser.
- Learn to organise our notes the way we organise a well-ordered pantry.
- Discover the study notebook and how to use it every day.
- Use the calendar and reminders to plan review.
- Take the first careful steps in searching for information online.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Notes | What we write while studying, like the recipe we copy down so we do not forget it. |
| Study notebook | A notebook only for learning, with the date and a title on every page. |
| Calendar | A place to mark study and review days and times. |
| Reminder | A notice that tells us when to do something, like the phone alarm. |
| Browser | The program that lets us view internet pages, like an entrance door. |
| Search | Writing a word to find information, like asking a librarian. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Let us imagine a well-organised pantry: labelled jars, legumes together, spices in sight. When we want to cook, we find everything right away. A good student organises their study like that pantry. Study tools are not complicated: they are simple, and they help us not lose anything we learn.

The first tool is the study notebook. It can be a lifelong notebook or a note on the phone. The key is that it is a fixed place, always the same one, where we write everything related to learning. If each thing goes to a different place, in the end we find nothing, like when we put the keys in a new place every day.

On each page of the notebook it is good to put the date and a title. For example: "Monday 15th, memory". That way, when the weeks pass, we can go back and find what we studied. It is like the index of a recipe book: it tells us where each thing is.

The calendar is the second tool. There we mark two things: when we study and when we review. Remember the previous level: spaced repetition needs marked days. Writing "review today" on the calendar turns it into a commitment, like the doctor's appointment: if it is written down, it gets done.

The phone reminder is the third tool. We can ask the phone to warn us. It is like having someone tap us on the shoulder and say: "hey, time to study." Reminders are not only for studying: also for reviewing, for checking the calendar or for calling a study partner.

The fourth tool is the browser, the program used to view the internet. It is like the library door: once open, there are millions of books. But careful: in the library, not all books tell the truth. On the internet there are also good pages and doubtful pages. Learning to tell them apart is part of studying.

When we search the internet, we write clear words in the search box. For example, "how to make homemade bread step by step". The browser shows us a list of results, like a librarian bringing us books. We click on the ones that seem most reliable: those from official bodies, universities or well-known sites.

A good habit is to ask where the information comes from. Who wrote it? Is it a serious institution or a stranger? Does it match what another source says? It is like when at the market we distrust a product without a label: we want to know what it contains and who made it.

Another valuable tool is the list of questions. In the notebook, we always leave a page for the questions we cannot answer. When we find the answer, we write it next to it. AI, which we will meet in the next level, will be a great companion for resolving those questions.

Study tools work together, like kitchen utensils: each one has its role. The notebook stores, the calendar plans, the reminder warns, the browser searches. None is more important than another; the magic is in using them all.

For people of our age, digital does not have to replace paper. Many people study better with a notebook and pen, and then move to digital. We can use both: writing by hand helps memory (we know this from the previous level), and digital helps search and keep copies.

A useful detail: organise a "study folder" on the phone or computer, where we keep photos of notes, links and documents. Like the kitchen folder with recipes: everything related to learning, in one place.

In this level we will set up our own "study pantry": notebook, calendar and reminders. With this we will prepare the ground for AI, which will come in later as a helper who knows the whole pantry.

Let us not forget the social side: studying with other people helps. We can look for a friend or relative with the will to learn, and arrange to tell each other what we have discovered. It is like cooking in company: you learn more and enjoy it more.

By the end of this level, we will have a simple study system working. Technology will come later to boost it, but the system, the pantry, is ours. And an ordered pantry is the basis of any good meal, that is, of any good learning.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Marta's pantry.** Marta has a study notebook with the date and a title on every page. When she wants to review something from last week, she finds it right away.
2. **The review appointment.** Pedro marked "review vocabulary" on the calendar three days a week. As if it were a doctor's appointment, he keeps it without excuses.
3. **The virtual librarian.** Ana wanted to know when her town was founded. She searched the internet on official pages of the town hall and the archive, and wrote the answer with its source in her notebook.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Get a new notebook or create a phone note called "My study notebook".
Step 2: Write on the first page: your learning purpose from level 00 and your three words from level 02.
Step 3: On every page you use, write the date and a clear title at the top.
Step 4: Open the calendar and mark your study and review days for the next two weeks.
Step 5: Set a phone reminder that says "Time to review" on one of the marked days.
Step 6: Open the browser and search for a simple question you have. Write the answer and the page you got it from in your notebook.
Step 7: Create a "pending questions" page in your notebook and write on it a question you want to solve in the coming levels.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the first study tool we saw? a) The notebook. b) The television. c) The video game.
2. What do we put on each page of the notebook? a) The date and a title. b) Random drawings. c) Nothing.
3. What is the calendar for in study? a) To mark study and review days. b) To decorate the wall. c) To count the months.
4. What is the browser? a) A boat. b) The program for viewing internet pages. c) A fruit.
5. Is all internet information true? a) Yes, always. b) No, you have to check who writes it. c) Only the shop ones.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- On the internet not everything is true: asking who writes each page is an honest and prudent habit.
- Let us not copy information without understanding it: studying is understanding, not photocopying.
- Respect other people's property: if you use someone else's text, say where you got it from.
- Personal information (addresses, relatives' details) must never be searched or shared carelessly.
- The notebook is private: if you do not want others to read it, keep it where only you can find it.
