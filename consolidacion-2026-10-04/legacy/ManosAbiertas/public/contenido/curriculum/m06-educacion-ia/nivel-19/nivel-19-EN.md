## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Gamification | Applying games to study: points, challenges and prizes to learn. |
| Trivia | A question and answer game, like TV contests. |
| Riddle | A question with clues so you guess the answer. |
| Points | The marks we earn for each correct answer, like school stars. |
| Challenge | A test to surpass oneself, like climbing one more step. |
| Points table | The record where each player's points are written down. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Do you remember when we were little and played riddles or contests? The game caught us without effort. That is what gamification does: turning study into a game to learn almost without noticing. It is like sweetening the medicine: learning enters better when it comes with the joy of the game.

Gamification is not new. All our lives we have played to learn: songs to learn the letters, board games to count, riddles to think. The difference is that today AI makes educational games for us, on any topic and in seconds. It is like having a games wizard in your pocket.

The simplest and most useful game is the trivia: a list of questions and answers. We ask AI: "Make me a 10-question trivia about [topic]". It gives them to us with the answers. Then we play them ourselves, with the family or at the club. Each correct question is a small victory, and each victory feeds the desire to continue.

Riddles are another wonderful game. We ask: "Make me 5 riddles about [topic]". AI hides the answer in the clues and challenges us to guess it. Riddles train memory and imagination at the same time, like the puzzle solved together around the table.

Challenges add excitement. A challenge can be: "Answer 5 questions in a row without failing", "explain the topic in 30 seconds", "tell me 3 examples from daily life". Challenges push us to take one more step, like the climber looking at the next foothold. AI proposes challenges according to our level.

The points table turns the game into a habit. Each correct answer is worth a point; each completed challenge, two. At the end of the week, whoever has the most points deserves a prize (a walk, a treat, choosing the movie). The points table is like the laundry chart: we see the work done at a glance.

Prizes must be healthy and small. The prize is not the goal of learning, but the recognition of effort. Like the star they put in our notebook: it did not make us smarter, but it made us feel our work was worth something. The prize says: "you have made an effort, I celebrate it".

AI games adapt to whoever plays. For children, games with drawings and easy questions. For older people, games with questions from daily life. We can ask: "Make a game to play with my 6-year-old grandchildren" or "for the senior club". AI adjusts the difficulty and the tone.

Playing in a group is learning in a group. In the family, a trivia after lunch turns study into a moment of togetherness. At the club, a team contest brightens the afternoon. The game does not divide, it unites: everyone laughs, everyone participates, everyone learns. It is like the dining table: what is shared tastes better.

Gamification also works alone. We can play against our own record: "How many questions do I get right today?" and try to beat it tomorrow. It is like someone who runs and times themselves: the goal is not to beat another, but to beat yourself. AI can keep count of our correct answers.

Let us not confuse the game with neglect. The game is not wasting time: it is a sweeter way of studying. After a well-played game, we remember better what we played than what we read. That is why gamification is not a break from study, it is study dressed up as a party, like a salad served as a special dish.

AI games can be combined with everything we have learned. A trivia can come from the spaced review cards. A riddle game can review the vocabulary of a language. Gamification crowns our whole study system: the habit, the plan, the evaluation and the review dress up as games.

There is one risk to watch: that the excitement of the game makes us memorize without understanding. Games should be used to review what we already understand, not to replace comprehension. A game without understanding is a copy without a soul. The rule: first we understand, then we play, and when playing, we remember.

In this level we will create our first educational game with AI: a trivia, some riddles and a challenge about our favourite topic. We will play it with someone and set up a small points table. When we finish, study will have a new flavour: the flavour of the game.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The bread trivia.** Marta asked for an 8-question trivia about bread and played it with her granddaughter. Each correct answer was worth a point, and the winner chose the afternoon movie.
2. **The club's riddles.** Pedro asked for riddles about the countryside for the hiking club. They solved them together while resting on the path.
3. **Ana's challenge.** Ana challenged herself to explain the history of the square in 30 seconds. The first time she got stuck; on the third, she said it without hesitating.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose the topic of your game (one you have already studied and understood).
Step 2: Ask AI: "Make me a 10-question trivia about [topic], with the answers".
Step 3: Also ask: "Make me 3 riddles about the same topic".
Step 4: Choose a challenge: ask AI for "a fun challenge for this topic".
Step 5: Gather one person (family member or friend) and play: you act as the host with the questions.
Step 6: Set up a points table: each correct answer is worth 1 point, each completed challenge 2.
Step 7: At the end, give a small prize to the winner (or to both, if they tie).
Step 8: Save the game in "My materials" so you can reuse it another day.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

3. What is the points table for? a) To see the effort at a glance and motivate yourself. b) To waste paper. c) It is useless.
4. Should games replace comprehension? a) No: first you understand and then you play. b) Yes, always. c) It depends on the mood.
5. Who can you play with? a) With family, at the club or alone. b) Only with children. c) With no one.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Play to learn, not to show off: the victory of the game is secondary to learning.
- Prizes must be healthy and small: do not turn study into a contest of greed.
- Do not mock those who fail in the game: mistakes are part of the game and of learning.
- Create inclusive games: everyone can participate, old and young.
- If an AI game contains something incorrect, correct it: truth is worth more than fun.
