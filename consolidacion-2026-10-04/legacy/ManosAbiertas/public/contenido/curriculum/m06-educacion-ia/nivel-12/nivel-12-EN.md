# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 12
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what spaced review is and why it is the queen of memory techniques.
- Learn to ask AI for a calendar of automatic reviews.
- Learn the concept of "memory card" and how AI generates them.
- Use AI to create reviews at 1, 3, 7 and 14 days.
- Keep learning alive without starting from zero again.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Spaced review | Repeating what we learned with ever larger gaps, like watering less as the plant grows. |
| Memory card | A card with a question on one side and the answer on the other, like those in board games. |
| Interval | The time between reviews: one day, three, seven, fourteen. |
| Review schedule | The table that tells us when each thing is due for review. |
| Consolidation | The moment when what we learned stays forever, like dry cement. |
| Fading | What happens if we do not review: what we learned fades away, like fire without wood. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In level 02 we saw spaced repetition: repeating with gaps. Today we will turn it into a complete system, with AI as secretary. Because there is a silent enemy of learning: fading. What we learned, if not touched, fades away. Spaced review is what throws wood on the fire just in time.

How does fading work? Let us say today we learn a new word. If we do not repeat it, in a week it will be almost erased. If we repeat it the next day, it holds longer. If we repeat it after three days, it holds even longer. Each spaced review extends the life of the memory, like a preserve in a well-closed jar.

That is why the magic calendar of intervals exists: review the next day, after 3 days, after 7 days and after 14 days. With those four reviews, what we learned moves from short-term to long-term memory, like when we let a stew rest and it gains flavour over the days. Four visits are enough for the memory to take root.

The memory card is the classic tool of this system. It is a card with two sides: on one, the question; on the other, the answer. Paper ones have existed forever, and now AI makes them for us. We give it the topic and it makes us the deck: twenty cards with question and answer.

AI also organises the reviews for us. We ask it: "Make me a review schedule for these cards: today, tomorrow, in 3 days, in 7 and in 14". It returns the table with the dates. It is like the garden watering plan: we know which day each plant is due, and none is forgotten.

For the system to work, each review is quick: we look at the question, try to answer silently, turn the card and check. If we get it right, that card moves to the next interval. If we fail, we put it back in the short interval. It is like sorting laundry: what is clean is stored, what needs more washing goes back to the machine.

AI can simulate the deck. We ask it: "Show me the first card". It shows us the question. We say the answer out loud (this is key, saying it out loud fixes it more). Then we ask: "Tell me the answer". We compare and decide: "review soon" or "review later". AI remembers the decision for the next session.

A trick to make it easier: group the reviews by topic. On Monday we review topic 1, on Tuesday topic 2, and so on. AI organises it for us. It is like the week's laundry: one day each colour, and nothing is mixed. Ordered study is reviewed in order.

Reviews should not last long: ten or fifteen minutes is enough. If we drag on, we get tired and abandon it. Spaced review wins by constancy, not by duration. Better to review a little five times than one hour all at once, like watering a little each day instead of a downpour.

The cards can also be used with what we learn from AI. Every time the tutor explains something that costs us, we ask it: "Turn this into a review card for me". Thus, each lesson becomes cards. It is like writing down the recipe while you cook: when you want to repeat it, you already have it written.

The system has a huge advantage: it frees us from starting again. Before, to avoid forgetting, we reread the whole topic. Now, we only review the cards. It is like preserving jam: no need to make new jam every week, just open the jar and take a spoonful.

Beware of a common mistake: reviewing what we already know and skipping what we do not. The temptation is to review the easy. But the spaced system says the opposite: what costs us needs more frequent reviews. AI helps us mark "review soon" on the difficult ones, so we do not forget them.

For the habit to take root, we join the review to the study plan from level 09. On the calendar we mark the review days as if they were appointments. And with the phone alarm, the review arrives on its own. Technology does not replace constancy, but it makes it easier, like the washing machine: the effort of washing is still ours, but it has become bearable.

In this level we will create our deck of cards with AI, set up the calendar of the 4 intervals and do the first two review sessions. When we finish, we will have a memory system truly working, with which no learning will escape us.

Spaced review is one of the most valuable tools we can take from this course. Because learning is beautiful, but remembering is the harvest. And with the intervals well watered, the harvest arrives whenever we need it.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Marta's deck.** Marta asked for 15 cards on bread-making techniques. She reviewed today, tomorrow and in 3 days. By day 14, she no longer needed to look at the answers: she knew them.
2. **Pedro's preserve.** Pedro learned 10 new words. He asked AI for a review schedule at 1, 3, 7 and 14 days. Each review was ten minutes, and the words were preserved like jam in a jar.
3. **The separated laundry.** Ana separated her cards: the easy ones to the long interval, the difficult ones to the short one. That way, what cost her received more visits and consolidated sooner.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose 5 things you have learned and want to keep (words, dates, ideas).
Step 2: Ask AI: "Turn these into 5 memory cards: question on one part and answer on the other". Send it the list.
Step 3: Ask for the schedule: "Give me a review plan: today, tomorrow, in 3 days, in 7 and in 14 days".
Step 4: Mark the dates on the calendar or ask for reminders on the phone.
Step 5: Do the first review TODAY: look at each question, answer out loud and ask for the answer to check.
Step 6: Decide for each card: "review soon" (I failed) or "review later" (I got it right).
Step 7: In the second review (tomorrow), review only the ones you marked "soon" and again the rest.
Step 8: Note in your notebook how you feel: does each review cost you less? That is the signal of the system.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is spaced review? a) Repeating with ever larger gaps. b) Repeating everything in one hour. c) Never repeating.
2. What are the classic intervals? a) 1, 3, 7 and 14 days. b) 1 and 30 hours. c) Mondays.
3. What is a memory card? a) A card with question and answer. b) A credit card. c) A game.
4. If we fail a card, what do we do? a) We review it soon, in the short interval. b) We throw it away. c) We forget it.
5. How long should each review last? a) Ten or fifteen minutes. b) Two hours. c) All day.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-a, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Spaced review is not magic: it requires your effort. Do not pretend you are reviewing if you are not.
- Do not use AI cards to pass without understanding: memory without understanding collapses.
- If you share your cards with the community, review them first: your responsibility is that they are correct.
- Respect other people's pace: not everyone needs the same intervals.
- Constancy is personal: do not boast about reviews you have not done.
