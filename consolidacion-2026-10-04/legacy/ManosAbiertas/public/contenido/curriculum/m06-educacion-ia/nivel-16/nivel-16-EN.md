## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| Synthetic voice | A voice created by the machine, without recording a person. |
| Narration | The voice that reads the script of our video. |
| TTS | Initials of "text to speech": turning written text into sound. |
| Audio file | The saved result: a sound that can be listened to and used. |
| Pace | The speed and pauses when speaking, like the beat of a song. |
| Punctuation | The commas and periods that make the voice breathe where it should. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous level we wrote the script of our first video. Today we are going to give it a voice. And that voice is not recorded by a person: it is created by the machine, with a technology called synthetic voice (or TTS, "text to speech"). It is like having a narrator at home who reads what we give them, always available and never tired.

Do you remember the automatic phone voices when you call a service? That is a synthetic voice. We already used them for the audios in other modules. Now we are going to use them for our educational video. The idea is simple: we put in the script, choose a voice and the program returns an audio that says it.

Open and free text-to-speech tools exist today. Some work on the computer, without internet, like Piper. Others work on the web. They all do the same thing: turn text into sound. At Manos Abiertas we teach you the one that best suits your equipment. The important thing is not the tool, but the method.

The method has three steps. First: we prepare the text (we already have it: our script). Second: we choose the voice and the pace. Third: we generate the audio and save it. Three steps, like setting the table: plate, cutlery and serving.

Choosing the voice is like choosing an outfit for the occasion. For a children's video, a warm and cheerful voice. For a history video, a serene and calm voice. The tools offer several voices per language. We can listen to samples and keep the one we like best. The ear is the best judge.

The pace is also adjusted. An educational video goes slower than an advertisement. If the voice goes too fast, the viewer gets lost; if too slow, they get bored. The right speed is the one that lets us follow without effort, like the calm step of a walk. We can try and change until we find it.

Punctuation is the secret key to good narration. The commas and periods are the voice's breathing orders. A well-punctuated text sounds natural; an unpunctuated one sounds rushed. That is why, before generating the audio, we review the script: are there commas where they belong? Is there a full stop where the idea ends?

It is also good to write numbers and abbreviations as we want them spoken. Instead of "1990", write "nineteen ninety". Instead of "the WHO", "the World Health Organization". It is like giving the narrator the text already prepared, with no surprises. That way the voice does not hesitate and we do not correct afterwards.

When we generate the audio, we listen to it carefully. Is everything clear? Is there a word that sounds odd? If something fails, we correct the text and generate again. Generating audio is like doing a taste test: you try it, adjust it and try again until it is just right.

The audio is saved as a file, usually in mp3 format. That file is the second station of our pipeline. We save it in the "My materials" folder, next to the script. When we reach the editing, we will use that voice file as the video's sound track.

We can generate the narration by scenes: one track for the introduction, another for each part and another for the closing. That way, if we want to correct only one part, there is no need to regenerate the whole video. It is like cooking each ingredient separately and joining them at the end: more control, less waste.

There is a lovely detail: the synthetic voice can also read us ourselves. We can listen to our own script out loud before recording, to notice the mistakes. It is like reading the letter to yourself before sending it: the voice reveals what the eye does not see.

An important tip: the synthetic voice does not intone like a person. It reads well, but sometimes it sounds flat. To give it life, we can indicate emotions with signs: "What joy!" with exclamation marks sounds more animated. Punctuation and signs are the stage directions for the voice.

In this level we will run our script through the voice machine: choose voice, adjust pace, generate the audio and listen to it. When we finish, we will have the narration of our video in hand. With script and voice ready, the video is already half done.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your text-to-speech tool (the one your teacher indicates, or search for "free TTS").
Step 2: Copy your script from level 15 into the text box.
Step 3: Review the punctuation: add commas and periods where needed so the voice can breathe.
Step 4: Write the numbers in words ("nineteen hundred" instead of "1900").
Step 5: Choose a voice in your language and listen to a sample.
Step 6: Generate the audio and listen to it carefully, like someone tasting soup.
Step 7: If something sounds odd, correct the text and generate again. Repeat until you like it.
Step 8: Save the audio in your "My materials" folder. You already have the narration of your video.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- A synthetic voice must not be used to impersonate a real person or to deceive.
- If your video uses a synthetic voice, you can say so: transparency avoids misunderstandings.
- Do not put false claims or words that could hurt someone in the video's voice.
- Respect copyright: use open and legal voices and tools.
- Your video's voice represents your community: use it with respect and responsibility.
