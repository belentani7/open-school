# Module 6: Education with AI — Level 07
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what explaining is and why a good explanation illuminates.
- Learn to ask AI for simple explanations with examples.
- Learn the trick of "explain it to me as if it were..." for difficult topics.
- Use comparisons and metaphors to understand new concepts.
- Ask for explanations at different levels depending on our need.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Explanation in simple words |
|---|---|
| To explain | Making something difficult understandable, like lighting up a dark room. |
| Metaphor | Comparing something unknown with something known, like "memory is a house". |
| Example | A concrete case that clarifies the idea, like a fabric sample before buying. |
| Depth level | How much detail we want: a small brush or a big one, depending on the wall. |
| Probing question | An extra question to make sure we understood well. |
| Analogy | Explaining the new by comparing it with what we already know. |

## 📚 Main lesson
We have all experienced this: someone explains something to us with many strange words and we come out knowing less than before. And the opposite too: someone explains it with an example, with a comparison, and suddenly we understand everything. Explaining is an art, and AI can be a great artist if we ask it well.

The golden rule for AI to explain well is simple: tell it for whom and with what comparisons. If we say "explain it to me for a person who knows nothing", it will lower the level. If we say "explain it to me as if it were a stew", it will look for cooking comparisons. AI is like a translator: we choose the output language.

There is a very powerful trick: "explain it to me as if it were...". We can complete it with whatever we like: "as if it were a recipe", "as if it were planting a vegetable garden", "as if it were washing clothes", "as if it were sewing a dress". By asking it to translate the topic into a world we know, the explanation becomes close to us.

Why does this trick work? Because our memory and our understanding rely on what we already know. When we compare the new with the known, we build a bridge. It is like learning to ride a bicycle: we do not start from zero, but we use the balance we already have to walk.

We can also ask for "an example from daily life". For example: "Explain what bank interest is with an example from daily life". AI will look for a case: saving money in a money box and, on top of that, being given a coin for trusting. The example turns the abstract idea into something you can touch.

Examples are the salt of the explanation. Without salt, food is bland; without examples, the explanation is bland. That is why we can ask not for one, but for several: "give me three different examples". The more cases we see, the more faces we see of the concept and the easier we recognise it in other situations.

Sometimes we need explanations at two levels. First a quick one: "explain it to me in one sentence". Once we have the idea in our head, we ask for more: "now give me more detail". It is like getting to know a house: first we see it from outside, then we go in, and only then do we visit each room. Little by little, without leaps.

There is another magical question: "what is the main idea?". Sometimes the problem is not that the explanation is bad, but that we do not know where to grab it. AI can distil the idea into one sentence. That sentence gives us the anchor: afterwards, everything else hangs from it. Like the clothesline hook: without it, the clothes fall.

And if AI uses a word we do not understand? We say to it: "what does this word mean?" or "I do not know that word, tell me in another way". We must not overlook any strange word: every unknown word is a loose tile. Asking about it keeps us from tripping later.

We can also ask it to teach us with an "oral exam". We ask it: "Explain this topic to me and then ask me if I understood". AI explains, asks, and depending on our answer decides whether to repeat or move on. It is the best method to check that the explanation has really sunk in.

One precaution: AI's explanation is not always correct. Sometimes it simplifies so much that it swallows nuances, and sometimes it drags on without clarifying. If something does not convince us, let us ask for a second version: "explain it to me another way". If the two versions match, better; if not, let us look for a third source.

The goal of all this is not for AI to explain better, but for us to understand. The perfect explanation is the one that switches on the light bulb in our head, even if it is simple and short. A wise student prefers a clear explanation to a brilliant one they do not understand.

In this level we will turn a difficult topic into something close. We will choose a concept that has always been hard for us and explain it with AI using daily-life comparisons. Then, in our own words, we will explain it to someone: that will tell us whether we really understood it.

Learning to ask for explanations is learning to understand the world. Every time we ask for "simpler" or "with another example", we are building a bridge between the unknown and our experience. And those bridges accompany us all our lives.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Bank interest.** Marta did not understand compound interest. She asked AI: "Explain it to me as if it were a money box". Now she understands: money grows and what grows also grows.
2. **Good and bad cholesterol.** Pedro asked: "Explain cholesterol to me with an example of pipes". AI compared arteries with pipes and cholesterol with limescale. Pedro understood at once.
3. **The garden's photosynthesis.** Ana asked for photosynthesis to be explained "as if it were planting tomatoes". The light, the water and the air became sun, watering and wind: everything clear.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose a concept that has always been hard for you to understand (a health, money or nature topic).
Step 2: Write to AI: "Explain [concept] to me in one simple sentence".
Step 3: Now ask: "Explain it to me as if it were [something you know: a recipe, the garden, the sewing]".
Step 4: Ask for two examples from daily life: "Give me two examples from daily life to understand it".
Step 5: Write down the word you do not understand and ask it: "What does that word mean, in simple terms?"
Step 6: Ask for the oral exam: "Explain it to me again and then ask me two things to see if I understood".
Step 7: Answer it in your own words and check whether you get it right.
Step 8: Now explain the concept to someone in your home in your own words. If that person understands it, you have understood it.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the golden rule for AI to explain well? a) Tell it for whom and with what comparisons. b) Shout at it. c) Give it more work.
2. What is the trick "explain it to me as if it were..." for? a) To compare the new with what I know. b) To confuse me. c) For nothing.
3. What does the example do in an explanation? a) It clarifies it, like salt in food. b) It spoils it. c) It does nothing.
4. If AI uses a strange word, what do we do? a) We overlook it. b) We ask it to explain it to us. c) We give up.
5. How do we check that we understood an explanation? a) By explaining it to another person in our own words. b) By repeating it without thinking. c) By forgetting it.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-a, 4-b, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- A simple explanation must not be a false explanation: simplicity does not excuse error.
- Do not explain to others topics you only know by hearsay: check first.
- If you do not understand something about health or money, go to a professional: AI does not replace the doctor or the advisor.
- Give thanks and credit: if an explanation has helped you, share it citing where it comes from.
- Do not take what the machine says for granted: the final understanding is always yours.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Explaining is illuminating: AI can be a great explainer if we tell it for whom and with what comparisons. The trick "as if it were..." translates the difficult into our world, and examples act like the salt in food. Explaining to someone else in our own words confirms that we understood. In the next level we move to practice: we will ask AI for exercises made to measure to train what we have learned.
