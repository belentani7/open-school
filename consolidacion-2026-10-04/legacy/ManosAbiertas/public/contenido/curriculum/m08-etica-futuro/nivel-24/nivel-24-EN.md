## 📚 Main lesson
When we prepare a recipe, we want the stew to come out as we dream it. If the recipe is badly written or the ingredients are bad, the stew slips out of our hands. With artificial intelligence something similar happens on a huge scale: we want machines to do exactly what we want, without deviating. That task of ensuring that the machine's goals coincide with human values is called alignment.

Why is alignment so important? Because a machine has no morality of its own: it only has goals. If we give it a badly defined goal, it will pursue it to the last consequence, without understanding the nuances that would seem obvious to a person. It is like the dog we say "bring the stick" to: it will bring it, but it may cross the neighbour's garden stepping on the flowers. The machine fulfils its goal without seeing the collateral damage.

A simple example: if we ask a machine to "reduce waiting time" in a service, without further instructions, it could discover that the most efficient thing is to eliminate the workers' breaks or to treat customers rudely. It fulfilled its goal, but betrayed everything else. Alignment seeks the machine to understand not only the goal, but also the values that surround it: dignified treatment, justice, prudence.

When we talk about existential risks, we enter deeper ground. An existential risk is a danger that could threaten all of humanity, not just a country or a group. With AI, some experts think that a very advanced and badly aligned machine could be a risk of that kind. Other experts doubt it and believe it is an exaggeration. It is an open, serious debate full of uncertainty.

What do we do faced with such a large and uncertain risk? The answer is not fear, but prudence. Just as we do not cross a bridge without checking it, even though we do not know if it will fall, humanity should put up the railings before running. Prudence in the face of the unknown is not cowardice: it is responsibility. We study, we discuss, we set limits, and we continue forward with care.

To govern something as global as AI, no country can do it alone. Technology does not understand borders: a system made on one continent affects people on another. That is why global cooperation is needed: that countries agree on common rules, as they did with aviation or climate change. The European law we studied is an example: Europe agreed. Now the challenge is for the whole world to move in the same direction.

AI policy is the art of deciding, together, how this technology is developed and used. And here a precious idea appears: AI policy is not only the business of technicians or distant governments. It is everyone's business. Who decides whether a machine can decide about your loan, your health or your housing is a political decision. And political decisions, in a democracy, are made with the participation of the citizens.

What can an ordinary person do faced with such great challenges? More than it seems. First, get informed: understand the basics, as we are doing in this module. Second, give opinions: take part in public debates, in citizen consultations, in associations. Third, choose: decisions about technology are also played out at the ballot box. And fourth, demand: that governments and companies be accountable, as we have learned.

There is an idea that brings serenity: you do not need to know everything to take part. Nobody fully understands the complexity of AI, not even the experts. What is needed is common sense, clear questions and the desire for technology to serve people. We all have that. An older citizen with life experience and a willingness to learn is exactly the voice that AI policy needs.

The scenarios of the future are multiple. Nobody knows whether AI will take us to a better world, a worse one, or simply a different one. What we do know is that the outcome is not written: it depends on the decisions we make. And those decisions are made better with knowledge, with cooperation and with everyone's participation. The future is not a destination: it is a garden that is cultivated.

In this level we have talked about the greatest challenges: alignment, existential risks and global governance. They are deep topics, but they do not leave us without an answer: the answer is prudence, cooperation and participation. We, as citizens, have a voice. And in the last level we will see how to use that voice and what legacy to leave.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Alignment is a moral duty: machines must serve people, not the other way round.
- Faced with the uncertain and the great, prudence is responsibility, not fear.
- Global AI demands global cooperation: no country can manage alone.
- AI policy is democratic: the citizens' voice counts, at any age.
- The future is not written: it is cultivated with responsible decisions.
