## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Deontology | The set of duties and rules of conduct of a profession. |
| Code of conduct | The document that writes down those rules for professionals. |
| Confidentiality | The duty not to reveal what we know about others. |
| Honesty | Telling the truth and not hiding errors or limits. |
| Prudence | Not promising more than can be fulfilled. |
| Conflict of interest | When a personal interest clashes with the professional duty. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Every profession has its rules of conduct. The doctor swears not to harm. The teacher keeps the trust of their students. The notary guarantees that documents are true. That set of duties is called professional deontology. And now, those who work with artificial intelligence also need their own deontology, so that their work serves people.

Why is deontology needed in AI? Because those who create and use automatic systems have great power over our lives. They decide what we see, what things cost us, sometimes what treatment or what loan we are given. Such power demands rules. Without deontology, power becomes abuse. With deontology, professional work protects people.

A code of conduct is the document that collects those rules. It is like the house rules, but written and signed by the professionals of a sector. It says, for example: do not lie about what the machine can do, do not use people's data without permission, warn about the limits of the system, do not hide errors, do not put the blame on the machine. Rules that seem like common sense, but which are worth writing down.

Confidentiality is one of the oldest and most valuable duties. Whoever works with people's data knows intimate things: their health, their money, their life. The professional has the duty not to reveal them. In AI, this duty extends to machines: the data with which systems are trained must be cared for like a family secret. A data leak is a wound of trust.

Honesty is another pillar. An honest professional does not exaggerate what their system can do, does not promise miracles, and warns of errors. If an AI makes a mistake, the error is not hidden: it is acknowledged and corrected. Honesty is the basis of trust. A professional who hides faults is like a cook who does not warn that the sauce has something odd: people find out too late.

Prudence also counts. Whoever works with AI must know when not to use the system: when the data are not sufficient, when the risk is high, when it cannot be supervised. Being prudent is knowing how to say "this is not ready". It is like the plumber who, faced with a doubtful fault, calls a colleague instead of improvising. Prudence avoids harm.

Another important concept is the conflict of interest. It happens when a personal interest (earning more money, looking good) clashes with the professional duty. For example, a professional who says their algorithm is perfect only because they want to sell it, although they know it has faults. Deontology obliges you to declare those conflicts and to put people's interest first.

And what happens if a professional breaches deontology? Just as in medicine or journalism, there are consequences: warnings, sanctions, even the prohibition to practise. It is not revenge: it is protection. If a professional does not respect the rules, they lose society's trust. And trust, once lost, takes a very long time to recover.

AI professionals also have a duty to keep up to date: to keep learning. Technology changes very fast, and whoever does not train falls behind and puts their users at risk. Staying up to date is not a luxury: it is a responsibility. It is like the driver who knows the new signs: if they do not know them, they endanger everyone.

Professional deontology protects us, the users. When we use a service with AI, we have the right for it to be managed by honest, prudent and trained people. Deontology is the guarantee that behind the machine there is a good professional. And when we find a bad professional (lies, badly used data, hidden errors), we have the right to report them.

In this level we close the Canopy band. We have seen how AI is governed in organisations, how committees and audits watch over it, and how professionals have rules of conduct. All that scaffolding exists to protect people. And we, citizens and users, are the recipients of that protection: we know the rules and we can demand that they be complied with.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The honest engineer.** An engineer at Rosa's company detects that the system makes errors with older people. Instead of hiding it, they report it and propose a correction. Professional honesty prevents harm.
2. **The hospital code.** The hospital where Carmen works signs an AI code of conduct: patient confidentiality, warning of limits and human oversight. Carmen reads it and feels protected.
3. **The declared conflict.** Antonio, a seller of AI software, declares that the system is not yet tested for a delicate use. His prudence costs him a sale, but earns him trust: the company hires him again.

## 🛠️ Guided activity

Step 2: Write the four key words: confidentiality, honesty, prudence, conflict of interest. Next to each one, a sentence in your own words.
Step 3: Ask a free AI chat: "What ethical duties do people who work with artificial intelligence have?". Read the answer.
Step 4: Also ask: "What is a conflict of interest and why must it be declared?". Write down one idea.
Step 5: Write an example from your life in which a professional was honest with you by admitting a limit.
Step 6: Think: if an AI professional hides an error that affects you, what would you do? Write it down.
Step 7: Reflect aloud: how does it make you feel to know that AI professionals have rules of conduct?

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Whoever works with AI has duties: confidentiality, honesty, prudence and keeping up to date.
- Hiding a machine error is a professional fault, not an accident.
- Declaring conflicts of interest is an act of honesty and respect.
- Deontology protects users: we can demand that the rules be complied with.
- A good professional puts people before their own benefit.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Deontology is the rules of conduct of the professions: confidentiality, honesty, prudence and the absence of conflicts of interest. AI professionals have the duty not to lie, not to hide errors and to keep training. Deontology protects us as users, and we can demand that it be complied with. With the Canopy band finished, we reach the summit: in the next levels we will talk about the future of humanity.
