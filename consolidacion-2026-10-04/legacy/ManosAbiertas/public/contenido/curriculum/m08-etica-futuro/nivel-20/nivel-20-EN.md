# Module 8: Ethics, Future and Governance — Level 20
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Canopy
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what AI governance in organisations is.
- Know that companies and institutions need internal rules for AI.
- Learn the elements of good AI governance: rules, responsibilities and control.
- Learn that the people who work with AI must be accountable.
- Discover the role of citizens in demanding good governance.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Governance | The way of directing and controlling something with clear rules. |
| Organisation | A company, a city council, a hospital: a group that works together. |
| Internal policy | The rules an organisation gives itself. |
| Accountability | The duty to explain and answer for what is done. |
| Oversight | The supervision that things are done well. |
| Organisational risk | The danger that AI causes harm within an organisation. |

## 📚 Main lesson
So far we have taken care of our house, our street and our branches. Now we climb to the tree's Canopy, where things are seen from above: how organisations, companies, hospitals and city councils govern artificial intelligence. Because AI is not only used by us at home: institutions use it, and those institutions need rules. That is governance.

What is governing AI? It is deciding, with clear rules, how artificial intelligence is used within an organisation. Which systems can be used, for what, with which data, who is responsible, what to do if something fails and who supervises. It is like the rules of a residents' association: you cannot do anything anywhere; there are agreed rules to live together.

An organisation without AI governance is like a kitchen without hygiene rules. It can work for a while, but sooner or later there will be a problem: badly used data, an unfair decision, an unsupervised error. Governance is not useless bureaucracy: it is the safety net that prevents errors from becoming harm. Whoever cooks with rules, cooks calmly.

What elements does good AI governance have? Several. First, an internal policy: a written document that says what can and cannot be done with AI. Second, clear responsibilities: someone specific who answers for the systems (we saw in the Stem band that there are always responsible people). Third, control: that there are people who supervise, not just machines. And fourth, review: that the system is reviewed from time to time, like the car is serviced.

Accountability is a beautiful word: it means that whoever makes decisions must be able to explain them and answer for them. In an organisation, if an AI system causes harm, there must be people who show their face and fix the problem. Accountability is what separates a serious organisation from one that blames the machine.

And the people who work with AI? They have a key role. It is not enough for management to sign a paper: workers must know how to use AI responsibly, know when to doubt its results and who to warn if something fails. It is like in a hospital: the regulation is not enough, nurses and doctors must know how to apply it day by day.

Governance also takes risks into account. Each use of AI has a risk, and the organisation must decide how to manage it. A system that suggests films has little risk. A system that decides about patients or loans has a lot of risk and needs more controls. It is the same logic of the European AI Act we saw: the higher the risk, the greater the control. The organisation applies that logic at home.

What role do citizens have in all this? A very important one. Public organisations (city councils, health, social services) belong to all of us. We have the right to have them explain how they use AI, to know which systems operate, to complain if they harm us. When a citizen asks "does your city council use AI for appointments? Who supervises it?", they are exercising governance from outside. Society also governs.

Moreover, transparent governance generates trust. An organisation that publishes its AI rules, explains its systems and answers questions earns people's respect. An organisation that hides how it uses AI sows distrust. Trust is built with open doors, not with secrets.

In this level we have climbed to the Canopy. We no longer only protect our house: we understand that institutions must also govern themselves with rules when they use AI. Governance is the safety net of organisations: policy, responsibility, control, review and accountability. And we, as citizens, have the right to look and to ask.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The hospital with rules.** In Rosa's hospital they use AI to classify emergencies. The hospital has a written policy, a responsible person and reviews the system every year. Rosa feels calmer.
2. **Antonio's company.** Antonio's company creates an AI governance document: which systems they use, who answers and how they are supervised. Workers receive training to use it well.
3. **Carmen's question.** Carmen asks the city council: "which AI systems do you use and who supervises them?". The city council replies in writing. Carmen exercises her right to look.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take your notebook and write: "AI governance".
Step 2: Write the four elements of good governance: internal policy, clear responsibilities, control and review.
Step 3: Think of an organisation you use often (bank, health, city council). Write it down.
Step 4: Ask an AI chat: "What is artificial intelligence governance in a company? Explain it in simple words". Read the answer.
Step 5: Also ask: "What risks are there if a company uses AI without rules?". Write down two risks.
Step 6: Write a question you would ask that organisation about how it uses AI.
Step 7: Reflect aloud: why is it important that organisations have rules for AI?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Organisations have the responsibility to govern AI with rules, not to improvise.
- Accountability separates the serious organisation from the one that blames the machine.
- The higher the risk, the greater the control: that logic must be applied within each organisation.
- Transparency in public organisations is a right of the citizens.
- Demanding good governance is an act of citizenship, not of distrust.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Governance is the way of directing AI in organisations with clear rules: internal policy, responsibilities, control and review. Accountability forces you to answer for what is done, and transparency generates trust. Citizens have the right to ask how our institutions use AI.
