# Module 8: Ethics, Future and Governance — Level 07
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what misinformation is and how it differs from a mistake or an opinion.
- Learn about hoaxes and fake news, and why they travel so fast.
- Learn what fake videos and audios made with AI (deepfakes) are.
- Develop the habit of checking before believing and before forwarding.
- Know how to use reliable sources and recognize the signs of a fake news story.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Misinformation | False or misleading information spread with the intention to deceive. |
| Hoax | A false news story that circulates as if it were true. |
| Deepfake | A fake video or audio created with AI that imitates a real person. |
| Source | The place information comes from (newspaper, body, person). |
| Verification | The act of checking whether information is true. |
| Junk mail | Mass, unwanted messages, often false or misleading. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In markets there have always been people who told stories to scare or to sell. Today, the internet and artificial intelligence have put a megaphone to those stories: they reach millions of people in minutes. When false information is spread with the intention to deceive, we talk about misinformation. And one of its favourite disguises is the hoax.

A hoax is a false news story that circulates as if it were true. "Eating this food causes this disease", "this body will charge for this", "tomorrow the water is being cut off": everyone has received one of those messages on WhatsApp. AI has made creating hoaxes much easier and much more realistic. A well-written fake news story helped by AI is hard to tell apart from a true one.

Another very dangerous invention is the deepfake: fake videos and audios that imitate a real person. With AI you can make a person seem to say or do things they never said or did. Imagine a video of a relative "saying" something false: it looks real, the voice is the same, but it is a montage. It is like a speaking portrait that lies with someone's face.

Why do hoaxes travel so fast? Because they stir emotions. A story that frightens, outrages or surprises is forwarded without thinking. It is like a spark in a dry field: if the person who receives it does not put it out, they pass it to ten others, and those ten to a hundred. Emotion is the fuel of hoaxes.

AI can also create incredibly realistic fake photos. An image of a catastrophe that never happened, a photo of a person who was never in a place. Before, "a picture is worth a thousand words". Today, a picture alone is no longer proof of anything. You must always ask: where was it published? who uploaded it? does it match what serious media say?

How do we protect ourselves? The first tool is calm. Before believing and before forwarding, stop. Ask yourself: does this make sense? Where does it come from? Who says it? A message that asks us to forward it "urgently" is a warning sign: hoaxes are always in a hurry.

The second tool is verification. Look for the same story in serious media (well-known newspapers, official bodies, news agencies). If it only appears in a WhatsApp message and in no serious source, it is very likely a hoax. It is like buying medicine: we do not trust a street vendor, we go to the pharmacy.

The third tool is attitude: it is better not to forward than to forward a hoax. Forwarding a fake news story makes us part of the problem, even unintentionally. Before pressing "forward", let us think whether we have checked it. If we have not checked it, better not to send it. Whoever receives it will appreciate not receiving more junk.

You should also know that there are bodies that debunk hoaxes: the fact-checkers. They are teams of people who investigate hoaxes and publish whether they are false. We can search for "fact-checker" on the internet or ask the media. It is like asking the teacher whether that "miracle recipe" is true.

Misinformation also has an effect on trust: when we do not know what to believe, we doubt everything, including what is true. That is why fighting hoaxes is not just protecting our minds: it is protecting the trust of the community. A society that cannot tell truth from deception is a sick society.

In this level we will learn to be people who check before believing. Our life experience gives us a great critical sense: we know that not everything that glitters is gold. We will apply that common sense to the messages we receive.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Grandma's message.** Carmen receives a WhatsApp message that "raw lentils cure everything". Before forwarding it, she looks it up in a serious medium and finds nothing. She decides not to forward it and warns the group it is a hoax.
2. **The politician's video.** Antonio sees a video in which a politician seems to insult someone. A relative explains to him that the voice and lips do not match: it is a deepfake made with AI.
3. **The catastrophe photo.** Rosa receives a shocking photo of a flood. The image circulates all over the country, but Rosa looks it up in the local newspaper and discovers the photo is from another country and from years ago.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your WhatsApp and find the group where you receive the most forwarded messages.
Step 2: Choose a recent message that seemed suspicious or shocking to you.
Step 3: Write in your notebook: who sends it? does it ask to be forwarded? does it seem urgent?
Step 4: Open the browser and search for the same story in a serious medium. Type: the message's topic + "fake news" or "fact-check".
Step 5: Also search on a hoax-checking page (for example, type "fact-checker" in the search engine).
Step 6: Compare: does what the message says match what the serious sources say? Write down the conclusion.
Step 7: Decide what you will do the next time you receive a message like that. Write your personal rule in the notebook.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a hoax? a) A false news story that circulates as if it were true. b) A type of recipe. c) A birthday message.
2. What is a deepfake? a) A fake video or audio created with AI that imitates a real person. b) A horror movie. c) A fun photo filter.
3. Why do hoaxes travel so fast? a) Because they are true. b) Because they stir emotions and are forwarded without checking. c) Because AI forces them.
4. Is an image always proof of the truth? a) Yes, always. b) No, today an image can be fake and created with AI. c) Only if it is in colour.
5. What do we do before forwarding a suspicious message? a) Forward it as soon as possible. b) Check it in serious sources and, if in doubt, do not forward it. c) Forward it only to the family.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Forwarding a hoax makes us part of the problem, even unintentionally.
- Deepfakes attack truth and trust: we must point out when a video is fake.
- Before believing and before forwarding, check: calm is the first defence.
- Misinformation makes the community ill: fighting it is protecting others.
- Our life experience gives us critical sense: applying it is a gift for our loved ones.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Misinformation is false news spread with the intention to deceive, and AI makes it more realistic than ever, deepfakes included. Hoaxes travel fast because they stir emotions and ask to be forwarded. The defence is calm, verification and not forwarding what has not been checked. In the next level we will learn to protect our data in daily life, the other great pillar of the Root band.
