## 🎯 Level objective

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| European AI Act | The first major law in the world that regulates artificial intelligence. |
| Risk | The probability that something will cause harm to people. |
| Prohibited use | An AI practice that the law does not allow under any circumstances. |
| High risk | AI uses that can greatly affect people's lives. |
| Human oversight | That a person supervises and controls the machine's decisions. |
| Sanction | The fine or penalty received by whoever breaks the law. |

## 📚 Main lesson
When the car was invented, traffic rules had to be created: traffic lights, speed limits, driving licence. Artificial intelligence is also a novelty that needs rules, so that it serves people without harming them. Europe has written that rule: the European Artificial Intelligence Act, the first major law in the world specific to AI.

How does this law work? With a very simple idea, similar to cooking: not all knives are the same. A table knife does not need the same control as a saw. The law classifies AI uses according to their risk: some are prohibited, others have very strict controls, others medium controls, and many uses remain almost uncontrolled because they are harmless.

The most serious category is prohibited uses. The European law prohibits practices it considers unacceptable: for example, systems that score people's "trustworthiness" (the so-called social score), those that manipulate vulnerable people into doing things they do not want, or uncontrolled facial recognition in public spaces. These are uses that attack dignity, and that is why the law cuts them off at the root.

Then comes the high-risk category. These are uses that can greatly affect people's lives: staff selection, granting loans, access to healthcare or justice, education, policing. For these uses, the law demands a lot: good quality data, records of how the system works, transparency and, above all, human oversight. That is, there must always be a person who can review and stop the machine.

Human oversight is one of the most beautiful ideas of the law. It means that no machine should decide alone about our life. If an AI decides on a loan, a contract or a treatment, there must be a human who understands the decision and can correct it. It is like in the hospital: the machine measures and proposes, but the doctor decides. The machine helps, the person is in charge.

The law also demands transparency. If we are talking to a machine or if a video is made with AI, we have the right to know. It is not about prohibiting, but about informing: that people know when there is a machine in front of them. It is like the signs in shops: they warn of what there is, and people decide with knowledge.

What happens to companies that do not comply? The law provides for sanctions, that is, significant fines. The idea is not to raise money, but to protect: the sanction is a reminder that AI is not above people. Whoever plays with people's health or trust answers to the law. It is like the driver who does not respect the traffic light: their fine arrives.

Since when is it in force? The law was approved and came into force gradually: some deadlines first, others later. Prohibited uses apply first, and the full requirements enter in phases, to give companies time to adapt. It is like moving house: you do not fill the whole house in one day, you go room by room.

This law is a world novelty. While other countries debate, Europe has already put rules on the table. That has a precious consequence for us: we live in a place where artificial intelligence is regulated with people in mind. We are not puppets of the machines: the law recognises our rights.

In this level we have learned about the rule. Now we know that not all AI is the same: there are prohibited uses, high-risk uses with controls, and light uses. And above all, we know that the law requires a person to look at important decisions. That is the heart of the European AI Act: the machine works, the person decides.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The prohibited social score.** Carmen reads in the newspaper that a country used AI to score people. European law prohibits it: no machine can label our "trustworthiness".
2. **Staff selection.** Antonio learns that companies using AI to hire must pass strict controls: quality data, transparency and human oversight.
3. **The machine sign.** Rosa talks to an "employee" of a help website and, thanks to the law, the website warns her that it is a machine. Rosa decides whether to continue or ask to speak to a person.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The law protects the most vulnerable people from manipulation and social scoring.
- No machine should decide alone about our life: human oversight is a right.
- Transparency is ethical: we have the right to know when we are talking to a machine.
- Companies must comply with the law, and sanctions exist to protect, not to raise money.
- Europe regulates AI with people in mind: that is a guarantee worth knowing and defending.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
The European AI Act is the first major world law for artificial intelligence. It classifies uses by risk: it prohibits those that attack dignity, controls high-risk ones and demands human oversight and transparency. It is a guarantee that the machine works, but the person decides. In the next level we will talk about a very human question: who is responsible when AI makes a mistake?
