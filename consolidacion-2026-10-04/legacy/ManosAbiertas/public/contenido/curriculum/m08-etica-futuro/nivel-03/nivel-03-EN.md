# Module 8: Ethics, Future and Governance — Level 03
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 1 hour

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand, in broad terms, how artificial intelligence chats work.
- Know what data we share when we talk to an AI app.
- Learn the golden rule: do not write personal data to the AI.
- Recognize that what we write to the AI can be stored.
- Use AI with confidence, but with common sense.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| AI chat | A written conversation with a machine that answers like a person. |
| Conversation | The history of messages we write to the AI and its replies. |
| Sensitive data | Very personal information: health, money, documents, account numbers. |
| Private | Something not shared with anyone other than who it is meant for. |
| Server | A distant computer, owned by a company, where data is stored. |
| Incognito | A mode in which the browser does not remember what we have done. |

## 📚 Main lesson
We have all asked a friend for help in the kitchen: "how much salt does the stew need?". Now we can ask a machine that answers as if it were a person. That machine is called an artificial intelligence chat. We write it a question and it answers us in writing. It is very useful and very practical, but it is worth knowing how it works inside.

When we write to an AI chat, our message does not just stay on the phone. It travels across the internet to a server: a very large computer owned by the company that made the app. There, the machine thinks of its answer and returns it to us. On that journey, the information passes through the company's hands, and the company may store it to train its machine better.

This means that what we write to the AI can be stored. It is not a secret between us and the phone: it is a conversation with a company. That is why there is a golden rule: never write our personal or sensitive data to the AI. Do not write your social security number, your address, your account number or your identity documents.

It is like talking to a friendly neighbour through the window. They can advise us on the recipe, but we do not hand them the house keys or the money. AI is useful for ideas, for explaining things, for writing texts. But it is not the place for intimate data. That stays for official offices and trusted people.

Sometimes the app itself warns us: "do not share personal information in this chat". That warning is not decorative: it is real security advice. It is worth heeding. Just as we look both ways before crossing the street, it is worth reading those warnings before writing.

There is another important detail: conversations with the AI can be used to train the machine. That is, what we write can serve as an example for the machine to learn to answer better. That can be good for improving the service, but it also means our text stays with the company. If we do not want that to happen, many apps have an option to turn off the saving of conversations.

There are also "incognito" modes in browsers: pages that open without the browser remembering what we have seen. They are useful for searches on a shared computer, for example. But be careful: incognito mode does not make us invisible to companies or to the internet. It only clears the computer's history, not the data the company receives.

The AI does not know us. When a machine answers, it does not know if we are tall or short, it does not know our age, it knows nothing about our life. It only knows the data it was trained on and what we write to it. That is why the conversation should always be anonymous and general. You do not need to tell it your life story to ask for a lentil recipe.

Does this mean we should not use AI? Not at all. AI is like a new saucepan: very useful, but you have to know its limits. Using it well means using it without giving it intimate data. We can ask it anything general: how to fix a door, how to write a letter, how to explain a concept. We just avoid personal and sensitive data.

In this level we will practise having a conversation with an AI chat, but carefully. We will learn to ask questions without telling our life story. It is good training for the whole module: use technology with joy, but also with your eyes open.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The lentil recipe.** Jorge asks the chat: "how long do lentils cook?". He does not write his address or his state of health. The question is anonymous and safe.
2. **The ignored warning.** Carmen wanted to ask the chat about a health problem. She remembers that health is sensitive data and decides to talk to her doctor in person, not with the machine.
3. **Incognito mode.** Rosa opens an incognito tab on the library computer to look for a surprise gift. She knows that in this way the computer does not keep the history, but also that the search company does receive her question.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open a browser on your phone or computer (Chrome, Firefox, Edge).
Step 2: Type in the search bar the name of a free AI chat, such as "ChatGPT" or "Copilot", and go to the page.
Step 3: If the app asks you to create an account, use your email and a strong password (as we learned in the previous level).
Step 4: Before writing, think: what do I want to ask without sharing personal data?
Step 5: Write a general question, for example: "Give me three ideas to organize the storage room" or "Explain to me what pruning rose bushes is".
Step 6: Read the answer. Ask yourself: did the machine ask me for my data? Did I write anything personal? If you wrote something personal, decide not to do it again.
Step 7: Look in the app for the option to turn off saving conversations (usually in Settings or Configuration) and activate it if you find it.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

2. What is the most important thing we should not write to the AI? a) Questions about cooking. b) Our personal and sensitive data. c) General knowledge questions.
3. Why do the "do not share personal information" warnings matter? a) They are decoration. b) Because the company can store the conversation. c) They only matter to children.
4. Does incognito mode make us invisible on the internet? a) Yes, totally. b) No, it only clears the computer's history, not the data the company receives. c) Only at night.
5. Does the AI know us? a) No, it only knows what we write to it and its training data. b) Yes, it knows everything about us. c) Only if we give it our name.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- What we write to an AI chat can be stored by the company: it is a conversation with a company, not with a friend.
- Never write sensitive data to the AI: health, money, addresses, account numbers.
- Companies must clearly warn that they store conversations and allow you to disable that.
- Using incognito mode does not make us invisible: prudence is worth more than incognito.
- We can enjoy AI with confidence if we use it without handing over our life.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
AI chats answer us like a person, but inside they are company servers that can store what we write. The golden rule is clear: do not share personal or sensitive data with the machine. Incognito mode helps, but it does not make us invisible. In the next level we will review the whole Seed band and plant our small protection plan.
