## 📚 Main lesson
This is the last level of Module 3. The tree is complete: we sowed in the Seed, the Root grew, the Trunk rose, the Branch opened, we reached the Canopy and now we harvest the Fruit. Today we do not learn a new tool: we learn the most important skill of all, the one that gives meaning to everything before it. It is called judgement: the ability to use data with sense, with heart and with honesty.

Let us review the journey. In Level 13 we met the data life cycle: collect, clean, analyse, visualise and decide. Then we learned to get real data (Level 14), to suspect false correlations (Level 10), to clean (Level 11), to build dashboards (Level 12), to predict with trend lines (Level 16) and to respect ethics (Level 19). In the Canopy and the Fruit we made our own project: question, cleaning, AI analysis and honest charts.

Now, judgement. Judgement is the attitude of not believing blindly. Behind every figure there is a source: an institution, a survey, a person. And every source has interests. Before believing a piece of data, ask four things: who collected it? how was it collected? how old is it? who does the result serve? If something fails in those four answers, distrust with elegance. Judgement is not being suspicious: it is being demanding.

Numbers do not speak by themselves. An average hides differences: the average temperature of a country can hide that the north freezes and the south burns. A chart can deceive with a cut axis (Level 12). A correlation can be sold as a cause without being one (Level 10). A model can inherit bias from its data (Level 19). Whoever has judgement reads the number, but also asks what it hides.

What does an honest conclusion look like? It has three signs. First, it cites the source: it says where the data came from. Second, it admits uncertainty: it says what it does not know, without inventing. Third, it does not sell more than it has: if there is only a correlation, it says correlation, not cause. These three signs are the same DNA of your final project: the honesty we worked on in Levels 19 and 20 is the signature of the good analyst.

Your final project is your letter of introduction. When presenting it, you do not need to be perfect: you need to be honest. Explain how you chose the question (Level 20), how you cleaned the data (Level 21), how you talked with the AI and verified the numbers (Level 22), and how you chose the charts without deceiving (Level 23). The listener does not want a show: they want to trust. And trust is earned by telling the truth, with humility and with pride.

Judgement also applies to the immigrant's daily life. Every day news, advice and "opportunities" arrive by WhatsApp, on TV and on social media. A headline with a terrifying percentage, a message about a "state grant", an offer "too good to be true": all of them deserve the same question. Where does this come from? Who says it and why? Verifying before sharing or acting is the difference between being the owner of your life and being the victim of a scam.

And one last lesson from this module: data is a tool to serve, not to dominate. Whoever knows how to read data can use it to help their family, their association and their neighbourhood. They can explain expenses better, defend a complaint better, decide better with their money. Judgement with data turns the immigrant into someone who understands the world around them and makes decisions with their head held high.

The fruit of this module is not a certificate: it is a way of thinking. You will no longer look at a chart on TV the same way. You will no longer repeat advice without asking where it comes from. You now know that the machine calculates and the person decides. That judgement is a fruit that no one can take from you, and that you can teach to others: teaching how to read data is sowing judgement in the community.

And now, the bridge. In the next module, "Content Generation", we will learn to create with AI: texts, images, presentations and more. And you will carry a huge advantage: while others generate content without understanding their data, you will know what to tell, with which numbers and with which honesty. The data you have learned to master in this module will be the raw material of everything you create. Today's judgement is the guarantee of tomorrow's content.

## 💡 Practical examples
### Example 1: María's news
María sees a headline: "unemployment in your neighbourhood rises 300%". Before panicking, she looks at the chart: the axis starts at 300, not at zero (Level 12). The real rise is small. She shares the truth with her family: judgement saved her a scare.

### Example 2: Luis's WhatsApp advice
Luis receives a message about "a state grant that nobody knows about". Before forwarding it, he verifies it on the official SEPE website. It does not exist. He does not forward it: verification cut the chain of a hoax.

### Example 3: Carmen's project
Carmen presents her final project at the association. She explains her question, shows her honest chart, cites the source and says with humility what she does not know. The assembly listens to her with respect: honesty is worth more than a hundred pretty charts.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. Before believing a figure, it is good to ask... a) Nothing, trust it. b) Who collected it, how and what for. c) Only the size of the number.
2. An average can... a) Always be the truth. b) Hide big differences between people or places. c) Not matter.
3. If a chart cuts the axis... a) It is a trick to exaggerate: read it carefully. b) It is the norm. c) It cannot be read.
4. What makes a conclusion honest? a) Being very forceful. b) Citing the source, admitting what is not known and not selling causation. c) Having many numbers.
5. The fruit of this module is... a) Knowing how to program. b) The judgement to use data with sense and honesty. c) Memorising formulas.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Judgement is a responsibility: those who verify protect their community from lies.
- Do not share figures without a source: a forwarded fake news story harms.
- Admitting uncertainty is more honest than asserting without proof.
- The AI helps to decide, but the responsibility is human (Level 19).
- What was learned is for sharing: teaching others to read data is the best fruit.
