# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 08
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what an AI image generator is and what it can do for you.
- Learn to use Bing Image Creator and Canva to create images without drawing.
- Master the description of a good request for images: subject, style and details.
- Learn to adjust and refine an image until it is what you want.
- Use the images for daily life: cards, posters, social media, memories.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Image generator | A tool that draws an image from your description, as if you described a painting to an artist. |
| Prompt | In images too, the prompt is your description of what you want to see. |
| Style | The visual way of the image: photo, painting, cartoon, watercolour. |
| Aspect ratio | The shape of the image: square, wide or vertical, like the size of a canvas. |
| Refine | Ask again with more details or changes until the image is right. |
| Edit | Change something in an existing image: text, colour, remove an element. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Up to here we have worked with words. Today we learn to work with images, and the surprising thing is that we will not draw anything. The AI image generator is a tool that reads your description and draws it for you. It is like having an artist inside the phone who paints whatever you tell him, as many times as you ask, and never complains.

How is this possible? Remember the chatbots: they learned from millions of texts. The image generators have learned from millions of images and their descriptions. They have seen so many paintings and photos that they know that if you say "a cat sleeping in the sun on a windowsill" they should draw exactly that. They do not "see" like us, but they know how to combine shapes, colours and lights.

The most accessible free generator is Microsoft's Bing Image Creator, which is based on a model called DALL-E. You write what you want in a box, and it returns several versions in a few seconds. It is free, works in the browser, and you do not need to install anything. Perfect to start.

The other great tool is Canva. Canva is a design workshop: it has ready-made templates for cards, posters, invitations, presentations, social media. And it also includes an image generator. The difference: Bing creates the image from scratch, while Canva combines and organises everything in an attractive design. Two different tools, two different uses.

Let us learn to write a good request for images. It has the same three ingredients as always, with a new one: the subject, the style and the details. The subject is what appears: "a grandmother and her granddaughter baking a cake". The style is how it is drawn: "in the style of a watercolour, warm and soft". The details add atmosphere: "morning light, rustic kitchen". The more you describe, the closer it gets to your idea.

A practical example. You want an image for the birthday card of your grandson. Bad request: "a birthday card". Generic. Good request: "a friendly cartoon of a bear holding a balloon with the word 'Happy Birthday', in bright colours, cheerful and simple, in a square format". Compare the results: the difference is abysmal.

And just as with text, you can refine. If the bear's face is not to your liking, add: "make the bear smiling". If you want more colours, "more colourful". Every change is a new attempt, and the tool gives you several versions every time. Do not settle for the first result: the magic is in insisting.

A very useful trick: use the images for your daily life. A card for the great-grandmother's anniversary, an invitation for the family lunch, a poster for the residents' meeting, a profile photo for WhatsApp. The AI does not replace your memories, it helps you express them. You create the image, print it or send it, and the detail is yours.

Now, a caution, because images also have their traps. The image generator can make mistakes: hands with extra fingers, texts with spelling errors, faces that look like real people. Look at the image with a critical eye before using it. And a very important ethical issue: do not ask for images of real people without permission, or images that imitate the style of a living artist. We will see this in the ethical section.

And one last practical advice: save your good requests. As with text, a request that works is a treasure. Write it in your notebook and reuse it for other occasions, changing the subject. Your collection of image recipes will grow with you.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The birthday card**: your wife is turning sixty and you want a special card. You write in Bing Image Creator: "a bouquet of red and yellow roses on a rustic table, watercolour style, soft light, square format". You choose the one you like best, print it on a card and write your message inside.
2. **The neighbourhood party poster**: you are organising a paella for the neighbours. You open Canva, choose the "poster" template, write "Neighbourhood paella, Saturday 12 o'clock" and add a generated image: "a rustic paella on a wooden table, warm light". You print it and hang it in the building.
3. **The photo for the WhatsApp group**: you want a discreet but personal profile image. You generate: "a simple watercolour of a small house with a garden and a cat on the doorstep, calm and warm colours". It does not show your face, but it says who you are.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Open Bing Image Creator (bing.com/create) in your browser. It asks you to log in with a Microsoft account.
2. In the box, write a simple subject: "a cat sleeping on a cushion". Press create.
3. Look at the four versions. Do they look like what you imagined?
4. Now improve the request with the three ingredients: "a grey cat sleeping on a blue cushion next to a window, morning light, in the style of a soft painting".
5. Create again and compare with the previous versions.
6. Refine: add a detail, for example "the cat has a small bell on its collar", and create again.
7. Download the best image to your phone or computer.
8. Open Canva (canva.com), choose a card template and put your image on it with a title of your choice.
9. Write in your notebook the good request you have discovered, to reuse it.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Images also carry responsibility. Do not generate images of real people without their consent, or images that could humiliate or deceive. Be careful with "deepfakes": an image that looks like a photo may be invented. And about copyright: a style inspired by a living artist without permission is a moral and legal problem. Use the generator to express your ideas, with respect for the people and the artists. And remember: an image is a power, and every power comes with responsibility.
