# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 09
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Consolidate everything learned about prompts in a clear and complete method.
- Master the five golden rules of a good prompt.
- Learn to avoid the most common mistakes when asking.
- Practise with text, images and translators using the same method.
- Create your personal prompt book with the recipes that work for you.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Method | A way of doing things in order, step by step, so it always works. |
| Golden rule | A basic advice that never fails, like "measure twice, cut once". |
| Concrete | A precise description, with names and details, not vague. |
| Example | A model of what you want, so the machine imitates it, like showing a pattern to a seamstress. |
| Constraint | A limit you set: length, format, audience, time. |
| Iterate | Try, adjust and try again, like a potter shaping clay. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the last levels we have learned many things. Today we put it all in order. We are going to learn a method, a way of doing things that always works, whether you are asking a chatbot for a letter, an image generator for a drawing or a translator for a version. The method has five golden rules, and whoever masters them masters the machine.

The first golden rule: be concrete. It is the mother of all rules. A vague prompt gives a vague answer. "Help me write something" does not work. "Write a message to the bank asking for the statement for last month, formal tone, four lines" works. The machine needs to know exactly what you want, like a carpenter who needs your measurements to make a table.

The second golden rule: give context. The machine does not know your life. If you ask "what do I do?", it does not know who you are. Give it the situation: "I am a retired woman, I have a granddaughter of seven years, and I want to prepare a game for her birthday." With context, the answers fit you. Without it, they are generic. Context is the frame of the picture.

The third golden rule: give examples. If you want a specific style, show it. "Write it in the style of the previous message I sent you", or "write a message like this one: [example]". The machine is an excellent imitator: with a good example, it copies the tone and the shape. It is like showing a pattern to a seamstress: you do not describe the dress, you show it.

The fourth golden rule: set limits. Say how long, what format, for whom. "Maximum ten lines", "in a table", "for my mother who does not understand technology". The limits are the fences that keep the answer inside your garden. Without limits, the answer grows everywhere. With limits, it grows where you want it.

The fifth golden rule: iterate. The first answer is rarely the best. Ask for changes: "shorter", "with more examples", "different tone". Each attempt improves. The machine does not get tired, and neither do you: you have the right to keep trying until it is right. The potter does not throw the clay away at the first turn of the wheel; he adjusts it.

Now, the most common mistakes, so you do not fall into them. The first: ask everything in one message. A huge prompt gives a confusing answer. Split: first "explain the idea", then "write the text", then "check it". Step by step, like assembling a piece of furniture. The second mistake: not reading the answer. Many people ask and do not look. Read it: it is the only way to know if you have to insist. The third mistake: repeating the same prompt expecting a different result. If it did not work, change something, do not repeat.

There is a bonus rule, the one that gives power: ask for your own recipe. At the end of the level you will have your prompt book, with the recipes that work for you. Each recipe is a prompt that has served you and that you save with its name. "Recipe: pension letter", "Recipe: granddaughter's birthday image". Your book grows with you, and one day you will be the one teaching the method to others.

Let us apply the method in the three worlds we know. In the text: "Act as a consumer advisor. Write a complaint letter about a broken appliance. Formal, ten lines." In the images: "A vase of sunflowers on a wooden table, impressionist style, warm light, square format." In the translation: "Translate this recipe into Portuguese, keeping the tone simple and warm." Same method, five rules, three worlds.

And remember why this method exists: not to complicate, but to save you time. A good prompt is the difference between an hour of frustration and five minutes of work. The machine is there to help you, but it needs to be told well. You are the boss: the better you give the orders, the better the work comes out.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The letter to the landlord**: there is a leak in the kitchen. You write: "Act as a tenant advisor. Write a polite but firm letter to the landlord explaining that there is a leak in the kitchen and asking for it to be fixed urgently. Maximum ten lines." Result: a letter that gets a response.
2. **The family menu**: Sunday, the whole family at home. You write: "Make me a Sunday menu for eight people, with a starter, a main course and a dessert, traditional food, simple to cook and with a budget of fifty euros. Format: list." With context and limits, the answer is practical.
3. **The image for the recipe**: you have discovered a recipe and want to put it in a nice document. You write in the image generator: "A bowl of lentils with vegetables on a rustic table, watercolour style, soft light, square format." You put it on the first page of your recipe book.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Open your notebook and title a page: "My prompt book".
2. Write the five golden rules in your own words: be concrete, give context, give examples, set limits, iterate.
3. Choose a real problem of your life: a letter, a menu, an image, a translation.
4. Write a prompt with the five rules for that problem, and test it in the corresponding tool.
5. Apply the fifth rule: ask for a change ("shorter", "different tone") and compare.
6. When the answer satisfies you, save the prompt in your book with its name.
7. Repeat with a second problem in another tool (text or images).
8. Read your two recipes and improve them: what would you add?
9. Commit to a habit: before asking, check the five rules in your head.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
A powerful method can be used for good or for bad. The same five rules serve to write a loving letter or to invent a deception. You choose the purpose, and you are responsible for it. Never use the method to deceive, manipulate or harm people. Also remember that a good prompt does not guarantee a true answer: it improves the form, not the fact. The final responsibility for verifying, deciding and acting is always yours.
