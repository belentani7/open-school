# Módulo 2: Herramientas sin Código — Nivel 23
## Idioma: EN · Dificultad: Fruto
## Tiempo estimado: 3 horas

## 🎯 Objetivo del nivel
- Understand what a workflow is and how it saves effort in your day.
- Learn to connect your tools so that they work for you: IFTTT, Make and Zapier.
- Create automations for the repetitive tasks of work and paperwork.
- Use ready-made templates from the community instead of starting from scratch.
- Deliver your work on time, without forgetfulness and with less fatigue.

## 📖 Vocabulario esencial
| Término | Explicación en palabras simples |
|---|---|
| Workflow | A series of connected steps that do a task alone, like an assembly line. |
| Applet | In IFTTT, a simple automation: if this happens, then do that. |
| Scenario | In Make, the name of the visual flow that connects modules. |
| Module | Each step of the flow in Make: read, save, send, alert. |
| Template | A flow already made by another person that you can copy and adapt. |
| Connector | The piece that joins one app with another: Gmail, Drive, WhatsApp. |

## 📚 Lección principal
In level 22 you put automation at the service of your home: invoices, birthdays, agenda. The tree is beginning to bear fruit. Today we take that same strength to your work, because many of us work in shifts, in hospitality, in construction, in cleaning or in caring for people. And in that work there are tasks that repeat: checking the schedule, alerting the family, asking for a day off, saving the payslip, booking an appointment with the SEPE.

A workflow is an assembly line: the tasks pass from one tool to another and at the end the result comes out, without you being at every step. You design the line once; it works every day. It is the same idea as level 22 —trigger, action— but now the actions are several and they work in a chain, connecting apps.

IFTTT is the simplest. Its name says it all: "If This Then That". You create an applet: if an email with a PDF arrives, then save that PDF in a folder. Or: if the work group posts the schedule, then alert me. Each applet is a small promise that the machine keeps for you.

Make is more powerful. You draw a scenario on the screen: one module reads, another decides, another saves, another alerts. You connect them with lines and the apps pass the work from one to the next. You do not write a single line of programming: you drag, choose and test. It seems complicated at first, but the templates make it easy.

Zapier is another very well-known connector, with thousands of apps. The rule is the same: something happens and something gets done. The difference between IFTTT, Make and Zapier is not important to start: choose one, learn to use it well, and the rest will seem almost the same.

Templates are your best friend. When you want to create a flow, do not start from zero: search in the template library of the program for "work schedule" or "save files from an email" or "appointment reminder". Someone has done it before you, and has shared it. You copy it, adapt it to your accounts and your schedule, and in ten minutes it is ready.

Let us look at examples from the real life of a worker in Spain. The "shift alert": the company posts the schedule in the WhatsApp group; the flow detects it, saves it in your "Shifts" folder and sends you an alert. The "SEPE appointment": when you get an appointment, the flow reminds you the day before and two hours before. The "saved payslip": the email with the payslip in PDF is saved by itself in the "Payslips" folder, so you always have it for the tax return or to ask for a loan.

Two rules so that the fire does not burn you. First: automate the mechanical, not the decisions. A flow can save, alert and copy, but it does not decide for you or speak for you: the important decisions are still human. Second: start small and test a lot, as in level 22. One automation that works is worth more than ten that fail.

And think about what you win back. Every repetitive task that the flow does for you is a moment less of fatigue and a moment more of life: dinner with your children, the call to your family in your country, the Sunday rest. The work does not disappear; but it stops eating your time.

In the next level, the assistants and the agents: tools that do not only do tasks, but think and act with you, for your paperwork, your work and your family.

## 💡 Ejemplos prácticos
1. **The shift alert:** the company posts the schedule → the flow saves it in your folder → alert on your mobile. Zero surprises.
2. **The SEPE appointment:** you get an appointment → the flow reminds you the day before and two hours before. Never miss one again.
3. **The saved payslip:** the email with the payslip arrives → it is saved by itself in "Payslips" → you have it for the whole year.

## 🛠️ Actividad guiada
Paso 1. Write in your notebook three tasks of your work that repeat (alert, save, remember, copy).
Paso 2. Choose one and divide it into steps: what happens first, what is done after.
Paso 3. Open IFTTT or Make (the one you chose) and look for its template library.
Paso 4. Look for a template similar to your task and copy it.
Paso 5. Connect your accounts: the app will ask for permission. Read what it asks and give only what is necessary.
Paso 6. Adapt the template to your case: your email, your folder, your schedule.
Paso 7. Test it with a real case (the "Run" or "Test" button). Check if it worked.
Paso 8. Fix what fails and test again until it works 5 times in a row.
Paso 9. Let it run for a week and check the log every day. Write down what you have gained.

## ⚖️ Dimensión ética
- Automate your things and those of your community with permission: do not connect other people's accounts without their consent.
- Check what the flow sends and to whom: an automatic alert that bothers is an annoyance that you have created.
- Protect your papers: payslips and personal data are kept with care and never shared without a reason.
- A flow does not decide for you: the important decisions are still human.
- If a flow that others use is going to change or disappear, warn them beforehand.

## 🧠 Resumen y puente
A workflow joins your tools so that repetitive work gets done by itself: shifts, appointments, payslips and alerts, with IFTTT, Make or Zapier and shared templates. You start small, test a lot and protect your data, and you win back the time that mechanical work was taking from you.
