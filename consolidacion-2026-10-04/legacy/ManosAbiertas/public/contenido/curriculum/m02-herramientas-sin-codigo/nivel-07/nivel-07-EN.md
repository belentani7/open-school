# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 07
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what an AI translator is and how it has improved over the years.
- Learn to translate texts, messages and documents between the languages of the course.
- Discover the free tools: DeepL, Google Translate and others.
- Learn to translate with the chatbots, asking for the tone you want.
- Practise verifying a translation and knowing when a human is still needed.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Translation | Changing a text from one language to another without changing the meaning. |
| Source language | The language in which the text is written originally. |
| Target language | The language you want the text to be in. |
| Translator | The tool that does the translation, like a multilingual assistant. |
| Context | The situation around the words; it helps to translate better, like knowing whether "bank" means river or money. |
| Review | Reading the translated text to check that it says what you want. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Today we cross a border without moving from home: we learn to translate with AI. In our course we work with four languages, Spanish, Portuguese, Catalan and English, and in our families there are relatives who speak one or another. Translating well is a gift that brings the family together, and AI has turned it into something that fits in your pocket.

Many years ago, to translate a letter you had to look for a person who knew the language. Then came the first automatic translators, and they were a joke: they translated word by word and produced nonsense. The same sentence, said and done wrong. That time is over. The translators of today, powered by artificial intelligence, understand the meaning and translate as a person would. The change is enormous.

How do they work? Like the chatbots we already know: they have learned from millions of texts in many languages, and they know that a sentence does not translate word by word but idea by idea. They know, for example, that "how are you" in Portuguese is "como estás" and not "como és você". They understand the meaning, and that is why the result is natural.

The most famous free translator is Google Translate, but there is another that many prefer for quality: DeepL. Both are free and work on the web and on the phone. You write or paste a text, choose the language you want, and in a second you have the translation. Also, if the translation is long, they have a function to translate whole documents: you upload the file and it comes back translated.

The trick to a good translation is to choose well the source language and the target language. You can let the tool guess the source, but it is better to tell it: it avoids confusion between similar languages, such as Portuguese and Spanish. And remember that the tools also let you translate from the voice: press the microphone, speak, and it translates what you said out loud. Useful for a conversation with the son-in-law who speaks English.

Now, the most valuable skill: translating with the chatbots. Why? Because a chatbot, apart from translating, respects the tone. Ask it: "Translate this letter into Catalan, keeping it formal and affectionate." The chatbot does not only translate words, it adapts the tone, which is exactly what you want when you write to a person. DeepL translates correctly; the chatbot translates and styles.

A practical example. You have a message from your daughter's school in English. You paste it into the chatbot and say: "Translate this message into Spanish. It is a note from my daughter's school. Use a clear and simple language." The answer will not only tell you what it says, but it will explain the important things to you. That is what a good translator does: it gives you peace of mind.

But be careful, and this is the lesson of the level: not everything translates with a machine. Family letters, emotions, humour, technical documents or legal ones often need a human. AI is excellent, but it can miss a nuance or a cultural detail. The rule is simple: for everyday things, machine translation is enough; for important things, always a review, and if it is very important, a human.

There is also a magical function that is now very useful: simultaneous translation in conversations. Some tools let you speak in your language and the other person hears it in theirs, in real time. It is not perfect yet, but for a trip or a visit it breaks down the wall. We will practise the simple version: text and voice.

And an idea to take home: use the translators to learn, not only to communicate. When you translate an English word and you do not know it, write it in your notebook. Every day, three words. In a year you will have a thousand. The translator is not only a bridge, it is also a teacher.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The news in English**: you read the headline of an English newspaper and you are curious. You copy the text, paste it into DeepL and select English to Spanish. In two seconds you understand the whole article.
2. **The message to the daughter-in-law**: your son's wife is Brazilian and speaks Portuguese. You ask the chatbot: "Write a WhatsApp message to my daughter-in-law inviting her and my son to Sunday lunch, affectionate and simple, in Portuguese." You copy it and send it. The family comes closer.
3. **The brochure from the trip**: they give you a leaflet in Catalan at the tourist office. You open Google Translate, choose the camera function, point it at the paper, and the mobile translates the text live. You read it without asking anyone.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Open DeepL (deepl.com) or Google Translate (translate.google.com).
2. In the left box, write a sentence from your life: "Tomorrow I have to go to the health centre for a blood test."
3. Choose the target language: Portuguese. Look at the translation. Does it say what you said?
4. Change the target language to English and then to Catalan. Compare how each language sounds.
5. Now open your chatbot and write: "Translate this sentence into English, keeping it natural and simple: 'The grandchildren are coming on Sunday and I want to prepare a good lunch.'"
6. Ask it to translate the same sentence into Portuguese in a more affectionate tone. Compare both versions.
7. Practise the voice: press the microphone in Google Translate and say a sentence in your language, choosing English as the target.
8. Write in your notebook three new words you learned today from the translations.
9. Reflect: in which situations of your life will translation help you most?

In very important matters, emotional, legal or technical texts, where a nuance can change everything.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Translation is a door, but it can also be a trap. A poorly translated document can cause problems: a medical consent, a contract, a bank form. Do not sign anything important with a machine translation without showing it to a person who knows the language. Also be careful when translating personal data: remember that everything you paste into a free tool is information that passes through a machine. For sensitive texts, use trusted tools and check their privacy policy.

## 🔓 Open tools
- **DeepL** (deepl.com) — a free translator, famous for its quality, with a document translation function.
- **Google Translate** (translate.google.com) — the most famous free translator, with camera and voice.
- **LibreTranslate** (libretranslate.com) — a free and open-source translator that you can even install on your own computer.
- **Argos Translate** (argosopentech.com) — an offline, open-source translator for privacy.
- **Reverso** (reverso.net) — a free translator with examples in real contexts.

Translating with AI is a bridge that brings the family and the world closer. Use DeepL or Google Translate for everyday texts, and the chatbots when you want to keep the tone. Choose languages well, review the important things and do not sign anything important without a human eye.
