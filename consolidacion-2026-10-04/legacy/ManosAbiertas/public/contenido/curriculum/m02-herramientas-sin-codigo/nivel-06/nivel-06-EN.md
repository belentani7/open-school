## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Writing assistant | A tool that helps you write texts well, as if you had a personal secretary. |
| Audience | Who the text is addressed to: the bank, a son, the town hall, a friend. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Writing is one of the tasks we do all our lives: letters, complaints, congratulations, messages to the school, requests to the town hall. Many times we do not know how to start, or we are afraid of saying something wrong. Today we learn that writing has an assistant, and that assistant turns a simple idea into a well-written text.

A writing assistant is a tool that helps you put your ideas into words. ChatGPT and Gemini can be writing assistants, but there are also specific ones. It is like having a personal secretary: you tell her what you want to say, and she writes it properly. Your job is not to write, but to explain well what you want. And that, as we saw in previous levels, is done with a good prompt.

The secret to a good written text lies in three ingredients that you must include in your request: the audience, the purpose and the tone. The audience is who will read it. It is not the same to write to the bank as to a grandson. The purpose is what you want to achieve: complain, thank, ask, say you will not be there. The tone is the mood: formal, affectionate, firm. With these three, the assistant knows exactly what to write.

Let us see an example. Bad request: "Write me a letter." Too vague. Good request: "Write a short letter to the school of my daughter telling them that next Friday she will not be able to attend because she has a doctor's appointment. Tone: polite and simple. Maximum one paragraph." With that, the assistant writes you the letter almost done.

Now, a very practical trick: ask the assistant to give you options. Instead of one version, ask for three: "Give me three versions of the message: one formal, one friendly and one shorter." Then you choose the one that suits you best, or mix parts of each. It is like trying on three jackets before buying one.

Another very useful trick: the assistant writes, but you are the one who decides. Read the text out loud. If something does not sound like you, correct it. You can ask: "change the greeting", "make it shorter", "put my name here". It is a conversation: you give ideas and the assistant polishes. The final text must sound like you, because you sign it.

Do you want the text to be perfect? Ask for a final review. "Check the spelling and grammar of this text." The assistant reviews it and corrects errors. It is like a proofreader who reads with fresh eyes. But remember: the assistant is good, but not infallible. Read the final text yourself. What is on paper is your signature.

Writing assistants are also great for translations between the languages of the course. If you need to send a message to a relative who speaks another language, ask: "Translate this message into Portuguese, keeping the friendly tone." It is a door to the family that the distance had closed.

A habit that will save you hours: build your own collection of templates. When the assistant writes you a letter that works well, save it in a document. When you need a similar one, you do not start from scratch: you take your template, change the data and the tone, and you are done. Your template box is your personal treasure.

And a warning, more useful than it seems: beware of texts that arrive already written to you. Today, anyone can write with AI, and also send false things. If you receive an urgent message from "your bank" or "your son asking for money", stop, call them on the phone and check. Writing with AI is a great gift, but it is also a tool that can be misused. We will see this in the ethical section.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The thank-you letter**: after the operation, the nurse was wonderful. You ask: "Write a short thank-you card to a nurse, affectionate and simple, in my name, as if I were a grateful patient." You copy it, adjust a couple of words and hand it to her.
2. **The message to the community**: you cannot go to the residents' meeting. You ask: "Write a message to the neighbours' association apologising that I cannot attend Saturday's meeting because of a family matter. Simple and kind, maximum five lines." Ready to send on WhatsApp.
3. **The complaint at the shop**: a product came broken. You ask: "Write a polite but firm message to an online shop to tell them that a product arrived broken and I want a replacement. Include that I have the order number." With that, you get a response or a refund faster.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Open your chatbot and create a new chat called "Writing".
2. Write this request: "Write a message to my neighbour asking if she can water my plants while I am away next week. Tone: friendly. Maximum five lines."
3. Read the result. Does it sound like you? If not, ask: "Make it more affectionate."
4. Ask for options: "Give me two more versions, one shorter and one more formal."
5. Choose the one you like best and correct it by hand: "Change the greeting to 'Hello neighbour'."
6. Do a final review: "Check the spelling and grammar."
7. Copy the final version and save it in your notebook or a document. Label it "Template: plants".
8. Repeat with a different purpose: a thank-you, a request or a complaint. Save another template.
9. Reflect: what have you saved today that you will not have to write again?

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises

2. What are the three ingredients of a good writing request?
3. Why is it useful to ask for several versions of the same text?
4. How do you make the assistant's text sound like you?
5. What is a template and why does it save you time?

**Answers:** 1. A tool that helps you put your ideas into words, like a personal secretary. 2. Audience, purpose and tone. 3. Because you can choose the one that suits you best or mix parts of each. 4. Reading it out loud, correcting what does not sound like you and asking for changes until it fits you. 5. A base text that you reuse and adapt; you avoid starting from scratch every time.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
Writing with AI raises a simple but important question: who signs the text? If an official document or a work letter carries your name, you are responsible for it, whether the AI wrote it or not. Read it, verify it and own it. And remember the dark side: AI can also write false or harmful texts. Do not use it to deceive, and protect yourself from those who use it against you: an urgent message asking for money or personal data is always verified by a phone call.

## 🔓 Open tools

- **Gemini** (gemini.google.com) — Google's assistant, very good at writing in several languages.
- **Claude** (claude.ai) — an assistant known for careful and natural writing.
- **DeepL Write** (deepl.com/write) — a free tool to polish and improve your texts.
- **LanguageTool** (languagetool.org) — a free spelling and grammar checker in many languages.

Writing has an assistant: you explain what you want, with audience, purpose and tone, and it writes it for you. Choose between versions, correct until it sounds like you, and save the templates that work. Writing well is a door that is opening for you.
