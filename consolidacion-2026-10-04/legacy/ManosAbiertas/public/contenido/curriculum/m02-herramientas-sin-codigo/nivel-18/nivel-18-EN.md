# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 18
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand that the voice is a door to technology for everyone.
- Learn to use tools that read a text aloud (text to speech).
- Learn to use tools that turn your voice into text (speech to text).
- Use the voice to write, to listen and to participate.
- Discover open tools like Piper and Whisper.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Speech to text | The machine listens and writes what you say: your voice becomes text. |
| Text to speech | The machine reads a text aloud: the text becomes voice. |
| Transcript | The written version of what has been said, like taking minutes of a conversation. |
| Dictation | Writing by speaking instead of typing, like dictating a letter. |
| Voice | The way of speaking to the machine: the hand that was tired can rest. |
| Open | Tools that everyone can use and improve, free and without walls. |

## 📚 Main lesson
We have saved the best of the Branch for the end, and it is wonderful: the voice. Until now we have written to the computer with our hands. Today we learn that you can also talk to it, and that it talks back. For someone whose hands are tired, or whose eyes are tired, or who never got used to typing, this changes everything. The voice opens the door that was closed.

Let us understand the two directions. The first, speech to text: you speak, and the machine writes. You tell it "dear neighbour, tomorrow the meeting", and it writes it for you. The second, text to speech: you give it a text, and it reads it aloud. You write the announcement, and it reads it with a calm voice. One direction listens, the other speaks. Two doors to the same house.

The tools of this level are open and wonderful. Whisper, from the company OpenAI, is a speech to text tool that is very good: you speak, and it writes what you say, even in different languages. Piper is a text to speech tool that reads aloud with natural voices, and it is so light that it works even on an old computer. Two tools created by people who share, from the world we discovered in level 15.

Let us think about daily life. For someone with arthritis in the hands, typing is a pain: every message, every letter costs. With speech to text, you speak and it writes. You can send the message, write the email, reply to the group, all with your voice. The voice is a liberation: the hand that was tired can rest.

And the other direction. For someone whose eyes are tired, reading is an effort. With text to speech, the machine reads for you: the notice, the recipe, the letter from your daughter's school. You listen while you cook, while you rest. The text comes to your ear instead of your eyes. It is like having someone who reads aloud, but without needing anyone.

And the most beautiful application: the transcript. Do you have a meeting of the association? You record the audio with your mobile, and Whisper writes it all. The minutes of the meeting write themselves. Do you want to remember what the doctor said? You record, it writes it, you keep it. Nothing escapes: the word that was lost, now written. A gift for the memory.

Let us talk about the voice in the family. A granddaughter who writes very long messages; you prefer to listen to them. You copy the text, the machine reads it. Or your son who does not want to write an essay; you suggest he dictate it and then correct it. The voice unites generations: the oldest speaks, the youngest writes, and the machine translates between both worlds.

And an honest lesson: the voice is not perfect. Sometimes the machine writes a word that is not right, especially with names or words from the region. That is why the golden rule returns: check. You speak, it writes, you read it, you correct. The voice does the heavy work, and your eye does the fine work. Between the two, everything comes out well.

Let us also speak of the respect for our voice. Your voice is yours, personal, unique. When you use a voice tool, it listens to you. Use the tools of trust, know what they do with your audio, and do not say things out loud that you would not say in writing. The machine listens, but the choice of what you say is yours.

And a word of hope: if until now you have felt left behind because you did not write fast, or because your hands hurt, today's lesson is for you. The voice does not care about speed, nor about age, nor about experience. It listens to you, and it writes. Technology, at last, is put at the height of the person. And that person, with their voice, is worth a fortune.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The minutes of the meeting**: at the association you record the meeting with your mobile. At home, you pass the audio to Whisper and it writes everything. You correct the names, you print, and the minutes are ready. What before was a boring afternoon, now is twenty minutes.
2. **The letter for your daughter**: your daughter asks you to write a letter to the school. You dictate it with your voice: "Dear director, I am writing to ask you..." It writes it, you read it, you correct it and you send it. Your hand rests, and your words are heard.
3. **The reading before sleeping**: your grandchildren like you to tell them stories, but your eyes are tired. You find a story, you pass it to Piper, and it reads it with a soft voice. The grandchildren listen, and you rest. The technology brings stories to your bed.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Open a speech to text tool: for example Whisper (openai.com) or the voice typing of Google Docs.
2. Find the microphone button. Look at the screen and prepare to speak.
3. Speak a sentence of yours slowly: "Good morning, this is my first dictation." Watch how the machine writes it.
4. Read what it has written. Correct the errors: the machine makes mistakes, your eye fixes them.
5. Now open a text to speech tool: for example Piper, or the reading function of your computer.
6. Paste a short text: a note, a greeting, a recipe.
7. Choose the voice and the speed. Listen to how it reads it.
8. Write in your notebook: which of the two directions is more useful for you, and why?
9. Think: who in your life would benefit from the voice? Tell them about it.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
The voice is personal: it is your way of speaking, your words, your privacy. Tools that listen deserve trust and care: use those you know, and check what they do with your audio. Do not record or transcribe other people without their permission: the meeting of the association, the conversation with the doctor, are not for capturing without consent. And remember that a transcript is a document: if it has data of others, protect it. The voice opens doors, but respect marks the path.

## 🔓 Open tools
- **Whisper** (openai.com) — the speech to text tool: it writes what you say, very well, in several languages.
- **Piper** (github.com/rhasspy/piper) — the light text to speech tool, with natural voices, free and open.
- **Google Docs voice typing** (docs.google.com) — dictation inside the documents you already know.
- **eSpeak NG** (github.com/espeak-ng) — an open-source reading voice, simple and free.
- **Mozilla Common Voice** (commonvoice.mozilla.org) — a project where people donate their voice to create open tools.

The voice is a door for everyone: you speak and it writes (Whisper), you give it a text and it reads (Piper). The transcript writes the minutes alone, and the reading reaches your ear instead of your eyes. Always check what the machine writes, and take care of your voice and that of others.
