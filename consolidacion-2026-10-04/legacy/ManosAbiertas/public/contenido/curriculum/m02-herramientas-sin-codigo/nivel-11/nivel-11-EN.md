# Module 2: Tools Without Code — Level 11
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 4 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a spreadsheet is and why it is useful in daily life.
- Learn to create a simple table in Google Sheets with the help of AI.
- Ask the AI to explain, create and fix formulas for you.
- Use AI to analyse a list and give you conclusions.
- Manage the household accounts or a list with confidence.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| Spreadsheet | A large table with boxes (cells) to organise data, like a squared notebook that calculates for you. |
| Cell | Each box of the table, where you write a number, a text or a formula. |
| Formula | An instruction that makes the table calculate, like telling it "add everything in this column". |
| Row | The horizontal lines of the table, where each case goes: one row, one person. |
| Column | The vertical lines of the table, where each type of data goes: name, amount, date. |
| AI help | Asking the assistant to explain or create formulas for you, without memorising them. |

## 📚 Main lesson
The spreadsheet is one of the most useful tools in daily life, but many people are afraid of it because of the word "formula". Today we learn a secret that changes everything: you do not have to learn formulas, you can ask the AI to explain them, create them and fix them. The spreadsheet becomes a table that calculates for you, with an assistant at your side.

Let us start with the basics. A spreadsheet is a huge table made of rows and columns. The rows are the horizontal lines and the columns are the vertical ones. The boxes where both meet are called cells. Imagine a wardrobe: the rows are the shelves and the columns the sections; each cell is where one garment goes. In the spreadsheet, each cell holds one piece of data.

What do we use it for in daily life? The household budget, the list of the association's members, the gifts to buy, the medicines and their schedules, the school contact list. Any list that has order has a place in a spreadsheet. And the beauty is that, once you organise it, the table can add, count, order and summarise for you.

The star tool is Google Sheets, free, which lives in your Google account, and has a great advantage: it is connected to Gemini, Google's AI. This means that, inside the spreadsheet itself, you can ask for help. There is a small icon or a bar to ask the AI: "add the column of expenses", "explain me what this formula does", "order the list by age". It is like having an accountant sitting next to you.

Let us build a practical example: the house budget. You create a table with three columns: what, amount and date. Each expense is a row. When you have five or six rows, you ask the AI: "Add column B", and the table tells you the total. Then you ask: "What did I spend most on?", and it answers you with a summary. Without formulas learned by heart, only with questions.

But it is also worth learning the three formulas that solve everything, because the AI will use them for you but it is good to recognise them. SUM adds, AVERAGE calculates the average, COUNT counts. You do not have to write them: you ask the AI "sum this column" and it writes the formula for you. Recognising them is enough: they are like knowing that the gas pedal accelerates and the brake stops.

Now, a very useful trick: ask the AI to explain a formula. If someone sends you a spreadsheet with a complicated formula, you select the cell and ask: "What does this do?" The AI explains it to you in simple words. It is like asking a mechanic what that part of the engine is for: you do not repair it, but you understand it.

Another great use: AI can help you keep the accounts. You ask it: "I have these expenses [list], make me a summary and tell me where I can save." The table, guided by you, calculates and the AI gives you the conclusions. It is a small miracle for people who have always kept the accounts in a notebook.

And one more application, very personal: health. A list of medicines with their schedules, a record of blood pressure, the dates of the medical visits. The table orders it, the AI remembers it for you. Many people discover that the spreadsheet is the best ally of the medicine cabinet.

Let us also talk about fear. The spreadsheet does not bite: if you write something in the wrong cell, you delete it and that is it. Nothing breaks, nothing deletes itself. The worst that can happen is a mistake, and you correct it. And if you get lost, you close and start again. The spreadsheet is a tool, like a notebook, but with a calculator and an assistant inside.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The household budget**: you create a table with income and expenses for the month. You ask the AI: "Sum the expenses and tell me the difference with my pension." In a minute you know whether you finish the month on time.
2. **The list of medicines**: you make a table with the medicines, the dose and the hour. You ask the AI: "Make me a table and remind me of the order." The table is printed and put on the fridge; you no longer miss a pill.
3. **The association's membership**: the residents' association wants to update the fees. You create a table with names and amounts paid. You ask: "Who still owes money?" and the table tells you the list in two seconds.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
1. Open Google Sheets (sheets.google.com) with your Google account.
2. Create a new empty spreadsheet. Look at the table: rows and columns.
3. Write a title in the first cell: "House budget".
4. Create three columns: What, Amount, Date. Fill in five expenses of yours.
5. Look for the AI help button in the spreadsheet (a small sparkle or the "Ask Gemini" bar).
6. Ask: "Add the amount column." Watch how the table calculates the total.
7. Ask: "What did I spend most on?" Watch how it answers you with a summary.
8. Ask: "Explain to me in simple words what SUM does." It explains it to you.
9. Write in your notebook: the three questions you asked and what the spreadsheet answered.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
A spreadsheet with AI works with your data, and those data are personal: your income, your medicines, your expenses. Before asking the AI about sensitive data, check where that information goes and who can see it. Also, do not enter personal data of other people (members, neighbours) without being clear that you are responsible for protecting it. The data protection law is not a bureaucracy: it is a right of every person. Handle the data of others as you would like them to handle yours.
