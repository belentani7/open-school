# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 08
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to solve problems when they arise, without getting scared.
- See the problem as a step on the road, not a wall.
- Know how to investigate the causes before jumping to solutions.
- Look for simple, cheap and possible solutions.
- Decide as a group: the meeting and the fair vote.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Problem | A difficulty that stops us from moving forward, like missing materials. |
| Cause | The reason why the problem happens, like the damp that rots the wood. |
| Solution | The answer that solves or reduces the problem. |
| Assembly | The meeting of the team where decisions are made together. |
| Vote | The way of deciding by counting everyone's votes. |
| Consensus | The agreement reached when everyone accepts a decision, even if not everyone thinks the same. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In projects, as in life, problems appear. The materials stop arriving, it rains on the day of the festival, someone falls ill in the week of the fair. There is no project without problems, just as there is no garden without weeds. The difference is in how we receive them: with fear and hanging arms, or with calm and a fresh mind.

The first lesson is this: a problem is not a wall, it is a step on the road. The wall makes us stop; the step makes us look for another road. When a problem arrives, the best sentence is not "everything is lost", but "okay, now what do we do?". That small change of attitude changes everything.

The second lesson is to investigate before acting. We want to solve things quickly, and that is good, but we must not jump to the solution without knowing the cause. If the garden is not growing, we can water more; but if the problem is the soil, watering will not help. Asking "why" before answering "how" is the good repairer's rule.

Investigating the causes is simple: we observe, we ask and we write down. "Why have the seeds not arrived?" Maybe the order was not made, maybe the shopkeeper forgot, maybe the order was placed late. Three possible causes, three different paths. Only when we know the cause does the solution hit the mark.

The third lesson is to look for simple, cheap and possible solutions. In the community project we do not have a marketing department or an endless account: we have creativity and companionship. Before thinking of an expensive solution, we think of the solutions around us: what we have, who we know, what we can do ourselves.

The best solutions are usually the simplest. If a volunteer is missing, maybe a neighbour wants to help. If there are not enough chairs for the meeting, maybe the churches or schools in the neighbourhood will lend them. The big, complicated solution is not better; many times it is the simplest that works first.

The fourth lesson is that deciding as a group gives better results. When there are several solutions, nobody imposes their own. We meet and talk. The assembly is the team meeting where decisions are made together: the problem is presented, the causes are heard and solutions are proposed.

In the assembly, everyone speaks and everyone listens. Whoever coordinates the meeting does not decide for everyone: they give the floor, organise and look for agreement. The ideas are written on a board or on a sheet, so nobody forgets them. Seeing the options in writing helps to compare them calmly.

When opinions match, the decision is easy. When they do not match, we turn to consensus: the solution that everyone can accept, even if it is not each person's favourite. Consensus does not mean everyone thinks the same: it means everyone is willing to go down the same road, without anyone feeling defeated.

If consensus is not possible, we vote. The vote is simple and fair: each person has one vote, and the one with the most votes wins. But there are rules for voting well. Before voting, everyone must understand the options. After voting, everyone accepts the result, even those who did not vote for it. A project with half the team against the decision is a project at risk.

Voting is done with respect. You do not vote to see who wins, but so the project can move forward. Whoever loses today can win tomorrow, and whoever wins today must take care of those who thought differently. The decision belongs to the group, not to the winner.

Sometimes the problem is deciding whether we continue or stop. At those crossroads, we remember the objective: why are we doing this? If the problem is a step, the objective is the goal. Looking at the goal gives us the strength to take one more step.

In this level we will practise all this with a problem that is imaginary and real at the same time: the problem of our own team. We will train the decision circle: explaining the problem, finding the cause, proposing solutions and deciding all together.

Let us remember: a problem well received is half the problem solved. Calm is not for others: it is for us. And the shared decision, like shared bread, feeds much more than the effort of one alone.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Rain on the day of the festival.** One day before the festival and the weather threatens rain. The team meets in assembly. Cause: the festival was planned in the open air. Solutions: putting up tarpaulins, hiring a tent or changing the day. They decide by vote: tarpaulins with help from the neighbourhood. The festival went well and nobody got wet working.
2. **The seeds that do not arrive.** At the garden, the seeds have not arrived. They investigate the cause: the order was placed late. Simple solution: a participant has a cousin who is a farmer and offers seeds. The problem that seemed like a wall became a step: they planted even earlier than planned.
3. **The missing chair at the table.** The meeting table has a broken chair. Instead of buying one, the team thinks: who can fix it? The blacksmith neighbour offers to help, and a week later the chair is as good as new. Cheap, simple and with a new friend on the team.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Write a real or possible problem of your project.
Step 2: Investigate the cause: write down two or three possible reasons why the problem exists.
Step 3: Choose the most likely cause and verify it with the team.
Step 4: Propose two or three simple, cheap and possible solutions. Write them all down, without discarding any.
Step 5: Bring the team together in a short assembly: explain the problem, the cause and the proposed solutions.
Step 6: Listen to everyone's opinions. Look for consensus. If there is none, vote in assembly, with one vote per person.
Step 7: After the decision, everyone accepts the result, writes it down and carries on. The decision belongs to the group, not to the winner.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a problem in a project? a) A wall that stops us forever. b) A step on the road that makes us look for another route. c) Proof that the project has failed.
2. What must we investigate before looking for the solution? a) Who is to blame. b) The cause of the problem. c) How much time we have.
3. Which solutions do we look for first? a) The expensive and complicated ones. b) The simple, cheap and possible ones. c) The ones that come from outside.
4. What is the assembly? a) The meeting where a single person decides. b) The team meeting where decisions are made together. c) The place where materials are kept.
5. What do we do when there is no consensus? a) We vote, with one vote per person. b) The loudest speaker gives the orders. c) We leave the decision to the coordinator.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The problem is never the fault of a single person: the causes are almost always shared.
- The solution is not imposed: it is proposed, listened to and decided together.
- In the vote, all votes have equal value, without favours or pressure.
- Whoever wins the vote takes care of those who thought differently; whoever loses accepts and carries on.
- Nobody is left out of the decision: whoever could not attend the meeting gives their opinion before or after.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Problems are steps on the road, not walls. We investigate the cause, look for simple solutions and decide in assembly, by consensus or by a fair vote. Calm and shared decision are our most valuable tools. In the next level we will learn to bring new people into the project: the advantages of growing and the ways of welcoming those who arrive.
