# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 24
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Fruit
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn what participatory evaluation is and what it is for.
- Look at the road travelled with truth, without blaming anyone.
- Learn from the mistakes without fear of making them.
- Celebrate the achievements of everyone, not only of the leaders.
- Build a culture of continuous improvement in the community.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Evaluation | Looking at the road travelled to see what went well and what did not. |
| Participatory evaluation | Evaluating all together: every voice counts, not only the leaders'. |
| Indicator | A sign that tells us if an objective has been achieved. |
| Feedback | The honest and respectful return about the work. |
| Continuous improvement | Always learning, one small step at a time. |
| Celebration | Stopping and recognising what the community has achieved. |

## 📚 Main lesson
The project has given fruit. Now we learn to look at it: evaluation. Evaluating is not judging nor blaming: it is looking at the road travelled, learning from it and improving what comes. A community that does not evaluate, repeats the same mistakes; a community that evaluates, always grows.

Participatory evaluation is evaluating all together. It is not only the coordinator who says how it went: every participant has a voice. Those who plant, who carry, who cook, who make the posters: everyone saw different things and everyone has something to say. Participatory evaluation brings those looks together and makes a complete portrait.

To evaluate, we need indicators: signs that tell us if an objective has been achieved. We defined the objectives in level 22; now we see if we achieved them. If the objective was twenty families, the indicator is the number of families. A good indicator is simple and visible: we can count it, see it, show it.

Evaluation begins with simple and honest questions. What went well? What went less well? What did we learn? What will we change? Four questions that any person can ask, without guilt and without defence. Evaluation does not look for the guilty: it looks for the learning.

The mistake is not the end: it is a teacher. Those who never made a mistake, never tried. The community that learns from the mistakes turns every fall into a lesson: it sees what failed, asks why and changes what is needed. The mistake is not hidden: it is shared, so it is not repeated.

Feedback is the honest and respectful return. Saying what we saw with truth, and with care: not "you did it badly", but "this part did not work, how can we improve it?". Feedback that corrects without humiliating and celebrates without exaggerating builds the team; the one that attacks or flatters, undoes it.

Evaluation also celebrates. It is not only about the problems: it is recognising what the community has achieved. Every small achievement deserves to be seen: the first harvest, the first event, the first meeting with a full room. Celebrating is not vanity: it is watering the confidence of everyone.

Celebration belongs to everyone: it is not only the leader who receives the congratulations. When the harvest goes well, the honour belongs to those who planted, who watered, who cared. Participatory evaluation makes sure that every hand that worked is seen. A shared achievement has a different taste.

Evaluation feeds continuous improvement: always learning, one small step at a time. We do not need big changes: we need constant small improvements. A shorter meeting, an earlier notice, an organised material: small steps that, together, make the project advance a lot.

Continuous improvement needs humility. Nobody has all the answers: not the coordinator, not the oldest participant. The community that learns is the one that asks, experiments and adjusts. Humility is not weakness: it is the door through which progress enters.

Evaluation must have a date and a moment. It is not something for "whenever": it is something marked. At the end of a phase, at the end of a year, after an important event: the community sits down and evaluates, as level 08 points out. The marked evaluation is the one that really happens.

Participatory evaluation is, at heart, respect: respect for the work of each person and respect for the truth. Looking at what we did with clean eyes, learning without fear and celebrating without vanity: that is the circle that makes the community grow, year after year.

In this level we will practise evaluation: looking at the road travelled, listening to all the voices, learning from the mistakes and celebrating the achievements. We will build a culture where improving is natural and where each person feels that their work is seen and valued.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The marked evaluation.** After the neighbourhood party, the community meets on Sunday. Four questions, all with a voice: what went well, what went less well, what we learned, what we will change. Next year's party will be better.
2. **The shared mistake.** The poster of the first event came out with the wrong date and few people came. Instead of blaming, the community asks why: a review was missing. From now on, every poster is reviewed by two people. The mistake was not repeated.
3. **The celebration of everyone.** The garden gave the first tomato harvest. The community meets, cooks a soup with the tomatoes and thanks out loud those who planted, watered and cared. Everyone feels part of the achievement.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Mark a date for the evaluation, after a phase or event.
Step 2: Prepare the four questions: good, less good, learning, changes.
Step 3: Give a voice to everyone: start with those who speak least.
Step 4: Look at the indicators: were the defined objectives achieved?
Step 5: Share a mistake and learn from it without guilt.
Step 6: Give respectful feedback and receive it with humility.
Step 7: Celebrate: recognise the achievement of everyone, with truth.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is participatory evaluation? a) Only the leader evaluates. b) Everyone evaluates together, with every voice counting. c) Not evaluating.
2. What is an indicator? a) A sign that says if an objective has been achieved. b) An opinion. c) A punishment.
3. What do we do with the mistakes? a) We hide them. b) We learn from them. c) We punish those who make them.
4. What is feedback? a) A criticism without respect. b) An honest and respectful return. c) An exaggerated compliment.
5. What feeds continuous improvement? a) Big changes suddenly. b) Constant small steps. c) Not changing anything.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Evaluation looks for learning, not for the guilty.
- Every voice counts: participatory evaluation is a right of everyone.
- Feedback corrects without humiliating and celebrates without exaggerating.
- The achievement belongs to everyone: every hand that worked is recognised.
- The truth is said with respect, and received with humility.
