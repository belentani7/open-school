# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 02
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to use WhatsApp to communicate with the project team.
- Know how to create a group and give it a clear name.
- Send text messages, photos, voice messages and reminders.
- Learn the rules of courtesy for writing in a group.
- Avoid the most common mistakes when sharing information on WhatsApp.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp group | A shared conversation between several people where everyone can write. |
| Message | A short text we send to another person or to a group. |
| Voice message | A spoken message: we record our voice and send it. |
| Emoji | A small face or drawing that expresses a feeling. It should be used in moderation. |
| Pinned | When we pin an important message so it stays at the top, visible to everyone. |
| Mute | Turning off a group's notifications so it does not keep ringing. |

## 📚 Main lesson
For a team to work, it has to communicate. In today's community projects, one of the most used tools is WhatsApp. Most of us already use it to talk with our families. Now we are going to use it for the project, as if we turned the family after-dinner chat into a working meeting.

Let us imagine the neighbourhood noticeboard. In the past, neighbours pinned papers to announce a meeting or an event. WhatsApp acts as a noticeboard, but better: every notice reaches every phone instantly, and anyone can reply.

The first step is to create the team's group. It is created from WhatsApp, you choose a clear name like "Clean Square Project" and you invite the team members. From that moment on, the group is our meeting room: there we share notices, decide dates and share out tasks.

And what can you send in a message? Almost anything. A text to say "meeting on Thursday at 6". A photo of a place that needs fixing. A voice message to explain something with your voice, which many people find easier than typing. A document with a quote. A reminder the day before the event.

Voice messages are very useful at our age. Typing with a small finger can be difficult; speaking is not. If we have something to say, we tap the microphone button, speak calmly and send it. It is like leaving a message on the answering machine back in the landline days.

Now, a group with many people can become noisy. To avoid that, there are rules of courtesy that make the group a pleasant place. The first one: write only what is necessary, and with a clear message that starts with the subject, for example "NOTICE: Thursday meeting".

The second rule: do not send chains, jokes or forwards that have nothing to do with the project. The group is the work table, not the TV sofa. What is left over in a project group is distraction.

The third rule: do not write in all caps. In the digital world, writing in caps is like shouting. Better to write normally and with kind words.

The fourth rule: confirm. When someone asks something, a short answer is enough: "Got it", "Yes", "I cannot". That way the person organizing knows the message arrived. It is the digital version of saying "here" when the roll is called.

Another very useful feature is pinning a message. If there is an important agreement, like the date of the event or the list of tasks, we pin it so it stays at the top and does not get lost among so many messages. It is like putting a big note on the fridge so the whole family can see it.

It is also good to know how to mute the group. When there are many conversations, the phone can ring non-stop. Muting is not ignoring: it is saving the notice to look at when we can. We go back to the group when we have a quiet moment and read everything.

Voice messages should also be used with care: if we are going to talk for five straight minutes, it is better to write a summary. A long voice message is like a speech; a short message is like a note on the table.

Now let us talk about safety. The group belongs to the project and its members. You do not invite just anyone without telling the team. You do not share anyone's phone number without their permission. And never, ever, ask for or share passwords or bank details over WhatsApp.

Another precaution: before forwarding a photo or a message from someone else, think twice. Once sent, it is very hard to delete completely. The golden rule: if you would not send it to a stranger, do not send it to the group.

With these few rules, WhatsApp becomes the heart of our team: a place where everyone learns everything, where help is asked for and support is given, and where communication is clear and kind.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The festival group.** For the neighbourhood festival, the team creates the group "Festival 2026". There they pin the list of tasks: who brings the chairs, who sets up the music and who watches over the drinks stall. Each task has its person in charge and everything is written down.
2. **The voice reminder.** Antonia cannot type well on her phone, but she speaks perfectly. The day before the event she records a voice message: "Neighbours, tomorrow at ten we meet in the square, do not forget your glove and your bag". Everyone listens and confirms.
3. **The photo of the damage.** Ramón is out for a walk and sees a broken bench. He takes a photo and sends it to the group with a message: "This bench needs a hand". Someone writes down the address and the next step is to ask the town hall for materials. With one photo, the problem is documented.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open WhatsApp on your phone.
Step 2: Tap the icon for a new chat or new group (it is usually a pencil or a plus symbol).
Step 3: Choose "New group". Search for and select two or three people you want to practise with (family members are fine).
Step 4: Give the group a name, for example "Practice Team". Tap the arrow or "Create".
Step 5: Send a greeting text message, for example: "Hello everyone, this is the team's group".
Step 6: Send a voice message: press and hold the microphone icon, speak calmly and release it to send.
Step 7: Tap the group's name at the top and look for the "Pin message" option. Pin your greeting message to try how it stays at the top. Then unpin it to leave everything as it was.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the project's WhatsApp group for? a) To forward jokes. b) To coordinate the team: notices, tasks and agreements. c) Only for calling.
2. What is a voice message? a) A message written in capital letters. b) A spoken message we record and send. c) A phone call.
3. What does writing in capital letters in a chat mean? a) That it is important. b) That we are shouting. c) That it is a pinned message.
4. What do we do if the group rings too much? a) Leave the group. b) Mute it and check it when we can. c) Delete the app.
5. Can we share a member's phone number without their permission? a) Yes, it is a group. b) No, never without permission. c) Only if we are the coordinator.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The WhatsApp group is a shared space: let us respect every person and their privacy.
- Let us not share photos or data of anyone without their explicit permission.
- Let us be careful with what we write: a message can hurt if it is not read with the tone the voice had.
- Let us not ask for bank details or passwords over WhatsApp; personal data is handled confidentially.
- If someone in the group makes a mistake, let us help them kindly. In a team, correcting is accompanying.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
WhatsApp is the team's noticeboard: a group with a clear name where dates are announced, tasks are shared out and everyone learns everything. It must be used with courtesy (clear messages, confirmations, no caps or chains) and with care (no personal data without permission). In the next level we will take a step further in communication: we will learn to meet face to face through the screen with video calls.
