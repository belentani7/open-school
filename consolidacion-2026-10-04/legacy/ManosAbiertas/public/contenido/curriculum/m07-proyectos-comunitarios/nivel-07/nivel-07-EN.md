# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 07
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to speak in public and before the group with calm.
- Prepare a clear message, with a beginning, middle and end.
- Beat the nerves with simple breathing and preparation techniques.
- Use body language, voice and eyes in our favour.
- Know how to listen and respond with respect to those who listen to us.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Message | What we want others to understand and remember. |
| Speech | The way of presenting an idea out loud, before people. |
| Beginning, middle and end | The three parts of every good message: present, develop, conclude. |
| Nerves | That butterfly feeling in the stomach before speaking; it is normal and it passes. |
| Body language | What we say with our hands, face and posture, without words. |
| Active listening | Really listening, without interrupting, in order to understand and answer well. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Speaking in public is, for many people, more frightening than climbing a mountain. But it is a skill that is learned, like riding a bicycle. And in the community project we will need it more often than we think: presenting the idea to the neighbours, explaining the plan to the town hall, motivating the team. Speaking well is not a gift: it is preparation and practice.

Every good message has three parts, like a sandwich: beginning, middle and end. The beginning is the top slice of bread: greeting and saying in a few words what we are going to talk about. "Good morning. I have come to tell you about our community garden project, which we want to start in spring." That way, in two sentences, people already know what is coming next.

The middle is the filling: the essential part of our message. Here it is best to say few things and say them well. A project cannot be explained in ten minutes, but in three clear sentences it can. What we will do, why, and when we start. People remember simple messages; long messages get lost.

The end is the bottom slice of bread: closing with a strong, clear idea. It can be an invitation, a question or a summary. "We invite everyone to take part on Saturday, at ten, in cleaning the plot. Your help makes a big difference." The end leaves the door open for action.

Preparation is half the success. Before speaking, we write our three sentences at home, in front of us, and read them out loud. We can rehearse alone, in front of the mirror, or with a family member. Rehearsing is not wasting time: it is gaining confidence. What we rehearse comes out better, like the music we have already played many times.

Nerves are normal. Even experienced speakers feel butterflies in their stomach. The difference is that they know it is energy and use it in their favour. A simple technique: before starting, breathe in deeply through the nose, count to four, and breathe out slowly through the mouth. Repeat two or three times. The body calms down and the mind becomes clearer.

Breathing deeply also helps us start. We are in no hurry. We stand up, get into position, look at the group and begin when we are ready. That small moment of silence is not strange: it shows confidence. Like the cook who looks at the recipe before pouring in the ingredients.

The voice is a tool. We speak slowly and a little louder than in normal conversation. We do not shout: we project. If we notice we are speaking too fast, we slow down and breathe. People understand better someone who speaks calmly than someone who speaks quickly, even if they say fewer words.

The eyes build the bridge with the audience. We do not need to stare, but to sweep the group with our gaze, slowly, pausing a second on each face. If we look at the ceiling or the floor, it seems we are talking to ourselves. If we look at people, they feel part of the conversation.

Hands help when they are simple. Keeping them loose and using them little, with natural gestures, gives strength to the words. Posture also counts: standing, straight but not stiff, like a tree. Someone who slouches transmits insecurity; someone who stands well transmits confidence, even if inside they feel cold.

Speaking in public is also knowing how to listen. After speaking, the group asks questions. Listening actively means not interrupting, not answering while the other person is still speaking, and thanking every question. "Very good question." Then we answer calmly and, if we do not know the answer, we say so honestly: "I will look into it and reply in the group." That is better than making it up.

Mistakes also have their place. If we make a mistake while speaking, it does not matter: nobody has a copy of the speech. We correct with a smile and carry on. Perfection is not the goal; communication is. Those who forgive themselves transmit calm, and calm is contagious.

In this level we will prepare a short message about our project, with a beginning, middle and end, and practise it with the group. It does not matter if we come out nervous: we all are. What matters is taking the step and realising that it is possible.

Let us remember: the best speaker is not the one who speaks most beautifully, but the one who is clearest and most sincere. The group trusts someone who speaks with truth, with simple words and with heart. And that is within everyone's reach.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The presentation to the neighbours.** At the neighbourhood meeting, the coordinator stands up and says: "Good evening. We want to create a community garden on that empty plot. We will start cleaning it on Saturday at ten and we need hands. Whoever can, please come." Three sentences, beginning, middle and end, and the message has arrived.
2. **The presentation to the town hall.** When asking for permission, they present the project out loud: "This project does good for the neighbourhood, costs the town hall no money and the neighbours are excited. We only ask for the use of the land." Clear, short, and with a concrete request.
3. **Motivating the team.** Before starting to work one Saturday, the person responsible says: "Today we are going to clean the first plot. When we finish, we celebrate with a snack. Every bag of weeds we fill is a step forward." The group smiles and works with more pleasure.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Write your message about the project at home: beginning (greeting and idea), middle (the essential in three sentences) and end (invitation or summary).
Step 2: Read the message out loud, alone. Adjust the sentences until they sound natural, as if you were speaking them.
Step 3: Rehearse in front of the mirror or with a family member. Time yourself: less than a minute is enough.
Step 4: Practise the breathing before speaking: breathe in through the nose counting to four, breathe out slowly through the mouth, three times.
Step 5: On the day of the group, stand up calmly, sweep the faces with your gaze and begin when you are ready.
Step 6: Speak slowly, with your hands loose and your voice firm. If the nerves tighten, take one more deep breath.
Step 7: At the end, invite questions and thank each one. If you do not know an answer, say you will find out and reply later.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What are the three parts of a good message? a) Many ideas, examples and jokes. b) Beginning, middle and end. c) Greeting, farewell and applause.
2. What is rehearsing for? a) To memorise word for word. b) To gain confidence and make the message come out better. c) To tire the voice.
3. What to do when we feel nervous before speaking? a) Leave. b) Breathe deeply and start calmly. c) Speak quickly to finish early.
4. Where do we place our gaze when speaking? a) At the ceiling. b) At the floor. c) On the faces of the group, sweeping them slowly.
5. What do we do when we do not know the answer to a question? a) We make something up. b) We stay silent. c) We say honestly that we will find out and reply later.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-c, 5-c.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Speak with truth: the message must say what will actually be done.
- Do not promise what we cannot keep in order to please the listeners.
- Respect other people's time: a short message is worth more than a long speech.
- Thank the questions and listen to them without interrupting.
- Leave space for those who find speaking harder: the group also listens in silence.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Speaking in public is learned: preparing the message with a beginning, middle and end, breathing to beat the nerves, using voice, eyes and hands, and listening with respect. Those who speak with truth and simplicity reach any group. In the next level we will learn to decide as a group: the problems, the solutions and voting in assembly.
