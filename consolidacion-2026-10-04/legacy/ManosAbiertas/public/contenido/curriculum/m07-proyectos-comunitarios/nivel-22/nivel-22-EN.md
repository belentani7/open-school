# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 22
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Canopy
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to think about the future of the project in an organised way.
- Get to know what strategic planning is and what it is for.
- See the different scenarios the future can bring.
- Take deep decisions with the participation of everyone.
- Build a clear course that lasts beyond enthusiasm.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Strategic planning | Thinking calmly about where we want to go and how to get there. |
| Vision | The dream with feet: the clear image of the future we want. |
| Mission | The reason for being of the project: why we exist. |
| Objectives | The concrete goals we want to achieve. |
| Scenario | A possible way of the future: what can happen. |
| Strategic decision | A deep choice that changes the course of the project. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous levels we planted, watered and made the project grow. Now we learn to look far: strategic planning. Those who do not think about the future live on improvisation; planning gives us a map so we do not walk in circles.

Strategic planning is not something for big companies nor for documents with many pages. It is simply asking: where are we? Where do we want to go? And how are we going to get there? Three questions that the whole community can ask, without hurry and with everyone seated around the table.

We begin with the vision: the dream with feet. It is not a vague wish: it is a clear image of the future we want. In five years, how do we see our community? The vision answers that question. It does not need to be long: a sentence that everyone understands and that warms the heart.

Then comes the mission: the reason for being of the project. Why do we exist? What do we do, every day, so the dream comes true? The mission is the north: when there are doubts, we return to it and ask "does this help our mission?". If it does not help, it is not for us.

Next, the objectives: the concrete goals. An objective is a bridge between the dream and reality. It must be clear and measurable: "open the seed bank before the summer", "get ten new families by the end of the year". Objectives like that give us signs that we are on the right road.

Then we look at the scenarios: the possible ways of the future. What can happen? The best, the worst and the likely. If water runs out, if the coordinator leaves, if there are more families than we expected: for each scenario, we think what we would do. It is not pessimism: it is preparation. Those who think about the scenarios do not get scared by them.

Strategic decisions are the deep choices that change the course. They are not the small everyday decisions: they are the ones that decide where we go. Prioritise a garden or a workshop? Grow fast or calmly? These decisions belong to everyone, as the assembly of level 08 decided: no voice is worth more than another.

Planning is not a document kept in a drawer: it is a compass that is used. The project must be reviewed: what worked, what changed, what was left behind. Once a year, the community meets, looks at the map and adjusts it. The plan is alive, like the project.

Planning protects enthusiasm. Enthusiasm is good and gives impulse, but it passes; planning gives memory: when enthusiasm calms down, the map reminds us of the course. The projects that last are not the ones that start with more fire: they are the ones that, even without fire, know where they are going.

Planning also protects the people. When the future is thought about, nobody feels lost and nobody decides alone in the dark. Each participant knows their place on the map and feels that their work has a purpose. A community that knows where it is going, walks together.

Planning starts small. We do not need to plan ten years or five: we can start with one year, or even one term. What matters is to start, with simple questions and honest answers. A small map made on time is worth more than a big map made late.

Let us remember: strategic planning does not take away the freedom of the community: it gives it a course. The compass does not replace the steps: it guides them. A project with a clear vision, mission and objectives is like a boat with a destination: the wind can change, but the course belongs to everyone and the journey is shared.

In this level we will build our map: the vision, the mission, the objectives and the scenarios. We will do it together, calmly and with truth. And we will remember: the future is not predicted, it is built, one step at a time.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The vision in one sentence.** After a conversation, the community writes its vision: "a neighbourhood where every family has enough to eat and nobody is alone". A sentence, said from memory by everyone, that warms each meeting.
2. **The measurable objectives.** The project defines: "by the end of the year, twenty families in the community garden". So in December, they count and they know: they got eighteen, two are missing. The objective told them the truth.
3. **The thought-out scenarios.** The team thinks: what if water runs out in summer? They prepare plan B: shared well and night watering. When summer arrives, the plan is already ready and nobody gets scared.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Gather the community and ask: where are we today, with truth?
Step 2: Write the vision in one sentence: how do we want our future?
Step 3: Define the mission: why do we exist, every day?
Step 4: Choose two or three clear and measurable objectives.
Step 5: Think about the scenarios: the best, the worst and the likely.
Step 6: Take a strategic decision with the participation of everyone.
Step 7: Write the plan in a notebook and mark the review date.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the vision? a) A vague wish. b) The clear image of the future we want. c) A long document.
2. What is the mission? a) The reason for being of the project. b) A meeting. c) A small objective.
3. What makes a good objective? a) It is vague and pretty. b) It is clear and measurable. c) It is secret.
4. What are the scenarios for? a) To scare. b) To prepare us for what can happen. c) To decide alone.
5. What is the plan like? a) Kept in a drawer. b) Alive, reviewed and adjusted. c) Unchangeable.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The future of the project is decided by everyone, not by a single person.
- The vision is an honest dream: it does not promise what it cannot fulfil.
- The objectives serve the people, not the other way round.
- Planning prepares, it does not scare.
- The compass guides the community; the community chooses the course.
