# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 03
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a video call is and what it is for in a project.
- Learn how to join a Google Meet video call.
- Know the basic buttons: camera, microphone, chat and leave.
- Know how to take part with respect: taking turns to speak and being on time.
- Resolve the most common doubts when something does not work.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Video call | A conversation where we see and hear each other through the screen. |
| Link | The internet address that opens the door to the video call. |
| Camera | The small lens of the phone or computer that shows us on screen. |
| Microphone | The device that picks up our voice so others can hear us. |
| Mute microphone | A button that turns off our voice so background noise is not heard. |
| Turn to speak | The order in which people speak so they do not interrupt each other. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Let us imagine a neighbours' meeting at the community centre. Everyone sits around a table, greets each other, listens and reaches agreements. A video call is the same, but through the screen: each person is at home, yet everyone sees and hears each other as if they were in the same room.

The video call is perfect for the project team because it avoids travel. Nobody has to take the bus or wait in the street. You just need to be at home, with your phone or computer, and tap a link at the agreed time.

One of the most used tools is Google Meet. It works on the phone, the tablet and the computer, and it has a very simple free version. To attend a meeting, usually someone from the team sends a link through WhatsApp. Tapping that link is like knocking on the door of the room: you enter the video call.

When you enter, you will see two or three things. The first, the tiles of the people: each person appears in a box, like in a photo. The second, a bar with buttons at the bottom or on the side. The most important buttons are the camera, the microphone and the leave button.

The camera button turns the image on or off. If we do not want to be seen, we tap it and it goes off; others will still hear us. The microphone button turns our voice on or off. When we are not speaking, it is recommended to keep the microphone off, so the TV, the dogs or the street noise are not heard.

The leave button is shaped like a red phone, just like when we hang up a call. When the meeting ends, you tap it and the video call closes. Nothing else is needed: hanging up is hanging up, like the phones we have always known.

For the meeting to be pleasant and productive, the rules are very similar to those of an in-person meeting. The first is punctuality: you must join a little before the time, like arriving early at the cinema so as not to disturb.

The second rule is taking turns to speak. On a video call, if everyone talks at once, nobody understands anything. It is best to have one person who moderates: they give the floor to each person and remind everyone of the agenda. Like at a family meal, people speak in turns.

The third rule is to look and listen. We can see other people's faces, so it is easy to notice if someone wants to speak. Raising a hand on screen or saying "I would like to add something" are simple ways to take part.

There is also the video call chat: a small window where messages are written during the meeting. It is useful for leaving a link, noting a date, or saying "my microphone keeps cutting out, I will type". It is the written note that passes under the table.

What do we do if something does not work? First, do not panic. If they cannot hear us, check that the microphone is not muted (the button usually gets crossed out or turns red). If they cannot see us, check the camera. If the image freezes or cuts out, it is usually the connection: it may be enough to leave and re-enter.

Sometimes the camera does not work, or we do not want to be seen. That is fine: you can take part with the microphone or by writing in the chat. Many older people prefer, the first time, to keep the camera off, to observe calmly. It is respectable and acceptable.

The video call also allows sharing the screen: showing others what is on our phone or computer, for example a document or a poster. It is like showing a photo on paper, but live. We will use this button later, when we learn to collaborate on documents.

At our age, the video call is a gateway to participation. It lets us be part of the team without leaving home, makes evening meetings easier and connects people who live far away. Many seniors' associations already meet this way every week.

In this level we are going to practise joining a test video call. The goal is not to master every button, but to lose the fear and see how simple it is to be face to face with our teammates through the screen.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The team's weekly meeting.** The community garden team meets every Tuesday on Meet at seven in the evening. Each person joins from home with the group's link. In twenty minutes they decide the task of the week and hang up. No more travelling.
2. **The neighbour who lives far away.** Carmen moved to another city, but she is still on the reading club team. Thanks to video calls she takes part every week: they see her, they hear her, and she feels part of the community, just like when she lived in the neighbourhood.
3. **The meeting with the town hall.** The team needs to speak with the councillor. Instead of booking an in-person appointment, they agree on a video call. Each side is in their own office, and in twenty minutes they sort out the permission to use the square.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Ask a trusted person (a family member or friend) to set up a test video call with Google Meet. You can also practise with yourself by sending yourself the link.
Step 2: When you receive the link by WhatsApp or email, tap or click on it. The Meet page or app will open.
Step 3: Before entering, check the two buttons: the camera and the microphone. Decide whether you want them on or off.
Step 4: Tap the "Join" or "Enter" button. You will see the tiles of the connected people.
Step 5: Greet everyone and try speaking: say "hello, can you hear me well?". Ask the other person to confirm that they see you and hear you.
Step 6: If they cannot hear you, check if the microphone button is crossed out. Tap it to reactivate. If they cannot see you, check the camera button.
Step 7: End the meeting by tapping the red phone to hang up. You have made your first project video call.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a video call? a) A normal phone call. b) A conversation where we see and hear each other through the screen. c) A text message.
2. What do we do to join a Meet meeting? a) Call on the phone. b) Tap the link we were sent and press "Join". c) Send an email.
3. What is muting the microphone for? a) So we are never heard. b) So we do not make noise when we are not speaking. c) To leave the meeting.
4. Which button do we use when the video call ends? a) The camera. b) The microphone. c) The red phone to hang up.
5. If they cannot see us on screen, what do we do? a) Give up on the project. b) Check that the camera button is on and re-enter if needed. c) Shout louder.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-c, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The video call enters our homes: let us respect other people's space and our own. Nobody is obliged to show their house or turn on the camera.
- Let us not record the meeting or take photos of the screen without the permission of all the participants.
- Personal and sensitive topics are not discussed in video calls with many people; they are talked about in private.
- Let us not share the meeting link with people outside the team; that way we avoid unwanted guests.
- Let us be patient with those who are learning to use the tool. We all started at some point.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
The video call lets us gather the team face to face from home, without travelling. You join with a link, you use the camera and microphone with simple buttons, you take part by taking turns to speak, and you hang up with the red phone. If something fails, you check the corresponding button and carry on. In the next level we will move from communication to the goal: we will learn to define simple, clear objectives for the project.
