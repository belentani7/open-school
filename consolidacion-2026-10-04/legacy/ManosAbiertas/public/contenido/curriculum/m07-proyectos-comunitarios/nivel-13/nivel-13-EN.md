# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 13
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn what partnerships are and why they make us grow.
- Get to know the possible partners: association, town hall, shopkeepers, school.
- Know how to ask for help and support with respect and clarity.
- Take care of the partnerships: keep your word, give thanks and inform.
- Multiply the reach of the project by working with others.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Partnership | The joint work between the project and another entity or person. |
| Partner | Someone who collaborates with the project: gives support, space, materials or knowledge. |
| Agreement | The clear commitment between the parts, of what each one gives and receives. |
| Support | The concrete help: material, economic or of services. |
| Network | The group of people and entities that collaborate with each other. |
| Shared responsibility | That all partners feel owners of the success, not just spectators. |

## 📚 Main lesson
So far, the project has grown with its own strength: the team, the plan, the spreading, the accounts. But there is a force that multiplies everything: partnerships. Working with others is not begging: it is recognising that together we reach further. Like the arms and the hands: each one is useful, but together they do more.

The possible partnerships are around any community project. The neighbourhood association, which has space and experience. The town hall, which can grant a plot of land or a licence. The shopkeepers, who can donate materials or spread the word. The school, which can open its doors to families. Each one can give what it has, and each one receives something in return.

The first partnership is the closest: the neighbourhood association. The association knows the community, has meetings, has perhaps a space. The project can use that space, and in return the project gives life to the association. It is a fair exchange: both grow. The partnership with the association is like the first stone of a bridge.

The town hall is the partner of the big resources: land, licences, authorisations. Asking the town hall is not asking for favours: it is presenting a serious project, with a plan and an objective, and asking how they can support it. The town hall does not say yes to everything, but an organised project with the community behind it has a lot of strength.

The shopkeepers of the neighbourhood are everyday partners. The baker can donate the leftover bread, the blacksmith can repair a tool, the stationery shop can print the posters. In return, the project spreads the word about their shops and fills them with people. The shopkeeper who supports the neighbourhood gains customers in the neighbourhood.

The school is a partnership that opens doors: the children carry the news home, and the families arrive at the project. An afternoon of presentation at the school, a joint activity in the yard, and the project gains a whole generation. The school and the project take care of the same thing: that the community lives well.

Asking for support has its art. First, the request is prepared: what we want, what it is for and what we offer in return. Then, it is asked with respect, to the right person and at the right moment. And you never ask for something vague: you ask for something concrete. "We need a corner of the warehouse" is better than "we need help".

A clear and fair request has more strength. The person we ask must be able to say yes: we do not ask for impossible or disproportionate things. And we must explain what the community receives. Whoever sees that their support has results, gives again; whoever sees support wasted, moves away.

The partnership does not end with the "yes". Taking care of the partnership is keeping your word, always giving thanks and informing about the results. The partner who supports the festival wants to know how it went. The shopkeeper who donated wants to see the project advance. A phone call, a photo in the group, an invitation to the opening: small gestures that keep the bridge standing.

Taking care of partnerships is also respecting the agreements. If we agreed on a date, we keep the date. If we agreed on a space, we leave the space as it was. A given word is the currency of trust, and trust is what sustains any partnership. Whoever breaks an agreement loses two partners: the current one and those who hear about it.

Partnerships create a network. The network is the group of people and entities that collaborate with each other: the project, the association, the shopkeepers, the school, the town hall. In a network, one support calls another: the baker talks with the blacksmith, the teacher with the association. The network multiplies resources without multiplying effort.

Shared responsibility is the ripe fruit of the partnership: that all partners feel owners of the success. When the festival goes well, the association feels it was also theirs; when the garden gives vegetables, the shopkeeper who donated seeds feels pride. The project stops being only the team's and becomes the whole neighbourhood's.

Not every partnership works the first time. Some entities say no, others say yes but then forget. That is not discouragement: it is a filter. Each no is a step towards the right yes, and each well-cared-for yes is a door that opens to many others. Patience is the ground where partnerships grow.

In this level we will build our first network: identifying the possible partners, preparing the requests and taking care of the agreements. We will make our project a meeting point, where the whole neighbourhood collaborates.

Let us remember: the community is bigger than the project, and the project is stronger within the community. A team that works alone does good things; a team that works with the neighbourhood does great things. Partnerships do not divide the merit: they multiply the results.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The partnership with the association.** The neighbourhood association lends its room for the meetings. In return, the project offers a gardening course for the members. The room fills up, the association has new life and the project gains a fixed space.
2. **The shopkeeper who supports.** The baker donates the end-of-day bread for the volunteers' snack. In return, the project puts a poster in the bakery's window and recommends it to new participants. The baker gains customers and pride in the neighbourhood.
3. **The town hall that grants the land.** The project presents its plan to the town hall and asks for the use of an abandoned municipal plot. The town hall accepts for three years. With the paper in hand, the project asks the school for a joint activity, and the garden becomes a project of the whole neighbourhood.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Make a list of the possible partners of your project: association, town hall, shopkeepers, school.
Step 2: Next to each one, write what it can give and what it can receive in return.
Step 3: Choose the first partner and prepare the request: what you want, what it is for and what you offer.
Step 4: Ask with respect, to the right person and with a concrete request.
Step 5: If they say yes, give thanks and agree the details in writing or in the group.
Step 6: Keep the agreement: date, space and given word.
Step 7: Afterwards, inform the partner of the results: a phone call, a photo, an invitation. A partnership is cared for like a plant.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is a partnership? a) The joint work between the project and another entity. b) A begging request. c) A competition between teams.
2. Who can be a partner of the project? a) Only the official entities. b) Association, town hall, shopkeepers, school and others. c) Only those with money.
3. How do you ask for support? a) With a vague request. b) With a clear, concrete and fair request. c) By imposing the project.
4. What sustains a partnership? a) The papers. b) Trust and a given word. c) Favours.
5. What is shared responsibility? a) That everyone feels owner of the success. b) That the project gives orders to everyone. c) That nobody does anything.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The partnership is a fair exchange: each one gives and receives in balance.
- A given word is sacred: what is promised is kept.
- Support is always thanked, publicly and with truth.
- The impossible is not asked nor is the project imposed.
- Partners are informed of the results: support deserves an answer.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Partnerships multiply the project: association, town hall, shopkeepers and school collaborate with what they have, and in return receive something. You ask clearly, keep your word and always give thanks. The project stops being the team's and becomes the neighbourhood's. In the next level we will learn to manage conflicts: the differences, the arguments and the art of reaching agreements without hurting.
