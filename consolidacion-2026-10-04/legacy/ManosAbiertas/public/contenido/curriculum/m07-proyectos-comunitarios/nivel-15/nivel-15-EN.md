# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 15
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to get to know the neighbourhood before serving it.
- Do a simple diagnosis: needs, resources and people.
- Ask and listen to the community with respect and curiosity.
- Discover the strengths and the weak points of the neighbourhood.
- Adjust the project to the reality of the community.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Diagnosis | The portrait of the neighbourhood: what it has, what it needs and who lives in it. |
| Need | What is missing or what could be improved in the community. |
| Resource | What the neighbourhood already has: spaces, services, talents and people. |
| Interview | The conversation with a person to get to know their opinion. |
| Survey | The list of simple questions to ask several people. |
| Neighbourhood map | The drawing of the neighbourhood with the places, the people and the resources. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Our project wants to serve the community. But, to serve well, you first have to know. No gardener plants without looking at the ground: the shade, the sun, the damp. The same happens with the neighbourhood. Before planting our project, we look at the ground where we are going to plant: the diagnosis.

The diagnosis is the portrait of the neighbourhood, made with our own eyes and ears. It has three parts: the needs, the resources and the people. The needs are what is missing or what can be improved. The resources are what already exists. The people are the neighbours, with their lives, their voices and their ideas.

Needs are not guessed: they are asked. What seems to be missing to one person, is not to another. The abandoned park may seem like a problem, but maybe what the neighbourhood needs most is a place for the children. Only those who live in the neighbourhood know what the neighbourhood needs. Our first tool is not the pen: it is the ear.

Asking the community has its art. The questions must be simple, open and without fear of the answer. "What is missing in the neighbourhood?" "What do you like most about here?" "What would you change?" These are questions that open conversations. You ask in the shops, in the square, at the school gate, at the meetings. The street is the best room for questions.

Resources are also discovered by looking. The neighbourhood has a square, an association, a school, shopkeepers, people with trades: the baker, the dressmaker, the gardener. Each resource is a seed of a project. The diagnosis does not only look for what is missing: it also looks for what already exists and can help.

The survey helps to ask many. A sheet with five simple questions, handed out in the square or at the church exit, gives a photograph of the neighbourhood. You do not need to ask everyone: a sample of neighbours is enough. The survey is like taking a photograph: it is not the whole street, but it shows the street.

Personal interviews are deeper. Sitting with a neighbour, having a coffee and talking calmly reveals what the survey does not see: the stories, the dreams, the fears. A good interview is not an interrogation: it is a conversation where the other feels heard. And whoever feels heard, opens up.

The neighbourhood map brings everything together. On a large sheet, the neighbourhood is drawn: the streets, the places, the resources. The needs are marked with one sign, the resources with another, the people with a third. The map gives shape to the diagnosis: the team sees the whole neighbourhood at a glance, like the pilot sees the map before the flight.

The diagnosis also reveals surprises. Sometimes, what we thought the neighbourhood needed is not what the neighbourhood needs. And that is a gift: the diagnosis saves us from planting in the wrong ground. A project born from the reality of the neighbourhood is a project with roots; a project born from imagination, without soil, dries up.

With the diagnosis in hand, the project is adjusted. Maybe the objective changes a little, maybe the date changes, maybe the place changes. Adjusting is not betraying the dream: it is making it possible. The dream is the star, the diagnosis is the map; we walk with both.

The diagnosis is also a way of welcoming and spreading the word. When we ask the community, we show that we care. When we listen to the neighbours, we make friends. The diagnosis is an invitation: "this project is yours, because first we listened to you".

The diagnosis never ends: the neighbourhood changes, and the portrait ages. A review of the diagnosis once a year is enough, like the plan review we learned in level 12. The neighbourhood is a living garden: what is a path today, may be a garden tomorrow.

In this level we will do our first diagnosis: the needs, the resources and the people of our neighbourhood. We will ask, listen, draw the map and adjust the project. We will plant our project in the right ground.

Let us remember: the project is not done for the neighbourhood, it is done with the neighbourhood. And the first step of "with" is listening. Those who listen first, sow better.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The abandoned park.** The team thinks the neighbourhood needs a new park. But the survey reveals something else: what is most needed is activities for the children. They adjust the objective: not a new park, but activities in the existing park. The project is born from what the community asked.
2. **The interview at the bakery.** A participant interviews the baker. He tells her that he has flour and an oven, and that he always dreamed of teaching how to make bread. A partnership is born: a bread course in the project, with the baker as the teacher. The resource was hidden in the neighbourhood itself.
3. **The neighbourhood map.** On the wall of the association, the team draws the map of the neighbourhood. They mark the square, the school, the bakery, the church and the empty plot. They stick notes: blue ones for the resources, red ones for the needs. The map reveals that the north side has no services, and the project decides to look there.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: On a large sheet, draw your neighbourhood from memory: streets, square, shops, school, green spaces.
Step 2: Take a walk around the neighbourhood on foot and check the map. Mark the places you see.
Step 3: Ask five people: what is missing in the neighbourhood, what do you like most and what would you change.
Step 4: Write down the answers and look for what repeats: the common needs.
Step 5: Make the list of resources: spaces, services, shopkeepers and talents of the neighbourhood.
Step 6: Mark on the map the needs and the resources with different signs.
Step 7: Compare the diagnosis with your objective: adjust the project to the reality you found.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is the diagnosis? a) The portrait of the neighbourhood: what it has, what it needs and who lives in it. b) The list of the team's complaints. c) The map of the whole city.
2. How are the needs of the neighbourhood discovered? a) By guessing. b) By asking and listening. c) By watching television.
3. What is a resource of the neighbourhood? a) What is missing. b) What already exists and can help. c) A problem.
4. What is the neighbourhood map for? a) To get lost. b) To give shape to the diagnosis and see the whole neighbourhood. c) To decorate the wall.
5. What do we do with the diagnosis in hand? a) We forget it. b) We adjust the project to the reality. c) We change neighbourhood.

Answers: 1-a, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The diagnosis listens to the community without judging its answers.
- You do not promise to solve everything: the project does what it can.
- The interviewed people know what their words are for.
- The diagnosis respects privacy: nobody is spoken about by name without permission.
- The project adjusts to the neighbourhood, not the other way around.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
The diagnosis is the portrait of the neighbourhood: needs, resources and people, discovered by asking and listening. With the map in hand, the project adjusts to reality and is born with roots. In the next level we will learn to define the mission of the project: the sentence that says what we are, why we exist and what we will leave in the neighbourhood.
