# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 01
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Seed
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what teamwork is and why it is so valuable.
- Recognize the advantages of doing things with other people.
- Learn the basic rules for living and working together in a group.
- Know that each person brings something different and necessary.
- Start thinking about the team of our own project.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Teamwork | Doing something among several people, collaborating to reach a common goal. |
| Collaboration | Each person contributing their part so the task is done better and sooner. |
| Teammate | A person with whom we share the project and who rows in the same direction. |
| Task | One of the small pieces of work that needs to be done. |
| Share out | Dividing the work among the people in the team, according to what each one knows. |
| Listen | Paying attention to what others say before answering or deciding. |

## 📚 Main lesson
In the previous level we saw that a project is a piece of work with a goal, a plan and an end. Now the question is: who does that work? In community projects, the answer is almost always the same: a team. And working as a team is a skill as old as humanity.

Let us think of the kitchen of a wedding. Nobody could prepare the food for two hundred people alone. There is someone who shops, someone who peels, someone who cooks, someone who serves and someone who washes up. Each person does one thing, but they all work towards the same dish. That is teamwork.

The first advantage is that the work gets done sooner. If four people paint a fence, it takes a quarter of the time. If only one person paints it, it can take weeks. Joining hands is joining time.

The second advantage is that it turns out better. Each person knows how to do something different: one is good with their hands, another with numbers, another with words and another with computers. When they come together, the result has a little of every talent.

The third advantage is perhaps the most important: the team sustains morale. Projects have good days and bad days. When one person falters, another lifts them up. When one is tired, another takes over. A team project is a stew that several people stir together.

However, teamwork is not automatic. It requires a few simple rules, similar to good coexistence at home. The first rule is to listen. Before imposing our idea, we must hear those of others. Sometimes the best idea is the sum of several.

The second rule is to share out. It is not about everyone doing everything, but each person doing what they are good at. Whoever can draw, draws the poster; whoever is quick with figures, keeps the accounts; whoever knows everyone, alerts the neighbours.

The third rule is to inform. If we cannot make it to the meeting or cannot do our part, we must say so in time. In a team, informing in time is almost as valuable as doing the task: it allows someone else to organize.

The fourth rule is to respect different paces. Not everyone learns at the same speed or can dedicate the same hours. A good team values the steady commitment of those who go slowly, and not just the speed of those who do a lot at once.

The fifth rule is to celebrate together. When the project advances, when a phase is completed, or when the goal is reached, the team celebrates. That shared joy is the glue that holds the group together for the next project.

We must also talk about fears. Many people of our age hesitate before joining a team: "I will not know how to do it", "I am too old", "I do not understand technology". These fears are normal and disappear with one truth: in a team nobody does everything, and there is always a suitable task for every person.

You do not need to know everything to start. You learn by doing, and you learn with others. Whoever teaches something to a teammate learns twice as much, and whoever receives the help feels accompanied. The team is also a school.

A very simple way to start is with a small team. Three or four trusted people are enough to launch a community project. Over time, the team can grow, like a family table that grows when guests arrive.

In our case, the team can be very varied: neighbours, family members, friends, people from the seniors' club or the community centre. What matters is not age or titles, but goodwill and the willingness to listen.

In this level we are not yet going to form the final team. We are only going to prepare our minds: understand what it means to collaborate, which rules will help us, and what we ourselves can offer. It will be like setting the table before the guests arrive.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The festival crew.** For the neighbourhood festival, six neighbours share out the tasks: Maria shops, Antonio sets the tables, Luisa cooks, Pedro entertains the children, Juana collects donations and Ramón cleans. None of them does everything, but together they make a great festival.
2. **The sewing club.** At the sewing club of the community centre, eight women meet every Thursday to make blankets for the hospital. Whoever can cut, cuts; whoever can embroider, embroiders; and whoever is learning sews under the guidance of the others.
3. **The building's WhatsApp group.** The residents of a building create a group to organize the works in the entrance. Each floor contributes an idea and a task: one talks to the building manager, another asks for quotes and another writes down the agreements.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Think of two or three people with whom you would like to start a small community project.
Step 2: Take the paper from the previous level (your idea and your plan) and read it again.
Step 3: Next to it, write down the names of those two or three people.
Step 4: For each person, write what you think they are good at: cooking, talking, numbers, gardening, sewing, computers, driving, etc.
Step 5: Ask yourself what you can offer. Write it down honestly, even if it is something simple.
Step 6: Think about the first small task of your project and share it out in your head among those people, according to their skills.
Step 7: Talk to one of those people and tell them your idea. Invite them for tea or coffee to discuss it calmly.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. Why is it worth working as a team? a) So the one who knows most gives the orders. b) Because the work gets done sooner, better and with more spirit. c) So you do not have to do anything.
2. What is the first rule of teamwork? a) Talking non-stop. b) Listening to others. c) Doing everything yourself.
3. What does sharing out tasks mean? a) Everyone does the same thing. b) Each person does what they are good at. c) One person does all the work.
4. If you cannot do your part, what should you do? a) Say nothing. b) Inform people in time so others can organize. c) Walk away without an explanation.
5. Do you need to know everything to join a team? a) Yes, always. b) No, in a team nobody does everything. c) Only if you are young.

Answers: 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- In a team, decisions are made together. It is not fair for one person to decide for everyone else without consulting.
- Every person has value, including those who are slower or still learning. Respecting different paces is a sign of affection, not weakness.
- Agreements are kept, and if they cannot be kept, people are told. A person's word is the basis of trust.
- Let us not use the team for personal benefit: the project belongs to the community.
- If a difference of opinion arises, it is discussed calmly and in private, without humiliating anyone. The team is a home, not a battlefield.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Working as a team means that each person contributes a part and everyone rows towards the same goal: the work gets done sooner, better and with more spirit. The basic rules are to listen, share out, inform, respect different paces and celebrate together. Nobody needs to know everything to be part of a team. In the next level we will learn to communicate with the team using WhatsApp, the most familiar tool for many of us.
