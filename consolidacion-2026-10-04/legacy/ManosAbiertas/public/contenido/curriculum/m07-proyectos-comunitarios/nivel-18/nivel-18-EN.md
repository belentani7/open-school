# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 18
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 3 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn where the project's money can come from.
- Get to know the ways of financing: membership fees, sales, donations and grants.
- Organise a fundraising campaign with honesty.
- Thank those who give and account for every euro.
- Keep the financing at the service of the mission, and not the other way around.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Financing | The way of getting the money the project needs. |
| Membership fee | The fixed, voluntary contribution that each member gives per month or per year. |
| Donation | The money or the material that someone gives without expecting anything in return. |
| Grant | The economic support of an entity, like the town hall, for a project. |
| Campaign | The organised effort, over a time, to raise funds. |
| Transparency | That every euro raised can be seen: where it comes from and where it goes. |

## 📚 Main lesson
Every project needs a little money. The seeds, the paint, the tarpaulins, the coffee at the meetings: everything costs. But money is not the heart of the project: it is the fuel. And like fuel, it is used with measure. In this level we learn to feed the project without the project becoming a slave to money.

The first way of financing is the simplest: the membership fees. Each person who belongs to the project gives a small, fixed and voluntary contribution, per month or per year. Five euros a month from twenty members is a hundred euros a month. The fees are the cleanest and most predictable money: they come from those who love the project, and depend on nobody.

Fees have rules to be fair. The contribution is voluntary: nobody is left out for not being able to give. What cannot be given in money is given in work: two hours of gardening are worth a fee. And the fees are written down and shown, as we learned in level 11. The members' money deserves members' accounts.

The second way is sales. The project makes things and sells them: vegetables from the garden, cakes at the festival, crafts at the fair. The sale has two benefits: money comes in and the project becomes known. But there is a rule: what is sold must be made with love and with quality. A stall of beautiful vegetables sells itself; a neglected stall pushes people away.

The third way is donations. There are people and entities that want to help without expecting anything in return: a shopkeeper donates material, a neighbour donates a tool, a family donates money. Donations are always thanked, from the heart and with words. And they are never asked insistently: the opportunity to help is offered, and those who want, give.

The fourth way is grants: the economic support of entities, like the town hall or the association. Grants are not alms: they are supports for projects that serve the community. To ask for them, the project is presented: what we are, what we do, what we need and how much it costs. A grant is won with a good project, written with truth.

Grants have small print: deadlines, papers, accounts to present. They are not for every project, nor for every moment. A small project can live on fees and sales; the grant arrives when the project grows. Patience and organisation decide when the time is right.

Fundraising campaigns bring several ways together in one. A campaign is an organised effort, over a time, with a clear objective: "raising three hundred euros for the garden's roof". The campaign is announced, tells the objective, and is accompanied by an account that goes up: the neighbours watch the money grow and feel part of it.

The campaign has its heart: the story. Why does the project need money? What is it for? People do not give to a cold account: they give to a story that touches them. "The garden's roof will allow the children to plant also in winter." With the right story, the money arrives and the community feels part of the miracle.

Honesty is the golden rule of money. Every euro raised has a name and a destination. The accounts are shown: where the money came from, where it went, how much is missing. Those who give have the right to know. A transparent campaign attracts more than a noisy one. Trust is the project's most valuable capital.

Money serves the mission, never the other way around. The project's objective is not to raise money: money is raised to fulfil the objective. When the mission asks for one thing and the money asks for another, the mission commands. The project does not change course because of a tempting grant: the lighthouse is the mission, as we learned in level 16.

Gratitude is the other face of financing. Those who give are thanked: with a word, a poster with the names, an invitation to the party. And what is thanked, is repeated. A thanked donation is a bridge to the next one. Gratitude is not courtesy: it is the seed of generosity.

Money is also managed with respect for people. Nobody is forced to give, and nobody is judged for not giving. The project does not measure a member's love by the size of their fee. Fees unite, they do not divide: whoever gives money and whoever gives work, gives the same heart.

In this level we will get to know the ways of financing and choose the one that best serves our project. We will prepare the account, the objective and the story of our campaign. And we will always remember: money is a good servant and a bad master.

Let us remember: the project does not need much money, it needs a little money well used. One clear euro is worth more than a hundred hidden. And a member who gives two hours of work is worth as much as a member who gives ten euros: the project's currency is the heart.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The garden's fees.** Twenty families give five euros a month. The treasurer writes everything down and shows the accounts every month. With the fees, the spring seeds and the new watering can are bought. A family that cannot pay gives two hours of work a month. They are all members, with the same right.
2. **The stall at the festival.** At the neighbourhood festival, the project puts a stall of vegetables and cakes. The vegetables come from the garden and the cakes from each family. They raise one hundred and twenty euros. The treasurer counts and announces: "with this, we buy the tarpaulins for the rains". The community applauds and helps fold the tarpaulins.
3. **The roof campaign.** The project needs three hundred euros for the garden's roof. They tell the story: the children of the neighbourhood want to plant in winter. They open an account and set the objective. The neighbours give: ten, twenty, fifty euros. In three weeks, the roof is paid for and the donors' names, on a poster, give thanks.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Calculate what your project needs: make the list of needs and their costs, as in level 11.
Step 2: Choose the ways of financing: fees, sales, donations, grants. Which ones serve your project?
Step 3: If you choose fees, define the value, the work rule and the accounts record.
Step 4: If you choose a campaign, write the objective in numbers and in words: how much, for what and for whom.
Step 5: Write the story of the campaign: why does this money change things in the neighbourhood?
Step 6: Organise the transparency: where the donations are written down, where the accounts are shown, how it is thanked.
Step 7: Plan the final thanks: a poster, a party, a public word. Gratitude closes the circle.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What are the fees? a) The fixed, voluntary contribution of the members. b) The town hall's money. c) The sale of the cakes.
2. What is a grant? a) The economic support of an entity to a project. b) A fine. c) A loan.
3. What is the golden rule of money? a) Luck. b) Honesty and transparency. c) Secrecy.
4. What commands when the mission and the money clash? a) The money. b) The mission. c) The coordinator.
5. What do we thank those who give? a) A criticism. b) A debt. c) The donation, with words and with gestures.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-c.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The project's money is sacred: every euro has a name and a destination.
- The fees are voluntary: nobody is left out for not being able to give.
- The accounts are always shown, without exceptions.
- The project does not change its mission because of money.
- Gratitude is obligatory: those who give deserve public thanks.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Financing feeds the project: fees, sales, donations and grants, with honesty and transparency. Money is a servant, not a master. In the next level we will learn to make the project's memory: the diary with the stories, the photographs and what we have learned, so nobody forgets the road travelled.
