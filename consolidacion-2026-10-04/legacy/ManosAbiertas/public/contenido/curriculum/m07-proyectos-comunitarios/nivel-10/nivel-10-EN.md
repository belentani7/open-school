# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 10
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to talk with the community to make the project known.
- Know how to choose the right places and occasions to spread the word.
- Prepare a short, clear presentation that fits in two minutes.
- Use simple publicity: posters, flyers and word of mouth.
- Invite people to take part without pressing them.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Spread the word | To make the project known to the community, so it reaches more people. |
| Publicity | The ways of informing and convincing, like posters and flyers. |
| Word of mouth | The oldest way of spreading the word: one neighbour tells another. |
| Poster | A large sheet with the project's message, to put in public spaces. |
| Flyer | A small sheet handed out in person, with the essential information. |
| Key message | The central sentence we want everyone to remember. |

## 📚 Main lesson
The project has been born; it has an objective, a plan, a team and a welcome. Now one essential step is missing: for the community to know it. A project that is not spread is like a closed flower: beautiful, but nobody sees it. Spreading the word is not vanity: it is inviting the community to take part in what belongs to it.

Spreading the word begins with the basics: knowing what to say. Before thinking about posters, we think about the message. The key message is the central sentence we want everyone to remember, short and truthful. "A garden for everyone, in the heart of the neighbourhood." "Let's make our square a better place." One sentence is enough.

Around that sentence, we build the short presentation: what we do, why we do it, when and where, and how to take part. All of this fits in two minutes. If the presentation is long, people switch off; if it is short and clear, they want to know more.

The second step is choosing where to spread the word. Every neighbourhood has its meeting places: the square, the shops, the church, the school, the association's headquarters. Where people gather is where the project is presented. Visiting the shops one by one, talking with the shopkeepers, asking permission to leave a poster: simple gestures, wide reach.

The third step is making the most of occasions. Neighbourhood festivals, fairs, residents' meetings and assemblies are moments when the community is together. On such an occasion, two minutes are enough to present the project and invite. The occasion opens the door; the clear message walks through it.

The fourth step is the simple materials. A well-made poster does not need a designer: it needs big letters, a clear message and contact details. The poster is placed where permission is asked and where it is well seen. The flyer is handed out in person, with a smile and an invitation sentence: "Want to know more? We are from the neighbourhood garden."

Word of mouth is the most powerful and cheapest form of spreading the word. When each participant tells their family and friends what the project does, the message walks by itself. And word of mouth has a secret: people believe more in those they know. The best poster is the smile of a participant who speaks.

Social networks also help, if we use them calmly. An open project group, with photos of the activities and the dates of the next ones, reaches far. But the social network does not replace the square: it completes it. The project lives in the street, not only on the phone.

There is an art to inviting: not pressing. We invite people to come, but we do not force them or make them feel guilty. Whoever does not want today may want tomorrow, and the invitation without pressure leaves the door open. "If one day you feel like it, you are invited" is better than "you have to come".

Spreading the word is also listening. When we present the project, people comment, ask and propose. Those voices are a gift: they tell us what the community feels. A project that listens to the square adjusts to the square, and a project adjusted to the community is a project that lasts.

Honesty is the basis of spreading the word. We do not promise what we cannot give, nor exaggerate the wonders. We tell the truth: what we are, what we want and what we need. The community forgives a lack of resources, but it does not forgive falsehood.

The poster, the flyer and word of mouth have one common goal: for someone who has never heard of us to take the first step. That first step is a victory of the spreading. Afterwards, the welcome must take care of them, as we learned in the previous level.

In this level we will prepare the spreading of our project: the key message, the two-minute presentation, the poster and the flyer. And we will go out into the street, because spreading the word is not done from the desk: it is done where the community lives.

Let us remember: the project belongs to the community and is for the community. Spreading the word is not seeking applause: it is giving back to the neighbourhood what was born from it. When the square knows the project, the square adopts it, and a project adopted by the community has deep roots.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The visit to the shops.** A participant goes through the neighbourhood shops with a smile and a flyer. She asks where she can leave a poster. Three shops accept. The following month, three new people arrive at the project saying: "I saw the poster at the bakery".
2. **The presentation at the neighbourhood festival.** At the festival, the coordinator asks for two minutes on the loudspeaker. He presents the project, invites people for Saturday and leaves the flyer at the association's stall. That week, five families arrive.
3. **The open group on the phone.** The team opens a project group with photos of the garden and the work dates. Participants invite their contacts. The digital spreading accompanies the street one and, little by little, the group grows as the garden grows.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Write the key message of your project in one sentence, short and truthful.
Step 2: Prepare the two-minute presentation: what we do, why, when and where, and how to take part.
Step 3: Make a list of the meeting places in your neighbourhood: square, shops, school, church, association.
Step 4: Draw a simple poster: big letters, clear message, date and contact. Check the spelling with a companion.
Step 5: Write a flyer with the essentials and print or photocopy a few copies.
Step 6: Go out with a companion to visit the places on the list, with the invitation ready and without pressing.
Step 7: Collect the results: who came? What did they ask? What does the community say? Write it down and adjust the spreading.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is spreading the project for? a) To have more of a name in the neighbourhood. b) So the community knows it and takes part. c) To fill the square by force.
2. What is the key message? a) The central sentence we want everyone to remember. b) The complete speech of the project. c) The list of tasks.
3. Where do we look for places to spread the word? a) Where people gather: square, shops, school. b) On the internet, always. c) At the post office.
4. Which is the cheapest and most powerful way of spreading the word? a) Expensive adverts. b) Word of mouth. c) Giant posters.
5. How do we invite without pressing? a) "If one day you feel like it, you are invited." b) "You have to come." c) "If you don't come, you fail."

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-a, 4-b, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- The spreading tells the truth: no promises, no exaggerations.
- Permission is asked before putting posters or flyers.
- Nobody is pressed: the invitation leaves the door open.
- What the community answers is listened to and the project is adjusted.
- The spreading serves the neighbourhood, not the team's pride.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Spreading the word is opening the doors of the project to the community: a clear message, the right places, simple materials and an invitation without pressure. Word of mouth and the street are the great allies. In the next level we will learn to take care of the materials and the accounts of the project: the inventory, the budget and transparency in money.
