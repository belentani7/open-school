# Module 7: Community Projects — Level 12
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Stem
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Learn to evaluate our work, without fear and without ego.
- Get to know the indicators of success: what tells us the project works.
- Do the honest review: what went well and what we can improve.
- Give thanks for the successes and learn from the mistakes.
- Prepare the adjustment of the plan with what we have learned.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Evaluate | To look at the work calmly and say, with truth, how it went. |
| Indicator | A sign that shows us if we are reaching the objective. |
| Review | The team's conversation about what went well and what to improve. |
| Improvement | The small change that makes the project advance more. |
| Success | That the objective has been fulfilled, wholly or in part. |
| Feedback | The opinions we receive to improve, given with respect. |

## 📚 Main lesson
After working for weeks, an important moment arrives: stop and look back. Evaluating is not looking for the guilty nor celebrating without measure: it is looking at the road travelled with truth and calm. A team that evaluates improves; a team that never looks back repeats the same mistakes.

Evaluation needs indicators. The indicator is a sign that shows us if we are reaching the objective. For the garden, the indicators can be: how many plots were sown, how many people come on Saturdays, how many kilos of vegetables were harvested. For the festival: how many people came, how much was raised, whether food was left over.

Indicators do not need to be complicated. Simple questions are enough: how many of us came, how much did we do, how did it go. What matters is that they answer the objective. If the objective is to bring the neighbourhood together, the indicator is how many people came. If the objective is learning, the indicator is what each one learned.

Choosing the indicators well is halfway there. A good indicator is clear, easy to measure and linked to the objective. "We held the festival and it seemed fine to us" is not an indicator; "eighty people came and we raised one hundred and fifty euros" is. With simple numbers, the team sees the truth.

The review is done in conversation, in the assembly, as we decided in level 08. Each person can speak, without fear and without attacking anyone. The review's questions are three: what went well, what went badly, what will we improve. Three questions, and the team learns the three answers.

What went well is thanked and celebrated. Celebrating is not vanity: it is recognising the effort and the joy of the road. A snack after the task, a word of congratulations at the meeting, a public thank-you in the group. Celebration gives strength to continue and makes the team want to repeat.

What went badly is not hidden or punished. It is written down honestly and the cause is looked for, as in level 08. Did it rain on the day of the festival? Maybe the plan should have foreseen the alternative. Was food missing? Maybe the accounts should have been reviewed. The mistake teaches when you ask the reason, not when you look for the guilty one.

The mistake is not the end of the project: it is a teacher. Whoever falls learns to get up better; whoever fails once learns not to fail the second time. The mature team is not the one that never errs, but the one that errs, learns and continues stronger. The gardener who loses one harvest knows more than the one who has never sown.

From the three questions the adjustment of the plan is born. With what we have learned, we change what needs to change: the dates, the tasks, the people responsible, the materials. The plan is not a stone: it is a plant, and the plant is pruned to grow more. Adjusting is a proof of humility and intelligence.

Feedback, other people's opinions, is a gift that sometimes comes wrapped in rough paper. Receiving criticism with respect, without taking it as a personal attack, is a team skill. And giving feedback also has its art: you point to the problem, not the person, and you always propose an improvement.

Evaluation is also done with the community. Asking the neighbours, the people who came, what they thought of the project, is listening to the truth from outside. A project that listens to the community adjusts to it and lasts. Other people's words are the mirror where the project sees itself without vanity.

The end of an evaluation is always the same: renew the strength and continue. Looking back is not to stay still: it is to walk better forward. The project is a road that is corrected while walking, like the river that changes its bed without stopping flowing.

In this level we will evaluate our work with calm and with truth. We will choose the indicators, do the review of the three questions and adjust the plan. And we will learn to receive and give feedback with respect.

Let us remember: evaluation is a mirror, not a court. The mirror returns the image without punishments and without flattery. Those who look at themselves in the mirror with courage, grow. The team that evaluates with love is a team that walks further.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The evaluation of the garden.** At the end of the quarter, the team counts the plots: six sown, two still to do. Eight people come on Saturdays, a stable number. Thirty kilos of vegetables have been harvested. With these indicators, they decide to sow one more plot and announce the project at the school.
2. **The review of the festival.** After the festival, the team meets. What went well: lots of people came and the music was good. What went badly: there was not enough food at the end. What we will improve: count the portions better and ask each family to bring a dessert. Next year will be better.
3. **The neighbour's feedback.** A neighbour tells the coordinator that the festival's music was too loud. Instead of taking offence, the team gives thanks and writes down: next year, less volume and earlier. The neighbour, surprised by the response, offers to help at the next festival.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Write the objective of your project in one sentence.
Step 2: Next to the objective, write two or three indicators: simple numbers that show if we are advancing.
Step 3: Bring the team together and do the review of the three questions: what went well, what went badly, what will we improve.
Step 4: Thank what went well and celebrate it with a simple gesture.
Step 5: For what went badly, look for the cause without looking for the guilty.
Step 6: Write the improvements in the plan: what changes, who does it and when.
Step 7: Ask two people from outside what they thought of the project. Listen, thank and write down.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is evaluating? a) Looking for the guilty. b) Looking at the work with calm and truth. c) Celebrating always.
2. What is an indicator? a) A sign that shows if we reach the objective. b) An excuse. c) A criticism.
3. What are the three questions of the review? a) Who came, who was missing, who gives the orders. b) What went well, what went badly, what will we improve. c) How much it cost, how much it is worth, how much is left.
4. What do we do with what went badly? a) We hide it. b) We look for the guilty. c) We look for the cause and learn.
5. How do we give feedback with respect? a) Pointing to the problem, not the person. b) Pointing to the person in public. c) Saying nothing.

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-b, 4-c, 5-a.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Evaluation looks for the cause, not the guilty.
- Success is thanked without vanity; mistakes are pointed out without humiliation.
- Feedback is received with respect and given with care.
- The community has a voice in evaluation: the project listens to those around it.
- Adjusting the plan is humility: no plan is perfect, they all improve.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
Evaluating is looking back in order to walk better forward: clear indicators, an honest review of the three questions and an adjusted plan. The mistake is a teacher and feedback is a gift. In the next level we will learn to build partnerships: working with the association, the town hall and the shopkeepers, and multiplying the reach of the project.
