# Module 5: Automation and Integration — Level 15
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Branch
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what a voice assistant is and what it's for.
- Get to know the most common assistants: Alexa, Google and the phone's.

## 📚 Main lesson
We start the Branch band. Until now we've automated inside screens: phone, email, integration tools. From today we leave the screen: we're going to talk to machines. Voice assistants let us give commands by speaking, without typing. It's the most natural way to automate that exists.

A voice assistant is a program that listens to what we say, understands the command and carries it out. It's like having an assistant at home who answers when you speak to them. There are many: the phone's assistant (Google's or Siri), the home smart speakers (Alexa or the Google speaker) and the computer assistants.

What do these assistants do? Many simple, useful things. They set alarms and timers, do maths, tell jokes, play music, tell the weather, read the news, make calls and control the home devices if they're connected. All of that without touching a screen.

The way to talk to them is simple: first you activate them. On the phone, with the wake word ("OK Google" or "Hey Siri") or by pressing a button. On the home speaker, with its word ("Alexa", "Hey Google"). Then you say the clear command: "Set the alarm for 8", "What's the weather tomorrow?". And the assistant answers.

We already know the phone assistant from the reminder levels: we used it to create notices by speaking. Now we'll use it for much more. And the home speakers extend that same idea to the whole house: you speak out loud, without needing the phone in your hand.

Smart speakers are the heart of home automation. When we say "Alexa, turn off the living room light", it's not magic: the speaker understands the command and sends it to the smart bulb. The automation has two parts: the ear (the speaker) and the hand (the connected device). We'll learn to connect devices in level 17.

You don't need to buy anything expensive to start. The phone assistant is already installed and free. You can also start with the computer assistant. Smart speakers are one more step, for when we really want to automate the house.

It's best to speak to the assistant calmly and clearly, without rushing. Assistants understand short, concrete commands better than long explanations. It's like talking to someone with very good hearing but little patience for rambling: direct and clear.

One important thing: voice assistants are always "listening" for the wake word. That means the microphone is attentive. Most devices have a button to mute the microphone, and it's worth using it when we don't want it to listen. Privacy is a serious matter, and we'll deal with it in the ethical dimension.

Assistants can do things that seem like magic and others that don't work quite right. Sometimes they don't understand, sometimes they're wrong. That's normal. Like all automation, assistants are learned through practice: trying, correcting, repeating. Nobody speaks to them perfectly on the first try.

You can also teach them routines, which are groups of commands that run together. "Good morning" can turn on the light, make the coffee and read the news. We'll look at that in depth in level 16. Today we'll just talk to the assistant and see that it obeys.

When you finish this level you'll have spoken to your voice assistant and given it real commands. That experience is the gateway to the automatic house. In the next level we'll set up the assistant and teach it complete routines.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Take your phone and open the assistant app (on many it's "Google", "Assistant" or "Siri"). If you have a smart speaker at home, use it too.
Step 2: Activate the assistant with its word or by pressing the corresponding button.
Step 3: Say a simple command: "What time is it?". Listen to the reply. Repeat with "What's the weather like today?".
Step 4: Ask it to do something: "Set a five minute timer". Wait for it to confirm. You'll see how a voice notice works.
Step 5: Ask it a household task: "Set the alarm for eight in the morning". You'll see the notice on the phone screen.
Step 6: Try a command it might not get right: "Tell me a joke" or "What's 34 times 6?". Observe the reply.
Step 7: Look in the assistant's settings for the microphone control or the mute button on your speaker, and learn to turn it off when you want.
Step 8: Write on your paper three commands you'd like to give it daily and three you'd never use.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- Voice assistants listen to part of what happens at home in order to activate: mute the microphone when you don't need it.
- Review the assistant's privacy settings: what it keeps, what it sends and how it's deleted.
- Don't say personal data (passwords, card numbers) in front of the assistant.
- Assistants can record by mistake: check the history from time to time and delete what you don't want kept.
- Buy the devices from trusted people and distrust offers of "free" speakers that only ask for data.
