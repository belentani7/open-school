## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| AI agent | A program that does a complete task, deciding the steps on its own. |
| Goal | The aim we give the agent: what we want it to achieve. |
| Plan | The steps the agent proposes to reach the goal. |

| Autonomy | The ability to work alone, without asking for help at every step. |

## 📚 Main lesson
We climb to the Canopy band, the highest of the practical bands. Until now, our automations did what we told them step by step: we set the rule and they followed it. Today we'll meet something more powerful and also more delicate: AI agents. An agent is a program that receives a goal and decides the steps to achieve it by itself.

To understand the difference, let's think of a kitchen helper. A bot is like a helper who only knows how to do one thing: when you say "chop", it chops; when you say "boil", it boils. An agent is like a helper you say "prepare dinner for four with what's in the fridge" to, and it decides what to cook, which steps to follow and which tools to use. The agent plans.

The key word is the goal. We give a bot a concrete command; we give an agent a goal and some room to decide. "Organise this week's emails and summarise the important ones" is a goal. The agent reads the emails, decides which are important, summarises them and presents them to you. We didn't tell it each step: we trusted it to find them.

How does the agent decide which steps to take? It uses tools. An agent can have tools: search the internet, read a document, send an email, consult a sheet, talk to another program. For each goal, the agent chooses the right tool at each moment, just as we choose the knife or the pan according to the task.

Agents work in steps, just like us. First they think: "what do I need for this?". Then they act: they use a tool. Then they look at the result: "does it work or do I have to try something else?". And they repeat until they finish. It's the same cooking cycle: taste, look, adjust. Except the program does it.

This autonomy is its great strength and its great danger. The strength: it does complete tasks without us hovering. The danger: if the goal is badly set, the agent can do things we didn't want, and very fast. That's why supervision is the golden rule of agents. We never leave an agent unwatched.

What are agents for in real life? For tasks with several steps that we repeat often: preparing a report, organising a folder, replying to a batch of messages, researching a topic, preparing an agenda. Any task we'd do by following a plan, an agent can try.

But not everything is for agents. If the task is fixed and always the same, a simple flow from the previous levels is better: cheaper, more predictable. The agent shines when the task changes, when we don't know the steps in advance, when you have to decide on the fly. It's like choosing between the washing machine (flow) and the cook (agent): depending on what's needed.

A good practice is to start with small agents and clear goals. The goal must say what we want, with which materials and what we don't want it to do. "Read the emails in the Invoices folder, summarise them and send me the summary. Don't send emails without my permission" is a good goal. The better the goal, the better the agent.

Agents are becoming an everyday tool: many programs already include them, and integration tools are adding agents to their flows. Learning to give them clear goals, supervise them and set limits is a skill of the future that is already of the present.

We must always remember that the agent doesn't understand like us: it understands instructions, not intentions. A badly written goal produces badly done work. And an agent that makes mistakes can make many mistakes in a minute. Supervision and review of its work are essential.

When you finish this level you'll understand what an agent is, how it differs from a bot and a flow, and how to set safe goals for it. In the next level we'll apply agents to a very practical area: business processes.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **The weekly summary.** The agent reviews the week's emails, picks the ones mentioning "project" and prepares a three-line summary for each one.
2. **The monthly agenda.** The agent looks at the minutes and the notifications, proposes the topics for the next meeting and orders them by priority.
3. **The neighbourhood research.** "Find grants for associations from our town council and summarise the three that fit best." The agent searches, compares and delivers the summary.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Choose one of your multi-step tasks: for example, preparing the club's weekly notice.
Step 2: Write the agent's goal clearly on your paper. It must include: what you want, with which materials and what it must not do.
Step 3: Write the list of tools the agent would need: read a sheet, look up a piece of data, write a text, send a message.
Step 4: Check whether your integration tool already offers agents (in Make search "AI" or "Agent"; you can also use an AI chat and give it the goal).
Step 5: Try giving your goal to an AI chat (like the one from module 4) and observe what plan it proposes. Evaluate: do the steps make sense?
Step 6: Correct the goal: add limits ("don't send anything without review", "use only this sheet").
Step 7: Give the corrected goal again and compare the two replies.
Step 8: Write the lesson on your paper: what changed when you improved the goal. Keep it for the next levels.

## ✍️ Self-assessment exercises
1. What is an AI agent? a) A program that does a complete task by deciding the steps on its own. b) A bot that only replies "hello". c) A virus.
2. What does the agent receive to start working? a) A clear goal. b) Instructions for each step. c) A phone.
3. How does it differ from a bot? a) It doesn't. b) The bot does one concrete command; the agent plans the steps to achieve a goal. c) The bot is more expensive.
4. What is the golden rule with agents? a) Leave them unwatched. b) Supervision. c) Never use them.
5. Which tasks do agents shine at? a) Fixed, always-the-same ones. b) Those that change and require deciding on the fly. c) None.

Answers: 1-a, 2-a, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- An agent with a bad goal can do harm quickly: set clear limits before letting it act.
- Never give an agent access to money, accounts or sensitive data without human supervision.
- The agent's work is always reviewed: what it does is a proposal, not a verdict.
- If the agent talks to people, it must say it's a program and that a human supervises it.
- You are responsible for what your agent does, just as you are responsible for what you do.

## 🧠 Summary and bridge
AI agents receive a goal and decide the steps with tools, working in cycles. They're powerful, but they require supervision, limits and well-written goals. You already understand the difference between bot and agent. In the next level we'll apply agents to business process automation: the repetitive work of a company or association.
