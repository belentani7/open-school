# Module 5: Automation and Integration — Level 05
## Language: EN · Difficulty: Root
## Estimated time: 2 hours

## 🎯 Level objective
- Understand what an integration tool like Zapier or Make is.
- Know what "connecting apps" means with a clear metaphor.
- Create a free account on an integration tool.
- Recognize a simple flow: trigger and action.
- Lose the fear of these platforms that look very technical.

## 📖 Essential vocabulary
| Term | Simple explanation |
|---|---|
| Integration | Joining two or more apps so they work together. |
| Trigger | The "if": the event that starts the automation. |
| Action | The "then": what the automation does. |
| Zap or scenario | The name of an automation in Zapier and Make. |
| App | A programme: Gmail, WhatsApp, Google Sheets, etc. |
| Account | The registration each person opens in an app. |

## 📚 Main lesson
So far we have automated within a single place: the phone, the email. From today we take a big leap: we are going to connect apps to each other so they pass the ball from one to another. And for that there are wonderful tools called Zapier and Make.

Let's think of a restaurant kitchen. There is someone who cuts, someone who cooks, someone who plates up. If each one worked on their own, the dish would never come out. But if there is a waiter who passes them the ingredients in the right order, everything works. Zapier and Make are that waiter: they take what one app produces and deliver it to another.

Let's imagine a simple case. We have a spreadsheet with the data of a club's members. We want that, when a new member arrives in the sheet, a welcome email is sent automatically. That can be done by hand: writing the email every time. Or it can be connected: when the sheet receives a new row (trigger), then the email is sent (action). That connection is an automation.

To make it clear, every integration has two parts. The trigger is the "if": the event that starts everything. The action is the "then": what is done next. In our example, the trigger is "new row in the sheet" and the action is "send welcome email".

These tools are like a universal remote control: instead of one remote for each device, a single one that talks to all. With Zapier or Make we can connect thousands of apps: email, spreadsheets, cloud storage, messages, calendars and many more.

The best of all is that there is no programming. You work with menus and cards: you choose the trigger's app, you choose the event, you choose the action's app, you choose what to do. It is like filling in a form, not like writing code. The blocks already come made; we just order them.

The first practical step is creating a free account. Zapier and Make have free plans to start. We register with an email and a password, and the tool guides us with examples and templates. There's no risk in trying: automations can be deleted.

These platforms have many ready-made templates. Instead of starting from scratch, we look for an example similar to ours and adapt it. It is like using another cook's recipe: if it already works, better than inventing our own.

A tip to start: choose a small, real automation from your life. Something that truly saves you a minute every week. Don't try to build the perfect flow of the world on the first day. Start with a single connection between two apps.

It's also good to know that these tools are the gateway to a huge world: everything we will learn in the coming bands (logic, variables, AI, agents) is built on this foundation of connecting apps. If you understand the trigger and the action today, you understand half of modern automation.

Don't be afraid of screens full of boxes and arrows. They are like a metro map: it looks messy, but each line is simple. One line: from the trigger to the action. Start reading the map line by line and leave the rest for when you need it.

When you finish this level you will have an account created and you will understand how two apps are connected. In the next level we will make our first real connection from start to finish, with your own data and your own account.

## 💡 Practical examples
1. **Welcome to the club.** Every new member who signs up in the spreadsheet receives an automatic welcome email, without anyone writing it.
2. **The payment reminder.** When a row in the club sheet turns one year old, the tool reminds the treasurer it's time to renew the payment.
3. **The photo of the day.** An association connects its photos page to its messaging account to share each new image with members.

## 🛠️ Guided activity
Step 1: Open your computer's browser and go to make.com (or zapier.com if you prefer). These are the tools we will use in this band.
Step 2: Press "Sign up free" or "Create free account". Use your email and create a strong password (a long phrase you remember).
Step 3: Confirm your email with the link they send you (check the inbox, even the Spam folder). Your account is ready.
Step 4: Look at the main dashboard. You will see buttons to create a new "scenario" (Make) or "zap" (Zapier). Don't create anything yet: just observe.
Step 5: Search the templates page for the word "welcome". You will see several ready-made automation examples. Read them calmly.
Step 6: Find in a template the two parts: the trigger and the action. Write them down on your paper.
Step 7: Sign out or simply close the browser. Your account is saved for the next level.
Step 8: On your paper, write your own connection idea: "When X happens in app A, then do Y in app B". Keep the paper.

## ⚖️ Ethical dimension
- These tools connect your accounts: give them access only to what's necessary, never more than the task needs.
- Before connecting two apps, think about what data will travel and with whom. Your data is valuable; don't give it away.
- Templates you copy from others may be badly designed: review them before activating.
- Don't connect your bank email with unknown apps.
- If an automation sends messages to other people, check the content twice before activating it.
